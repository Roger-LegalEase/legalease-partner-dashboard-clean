## 1. No DOC: adopt a bounded published-workflow rule, not a statutory exemption

The retained Judicial Branch checklist (08/24/2020), PDF page 4, expressly describes the post-2018 violation/Class B conditional-order process without a DOC investigation. Page 3 makes the DOC fee contingent on referral. NHJB-3057-DSe (08/06/2019), pages 2-3, separates the violation/Class B procedure from the Class A/felony DOC-report procedure.

RSA 651:5 IX nevertheless retains broad DOC-report language without an express streamlined exception. Preserve that conflict. Recommended product treatment: for independently established III(a)(2)/III(b)(2) eligibility, follow the published streamlined instructions, do not initiate a DOC referral or questionnaire, and do not include a routine DOC investigation charge in the expected route cost. Do not tell the participant that the statute expressly exempts them or that the platform has waived a government fee. A contrary case-specific court referral or DOC demand must trigger confirmation/review; never instruct the participant to disregard it.

Do not export this treatment to standard, pre-2019, vacated, marijuana, Class A, felony, or uncertain-highest-offense cases.

## 2. State Police: the expected post-order fee is not merely an unspecified possibility

Replace the draft's vague "possible ... up to $100" participant-facing treatment with an expected $100 Department of Safety/State Police record-correction charge following a successful conviction annulment, subject to applicable waiver/exemption and the agency's actual assessment. The checklist identifies the $100 charge, agency notification by letter, and payment directly to that agency. RSA 651:5 IX states the successful-petitioner charge; X(d) supplies the cap, removal step, and indigency-affidavit requirement.

Maintain distinct cost records for the court filing charge, DOC investigation (not routine in this bounded branch), State Police post-order correction, and any separately assessed criminal-history request. Do not count the IX and X(d) descriptions as two separate correction charges without evidence of two distinct assessments. A court fee waiver is not proof the agency has granted its own waiver. The agency waiver needs its own documented request/decision and applicable supporting affidavit.

Keep the draft's $125 court-location filing amount tied to its supporting schedule/checklist and ordinary currentness checks; this review does not independently certify a newly downloaded fee schedule or a universal waiver form.

## 3. Conditional grant: separate receipt, final court action, and agency implementation

Keep the court-controlled notice procedure. Correct the reasoning about the form's "Date Sent to Prosecutor": sending is not receipt. Do not calculate the statutory objection deadline from the filing date, sending date, or the participant's receipt date. Prefer a court-specified deadline; otherwise require a verified prosecutor-receipt anchor and applicable time-computation rules. If the anchor is unavailable, display an unconfirmed deadline, not an invented date.

Do not report a completed annulment merely because twenty days have elapsed. Keep distinct, evidenced workflow facts: conditional/interim order; objection window; any objection and court disposition; final entered annulment order/certificate; State Police payment or waiver and record-update confirmation. These are conceptual states, not instructions to invent new repository enums.

Recommended participant instructions: retain the order, monitor correspondence, comply with court requests, obtain legal help for opposition or disputed eligibility, obtain confirmation of the final court action, and complete any separately notified agency payment/waiver requirement. An ordinary status inquiry or document request does not itself require a lawyer. Do not invent a second petition or routine participant service of the prosecutor. No guaranteed certificate-mailing or database-update deadline is supplied by this review.

## 4. Highest offense: a bounded manual-review branch, not a family-wide freeze

Retain the draft's refusal to resolve ambiguous cross-date relationships through date alone. Different disposition dates are not, by themselves, a statutory disqualification. A record-dependent ambiguity should produce a manual legal-review outcome rather than automatic ineligibility, an unsupported standard-route referral, or a global block on all streamlined cases.

Require a documented court-record basis for the highest-offense conclusion. Do not require a new judicial finding or a magic "highest offense" label for a genuinely unambiguous record. Where the comparison group, offense classification, or disposition relationship remains uncertain, no automated positive eligibility or speculative filing.

Keep whole-record eligibility independent of the highest-offense screen. Explicitly include the paragraph III subsequent-conviction/clean-period condition, not only elapsed years since sentence completion. Preserve the applicable III exceptions, IV denial restriction, V/VI restrictions, VI-a out-of-state equivalence treatment, and VII pending-charge rule. Do not reduce eligibility to the maximum of completion dates plus waiting periods, and do not treat every pending matter as the same statutory bar without checking VII's predicate. An additional conservative product stop must be labeled as such.

An actual adoption can approve this bounded decision process without purporting to settle the legal construction for every cross-date case. Family acceptance still depends on the governing coverage contract and proof of both allowed and withheld branches. Do not remove a route, relabel a packet as guidance-only, or count excluded scenarios as delivered packets to improve the denominator.
