## 1. Hawaii — actual Chapter 853 deferral of a §712-1200 charge

### Source-derived answer

HRS §712-1200(4)(c) supplies a **three-year period following discharge** where proceedings were actually deferred under Chapter 853. Subsection (4)(b) disallows that deferral for a subsequent offense. Chapter 853 ordinarily joins successful discharge with dismissal; require the real disposition rather than equating ordinary probation with deferred acceptance. [HI-1, HI-2]

The issuer's one-page application, **HCJDC 159(b), Rev. 06/03/2026**, also prints the three-year §712-1200 rule in its non-conviction section. The general HCJDC webpage still uses four-year language. Preserve that source conflict in the research record; use the current statutory subsection and the matching application as the basis for the proposed correction, rather than treating a stale general webpage as a new four-year requirement. [HI-3, HI-4]

The ordinary Chapter 853 one-year period is not added to the special three-year period. Calculate from the discharge date. Where records show separate discharge and dismissal events, require dismissal as well as expiration of the period after discharge; do not silently change the statutory anchor to an arrest or conviction date. [HI-1, HI-2]

### Proposed participant instruction

> This application concerns a prostitution charge under HRS §712-1200 for which the court granted deferred proceedings under Chapter 853 and later discharged you and dismissed the charge. The special waiting period is three years after discharge. Check the discharge-and-dismissal order before applying. An ordinary conviction or probationary sentence does not qualify for this particular deferred-disposition route.

### Actual-form mapping boundaries

- **SSN is optional on the issuer form.** Do not turn it into a required application field or display an invented value.
- The form asks the person to initial the applicable application statement. For this route, use the non-conviction treatment, not the separate first-time drug/property/DUI-conviction application statement. Do not draw a fabricated checkbox over an initials field, or execute the initials/signature/date on the participant's behalf.
- Do not automatically assert that the person is not a fugitive merely because the selected route is a deferred disposition. The person must truthfully review and execute the application statement.

- The current form has no arrest/case table. Collect necessary disposition and timing facts upstream; do not invent a table on top of the issuer form.
- The form's demand for a court order **granting expungement of a conviction** belongs to its conviction branch. Do not misapply it to this non-conviction application. ## 2. Nebraska — §29-3005 set-aside, then adult sealing

### Correction to earlier research

A direct-result-only description is too narrow. The current statute distinguishes:

1. A **prostitution-related offense**, as defined in §29-3005(1), committed while the movant was a victim of sex trafficking **or** proximately caused by that status.
2. An **other offense** committed as a direct result of **or** proximately caused by that status.

After completion of the sentence or juvenile disposition, the movant may file in the county, district or separate juvenile court of the county of conviction/adjudication. The statute supplies the findings that require the court to grant the motion. It does not impose a further multi-year waiting period in this section. [NE-1]

Do not collapse the two branches, omit proximate cause, or collect a mandatory trafficking nexus on the first branch that is narrower than the statute. The application should gather the participant's facts; the court makes the necessary findings.

### Evidence and privacy provisions that must not be lost

Government documentation is not the only documentation that qualifies for the statutory rebuttable presumption. Section (4) also identifies affidavits or sworn testimony from an attorney, clergy member, medical professional, trained victim-services professional, or other professional from whom the movant sought relevant assistance. The presumption concerns victim status; it does not automatically establish every other element. Official documentation is not a prerequisite to relief. Other sufficiently credible and probative evidence may be considered. [NE-1]

On the movant's request, a hearing on this motion **must be held in camera**; the statute also makes the rules of evidence inapplicable to such a hearing. This does not authorize a promise that all filings are automatically confidential or that the later sealing hearing follows the same rule. Do not solicit unnecessarily graphic evidence or invent testimony. [NE-1]

### Proposed first-stage drafting blueprint

This is a custom motion under §29-3005, not CC 6:12. Use the actual original case caption and identify the offense, adjudication/conviction, sentence/disposition completion, selected statutory branch and the factual basis supplied by the participant.

Proposed branch-specific clauses for counsel's adaptation:

> **Prostitution-related branch:** The movant seeks relief under §29-3005(2)(a) and (3)(a). The offense identified in this motion is prostitution-related within subsection (1). The supporting account explains the applicable basis: the movant was a victim of sex trafficking at the time of the offense, or participation in the offense was proximately caused by that status.

> **Other-offense branch:** The movant seeks relief under §29-3005(2)(b) and (3)(b). The supporting account explains how participation in the identified offense was a direct result of, or proximately caused by, the movant's status as a victim of sex trafficking.

> **Optional hearing request:** The movant requests that any hearing relating to this motion be conducted in camera under §29-3005(6).

Do not print both factual alternatives as already true. Include only the selected, fact-supported allegations. Attach an appropriate sworn factual account and available supporting material using the court's accepted execution and filing procedure. Proposed findings/order remain unexecuted; do not represent a court finding as an established participant fact.

### Second-stage adult sealing

After a signed trafficking-based set-aside order, an adult may seek sealing under §29-3523(4), using **CC 6:12, Motion to Seal Adult Criminal Record, Rev. 04/2024**. It is a two-page form; the companion **CC 6:12a instructions are Rev. 06/2024**, at the non-obvious `CC-6-12-a.pdf` URL. The form assumes the set-aside has already occurred. [NE-2, NE-3, NE-4]

The actual form asks for the original case, charges and dates, the applicable disposition and the claim that the record remains public. Do not automatically check all grounds, turn a charge date into a conviction date, or assert continued public access without the relevant fact. Its second page includes participant signature/contact information and an inability-to-receive-email alternative. Preserve the actual execution fields. The court's adult-sealing guidance says the court notifies the county or city attorney and schedules the matter. [NE-3, NE-5]

Juvenile adjudications use the juvenile-sealing mechanism referenced in §29-3523(4), **not the adult CC 6:12**. [NE-2]

**Remaining narrow requirements:** First-stage local motion service/scheduling, accepted declaration execution and any sensitive-filing protections must be specified for the actual court. Do not copy the second-stage clerk-notice procedure into stage one. Preserve the repository's genuine counsel-classification requirement; this research supplies the corrected law and drafting structure, not that approval.

## 3. Washington — deceased homicide victim, family-member applicant

### Source-derived answer

RCW 9.96.060(7) authorizes a family member to apply to the **sentencing court** on behalf of a deceased homicide victim with a prostitution conviction under **RCW 9A.88.030**. It does not add a trafficking finding, an estate appointment or a waiting period in that subsection. Keep municipal-ordinance convictions or disputed family relationships for the appropriate narrower review rather than silently broadening the listed offense. [WA-1]

The applicant is the living family member. The defendant in the original criminal caption is the deceased person. A declaration can state the applicant's own identity and relationship in the first person, while describing the deceased person's conviction and homicide status in the third person, with a factual source identified. Never make the applicant adopt a living defendant's personal conviction history as their own.

### Concrete official local implementation

Kitsap District Court publishes **Form 950780, revised 08/08/2024**, a three-page petition, declaration and instructions. Page one retains the defendant caption and introduces the applicant separately. Page two separates the applicant's name and relationship from the defendant's convictions and homicide status. This is direct evidence of a workable applicant/victim distinction, not a form to promote statewide. [WA-2]

Its instructions specify notice form **950790**, copies of the petition and notice to the prosecutor on the same day as filing, and hearing attendance. They expressly say that the applicant does **not** need to prepare a proposed order because the judge prepares it at the hearing. Do not insert a mandatory proposed-order task into this Kitsap branch. [WA-2]

The form contains a citation missing the "A" in one reference to the prostitution statute. The correct governing citation is RCW 9A.88.030. Preserve the original source bytes and record the discrepancy; do not invent a corrected issuer edition. Use the correct citation in separately authored LegalEase instructions and pleadings. [WA-1, WA-2]

### Proposed non-Kitsap drafting blueprint

Use a custom pleading only through the actual court's accepted vehicle and the repository's applicable design approval. The following is a proposed content outline, not a statewide official form:

> Applicant [family member's full name], [relationship] of deceased defendant [original defendant's name], requests vacation of the identified conviction under RCW 9.96.060(7). The original case is [court and cause number]. The conviction to be addressed is [exact RCW 9A.88.030 conviction and identifying record]. The attached or described records support that the defendant is deceased and was a homicide victim. The applicant requests relief on the deceased defendant's behalf under subsection (7).

The supporting declaration should state the applicant's relationship, the basis of their knowledge, the record sources and any authentication facts they can truthfully supply. The court determines whether the applicant qualifies. Do not state that a particular death or relationship certificate is universally mandated by subsection (7); distinguish supporting evidence, product safeguards and actual local requirements.

**Remaining narrow requirements:** Outside Kitsap, confirm local scheduling/service, accepted declaration execution, any proposed-order practice and the custom filing vehicle. The statewide inventory of general CrRLJ vacatur forms is not evidence that the living defendant's first-person affidavit should be signed by a relative. [WA-3]

## 5. Delaware — the correct SBI process, not a generic background check

The official SBI page distinguishes the expungement/pardon enrollment code **27S23V** from the personal-use history code **27RVGT**. Its linked "Expungement Application" is a one-page service-code/enrollment instruction sheet, not a standalone initiating court petition or an eligibility determination. [DE-1, DE-2]

For the agency application described on that page, the initial fee is **$72**. After review, a mandatory-eligible applicant must return signed paperwork **within 30 days of receipt**, with a **$75 money order payable to Delaware State Police**. Otherwise SBI sends discretionary-process information. Do not collect or present the second fee as unconditional; do not turn this into a claim that every person needs a paid application. Preserve existing automatic-relief screening. [DE-1]

### Proposed participant wording

> For the SBI expungement application process, enroll through the State Police's linked fingerprint service using code **27S23V**. The initial application fee is **$72**. Follow the registration instructions for the appointment or permitted mail-in option and identification. After SBI reviews the application, read its eligibility notice. If SBI says you qualify for mandatory expungement, return the signed paperwork within **30 days after you receive it**, together with a **$75 money order payable to Delaware State Police**. If SBI instead sends discretionary-expungement information, follow that separate court process. Do not use the personal-background-check code **27RVGT** as a substitute.

The issuer service-code sheet describes online enrollment and a telephone alternative, **1-866-761-8069**. It requires valid, unexpired photo identification and addresses name-change documentation. Do not hardcode an unverified mail-in destination; use the registration instructions. A date embedded in the sheet's URL is not a printed revision date. [DE-1, DE-2]
