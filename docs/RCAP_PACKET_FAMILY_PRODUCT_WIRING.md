# Packet-family product wiring

What connects a completed packet family to the paid product path, and what
refuses to.

## What was open

The packet route resolver is the single authority on what a packet route may
do, and it answers three different questions per route:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| `rendererKind` | can the shared factory produce bytes for this route? |
| `sellable` | may this route take money? |
| `creditConsumable` | may this route spend a sponsored allocation? |

For every route kind except `factory_v2` the three moved together: no renderer
meant no sale and no credit. `factory_v2` is the one branch where they come
apart on purpose. It resolves **with** the shared renderer and **with**
`sellable: false, creditConsumable: false`, because — in the branch's own words
— "being buildable is not the same question as being approved, technically
current, free of a problematic-PDF hold, or public. Those gates live elsewhere."

Nothing read `sellable` or `creditConsumable`. The money gate
(`assertPacketRouteCanDeliver`, `createConsumerPaymentPlaceholder`) and the
credit gate (`buildRenderJobSpec`) both asked `packetRouteCanRender`, which is
the renderer question. So a `factory_v2` route the resolver declared unsellable
was shown a $50 price and admitted to Stripe Checkout, and a durable render job
— the thing that finalizes an artifact and draws the one packet credit on a
sponsored generation — was built for it.

Measured at the time of the change: **60 `factory_v2` routes were
payment-eligible at the evaluator**, of which 39 carried no recorded counsel
approval for the packet, and **all 60** were served by packet families whose own
completion proofs record `runtimeStatus: runtime_disabled`, `packetReady:
false`, `productionEnabled: false` and `counselAdopted: false`.

The money-gate verifier did not catch this. It probed with `pathway.label`,
while the runtime carries the pathway **id** in `pathwayLabel`
(`eligibility-adapter.ts` sets `pathwayLabel: engineResult.pathwayId`). A label
matches no compiled pathway id, so every probe fell through to `guidance_only`
and the `factory_v2` branch was never exercised once.

## What now holds

`packetRouteCanSell` and `packetRouteCanConsumeCredit` in
`src/lib/rcap/documents/packet-route-resolver.ts` are the resolver's own
decisions, intersected with renderability. Money is the intersection, never the
union.

```text
money      ⊆ sellable          ∩ renderable
credit     ⊆ creditConsumable  ∩ renderable
```

- `createConsumerPaymentPlaceholder` shows a price only where
  `packetRouteCanSell`.
- `assertPacketRouteCanDeliver` refuses Checkout with
  `ConsumerPacketNotDeliverableError` anywhere it does not hold.
- `buildRenderJobSpec` returns no job where `packetRouteCanConsumeCredit` does
  not hold, so there is no artifact finalization and no path into packet-credit
  accounting.

The preserved legacy generators (MS, IL, DC, PA, TX) resolve `legacy_verified`,
which is sellable and credit-consumable, and are untouched. An accepted exact
deferral, component deferral or complete-guidance treatment keeps outranking the
jurisdiction's classification exactly as before.

## The precheck

`data/record-clearing/packet-family-product-readiness.json`, regenerated by
`npm run rcap:generate-packet-family-readiness`, answers per family whether it
may be connected at all, from committed evidence:

| Condition | Measured against |
|---|---|
| `artifactPresent` | every declared implementation output exists in this tree |
| `artifactHashCurrent` | every present output hashes to its recorded sha256 |
| `specificationCurrent` | the adopted legal-design record and the legal design memo hash to their recorded sha256 |
| `sourceIdentityCurrent` | the packet proof hashes to the sha256 the counsel manifest recorded |
| `staleArtifactBlockClear` | no problematic-PDF register record blocks a form the family's routes need |
| `outputLegalApprovalKnown` | the owner legal decision names the family, or does not — never inferred from its jurisdiction |
| `familyRuntimeEnabled` | the family's own proof records runtime-enabled, packet-ready, production-enabled |
| `routeIdentityExact` | every route key the family claims is recorded under it in the factory_v2 registry |

`npm run rcap:verify-packet-family-readiness` proves the runtime honours it, by
driving the shipped resolver, the shipped checkout guard and the shipped job
builder over every route every family claims.

Position at this commit: **0 of 57** completed packet families are product-ready.
57 lack their implementation output and their legal design memo in this tree —
the evidence import that brought the families here was deliberately data-only
("no src, no image inputs") — 53 record `runtime_disabled` / `packetReady:
false` / `productionEnabled: false` in their own proof, 22 have no route
recorded under them in the factory registry, and 3 (IN, MS, ND custom pleading)
carry technical evidence the owner decision's annex records as superseded.

## What this does not do

It makes nothing sellable, and it is not the route-enablement decision.
`auth-2026-08-19-owner-legal-approval-completed-output` is the only owner legal
decision in `data/rcap-authorization-queue.json`, and it names its own limits:
`turning any route public or sellable` is one of the eight things it expressly
does not authorize. No `route_enablement` entry exists. A family that becomes
product-ready still needs that decision before any route may be flipped, and the
readiness verifier asserts the withholding is still on record — if it changes,
the fence's premise has changed and must be re-read before anything is wired.

## Known red gate: the worker image fingerprint

`node scripts/generate-rcap-staging-action.mjs --check` is red on this branch,
and it is red *because of* this change. It was green at the parent commit.

This is the gate working, not a defect. `IMAGE_INPUT_PATHS` in that generator
covers all of `src/` plus `package.json`, so **any** change under `src/` makes
the recorded worker-image fingerprint stale and makes the already-published
worker image "an image for other bytes". Binding the money and credit gates
changed four image inputs:

- `package.json`
- `src/lib/expungement-ai/payment-adapter.ts`
- `src/lib/rcap/documents/packet-route-resolver.ts`
- `src/lib/rcap/render/job-contract.ts`

Clearing it requires regenerating the fingerprint at the accepted tip **and
republishing the worker image at that SHA**. Regenerating the fingerprint alone
does not clear it — the generator then refuses on the publication evidence
instead. Both belong to whoever owns the freeze, and this workstream is
forbidden from republishing that worker or deploying anything, so it does
neither. `docs/RCAP_SELLABLE_PATHWAY_CLOSURE.md` records the same condition for
the delivery-gate binding that preceded this one.

The alternative was refused for the same reason it was refused there. Reverting
would restore a $50 price and a Stripe Checkout on 60 payment-eligible routes
that the packet route resolver declares unsellable, served by packet families
that are runtime-disabled, on routes no decision on record authorizes turning
sellable. A red freeze gate on a branch that is not being deployed is the
correct cost.
