## What this document is

The track/pathway crosswalk pairs each *registry track* (a catalogued statutory relief
mechanism) with the *compiled pathway* that actually implements it in the runtime. Thirty-eight
of those pairings could not be made automatically and were opened as Milestone 1 item 2
blockers. This lane owned nine of them — every unmapped compiled pathway in Illinois, Montana,
New Hampshire and Vermont.

For each subject the question is narrow: **does a registry track correspond to this pathway,
and can that be shown on authority already committed to this repository?** Three answers are
permitted — *resolved* (a counterpart is named on citation), *terminalized* (it is shown that no
counterpart exists), or *still blocked* (the answer needs primary authority this repository does
not hold). "No citation available" is not an answer, and a mapping asserted without a rule a
later pass can re-apply is not an answer either.

### 1. Criminal identity-theft / mistaken-identity relief — **terminalized (registry gap)**

`compiled_pathway:IL:criminal-identity-theft-mistaken-identity-relief` · confidence **medium**

**What the pathway does.** Where a person was arrested or convicted under the name of someone
whose identity was stolen or used without authorisation, the aggrieved person may petition the
chief judge of the circuit where the arrest occurred to correct the arrest, conviction and
official records — striking their name and substituting the true offender's name if it is known.

**Conclusion.** No Illinois registry track corresponds to this pathway, and the reason is one of
kind rather than of coverage. This is a **record-correction** remedy: it does not clear a record,
it fixes whose record it is. Every Illinois registry track is a record-*clearing* mechanism.

**How that was established.** The crosswalk carries exactly seventeen Illinois registry tracks
(`il-auto-seal-2028`, `il-auto-seal-2029`, `il-cannabis-auto`, `il-cannabis-vacate`,
`il-exp-nonconv`, `il-exp-pardon`, `il-exp-precompletion`, `il-exp-qualprob`,

`il-prostitution-j-vacate`, `il-seal-2yr`, `il-seal-3yr`, `il-seal-edu`, `il-seal-nonconv`).
That is a closed set, and every member of it is an expungement, sealing or vacatur mechanism
under 20 ILCS 2630/5.2 subsections (b), (c), (d), (e), (e-5), (e-6), (g), (i), (j), (k) and (l),
together with 410 ILCS 705/10-5, 730 ILCS 5/3-3-2(a)(10), 730 ILCS 5/5-5.5, Ill. S. Ct. R. 9 and

**Authority.** 20 ILCS 2630/5.2 (Criminal Identification Act), identified in the compiled
profile as the Illinois primary statute.

**Why confidence is medium.** The operative *subsection* is not pinned anywhere in this
repository — the compiled pathway states the rule and cites the statute as a whole. The gap

## New Hampshire

### 4. DWI / DUI annulment — **resolved**

`compiled_pathway:NH:dwi-dui-annulment` · confidence **medium**

**Counterpart:** `nh_conviction_standard` — "Petition to Annul the Record of Arrest, Conviction
and Sentence, Standard Route (RSA 651:5, III(a)(1), (b)(1) and (c) through (i))."

**Authority.** RSA 651:5, the annulment statute, with the DWI waiting-period carve-out at
RSA 265-A:21. Pin cite: RSA 651:5, III — the conviction waiting-period subsection.

**The reasoning.** RSA 265-A:21 does not create a separate annulment petition. It lengthens the
waiting period for one class of conviction that is annulled under RSA 651:5 like any other. A
petitioner with a DWI files the same RSA 651:5 annulment petition as any other convicted
petitioner; what changes is the clock — generally ten years from conviction rather than the
ordinary class-based period.

Two committed sources place the DWI rule *inside* the RSA 651:5 structure rather than beside it:

1. The compiled pathway states that "New Hampshire's **main statute** carves out RSA 265-A:21
   from the ordinary waiting-period structure." The carve-out is an exception within RSA 651:5,
   and its subject is the waiting period, not the remedy.
2. The New Hampshire state pack enumerates "Conviction waiting periods (RSA 651:5): violation —
   1 year; Class B misdemeanor — 2 years; … DWI/DUI-type offense — special 10-year rule (verify
   under RSA 265-A:21)" — placing the DWI period as the last entry in the RSA 651:5 conviction
   waiting-period list itself.

The registry counterpart is therefore the track that owns that petition,
`nh_conviction_standard`, whose authorities include RSA 651:5, III and whose stated span,
III(a)(1), (b)(1) and (c) through (i), contains the class-by-class waiting periods.

**What this means for coverage.** This pathway is a **variant** co-mapped to
`nh_conviction_standard` alongside the already-mapped `conviction-annulment-under-rsa-651-5`. It
does not add a new track to the denominator; it is a second runtime entry point to the same
statutory remedy.

**The competing candidate, and why it was rejected.** The only other unmapped New Hampshire
conviction track is `nh_conviction_streamlined`, the mandatory streamlined route for violations
and Class B misdemeanours under RSA 651:5, III(a)(2) and III(b)(2). A first-offence New
Hampshire DWI is a Class B misdemeanour, so the track deserved a look. It was rejected on the
operative rule: the streamlined route is defined by *short mandatory timing*, and this pathway is
defined by being carved **out** of the ordinary timing onto a ten-year wait. The two are
incompatible.

**Why confidence is medium.** No New Hampshire registry track names RSA 265-A:21, and this
repository does not pin which paragraph of RSA 651:5, III carries the DWI carve-out. The mapping
rests on the state pack classifying the DWI rule as one of the RSA 651:5 conviction waiting
periods — solid, and committed, but short of a citation identity. Counsel should confirm the
paragraph.

### 5. Out-of-state, federal, or military record guidance — **terminalized (scoped out / routing)**

`compiled_pathway:NH:out-of-state-federal-or-military-record-guidance` · confidence **high**

**Conclusion.** This pathway is not a relief mechanism, so no registry track can be its
counterpart. It is a jurisdictional-referral advisory: it tells a user that New Hampshire courts
cannot annul federal, military, Tribal or out-of-state records, and that relief must be sought in
the jurisdiction that issued the record.

**How that was established.** Four markers on the compiled pathway object each independently
establish the class — `routeType` is `out_of_scope`; `filingRequired` is `false`;
`suggestedResultCode` is `not_covered_yet`; and the packet mode is
`automatic_relief_verification_and_guidance`. Its operative content is the assertion that New
Hampshire offers *no* remedy: "New Hampshire courts can only annul New Hampshire records… A New
Hampshire annulment petition can address New Hampshire arrests, charges, convictions, and
sentences. It does not directly annul a federal, military, Tribal, or out-of-state conviction."
The state pack says the same: "Federal/military/Tribal/out-of-state records cannot be annulled
through a New Hampshire court."

The registry catalogues New Hampshire statutory relief mechanisms. A pathway whose content is
the *absence* of one has no possible registry counterpart — and mapping it to any of the eight
New Hampshire tracks would assert relief that this pathway exists to deny.

**Authority.** RSA 651:5 (with RSA 651:5-b), the New Hampshire annulment power, which reaches
only New Hampshire court records. The scope limit is stated in the repository as a rule rather
than quoted from statutory text, and is not pinned to a subsection here.

**Note for QA.** This is a correct and legally necessary guardrail. Terminalizing it removes it
from the coverage denominator; it does **not** remove it from the runtime, and it should not be
removed from the runtime. A user with a federal or out-of-state record needs exactly this answer.

### 6. Adult conviction expungement — narrow statutory route — **resolved**

`compiled_pathway:VT:adult-conviction-expungement-narrow-statutory-route` · confidence **high**

**Counterpart:** `vt_exp_decriminalized` — "Expungement of a conviction for conduct no longer
prohibited, 13 V.S.A. § 7602(a)(1) and (b)."

**Authority.** 13 V.S.A. § 7602(a)(1) — the eligibility trigger: conviction for an offence whose
underlying conduct is no longer prohibited by law or no longer designated as a criminal offence;
and § 7602(b) — the grant standard: the court grants on completion of sentence or supervision
with restitution and surcharges paid unless waived.

**How the mapping was made.** The registry track names two elements. The compiled pathway's
defining clause states both, in the same order: the § 7602(a)(1) trigger ("may petition to
expunge a conviction record **only if** the person was convicted of an offense where the
underlying conduct is no longer prohibited by law or no longer designated as a criminal offense")
and the § 7602(b) grant standard ("The court grants if the person completed sentence/supervision
and paid restitution and surcharges, unless surcharges were waived"). The Vermont state pack
carries the same rule with the explicit citation field `13 V.S.A. § 7602`.

**Discriminator.** The only other unmapped Vermont *expungement* track,
`vt_exp_deferred_sentence`, is scoped to 13 V.S.A. § 7041(e) and 28 V.S.A. §§ 204/204a —
discharge from a deferred sentence — a different statute that appears nowhere in this pathway.
The sealing tracks are excluded on the face of § 7602(a), which separates expungement at
(a)(1) from sealing at (a)(2).

**A practice note carried forward.** The pathway warns that old cannabis conduct is the common
example but not a sufficient one — the exact statute, amount, offence and current law must be
verified before telling a user the route fits.

### 9. Juvenile sealing — **terminalized (registry gap)**

`compiled_pathway:VT:juvenile-sealing` · confidence **high**

**What the pathway does.** For delinquency matters adjudicated on or after 1 July 1996, Vermont
generally seals the record two years after final discharge, unless the State's Attorney objects
and the court finds a listed-crime issue or a lack of rehabilitation. Dismissed juvenile matters
charged on or after 1 July 2006 and before the age of majority are sealed immediately on
dismissal; older dismissed matters may be sealed two years after dismissal.

**Conclusion.** Vermont juvenile delinquency-record sealing is implemented in the runtime but has
no registry track. **The gap is on the registry side.**

**How that was established.** No Vermont registry track mentions juvenile or delinquency records
anywhere in its record — a case-insensitive search for "juvenile" or "delinquen" across all
eleven Vermont tracks returns zero hits.

Two Vermont tracks cite 33 V.S.A. § 5119, and neither is the delinquency branch:

- `vt_seal_under_25` is scoped by its own authorities to § 5119(g), (g)(1)–(3), (j), (k) and (m),
  and is "Sealing of records of an offence committed before age 25" — an **adult criminal-court**
  route for young offenders. It is already mapped to the compiled pathway
  `offense-before-age-25-sealing-under-33-v-s-a-5119-g`.
- `vt_seal_dui` lists § 5119 only as a cross-reference for a DUI committed under 25.

The compiled runtime keeps the branches apart, and so does the state pack, which lists
"Under-25 sealing … (33 V.S.A. § 5119(g))" and "Juvenile sealing — delinquency records …
(33 V.S.A. § 5119)" as separate pathway entries with separate citation fields, and gives them
separate waiting-period rules — "Under-25 sealing (33 V.S.A. § 5119(g)): 2 years after final
discharge, plus no listed-crime conviction/adjudication in the prior 10 years" against "Juvenile
delinquency sealing (33 V.S.A. § 5119): generally 2 years after final discharge for adjudications
on/after July 1, 1996."

**Authority.** 33 V.S.A. § 5119, delinquency branch. The paragraph letters of that branch are not
pinned in this repository — a citation-granularity residual that does not affect the gap finding,
which rests on the complete enumeration of the eleven committed Vermont tracks.
