### A method note that matters for reading the Texas and Utah sections

Several compiled profiles append a shared corpus of agent-training text to *every*
pathway in the state, so the mere presence of a citation inside a pathway record proves
nothing about which pathway it belongs to. The E2 evidence wave established the correct
handling for this (originally on Alabama): only the **pathway-defining text** — the
label, the summary, and the leading rule clauses unique to that pathway — may be used as
the discriminator. This lane follows that rule throughout. It bites hardest in Texas,
where all five expunction pathways share one identical summary and one identical rule
block, leaving the label as the only discriminating text.

## Connecticut

### `compiled_pathway:CT:cannabis-conviction-erasure` — Cannabis-conviction erasure

**Resolved →** registry track **`ct-cannabis-auto`** (Automatic Erasure of Certain
Cannabis Possession Convictions).
**Authority:** Conn. Gen. Stat. § 54-142u. **Confidence:** high.

Connecticut splits cannabis record clearing into two mechanisms, and the registry names
both: an automatic branch under § 54-142u (`ct-cannabis-auto`) and a petition branch under
§ 54-142v (`ct-cannabis-petition`).

The compiled pathway is the automatic branch. It is configured as `automatic: true`,
`filingRequired: false`, `routeType: "automatic"`, and its defining clause reads:

> Cannabis erasure — Auto-erasure of § 21a-279(c) possession convictions
> (1/1/2000–9/30/2015); took effect Jan 2023

That is the offense statute, the covered window and the effective date of the automatic
mechanism. The repository confirms independently that the window belongs to § 54-142u:
`registry-crosswalk-projection.json` records the scope note *"The conviction falls outside
the § 54-142u window, which routes to the cannabis erasure petition track."*

**Rule a later pass can re-apply:** a Connecticut cannabis pathway that is automatic and
bounded by the 1/1/2000–9/30/2015 window is § 54-142u / `ct-cannabis-auto`; one that
requires a filing, or that reaches convictions outside that window, is § 54-142v /
`ct-cannabis-petition`.

**A conflict resolved.** The E2 wave left two inconsistent findings on this point:
`E2-B1.json` proposed `ct-cannabis-petition` as a co-mapped track for this pathway, while
`E2-A1.json` found `ct-cannabis-petition` terminal and *missing from compiled runtime*.
They cannot both stand. This lane adopts E2-A1, which is also what the committed
crosswalk records: the pathway contains no filing branch at all, so it cannot represent
the § 54-142v petition. **`ct-cannabis-petition` is therefore not co-mapped and remains
an unimplemented registry mechanism** for the registry-owner-action lane.

**Subsection granularity:** § 54-142u is cited as a whole for the erasure grant. The
committed authority set pins only § 54-142u(b) (multi-count bar), (f), (g) and (h); the
grant paragraph itself is not pinned in this repository, and no pin is asserted for it.

## Massachusetts

### `compiled_pathway:MA:non-time-based-expungement-...-100k` — Non-time-based expungement (§ 100K)

**Resolved →** registry track **`ma-expunge-k`** (Petition for Expungement under
G.L. c. 276, § 100K).
**Authority:** Mass. Gen. Laws c. 276, § 100K. **Confidence:** high.

This is the cleanest resolution in the lane, and it rests on two independent identities
either of which is sufficient.

**Statutory identity.** The compiled profile states in terms: *"Non-time-based
expungement is governed by M.G.L. c. 276, 100K."* The registry track's legal name is
*"Petition for Expungement under G.L. c. 276, § 100K."* Same section, same remedy. The
coded state pack says the same thing a third time, with the grounds spelled out:
*"Non-time-based expungement — record created by false ID, identity theft, error, fraud,
or an offense no longer a crime (M.G.L. c. 276, § 100K)."*

**Official-form identity.** `src/lib/rcap/state-packs/massachusetts/official-forms.ts`
carries an entry with `officialName: "Petition for Expungement (non-time-based)"`,
`role: "non_time_based_expungement"`, `citation: "M.G.L. c. 276, § 100K"`, and
`blankPdfInSource: true` — the same official petition the registry track is named for.
This satisfies the job graph's alternative stop condition (an official form identifier
shared with a registry track) on its own.

The profile also reproduces all seven statutory grounds — false identification;
unauthorized use or theft of identity; offense no longer a crime; law-enforcement error;
civilian or expert-witness error; court-employee error; fraud on the court — together
with the clear-and-convincing evidentiary standard and the written-findings requirement.
Neither the profile nor the registry pins those grounds at paragraph granularity, so no
paragraph pin is asserted here.

**Rule a later pass can re-apply:** an MA expungement pathway grounded in § 100K and
served by the "Petition for Expungement" form is `ma-expunge-k`; one grounded in § 100K¼
and served by the "Petition for Expungement of Marijuana Offenses" form is
`ma-expunge-mj` (already mapped); one grounded in §§ 100F–100J is `ma-expunge-time`.

**QA note (does not affect this mapping).** `official-forms.ts` records the *marijuana*
form's citation as "M.G.L. c. 276, § 100K" where the registry has § 100K¼. That is a
state-pack citation slip worth correcting, but it does not disturb this resolution,
because the two official-form **names** still discriminate the two tracks cleanly.

## Texas

All four Texas subjects sit in Chapter 55A of the Code of Criminal Procedure. As flagged
above, the compiled profile gives all five Texas expunction pathways an **identical**
summary and an **identical** rule block — the profile's "Eligible categories" list — so
the shared text cannot discriminate between them. What each pathway's label does is
select one row of that shared list. Every resolution below names the row it selects.

### `compiled_pathway:TX:expunction-after-acquittal-not-guilty-disposition-chapter-55a`

**Resolved →** registry track **`tx_exp_acquittal`** (Expunction after trial court
acquittal).
**Authority:** Tex. Code Crim. Proc. arts. 55A.002 and 55A.201; pin cite art. 55A.201(b)–(d).
**Confidence:** high.

The row this label selects is:

> Acquittal (found not guilty) — Ch. 55A provides a streamlined procedure; on request and
> after notice to the State, the court may enter an expunction order as early as the 30th
> day after acquittal.

That streamlined procedure — order on request, notice to the State, available from the
30th day after acquittal — is the art. 55A.201 mechanism, and `tx_exp_acquittal` is the
track the registry names for it. Subsections (b), (c) and (d) are pinned in the committed
authority set.

**Discriminator against the neighbouring track:** this is *not* `tx_exp_discretionary`
(art. 55A.101), which the registry reserves for expunction on **appellate** acquittal or
on prosecutor recommendation. Neither trigger appears in this pathway's label or in the
acquittal row; the 30-day-after-acquittal order marks it as trial-court acquittal.

### `compiled_pathway:TX:expunction-after-pardon-or-actual-innocence-relief`

**Resolved →** registry track **`tx_exp_pardon_innocence`** (Expunction after a pardon or
other relief on the basis of actual innocence).
**Authority:** Tex. Code Crim. Proc. arts. 55A.003 and 55A.202; pin cite art. 55A.202(b).
**Confidence:** high.

This resolves on name identity, which is the strongest signal available given the shared
Texas text. The compiled label —

> Expunction after pardon or actual-innocence relief

— reproduces the registry legal name —

> Expunction after a pardon or other relief on the basis of actual innocence

modulo hyphenation and the dropped word "other". Same remedy, same disjunction. The
corresponding row of the shared list is *"Pardon — a full pardon by the Governor of Texas
or the President, including a pardon based on actual innocence (and certain other
pardons)."*

**A scope limit deliberately not overreached.** Texas has a second pardon track,
`tx_exp_pardon_other` (art. 55A.004, pardon granted for a reason *other than* actual
innocence). The only text in this pathway that touches it is the parenthetical "(and
certain other pardons)". A parenthetical is not a pathway-defining label, so this lane
does **not** claim `tx_exp_pardon_other`; it remains missing from compiled runtime and
belongs to the registry-owner-action lane. This narrows E2-B3, which proposed both tracks.

### `compiled_pathway:TX:expunction-after-qualifying-dismissal-or-quash`

**Resolved →** registry track **`tx_exp_dismissed`** (Expunction where the charge was
dismissed or quashed).
**Authority:** Tex. Code Crim. Proc. arts. 55A.051 and 55A.053; pin cite art. 55A.053(a)(2),
with the further pinned subsections (b), (c) and (d).
**Confidence:** high.

Name identity again: *"Expunction after qualifying dismissal or quash"* against the
registry's *"Expunction where the charge was dismissed or quashed"* — both disjuncts, same
order. The pathway selects two rows of the shared list: the dismissal row (*"the charge
was dismissed and the person was never placed on court-ordered community supervision for
that offense"*) and the quash row (*"Statute of limitations expired with no charge, or a
void/quashed charging instrument"*).

**Discriminator:** the limitations-with-no-charge branch **alone** belongs to
`tx_exp_limitations` (art. 55A.054), which is already exactly mapped to a different
pathway (`expunction-for-arrest-with-no-charge-filed-after-the-limitations-period`). It is
the **quash** branch that makes this pathway `tx_exp_dismissed` rather than that one.

### `compiled_pathway:TX:expunction-after-qualifying-class-c-deferred-disposition`

**Resolved →** registry track **`tx_exp_dismissed`** (as a second compiled variant).
**Authority:** Tex. Code Crim. Proc. arts. 55A.051 and 55A.053; pin cite art. 55A.051(3),
the court-ordered-community-supervision carve-out.
**Confidence: medium** — the only entry in this lane below high.

A Class C deferred disposition is not a freestanding Texas relief mechanism. It is the
express exception written into the dismissal mechanism's community-supervision bar. The
row this label selects makes that explicit:

> Dismissal — the charge was dismissed and the person was never placed on court-ordered
> community supervision for that offense **(limited exceptions for certain Class C
> deferred dispositions)**.

The registry locates that same exception precisely, in its own scope statement: *"Whether
a Class C deferred disposition counts as court-ordered community supervision for the art.
55A.051(3) carve-out."* Article 55A.051 is `tx_exp_dismissed`'s own authority, so this
pathway is the Class C branch of that track, not a separate mechanism.

**Consequence for the crosswalk:** this pathway and
`expunction-after-qualifying-dismissal-or-quash` are **two compiled variants of one
registry track** — the same `represented_by_compiled_variants` shape the crosswalk already
records for Massachusetts `ma-seal-admin`.

**Why medium and not high.** The registry itself records the carve-out's *application* as
an open scope question rather than a settled criterion. The **mapping** does not depend on
how that question resolves — the pathway plainly lives inside art. 55A.051/55A.053 either
way. But the **runtime eligibility rule** does depend on it, and counsel should treat the
question of whether a completed Class C deferred disposition counts as court-ordered
community supervision as live when this pathway is implemented.

## Utah

Utah's compiled profile lays out its relief in lettered paths, and those paths are
organised **hierarchically** while the registry is organised **flat**. That mismatch, not
any missing authority, is what blocked all three Utah subjects. Read against the
registry, Utah's scheme has two umbrella nodes with branches beneath each:

| | Umbrella pathway | Branch pathways | Registry tracks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Automatic side | **Path A** (this lane) | Paths B, C, I | `ut_auto_clean_slate`, `ut_auto_nonconviction`, `ut_auto_traffic` |
| Petition side | Path D (already mapped) | **Paths E, F** (this lane), J | the seven `ut_pet_*` tracks |

The crosswalk had already resolved the petition-side umbrella (Path D) to all seven
petition tracks at once, and had already resolved the automatic-side branches (Paths B, C,
I). What it had left unresolved was the automatic-side *umbrella* and the petition-side
*branches* — an artefact of which text happened to carry a citation token, not a legal
distinction. This lane closes both gaps consistently with how the crosswalk already
treats the other half of the same structure.

### `compiled_pathway:UT:path-a-automatic-clean-slate-expungement` — Path A

**Resolved →** registry track **`ut_auto_clean_slate`** (primary), co-mapped with
**`ut_auto_nonconviction`** and **`ut_auto_traffic`**.
**Authority:** Utah Code §§ 77-40a-202, 77-40a-205 and 77-40a-206, administered under
§ 77-40a-204. **Confidence:** high.

Path A is the umbrella over Utah's entire automatic-expungement scheme. Its defining
summary carries the four-row automatic case-type table, and each row is a different
statute:

| Row in the compiled table | Statute | Registry track |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acquittal on all charges — goal 60 days | § 77-40a-206 | `ut_auto_nonconviction` |
| Dismissal with prejudice — goal 180 days, excluding plea-in-abeyance dismissals | § 77-40a-206 | `ut_auto_nonconviction` |
| Clean Slate eligible conviction or dismissal, after the statutory waiting period | § 77-40a-205 | `ut_auto_clean_slate` |
| Certain traffic cases — automatic deletion | § 77-40a-202 | `ut_auto_traffic` |

The coded state pack states the same union in a single sentence: *"Automatic Clean Slate
expungement — the court identifies and clears eligible cases without a petition:
acquittals (goal 60 days), dismissals with prejudice excluding plea-in-abeyance
dismissals (goal 180 days), and eligible lower-level convictions/charges after the Clean
Slate waiting period."*

Section 77-40a-204 (including § 77-40a-204(3)) is the shared automatic-process authority,
and the registry cites it *inside* each of the three tracks rather than giving it a track
of its own — which is exactly why no single registry track answers to "automatic
expungement" as a whole.

**Why resolved and not terminalized as a routing node.** The alternative reading is that
Path A is a pure umbrella with no counterpart. This lane rejects it for a structural
reason: the crosswalk **already** treats the mirror-image petition-side umbrella (Path D)
as a direct runtime representation of all seven petition tracks simultaneously. Treating
Path A differently would make the Utah crosswalk internally inconsistent about the same
shape. Primary counterpart is `ut_auto_clean_slate` because the pathway is named for Clean
Slate; the co-mapped set is all three automatic tracks. No double-count results — Paths B,
C and I carry the branch-specific one-to-one mappings while Path A carries the umbrella,
the same one-track-to-several-pathways shape the crosswalk records for `ma-seal-admin`.

### `compiled_pathway:UT:path-e-petition-based-non-conviction-expungement` — Path E

**Resolved →** registry track **`ut_pet_acquittal`** (primary), co-mapped with
**`ut_pet_dismissed_with_prejudice`**, **`ut_pet_dismissed_without_prejudice`**,
**`ut_pet_no_charges`** and **`ut_pet_limitations`**.
**Authority:** Utah Code § 77-40a-302(1). **Confidence:** high.

The pathway's defining summary enumerates the § 77-40a-302(1) non-conviction certificate
outcomes verbatim and exhaustively:

> A BCI certificate can be issued if at least 30 days have passed after arrest or charge
> and one of the statutory outcomes applies: no charges will be filed, all charges were
> dismissed with prejudice, charges dismissed without prejudice or condition with
> prosecutor consent or 180 days passed, acquittal on all charges, or statute of
> limitations expired.

The state pack repeats the same enumeration and the same disqualifier list (pending
proceeding, pending plea in abeyance, current incarceration/parole/probation, protective
order or stalking injunction in effect).

The registry decomposes § 77-40a-302(1) into exactly five outcome-specific tracks, and
**every one of them carries `Utah Code 77-40a-302(1)` in its authority set**. That makes
the mapping mechanically re-checkable: a Utah petition pathway whose eligibility set is
the § 77-40a-302(1) outcome list maps to precisely those UT registry tracks whose
`mechanismAuthority` contains `"Utah Code 77-40a-302(1)"` — a set-membership test over the
committed crosswalk, requiring no judgement.

Within that set the repository pins § 77-40a-302(1)(b)(v) for the limitations branch and
§ 77-40a-306(2) for the dismissed-without-prejudice branch. Primary counterpart is
`ut_pet_acquittal` as the first-listed 302(1) track; the five-track set is the substantive
result. **Discriminator against Path F:** conviction status — § 77-40a-302 governs
non-conviction records, § 77-40a-303 governs convictions.

### `compiled_pathway:UT:path-f-petition-based-conviction-expungement` — Path F

**Resolved →** registry track **`ut_pet_conviction`** (Expungement of a conviction on a
certificate of eligibility).
**Authority:** Utah Code § 77-40a-303. **Confidence:** high.

Path F's own record in the compiled profile is almost entirely the shared Utah petition
boilerplate, which is why the automated matcher and the E2 wave both stalled here. The
discriminating authority comes instead from the coded state pack, which uses the *same
nine-pathway taxonomy* as the profile and attaches the statute directly to the conviction
branch.

`src/lib/rcap/state-packs/utah/eligibility-rules.ts` enumerates the taxonomy —
*"automatic Clean Slate expungement, petition-based expungement with a BCI Certificate of
Eligibility, petition-based non-conviction expungement, petition-based conviction
expungement, traffic offense deletion/petition, cannabis-possession petition without a BCI
certificate, pardon-based expungement, juvenile expungement, or vacatur/human-trafficking
expungement"* — and then assigns the conviction branch its statute: *"Certificate
eligibility bars (§ 77-40a-303): Utah bars ordinary certificate eligibility for a capital
felony, first-degree felony, violent felony, felony DUI under § 41-6a-501(2), and
sex/kidnap/child-abuse registry offenses..."*

`src/lib/rcap/state-packs/utah/pathways.ts` states the same link as a structured data
field: pathway `petition_certificate_conviction_expungement`, citation *"Utah Code Title
77, Chapter 40a; § 77-40a-303"*.

The registry names § 77-40a-303 for exactly one track — `ut_pet_conviction`, *"Expungement
of a conviction on a certificate of eligibility, Utah Code 77-40a-303"* — and the
committed authority set pins § 77-40a-303(1), (4), (5), (6) and (7).

**Change from the prior wave.** E2-B3 reached the same track at **medium** confidence,
resting on the boilerplate profile text and on the pathway's position in the profile's
ordering. This lane raises it to **high**, because the state pack supplies an explicit,
committed, citable assignment of § 77-40a-303 to the conviction-petition pathway that the
earlier pass did not reach.

## Items this lane hands onward

None of these block Milestone 1 item 2, and none is this lane's to fix, but each was
surfaced by the work above and should not be lost:

1. **`ct-cannabis-petition` (C.G.S. § 54-142v) has no compiled runtime representation.**
   The Connecticut cannabis pathway covers only the automatic branch. The petition branch
   for out-of-window cannabis convictions is a genuine runtime coverage gap, and the two
   E2 findings on it disagreed; this lane adopted the one the committed crosswalk follows.
2. **`tx_exp_pardon_other` (art. 55A.004) has no compiled runtime representation.** The
   Texas pardon pathway is defined for actual-innocence pardons; pardons granted for other
   reasons are reached only by a parenthetical, which this lane declined to treat as a
   mapping.
3. **Texas Class C deferred disposition is an open eligibility question, not just an open
   mapping.** Whether a completed Class C deferred disposition counts as court-ordered
   community supervision under the art. 55A.051(3) carve-out is recorded in the registry as
   unsettled. It should be resolved before that pathway is implemented in runtime.
4. **Massachusetts state-pack citation slip.** `official-forms.ts` cites the marijuana
   expungement form to § 100K rather than § 100K¼.
5. **Subsection-granularity residuals.** Conn. Gen. Stat. § 54-142u's erasure-grant
   paragraph and Mass. Gen. Laws c. 276, § 100K's seven enumerated grounds are not pinned
   at paragraph granularity anywhere in this repository. Both mappings stand on the section
   as committed; the pins would improve citation quality only.
