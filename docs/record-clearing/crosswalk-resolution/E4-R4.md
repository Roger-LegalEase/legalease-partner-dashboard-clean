## What this lane was asked to do

The track/pathway crosswalk is a bidirectional map between the nationwide **registry tracks** (the
catalogue of real record-clearing mechanisms, one entry per statutory route and official form) and
the **compiled pathways** (what the runtime actually offers a participant). Thirty-eight subjects
could not be matched. This lane owns ten of them, all in Kentucky, Minnesota, North Dakota and
South Carolina.

A subject is not a legal question about whether relief exists. It is a question about whether the
thing the runtime offers and the thing the registry catalogues are **the same mechanism**, and if so
which one. The answer has to be re-checkable: a citation to a document committed in this repository,
plus a stated rule a later automated pass can re-apply to reach the same answer without trusting this
lane's judgement.

Nine of the ten failures were **matching failures, not knowledge gaps** — the automated crosswalk
generator scores candidates by counting shared citation tokens, and that method breaks in four
recurring ways which this lane's subjects illustrate exactly:

| Failure mode | Why token-counting breaks | Subjects |
|---|---|---|
| One official form carries several statutory branches | Every branch shares the same section number, so several registry tracks tie | KY felony trio |
| The registry cites a whole chapter, the runtime cites a section | No literal token overlap; every section in the chapter ties | MN mistaken identity |
| One runtime pathway is deliberately several mechanisms | Scored as a single-track match, it loses to every rival | MN cannabis, SC diversion |
| A registry track declares a branch in prose, not in a structured field | The generator reads only the structured field and sees none | KY juvenile |

The tenth — the South Carolina trafficking-survivor pathway — is a genuine knowledge gap and is
returned unresolved.

# Kentucky

Five of the ten subjects are Kentucky, and four of them are one knot. The registry catalogues three
separate Kentucky conviction-side mechanisms that all cite KRS 431.073 or KRS 431.079, and the
runtime offers one felony pathway. The generator saw three tracks competing for one pathway, could
not separate them on statute alone, and published the conflict.

**The knot unties on the AOC form number, not on the statute.** Kentucky's Administrative Office of
the Courts issues a different numbered form for each of these instruments, the compiled profile
embeds the body text of the forms it was built from, and the registry projection records which form
each track uses. Once the forms are lined up the three-way conflict resolves cleanly into two tracks
sharing one form and a third track on a form of its own that the runtime does not implement at all.

| Instrument | AOC form | Registry track | Runtime |
|---|---|---|---|
| Application to vacate and expunge a felony conviction | **AOC-496.3** (order: AOC-496.4) | `ky_felony_vacatur_expungement` | `felony-conviction-431073` |
| Same application, full-pardon branch | **AOC-496.3** (same form, third checkbox) | `ky_felony_expungement_after_pardon` | `felony-conviction-431073` |
| Expungement certification request to the AOC | **AOC-RU-009** | `ky_expungement_certification` | *none — see terminalization* |

## 1. `compiled_pathway:KY:felony-conviction-431073` — **resolved**

**Counterpart:** `registry_track:KY:ky_felony_vacatur_expungement` (head), composed with
`registry_track:KY:ky_felony_expungement_after_pardon`
**Operative authority:** KRS 431.073 — subsections (1)(a) and (1)(d) for the head track, (1)(c) for
the co-mapped pardon track
**Confidence:** high

The compiled profile embeds the body text of **AOC-496.3, Application to Vacate and Expunge Felony
Conviction, Rev. 6-23**. Its eligibility block is a single "check only one" list covering every
statutory route:

> ❑ the offense is one of the eligible offenses listed in KRS 431.073(1)(a).
> ❑ the offenses are a series of eligible offenses listed in KRS 431.073(1)(a) which arose from a single incident.
> ❑ a full pardon has been granted by the Governor, a copy of which is attached.

and a second section headed "(Complete this section only if applying for expungement under KRS
431.073(1)(d)".

So Kentucky does not have two felony applications. It has **one application with four eligibility
branches**, and the registry — correctly, because the eligibility standards genuinely differ —
catalogues two of those branches as separate tracks. One pathway therefore represents two tracks.
That is a `composed_unit_of_registry_track` relationship, which the crosswalk vocabulary already
supports; the generator simply had no rule that would produce it here.

The same evidence excludes the third claimant. `AOC-496.3` appears in the `officialFormRefs` of the
vacatur and after-pardon tracks and **not** in those of the certification track, which carries
`AOC-RU-009` instead.

**Re-checkable rule:** a compiled pathway whose committed source text is one official form maps to
the set of registry tracks carrying that form identifier in `officialFormRefs`, and to no track
carrying a different one.

## 4. `registry_track:KY:ky_expungement_certification` — **terminalized**

**Counterpart:** none, and none should exist
**Operative authority:** KRS 431.079(1), read with KRS 431.073(2)(a) and KRS 431.078(3)
**Confidence:** high

Under KRS 431.079 the participant asks the Administrative Office of the Courts for a certification
of eligibility, on form **AOC-RU-009**, before filing any conviction expungement. KRS 431.073(2)(a)
(felony) and KRS 431.078(3) (misdemeanor) each make that certification a required attachment.

It is a **precondition instrument, not a remedy** — and the registry says so itself:

> The certification is an evidentiary attachment: it decides nothing, and an adverse certification
> does not stop the clerk from accepting a petition.

Three mechanical checks, each reproducible against committed bytes, establish that no compiled
pathway is or should be its counterpart:

1. **The section is absent from the runtime.** The string `431.079` occurs **zero times** in
   `src/lib/rcap-engine/compiled/profiles/KY-kentucky.json`.
2. **The form is absent from the runtime.** `AOC-RU-009` appears in no compiled profile and no state
   pack — only in the registry projection and the authority ledger. The two candidate pathways are
   compiled from AOC-496.3 and AOC-497.2.
3. **Both candidates carry it in the posture of a prerequisite, not a remedy.** The misdemeanor
   pathway: "The source requires an eligibility certification, a separate petition for each case…".
   The felony pathway: "The source requires eligibility certification, prosecutor response within 60
   days…".

Mapping the certification to either conviction pathway would double-count one remedy; mapping it to
both would assert a one-to-many relationship the registry does not declare. The right answer is that
it has no runtime representation — terminal class `missing_from_compiled_runtime`.

**Published blocker.** `ky_expungement_certification` has no runtime representation and adds none;
it stays out of the runtime-coverage numerator. Its separate **authority-clearance blocker is
untouched by this terminalization and remains open**: whether the AOC waives the $40 certification
fee on indigency, and on what criteria — AOC-RU-009 refers to a fee exemption "if applicable" but
neither the form nor the AOC's published process states who qualifies or how to ask.

**One implementation consequence, for the packet lane and not for the crosswalk:** because no
pathway implements the certification step, the Kentucky felony and misdemeanor packets must keep
carrying it as a prerequisite instruction. Terminalizing the track must not be read as deleting the
step from the participant's workflow.

## 5. `compiled_pathway:KY:juvenile-automatic-dismissal` — **resolved**

**Counterpart:** `registry_track:KY:ky_juvenile_record_expungement`
**Operative authority:** KRS 610.330(7)
**Confidence:** high

The E3 rejection reads: *"track ky_juvenile_record_expungement declares no branching and this claim
is not uniquely citation-grade."* That is true of the track's **structured** fields —
`compositionMode` is null and `unitIds` is empty — and false of its **prose**. The registry
projection's `mechanismExcerpt` for that track ends:

> There is a **separate automatic branch under KRS 610.330(7)** where a petition is dismissed or the
> child is found not delinquent, which requires no action by the child.

KRS 610.330(7) is also in the track's authority array. The registry did declare the branch; the
generator was reading the wrong field.

The compiled pathway is that branch, and the Kentucky forms separate the two branches explicitly.
The profile embeds the body of **AOC-JV-29 Rev. 8-23**, "ORDER FOR EXPUNGEMENT OF JUVENILE RECORD
UPON DISMISSAL / FINDING-ADJUDICATION OF NOT DELINQUENT", headed:

> the following expungements are required by law and DO NOT require any petition, motion, or other
> action of the child.

Its Section II is entered "pursuant to KRS 610.330" on a finding of not delinquent or a dismissal
for any other reason. The sibling pathway `juvenile-petition-610330` is the petition branch, built
from **AOC-JV-30** (Petition to Vacate and Expunge Juvenile Record) and **AOC-JV-29.1** (Order upon
Petition) — and AOC-JV-29.1 states the division on its own face:

> ORDERS FOR EXPUNGEMENT UPON DISMISSAL OF PETITION OR FINDING/ADJUDICATION OF NOT DELINQUENT SHOULD
> BE COMPLETED ON THE AOC-JV-29.

So one registry track is represented by two compiled variants — automatic (JV-29) and petition
(JV-30 / JV-29.1) — which is the same shape the crosswalk already accepts elsewhere.

**Re-checkable rule:** where a registry track's `mechanismExcerpt` names a subsection and describes
a no-petition branch, a compiled pathway implementing that branch is a
`compiled_variant_of_registry_track` of that track, even though the track declares no structured
composition.

# Minnesota

Both Minnesota subjects failed for the same structural reason: **the registry cites Minnesota's
expungement chapter, `Minn. Stat. ch. 609A`, while the runtime cites sections inside it.** Chapter
609A holds at least six distinct mechanisms, so chapter-level matching produces a tie among all of
them. The coded state pack at `src/lib/rcap/state-packs/minnesota/pathways.ts` is the key: it is a
committed table binding each named Minnesota mechanism to exactly one section and one remedy type.
Refining the registry's chapter cite through that table converts an unresolvable tie into a
one-to-one match.

## 6. `compiled_pathway:MN:automatic-mistaken-identity-expungement-under-609a-017` — **resolved**

**Counterpart:** `registry_track:MN:mn_mistaken_identity_court`
**Operative authority:** Minn. Stat. § 609A.017 (within the registry's cited Minn. Stat. ch. 609A)
**Confidence:** high

The E3 rejection is *"identity tie in MN (3 vs 8)"*, and the review adds a warning worth answering
directly: *"an automatic twin out-scores it on the pathway text — the lane pick is likely wrong, not
just unlicensed."* The twin is `mn_auto_clean_slate`. It is not available: the state pack binds
Clean Slate to § 609A.015, and the crosswalk already holds it in a **direct 1:1** with
`automatic-clean-slate-expungement-under-609a-015`. Taking it here would break a settled mapping.
The high token score is an artefact of both mechanisms being automatic, not evidence of identity.

The real question is which of Minnesota's **two** mistaken-identity tracks this is, and the registry
splits them by remedy in its own words:

| Registry track | Its own `mechanismExcerpt` | Authority | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mn_mistaken_identity_court` | "Where a record was created through mistaken identity, **the court expunges it** without a participant petition." | Minn. Stat. ch. 609A | Court expungement |
| `mn_mistaken_identity_iddata` | "**Identification data** created through mistaken identity is **destroyed** without a participant petition." | Minn. Stat. § 299C.11 | BCA data destruction |

The state pack draws the same line: `mistaken_identity_automatic` → § 609A.017, remedyType
`automatic_expungement`; `arrest_record_destruction` → § 299C.11, remedyType
`arrest_record_destruction`. This pathway is a court expungement under § 609A.017, and § 609A.017
sits inside ch. 609A, so the refinement is consistent. The § 299C.11 side is already occupied by
`arrest-identification-data-destruction-when-no-charges-were-filed-minn-stat-299c-11`.

**Re-checkable rule:** refine a chapter-level registry authority to a section using the coded state
pack's mechanism→citation table, then require the remedy type of the pathway to equal the remedy
type named in the track's `mechanismExcerpt`.

## 7. `compiled_pathway:MN:cannabis-automatic-or-board-reviewed-expungement-under-609a-055-06` — **resolved**

**Counterpart:** `registry_track:MN:mn_auto_cannabis_nonfelony` (head), composed with
`registry_track:MN:mn_ceb_felony_cannabis`
**Operative authority:** Minn. Stat. § 609A.055 (head) and § 609A.06 (co-mapped); both enacted by
Laws 2023, ch. 63
**Confidence:** high

The E3 rejection is *"only 2 distinctive tokens shared with mn_auto_cannabis_nonfelony"* — a correct
verdict on a one-track proposal, and the wrong question. **This pathway is two mechanisms, and says
so in its label**: "Cannabis automatic **or** board-reviewed expungement under §§ 609A.055–.06". The
disjunction is the composition.

Its own rule clauses carry the two halves separately:

> Certain nonfelony cannabis records may be automatically expunged under 609A.055. The BCA
> identifies eligible records and seals them without an application, petition, or motion.

> For felony and some other cannabis-related cases, Minnesota created the Cannabis Expungement
> Board. The Board reviews eligible cases, often anonymously, and determines whether the conviction
> should be expunged, vacated, dismissed, or resentenced… (609A.06)

The state pack splits the same two at section level (`cannabis_automatic` → § 609A.055;
`cannabis_board_review` → § 609A.06), and the registry carries exactly those two, distinguished by
actor and by offence level:

- `mn_auto_cannabis_nonfelony` — "The Bureau of Criminal Apprehension identifies qualifying

- `mn_ceb_felony_cannabis` — "The Cannabis Expungement Board reviews **felony** cannabis records on

**Re-checkable rule:** when a compiled pathway label joins two section citations with "or",
decompose it into one component per section, bind each to the track whose `mechanismExcerpt`

**Side effect:** this also discharges `mn_ceb_felony_cannabis`, which was unmapped for the same

## 8. `compiled_pathway:ND:marijuana-specific-summary-pardon-or-sealing-relief` — **resolved**

**Counterpart:** `registry_track:ND:nd-summary-marijuana-pardon`
**Operative authority:** N.D.C.C. § 12-55.1-01(4) (ch. 12-55.1; form SFN 61663)
**Confidence:** high

The E3 rejection is *"identity tie in ND (4 vs 4)"*. The tie is against `nd-regular-pardon`, and it
is a real tie on citations: both pardon tracks cite the same four sections of N.D.C.C. ch. 12-55.1,
because both are applications to the same Pardon Advisory Board under the same chapter. **The
difference between them is not statutory — it is which state form you file**, SFN 61663 (summary
marijuana) or SFN 14859 (regular clemency).

Two committed facts break the tie.

**First, the pathway states the summary form's scope and expressly excludes the rival.** Its own
rule clauses read:

> people prosecuted and convicted of misdemeanor marijuana possession, marijuana ingestion, or
> marijuana paraphernalia who have had no other criminal convictions in the past five years may
> submit a Summary Pardon Application. **Felony convictions are not eligible for the summary
> marijuana application and must use the regular pardon application.**

That three-element scope is `nd-summary-marijuana-pardon`'s own `mechanismExcerpt` — "the summary
marijuana process uses SFN 61663 and covers misdemeanor convictions for possession of marijuana,
ingestion of marijuana and possession of marijuana paraphernalia" — and the felony carve-out is
`nd-regular-pardon`'s own claim: "It is the required route for any felony and for any applicant
excluded from the summary marijuana process." The pathway does not merely resemble the summary
track; it names the tied candidate as the alternative it is not.

**Second, the disjunctive label resolves by exclusion.** The pathway is "summary-pardon **or**
sealing relief". Its sealing component is the N.D.C.C. § 19-03.1-23(9) first-offence motion, and the
crosswalk already holds that component in a direct 1:1 (`first-offense-possession-sealing` →
`nd-marijuana-first-offense-seal`). The summary-pardon component is the only unbound half.

The pathway keeps the two apart deliberately — "Marijuana sealing and marijuana summary pardon are
not the same thing. One is a court-record sealing process; the other is an executive pardon process"
— which is the runtime implementation of the summary track's own stop condition, "a participant
confusing the executive pardon with the court sealing route under N.D.C.C. § 19-03.1-23(9)".

> **Counsel should note** what the registry records about this remedy's limits: "A pardon removes
> punishment but does not remove the fact of the conviction unless the certificate of pardon says
> so, and the form warns in terms that a pardon request will not expunge a criminal history record."
> A participant routed here is not getting an expungement. The pathway's guidance must not imply
> otherwise. Separately, the authority ledger carries an open source conflict on this track: the fax
> number on the face of SFN 61663 differs from the one on the Department of Corrections and
> Rehabilitation web page; the form's number is used and the conflict is recorded.

**Re-checkable rule:** on a citation tie between two tracks in one chapter, prefer the track whose
scope tokens the pathway reproduces and whose rival the pathway names as the excluded alternative;
then confirm no component of the pathway is left double-bound.

## 9. `compiled_pathway:SC:diversion-or-program-completion-expungement` — **resolved**

**Counterpart:** `registry_track:SC:sc_pti_17_22_150` (head), composed with `sc_aep`, `sc_tep` and
`sc_conditional_discharge_44_53_450`
**Operative authority:** § 17-22-150(a) (PTI); § 17-22-530(A) (AEP); § 17-22-330(A) (TEP);
§ 44-53-450(b) (conditional discharge)
**Confidence:** high

The E3 rejection is *"only 2 distinctive tokens shared with sc_pti_17_22_150"*. Again the right
verdict on a one-track proposal and the wrong question. This pathway is **four** mechanisms.

Its ten rule clauses are four labelled blocks and nothing else:

1. **Pretrial Intervention (PTI)** — "After successful PTI completion, the Solicitor effects a
   noncriminal disposition, and the person may apply to destroy official arrest records."
2. **Alcohol Education Program — AEP** — eligibility (17 to under 21, no prior alcohol-related
   offence) and the $250 programme fee with inability-to-pay protection.
3. **Traffic Education Program — TEP** — fine-only offences, four points or less, not available
   where the offence caused death or serious bodily injury.
4. **Conditional discharge — drug possession** — "Under 44-53-450, a person with no prior qualifying
   drug conviction… may receive a conditional discharge… and the person may apply to expunge
   official records. This route is one-time only," plus the $350 / $150 fee clause.

The coded state pack binds each program name to exactly one section: PTI → § 17-22-150; AEP →
§ 17-22-530; TEP → § 17-22-330; conditional discharge → § 44-53-450. Each of those four is the
**naming citation** of exactly one registry track, and all four of those tracks are presently
unmapped:

| Program | Section | Registry track |
|---|---|---|
| Pretrial Intervention | § 17-22-150(a) | `sc_pti_17_22_150` |
| Alcohol Education Program | § 17-22-530(A) | `sc_aep` |
| Traffic Education Program | § 17-22-330(A) | `sc_tep` |
