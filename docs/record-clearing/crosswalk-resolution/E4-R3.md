citations, rather than the whole bled-in table, resolves both — and closes those two registry-side gaps

narrower rule.

*Second, no conclusion here rests on anything fetched from the internet.* This terminal has no outbound

## Delaware

### 1. Compiled pathway `discretionary-court-expungement-under-11-del-c-4374` — **resolved**

**Counterpart:** registry track `de_discretionary_superior_court`, "Petition for Expungement of Adult
Record, Superior Court, 11 Del. C. § 4374".

**Authority:** 11 Del. C. § 4374 — discretionary expungement on petition, granted where the petitioner
shows that continued maintenance of the record works a manifest injustice, with the Attorney General as
opposing party.

Three Delaware registry tracks cite § 4374, so the question was which one this pathway implements. Two
are excluded on the face of the statute and the profile.

*The prosecutor-initiated track is excluded on who moves.* `de_attorney_general_expungement` rests on
§ 4374(h) and (f): at a State motion to dismiss or a nolle prosequi, the prosecutor may petition, and
such a petition must be granted; the participant files nothing. The compiled pathway describes the
opposite posture — the petitioner files and "the Attorney General may object within 120 days."

*The Family Court track is excluded on forum.* `de_discretionary_family_court` is pinned to § 4374(c),
which compels Family Court filing only where every charge and conviction sought to be expunged was
disposed of in Family Court, and its primary filing is Family Court Form 281. The compiled pathway is
fixed in Superior Court throughout: its filing-destination rule reads "discretionary is filed with the
Superior Court (Family Court for juvenile)"; its packet is the "Expungement Petition packet
(Superior … available at courts.delaware.gov under Superior Court → Expungement)"; and its worked
example is captioned "SUPERIOR COURT OF THE STATE OF DELAWARE … PETITION FOR DISCRETIONARY EXPUNGEMENT
(11 Del. C. 4374)". The strings `281`, `Form 281` and `FORM-281` appear nowhere in the Delaware profile.

What is left is `de_discretionary_superior_court`, whose whole authority is the bare, unsubsectioned
"11 Del. C. § 4374" — the same citation the pathway's id and label carry — and whose registry
description matches the pathway clause for clause, including the four discretionary waiting periods
(three years for a single-case misdemeanour not listed in § 4373(b), five years for multiple violations
or misdemeanours across cases, seven years for a § 4373(b)-listed misdemeanour, seven years for a
single nonviolent felony).

One caveat for counsel: the Family Court adult route under § 4374(c) remains unrepresented in the
compiled runtime. That is a coverage gap on the Delaware profile, not a defect in this mapping, and it
is not one of this lane's ten subjects.

## Maryland

### 2. Compiled pathway `juvenile-expungement` — **terminalized (registry gap)**

**Counterpart:** none exists.

**Authority:** Md. Code, Cts. & Jud. Proc. § 3-8A-27.1, with Md. Rule 11-506.

Maryland juvenile record expungement is a separate proceeding from adult expungement: it is brought in
the court where the juvenile petition or citation was filed, it carries no filing fee, and it runs on
Courts & Judicial Proceedings § 3-8A-27.1 and Maryland Rule 11-506 — not on Criminal Procedure Title 10.
The repository is consistent on this across three independent places: the compiled profile identifies
the governing law as "Courts & Judicial Proceedings 3-8A-27.1 and Maryland Rule 11-506"; the coded state
pack lists `juvenile_expungement` with citation "Md. Code, Cts. & Jud. Proc. § 3-8A-27.1"; and the state
pack's form catalogue lists the juvenile petition as "JUV-11-506.1 / juvenile forms index" against the
same citation.

All eleven Maryland registry tracks are adult Criminal Procedure Title 10 mechanisms — §§ 10-103,
10-103.1, 10-104, 10-105, 10-105.1, 10-110, 10-112, and 10-301 through 10-306. Not one cites CJP Title 3
subtitle 8A or Rule 11-506, and the form JUV-11-506.1 appears in no registry packet set. (The word
"juvenile" does appear in two § 10-105 tracks, but only as the adult disposition "transfer to juvenile
court" — a different thing.)

So this is not an unresolved mapping; it is a mechanism the runtime implements that the registry has
never enrolled. That is the same disposition already adjudicated for the two sibling juvenile pathways
in this lane's jurisdictions, `DE:juvenile-expungement-under-10-del-c-1017-1019-1017a` and
`WV:juvenile-record-relief`, both recorded as unregistered relief mechanisms.

**Blocker published for the registry owner:** Maryland has no juvenile registry track. Either add one
under CJP § 3-8A-27.1 / Md. Rule 11-506, or record juvenile relief as deliberately outside registry
scope. Until one of those happens the compiled pathway contributes no registry coverage.

*Citation residual:* no committed source pins this mechanism below section level. That does not weaken
the terminalization — the section alone establishes that the mechanism sits outside Criminal Procedure
Title 10 — but a later pass wanting subsection granularity will need the statute itself.

### 3. Compiled pathway `second-chance-act-shielding` — **resolved**

**Counterpart:** registry track `md_second_chance_shielding`, "Petition for Shielding Under the Maryland
Second Chance Act".

**Authority:** Md. Code, Crim. Proc. §§ 10-301 through 10-306, with Md. Rule 16-934.

The job asked for "a statutory citation on the compiled pathway, or a component identifier unique to one
track." The component identifier exists and is unique. The Maryland profile's form table binds this
pathway's own row to **CC-DC-CR-148 — Petition for Shielding Under Maryland Second Chance Act**, and
CC-DC-CR-148 appears in the packet set of exactly one Maryland registry track: `md_second_chance_shielding`,
as its primary filing. The coded state pack independently binds the same form to "Md. Code, Crim. Proc.
§§ 10-301 to 10-306", which is that track's authority verbatim.

The substance corroborates the form. Both sides describe the same remedy in the same terms: a closed
list of shieldable convictions; a petition no earlier than three years after the sentence for every
conviction being shielded is satisfied, including parole, probation or mandatory supervision; and one
shielding petition per lifetime. Both distinguish shielding from expungement — the record is removed
from public view and from Judiciary Case Search but is not destroyed, and remains visible to law
enforcement, courts and certain agencies.

Note for the record: `md_second_chance_shielding` was *not* among the five candidates the crosswalk
offered for this pathway, because the crosswalk had separately filed that track as "missing from
compiled runtime" — the two halves of one mapping were sitting in two different failure buckets.
Resolving this pathway closes that track's gap as well.

Of the five candidates the crosswalk did offer, all are excluded: `md_10105_early`, `md_10105_favorable`,
`md_10110_conviction` and `md_cannabis_petition` are expungement routes under Criminal Procedure
§§ 10-105 and 10-110 with the CC-DC-CR-072 form family, and `md_pardon_expungement` is a § 10-105(a)(8)
expungement. Shielding is a different remedy under a different subtitle on a different form.
