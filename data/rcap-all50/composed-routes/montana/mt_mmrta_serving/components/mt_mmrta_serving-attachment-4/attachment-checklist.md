# Records you have to attach

Your petition is filed with records attached. This page tells you which records to get and what to check them against. It is for you — it is not one of the documents you file.

## Two records you need before you file

**1. Your criminal record from the Montana Department of Justice.**
The form marks this as a required attachment. You get it from the Montana Department of Justice, Criminal Records Information Services Section. If you are in custody, ask your attorney, a family member, or the facility's law library about how to request it.

**2. Court documents for the conviction and the current sentence.**
Ask the clerk of the court that sentenced you for the judgment and the sentencing order. If you are in custody, your attorney or a family member may need to get them for you.

## Check your answers against those records

- Check the case number you gave against your Montana Department of Justice criminal record. If they disagree, correct the packet.
- Check the conviction date you gave against your court documents. If they disagree, correct the packet.

Getting a copy of your criminal record matters for a second reason: some Montana convictions may not appear on the background check, so it is worth checking both your criminal record and the court's own records for the case.

## One more record, but only later, and only sometimes

**A certified copy of the court order of expungement.**
This one is not needed to file. You only get it *after* the court has granted expungement — ask the clerk for a certified copy, and enclose it with the Department of Justice request.

If the court resentences you instead of expunging the conviction, you do not need this and there is no Department of Justice step at all. The conviction still stands, with a changed sentence, so there is nothing for the Department of Justice to delete.

## What is left blank

- Nothing on this page fills in a court, a county, a case number, a judge, or a county attorney. Those come from your own paperwork and your own answers.
- Your date of birth and any identifying numbers are not filled in.
- Nothing here says what the county attorney will do or how the court will rule.

## Stop and get a lawyer if

- You are currently incarcerated. That alone is a reason to talk to a lawyer about this petition.
- You are not sure whether what you were convicted of is something Montana now allows, or something that would now carry a lesser sentence.
- Your offence is not on the list the form gives you.
- The county attorney opposes your petition, or you want to ask for a hearing.
- You are not a U.S. citizen.
- You are weighing expungement against resentencing and the difference matters to you.
