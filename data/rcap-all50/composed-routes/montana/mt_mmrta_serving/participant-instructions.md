# Marijuana conviction expungement or resentencing while serving a sentence — what you do

This packet does not include the official Montana forms for this filing. Montana publishes them. Before you fill anything in, get the current MMRTA form set for someone **currently serving a sentence** — the petition form, the proposed order, and the certificate of service — from the Montana Judicial Branch:
https://courts.mt.gov/Forms/mmrta

The Department of Justice request you may need later is published separately:
https://dojmt.gov/dci-home/conviction-expungement-process/

## First, know which route you are on

Montana is not one route. Misdemeanor conviction expungement, non-conviction removal, deferred-sentence dismissal, and marijuana relief are separate, with separate forms. This packet is the marijuana route, for a conviction where you are **still serving** the sentence. If your sentence is finished, that is a different form.

A separate form set is required for each conviction, even if the convictions happened at the same time. Do not combine them.

## Before you file

- **Get your Montana Department of Justice criminal record.** It is a required attachment. If you are in custody, ask your attorney, a family member, or the facility's law library how to request it.
- **Get your court documents** — the judgment and the sentencing order — from the clerk of the court that sentenced you. If you are in custody, your attorney or a family member may need to get them for you.
- **Check your answers against those records.** Check your case number against the criminal record and your conviction date against the court documents, and correct the packet if they disagree.
- It is worth checking both the criminal record and the court's own records, because some Montana convictions may not show up on the background check.

## Steps

1. **Complete the form set** — the petition form for someone currently serving, the proposed order, and the certificate of service.
2. **Choose what you are asking for.** The form makes you choose between expungement and resentencing. That choice is yours alone. We do not make it, and we do not tell you which is better — they are different outcomes with different consequences.
3. **Write your own explanation** in the free-text space on the form: why the conviction is eligible — what makes the conduct something Montana now allows, or something that would now carry a lesser sentence. Those are your words.
4. **Sign and date the petition.** You sign your own petition. Nothing on the face of the form calls for a notary.
5. **Check the local rules.** You must follow all applicable filing rules, including local filing rules. Use the Montana Court Locator to find the local rules and contact information for your court, and ask the clerk if anything is unclear.
6. **File in the court that sentenced you** — district court, justice court, or municipal or city court — with the proposed order and the certificate of service.
7. **Pay the filing fee, or ask for a waiver.** The fee depends on the court level, so ask the clerk. If you cannot pay, the court instructions point you to the Statement of Inability to Pay Court Costs and Fees on montanalawhelp.org. We link you to it; we do not fill it in or include it.
8. **Serve the county attorney** for the county you filed in, and complete the certificate of service. Under the court instructions the county attorney has 21 days to oppose.
9. **Wait for the court.** The county attorney may respond. The court may set a hearing.

## If the court grants expungement, you are not finished

A court order is not the end of this in Montana. Sending the order onward is your job — not the court's and not the clerk's.

10. **Get a certified copy of the expungement order** from the clerk.
11. **Send the Montana Department of Justice request** with the court order, along with fingerprints and the Department of Justice form, to the arresting agency, the prosecutor, the clerk, and the Department of Justice. Allow about 30 days for processing. There is no fee for the request. Send it to:

    Montana Department of Justice, Division of Criminal Investigation, CRISS Expungement
    2225 11th Ave., P.O. Box 201403, Helena, MT 59620
    (406) 444-3625 · dojcriss@mt.gov

**If the court resentences you instead**, there is no Department of Justice step. The conviction stands with a changed sentence, so there is nothing for the Department of Justice to delete.

## Words that mean different things here

- **Expungement** in Montana means permanently destroying, deleting, or erasing a record from the state criminal history system the Department of Justice keeps.
- What happens at the courts, police agencies, and prosecutors' offices is **sealing** — not destruction.
- **Removal** is the Department of Justice's word for deleting a record of something that was never a conviction.
- **Resentencing** and **redesignation** are outcomes of their own, and neither one is expungement or sealing.

## What is left blank for you

- The court, the county, the case number, the judge, and the county attorney are not filled in. Take them from your own court paperwork and your criminal record.
- Your date of birth and identifying numbers are not filled in.
- Your signature and date are not filled in — on the petition or on the Department of Justice request.
- Nothing in this packet argues that granting your petition would not pose a risk to public safety. That is the court's call, and we do not write it for you.
- Nothing in this packet works out what a new sentence would be.
- Nothing in this packet says what the county attorney will do, whether you will get a hearing, or how the court will rule.

## Costs

We do not have a fee amount for this filing. The fee depends on which level of court sentenced you, so ask the clerk before you file. There is no fee for the Department of Justice request.

## What this does not promise

This does not promise a record will be erased, that nobody will ever see it, or that you qualify. It does not promise Montana relief will reach federal or FBI records, Tribal or out-of-state convictions, copies already held by private background-check companies, firearm rights, immigration consequences, or professional licensing. It says nothing about whether you may deny the conviction afterward — that is a question for a lawyer.

## Stop and get a lawyer if

- **You are currently incarcerated.** That alone is a reason to talk to a lawyer before going further with this petition.
- Anything about your case makes public safety a live question.
- You need to know what a new sentence would be.
- You are not sure whether what you were convicted of is now allowed in Montana, or would now carry a lesser sentence.
- Your offence is not on the list the form gives you.
- The county attorney opposes your petition.
- You want to ask the court for a hearing.
- You are not a U.S. citizen.
- You are weighing expungement against resentencing and the difference matters to you.
