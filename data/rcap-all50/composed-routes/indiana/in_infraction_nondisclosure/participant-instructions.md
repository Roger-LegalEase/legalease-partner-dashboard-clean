# Keeping an infraction off background checks - what to do now

Indiana has a rule about infractions. In some situations the court must tell the clerk, and whoever runs the case management system, not to give out information about your infraction to organisations and people outside the criminal justice system.

This packet takes you through the first step: finding out whether the court has already done that.

## Step 1 - Gather your answers

Have these ready:

- the Indiana county where the infraction was handled
- the cause number, if one was assigned
- how the matter ended: never prosecuted, dismissed, found not to have committed it, an adjudication later vacated, a deferral programme completed, or a judgment satisfied
- the date that happened
- whether prosecution was deferred

## Step 2 - Ask the clerk

Contact the clerk of the court where the infraction was handled and ask whether the court entered an order under I.C. 34-28-5-15 in your cause.

You do this yourself. LegalEase does not contact the clerk on your behalf, and does not receive or look at the clerk's answer.

## Step 3 - Compare the answer with what you told us

If what the clerk tells you disagrees with the answer you gave to "Has the court already ordered the clerk not to disclose the infraction?", correct it before going any further.

## Step 4 - If the order was already entered

There is nothing to file. The court has done what the law asks of it. Stop here.

## Step 5 - If the court did not act

There is a petition branch of this law for that situation, and for people who completed a deferral programme or satisfied the judgment imposed.

That petition is not ready. Two questions have to be answered first:

- whether Indiana publishes a statewide form for this petition, and
- how counties assign a case type where no cause number was ever assigned to your matter.

Until both are settled, this packet stops at the check. Nothing is filed and there is nothing to sign.

Here is what is already known about that petition, so you can plan:

- It is filed in the court where the charges were brought or the trial was held. If prosecution was deferred, it goes to the court with jurisdiction over the violation.
- It is filed under your original cause number. If no cause number was assigned, it is opened as its own case, and how that is done is one of the two open questions.
- There is no fee, so there is nothing to pay and no fee waiver to apply for.
- You verify and sign it yourself. Nobody signs it for you.
- It is served on the prosecuting attorney, who then has thirty days to file a notice in opposition.
- After that the court may grant it without a hearing, set a hearing, or deny it without a hearing. If the prosecuting attorney opposes it and the court does not rule without a hearing, there will be a hearing.

## If prosecution was deferred

The part of the law where the court acts on its own does not cover deferred prosecutions. You are on the other branch, and that branch has a five-year wait that starts when you satisfy the conditions of the deferral programme or the judgment.

## About your fees, copies and local requirements

Where fee, copy or service details are not stated above, check the current local requirements with the court or agency you are filing in. Do not assume a fee or an address from another county.

## Two things to know about Indiana records

- Records are not destroyed. This relief stops information from being handed out to organisations and people outside the criminal justice system. It does not delete anything.
- If a case file is opened to ask a court for record relief, that file is public until the order is granted.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- The prosecuting attorney files a notice in opposition.
- The court sets a hearing.
- Prosecution was deferred and the five-year period has not run.
- Your county's handling of the case-type assignment is unclear.
