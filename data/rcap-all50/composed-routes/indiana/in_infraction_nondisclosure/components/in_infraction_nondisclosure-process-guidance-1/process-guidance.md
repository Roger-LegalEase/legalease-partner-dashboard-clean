# Check whether the court has already done this

Indiana law puts this duty on the court itself. In four situations the court is the one that must order the clerk, and whoever runs the case management system, not to give out information about your infraction to organisations and people outside the criminal justice system:

- you were never prosecuted for the conduct
- the charge was dismissed
- you were found not to have committed the infraction
- an adjudication against you was later vacated

So the first thing to find out is whether that order was already entered. If it was, there is nothing for you to file.

This step does not produce anything to file. It is a check.

## How to check

Ask the clerk of the court where the infraction was handled whether the court entered an order under I.C. 34-28-5-15 in your cause.

You do this yourself. LegalEase does not contact the clerk, and does not receive or look at what the clerk tells you.

When you have the answer, compare it with what you told us. If the clerk's answer disagrees with your answer, correct it before going any further.

## What you will be asked

- In which Indiana county was the infraction handled?
- What is the cause number, if one was assigned?
- How did it end - never prosecuted, dismissed, found not to have committed it, an adjudication later vacated, a deferral programme completed, or a judgment satisfied?
- On what date did that happen?
- Was prosecution deferred?
- Has the court already ordered the clerk not to disclose the infraction?

## If prosecution was deferred

The part of the law where the court acts on its own does not cover you. Deferred prosecutions go down a different branch, and that branch has a five-year wait that starts when you satisfy the conditions of the deferral programme or the judgment.

## What happens if the court did not act

There is a petition branch for that, and for people who finished a deferral programme or satisfied a judgment.

We are not preparing that petition yet. Two things have to be settled first: whether Indiana publishes a statewide form for it, and how counties handle the case-type assignment where no cause number was ever assigned to your matter. Until those are answered, this packet stops at this check.

## What this step does not do

- It does not tell you whether you qualify.
- It does not work out your earliest filing date.
- It does not create, replace, or stand in for any court form.
- It does not contact the court, the clerk, or the prosecuting attorney on your behalf.

## Two things to know about Indiana records

- Records are not destroyed. This order stops information from being given out to organisations and people outside the criminal justice system. It does not delete anything.
- If a case file is opened to ask a court for this kind of relief, that file is public until the order is granted.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- The prosecuting attorney files a notice in opposition.
- The court sets a hearing.
- Prosecution was deferred and the five-year period has not run.
- Your county's handling of the case-type assignment is unclear.
