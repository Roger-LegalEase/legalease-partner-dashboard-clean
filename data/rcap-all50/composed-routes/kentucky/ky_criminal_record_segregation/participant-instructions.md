# How to complete, sign, send and file your segregation papers

Read the routing sheet first. It answers a question that comes before all of this: whether segregation is the right thing to ask for at all, or whether you should be asking for expungement instead.

If you have done that and segregation is what you want, here is what to do.

## What you have

Depending on the route you chose, one or both of these:

- **An application to the court**, asking for one order that covers every law enforcement agency.
- **A letter to each agency you named**, asking that agency directly.

There is nothing else. No form to buy, no official form to download — there is no official form for either of these.

## 1. Check how the case ended against your own paperwork

You told us how it ended: found innocent, all charges dismissed, or all charges withdrawn. Check that against your own paperwork before you send anything, and correct the packet if the two disagree.

If you do not have anything showing how it ended, ask the clerk for the dismissal or acquittal order. If the charges were withdrawn before anything was ever filed, ask the arresting agency or the prosecutor's office what record exists.

You get that document yourself. We do not obtain it, receive it or look at it. It is not required to be filed with anything.

## 2. The one thing that is yours alone

**Whether *all* the charges relating to the offence were dismissed or withdrawn.** That is your own statement of fact. Nobody supplies it for you and nobody works it out for you from a record.

If some charges were resolved in your favour and others were not, this route does not reach the arrest. And if anyone disagrees with you about it, stop and talk to a lawyer.

## 3. Sign everything

You sign the application. You sign every agency letter. All of them are yours.

**Nothing needs to be notarized.**

## 4. If you are writing to agencies

Address an envelope for each letter and post them. One letter per agency.

Think carefully about who actually holds a copy of the record: the department that arrested you, the jail that booked you, the state police, anyone else involved. The letters only reach the agencies you send them to.

You post them yourself.

## 5. If you are applying to the court

File the application with the circuit court clerk in the county where the case was tried.

If charges were never filed, it goes to the court where the case *would* have been tried. If you do not know which court that is, ask the circuit court clerk in the county where you were arrested.

**There is nobody to serve.** The law does not require you to notify a prosecutor or anyone else about this application, and there is no certificate of service in this packet.

## 6. Ask the clerk about the fee

We do not know whether the court charges a filing fee for this application. Nothing in the law we read sets one, and nothing we read says there is none either.

So ask the clerk before you go. Do not assume it is free and do not assume it costs something.

## What happens next

On the court route, the law says the court issues the order to all law enforcement agencies straight away once it has your application, and each agency that segregates has to notify every agency it passed your records on to.

On the letters route, the duty lands on each agency when your letter arrives.

We cannot tell you how long any of that takes in practice, because nothing we read says.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- Your records could be expunged under Kentucky's acquittal, dismissal or conviction expungement statutes. Expungement is much stronger than segregation.
- You were expecting the court's own file to be reached. It cannot be.
- You were expecting background checks to come back clean. Segregation does not delete anything.
- Anyone disagrees with you about whether all the charges relating to the offence were dismissed or withdrawn.
