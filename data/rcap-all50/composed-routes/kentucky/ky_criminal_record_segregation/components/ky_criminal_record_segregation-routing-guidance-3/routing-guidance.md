# Before you use this: is segregation even the right thing to ask for?

Segregation is a fallback. It is worth being honest about that before you spend any effort on it.

## What segregation actually is

An agency that holds the record of your arrest — the arrest report, your fingerprints, your photographs, other data — has to file that record separately from the records of people who were convicted, if you were found innocent or if all the charges relating to the offence were dismissed or withdrawn.

That is the whole of it. The record still exists. It is still there, and it still shows how the case ended. It has been moved into a different drawer.

It is not expungement. Nothing is erased. Nothing is destroyed. If you are hoping a background check comes back clean, this is not the thing that does that, and you should stop here and talk to a lawyer about what would.

Court records cannot be segregated at all. The court's own file stays exactly where it is.

## First question: should you be asking for expungement instead?

This is the important one.

Kentucky law says that records covered by its expungement statutes — KRS 431.076 for acquittals, dismissals and failures to indict, and KRS 431.078 for convictions — get handled under those statutes instead of this one. In practice that covers most acquittals and most dismissals.

Expungement is much stronger than segregation. If you can expunge, expunge.

So:

- **Were you acquitted, or were the charges dismissed?** Then look at expungement first. That is very likely your route, not this one.
- **Were the charges withdrawn before anything was decided?** That is the situation this route most clearly still fits, because the expungement statute for acquittals and dismissals does not name withdrawn charges.
- **Have the records already been expunged?** Then there is nothing here to do.

If you are not sure which bucket you are in, stop and ask a lawyer. Getting this wrong costs you the stronger remedy.

## Second question: which of the two routes?

If segregation really is what you want, there are two ways to ask, and they are independent. Either one gets you segregation. You can use one, or the other, or both.

### The court route

You apply to the court. On receiving your application the court issues an order to **all** law enforcement agencies — not just the ones you thought of.

That order also puts each agency under a duty to notify every other agency it passed your records on to.

One filing, and it reaches everyone. That is its advantage, and it is a real one, because you almost certainly do not know every agency that has a copy.

You file it in the court where the case was tried. If charges were never filed at all, it goes to the court where the case *would* have been tried — and if you do not know which court that is, ask the circuit court clerk in the county where you were arrested.

### The letters route

You write directly to each agency yourself. The duty to segregate runs to the agency the moment it gets your written request. No court is involved at all.

This works, but it only reaches the agencies you write to. You need to think about who actually holds a copy: the department that arrested you, the jail that booked you, the state police, anyone else.

### Or both

Nothing stops you doing both. The court order sweeps broadly; the letters make sure the agencies you know about have heard from you directly.

## What you will be asked

- In which Kentucky county were you arrested, and on what date?
- What were you arrested for?
- Were charges ever actually filed, or did the matter end before that? If they were filed, what is the case number?
- How did it end — were you found innocent, were all charges dismissed, or were all charges withdrawn? On what date?
- Have these records already been expunged, or could they be expunged under Kentucky's acquittal, dismissal or conviction expungement statutes?
- Which agencies hold records of the arrest?
- Do you want the court order, the letters, or both?

## What this step does not do

- It does not decide whether you are eligible for anything.
- It does not tell you whether your records were already segregated or expunged.
- It does not contact any court or any agency for you.
- It does not promise that any agency will act, or say when.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- Your records could be expunged under KRS 431.076 or KRS 431.078. Expungement is materially stronger and you should not settle for segregation.
- You were hoping this would make background checks come back clean. It will not.
- You were hoping the court's own file would be reached. It cannot be.
- Anyone disagrees with you about whether *all* the charges relating to the offence were dismissed or withdrawn.
