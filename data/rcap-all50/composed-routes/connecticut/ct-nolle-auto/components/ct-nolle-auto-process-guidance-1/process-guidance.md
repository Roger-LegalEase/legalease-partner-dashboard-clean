# After a nolle: what happens on its own, and what does not

Connecticut calls this erasure. Not expungement, not sealing.

## If your charge was already nolled

You do not file anything. The law erases the records once **thirteen months** have gone by since the nolle.

That covers police records, court records, and the records of any state's attorney relating to the nolled charge.

What you should do is check that it actually happened. Connecticut's automatic erasure ran into years of data and technology problems and only got going at scale in late 2025, so a record that should have been erased may still be sitting there. Nobody writes to tell you either way.

Look the case up on the Judicial Branch online case look-up and confirm the nolle date. **A case that has been erased will not appear** — so if it is gone from the look-up, that is the answer you were hoping for. You obtain this yourself; LegalEase does not look it up for you.

If the thirteen months have passed and the record is still there, that is a different path — asking the state to fix a record that should have been erased. It is an agency process, not this one.

## If your charge is still being continued at the prosecutor's request

This is the part that is not passive.

Where a charge was continued at the prosecutor's request and thirteen months have gone by with no prosecution and no other disposition, the law says the charge **shall be nolled** on the motion of the arrested person. That is a motion you can make. Once the nolle is entered, the erasure rules above take over.

**This packet cannot prepare that motion yet.** No form for it was found, and how it should be captioned, where exactly it should be filed, whether anything must be served and what the clerks actually accept have not been settled. Preparing papers on guesswork would put you in front of a clerk with something that may be rejected. If this is your situation, talk to a lawyer or a legal-aid clinic.

## One older situation

If your nolle was entered before 1 April 1972 in one of the older courts the statute lists, the records are already deemed erased by operation of law, and the arrested person or an heir may petition to have them erased. Nothing beyond that is settled here, so this packet does not prepare that petition either.

## What you will be asked

- Was the charge nolled, or is it still being continued at the prosecutor's request?
- On what date was the charge nolled?
- On what date was the charge continued at the prosecutor's request?
- Has there been any prosecution or other disposition since then?

Your answer to the first question decides which of these paths you are on. This packet does not choose for you.

## Costs

No court fee is charged in any court for a petition under this statute.

## What this step does not do

- It does not tell you whether your record qualified.
- It does not count the thirteen months for you — check the dates against your own record.
- It does not look up your case or contact the court, the state's attorney or the state police on your behalf.
- It does not prepare a motion or a petition.

## Stop and get a lawyer if

- You need the motion to nolle a continued charge — how it must be filed is not settled.
- The thirteen months have not yet gone by.
- The record was not erased when it should have been.
- You have an immigration matter.
