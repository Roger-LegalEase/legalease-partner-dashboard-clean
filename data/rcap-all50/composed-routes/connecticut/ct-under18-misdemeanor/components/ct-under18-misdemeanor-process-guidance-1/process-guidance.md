# A misdemeanor from something that happened before you turned 18

First, what this is not. This is an **adult record** — an adult conviction for conduct you committed before you turned 18. It is not juvenile delinquency relief, and it does not work the same way.

Connecticut calls this erasure. Not expungement, not sealing.

There are two paths, and the date the offense happened decides which one you are on. Nobody picks for you.

## If the offense happened on or after 1 January 2000 and before 1 July 2012

Nothing to file. Misdemeanors committed while you were under 18 in that window are erased, or deemed erased by operation of law where the records were scanned or were never electronic.

Two things this path does not reach:

- a motor vehicle offense, or a violation under title 14
- a violation of section 51-164r

Copy the statute sections straight off your own record and check them against that list yourself. This packet does not classify your offense for you.

What you should do is confirm it actually happened. Automatic erasure in Connecticut stalled for years on data and technology problems and only got going at scale in late 2025, and nobody writes to tell you either way. Look the case up on the Judicial Branch online case look-up — an erased case will not appear. If it is still showing, the record was not erased when it should have been, and that is a different path: an agency process for fixing a missed erasure.

## If the offense happened before 1 January 2000

There is no automatic erasure for that window. The law says you **may file a petition** with the Superior Court at the location where the conviction was effected, and the court **shall** direct erasure.

**This packet cannot prepare that petition.** No form for it was found. There is a Judicial Branch form called JD-CR-202, but on its face that is a Clean Slate form rather than a form for this subsection, and whether it is used for this in practice has not been established. The rule about how offenses from that era were classified is also unsettled. If this is your situation, talk to a lawyer or a legal-aid clinic before you file anything.

## The rule that can undo either path

If there was a conviction on the **same information** for a charge that could not be erased, this relief does not apply — and that is true even though a different rule normally handles multi-count cases.

So look at everything charged on the same information, not just the count you care about. This packet draws no conclusion about your information.

## What you will be asked

- On what date did the offense happen?
- Were you under 18 when the offense happened?
- Which statute sections were you convicted under? Copy them from your record.
- Were there other charges on the same information, and was any of them one that could not be erased?
- At which court location was the conviction effected?

## Costs

No fee.

## What this step does not do

- It does not tell you whether you qualify or classify your offense.
- It does not look up your case or contact any court on your behalf.
- It does not prepare a petition.

## Stop and get a lawyer if

- The offense happened before 1 January 2000 — no accepted way to file has been identified.
- Another charge on the same information could not be erased.
- Your offense is a motor vehicle offense, a title 14 violation or a section 51-164r violation.
- You have an immigration matter.
