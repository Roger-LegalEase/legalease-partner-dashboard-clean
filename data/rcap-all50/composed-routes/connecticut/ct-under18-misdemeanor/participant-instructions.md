# Erasing a misdemeanor from conduct before you turned 18

This is an **adult record** — an adult conviction for something you did before you turned 18. It is not juvenile relief.

Connecticut calls this erasure. Not expungement, not sealing.

## 1. Look your case up first

Look the case up on the Connecticut Judicial Branch online case look-up and confirm:

- the date the offense happened
- every charge on the information
- the court location where the conviction was effected

You obtain this yourself. LegalEase does not obtain, receive or inspect it. If what you find disagrees with the answers you gave, correct the packet.

## 2. Work out which path the offense date puts you on

- **Offense on or after 1 January 2000 and before 1 July 2012:** erased automatically. Nothing to file.
- **Offense before 1 January 2000:** you may file a petition with the Superior Court at the location where the conviction was effected, and the court shall direct erasure.

Nobody picks the path for you. Read the date off your own record.

## 3. If you are on the automatic path

There is nothing to complete, sign, serve or file.

Check that it actually happened: look the case up again — an erased case will not appear. If the case is still showing, the record was not erased when it should have been, which is a different path (an agency process for a missed erasure).

Two kinds of offense the automatic path does not reach: a motor vehicle offense or a violation under title 14, and a violation of section 51-164r. Copy the statute sections off your own record and check them yourself.

## 4. If you are on the petition path

**This packet cannot prepare that petition.** No form for it was found, and the Judicial Branch form JD-CR-202 is, on its face, a Clean Slate form rather than a form for this subsection — whether it is used for this in practice has not been established. The rule about how offenses from that era were classified is also unsettled.

Take this to a lawyer or a legal-aid clinic rather than filing something built on a guess.

## 5. The rule that can undo either path

If there was a conviction on the **same information** for a charge that could not be erased, this relief does not apply — and that is so even though a different rule normally handles multi-count cases. Look at everything charged on the same information.

## Fees, signing, notarizing, service

- **Fee.** None.
- **Signing.** Nothing to sign on the automatic path. Unresolved on the petition path, because it depends on the vehicle.
- **Notarizing.** The sources do not state a requirement.
- **Service.** The sources do not state a requirement.

## Stop and get a lawyer if

- The offense happened before 1 January 2000.
- Another charge on the same information could not be erased.
- Your offense is a motor vehicle offense, a title 14 violation or a section 51-164r violation.
- You have an immigration matter.
