# Asking the state to fix a record that should have been erased

This one does not go to a courthouse. It goes to the Department of Emergency Services and Public Protection — the department that keeps Connecticut's official criminal history.

Connecticut calls this erasure. Not expungement, not sealing.

## 1. Order your criminal history record

Request your Connecticut criminal history record from the State Police Bureau of Identification using form DPS-0846-C. Mail it, with a check or money order payable to "Treasurer-State of CT," to:

    DESPP-SPBI
    1111 Country Club Road
    Middletown, CT 06457-2389

You do this yourself. LegalEase cannot complete this request, and does not obtain, receive or inspect the record that comes back.

There is a charge for the record. The copy of the request form on file here is a 2017 revision, so check the current fee and the current process with the department before you mail anything rather than relying on what is printed on an old form.

## 2. Read the record

Find the conviction you believe should have been erased and look at whether the information is **marked as erased**.

The law requires you to include a copy of this record search showing that the information has **not** been marked as erased. That copy is the evidence for the whole request.

If your answer to "does the record show it as erased" turns out to be different from what you told us, correct the packet.

## 3. Send the request to the department

This is the step this packet cannot complete for you.

The law says the request must go to the department **in the form and manner the department determines**. That form and manner is not written into the statute, and it has not been located. Nothing in this packet invents one.

Before you send anything, contact the Department of Emergency Services and Public Protection and ask how a request for review of a missed erasure under section 54-142t(g) must be submitted.

## 4. Know what you are walking into

The department decides the question **following a contested hearing**. That is an adversarial proceeding run by the agency, and the decision that comes out of it is a final decision under the state's administrative procedure act — with the appeal rights that go with it.

That is a lawyer's proceeding. Get a lawyer before the hearing.

## Signing, notarizing, fees and service

- **Signing.** Unresolved, because it depends on the form the department requires.
- **Notarizing.** The sources do not state a requirement.
- **Fee for the request itself.** No fee is stated. The real cost is the record check.
- **Service.** The sources do not state a service requirement.

## Stop and get a lawyer if

- You have reached the submission and the department's required form and manner is not settled.
- The department sets the contested hearing.
- You have an immigration matter.
