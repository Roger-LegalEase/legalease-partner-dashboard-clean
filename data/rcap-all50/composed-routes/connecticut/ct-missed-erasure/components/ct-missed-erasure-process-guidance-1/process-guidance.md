# Checking whether your record was actually erased

Connecticut erases some records automatically, by operation of law. Nobody writes to tell you it happened. The automatic system also stalled for years on data and technology problems and only resumed at scale in late 2025, so a record that should have been erased may not have been.

So the first question is not "how do I fix it" — it is "was it actually erased?" There is only one way to answer that: read your own criminal history record.

## Step one — order your criminal history record

Connecticut's official criminal history is held by the State Police Bureau of Identification, part of the Department of Emergency Services and Public Protection. You request it on form DPS-0846-C and mail it in.

You do this yourself. LegalEase cannot make this request for you, and does not obtain, receive or look at the record that comes back.

There is a cost, and the request form on file here is an old revision. Check the current fee and the current process with the department before you send anything — do not rely on a figure from an old form.

## Step two — read it

Look for the conviction you think should have been erased. The thing to look for is whether the information is **marked as erased**.

If it is marked as erased, the state reached it and there is nothing to fix.

If it is not marked as erased, and you believe the law required it to be deemed erased, that record is the evidence — the law requires a copy of exactly this search, showing that the information has not been marked as erased.

## Step three — what comes next

The next step goes to the department, not to a court. There is no courthouse, no docket number and no judge on this path.

**And this is where this packet stops.** The law says the submission must be made in the form and manner the department determines, and that form and manner is not written into the statute. It has not been located. Nothing here invents one, because a submission sent the wrong way is worse than no submission.

What happens after the submission is also worth knowing before you start: the department decides the question following a **contested hearing**. That is an adversarial agency proceeding, and its decision is a final decision under the state's administrative procedure act. It is a lawyer's proceeding. Plan on getting a lawyer for it.

## What you will be asked

- Which conviction do you believe should have been erased automatically, and on what basis?
- On what date did the offense happen?
- What is the date of your most recent judgment of conviction for any crime?
- Have you obtained your criminal history record search yet?
- Does the record show the information marked as erased?

## What this step does not do

- It does not tell you whether your record qualified for automatic erasure.
- It does not order, receive or read your criminal history record.
- It does not contact the department, any court, or any agency on your behalf.
- It does not create a submission to the department.

## Stop and get a lawyer if

- You have reached the submission itself — the department's required form and manner is not settled.
- The department sets a contested hearing.
- You have an immigration matter.

---

Connecticut calls this erasure — not expungement, not sealing. Use that word when you call.

This is a document-preparation step, not legal advice.
