# How to ask the court to seal your arrest after diversion or a deferred entry of judgment

California — Penal Code section 851.90

This is your checklist. It is for you to keep. Do not file it.

This is not an official court form.

---

## Before you start: what this is, and what it is not

This asks the judge to order your **arrest records sealed**. It is not a
dismissal, it is not a set-aside, and it is not an expungement. Those are
different things.

The judge may order the sealing on a written motion, on an oral motion by any
party, or on the court's own motion. Notice must go to all parties.

Whether the judge grants it is up to the judge.

**California has no statewide form for this.** Each county publishes its own
petition and order. We are not choosing a form for you, because we have not
collected or approved a local form set for any county.

---

## What you need to have ready

- Your full legal name, and any other names you have used.
- Your contact information.
- The case number.
- The California county that handled the case.
- The court that handled the case.
- The date you were arrested.
- What the arrest was for.
- Which programme you were in — a drug diversion programme, or a deferred entry
  of judgment programme.
- The date you finished the programme, if you know it.
- Your records or background report, if you have one.

If you do not know something, leave it blank and say so. Do not guess.

---

## Step 1 — Get your proof of programme completion

Ask the superior court that handled your case, or the programme provider, for
documentation that you completed the programme.

We do not obtain, receive, or inspect this document. You get it yourself.

Do this before you file.

## Step 2 — Check your answer against the proof

You answered "yes" to *Did you successfully complete the programme?*

Read your proof of completion and confirm that it agrees. If it does not agree,
correct your answers before you go any further.

If there is any dispute about whether you completed the programme, stop and talk
to a lawyer.

## Step 3 — Get the county's own petition and order forms

Contact the superior court clerk, or the court's self-help centre, in the county
that handled your case.

Ask them: **which local form applies to a Penal Code section 851.90 sealing?**

Ask for both the petition and the proposed order.

San Diego is known to use a local criminal form numbered SDSC CRM-307, and Kern
County is known to publish its own local petition. We have not verified that
either is current. Confirm with the clerk either way.

While you have the clerk on the line, also ask:

- Where do I file this?
- Is there a filing fee, and how much is it?
- Does the petition have to be notarized?
- How and when do I have to give notice to the other parties?

We have not been given answers to any of those four questions, so we are not
printing answers we do not have. The clerk or self-help centre can tell you.

## Step 4 — Fill in the county's petition

Fill it in with the information you gathered above.

Leave blank anything you do not know. Do not invent a date, a fee, a court
department, a judge's name, or anything about what the prosecutor thinks.

## Step 5 — Sign and date it

You sign the petition yourself, in the signature block on the county's form, and
you date it.

Nobody else signs it for you.

Whether it must be notarized is not something we can tell you. Ask the clerk in
Step 3.

## Step 6 — If you cannot pay a filing fee

We do not have fee information for this route, so we cannot tell you what it
costs or whether it is free.

If the clerk tells you there is a fee and you cannot pay it, ask for California's
general fee waiver form, **FW-001**, and file that too.

## Step 7 — Give notice to the other parties

Notice to all parties is required on a motion under section 851.90.

We have not been given a separate service rule beyond that. Ask the clerk what
notice the court expects, in what form, on whom, and how far in advance.

## Step 8 — File

File the county's own petition in the superior court that handled your diversion
or deferred entry of judgment.

Use the filing location the clerk or the county's own form tells you to use. We
are not printing a court address, because we have not been given one.

---

## After you file — what the court does

If the judge signs the order:

- The court gives a copy of the order to you and to the prosecuting attorney.
- Within 30 days the court forwards the order to the arresting agency and to the
  other agencies that took part.
- Local records are marked "arrest sealed."
- State records are marked "arrest relief granted."

Those are the court's steps, not yours.

---

## Stop and get a lawyer if

- The prosecutor opposes the motion.
- The court sets a contested hearing.
- There is a dispute about whether you successfully completed the programme.
- You cannot find out from the clerk which local form your county uses.

A routine hearing by itself is not a reason to stop.

---

## Things you must verify yourself

We have deliberately left these blank rather than guess. Verify each one with the
filing court or agency:

- The name and address of the court and the department to file in.
- The filing fee, if any.
- Whether the petition must be notarized.
- How and when notice must be given to the other parties.
- Which local petition and order form your county currently uses.
- Whether any additional conditions apply — the full text of Penal Code sections
  851.87, 1000.4 and 1001.9 has not been reviewed, so there may be requirements
  or exclusions this checklist does not mention.

This is not legal advice and it does not guarantee that the court will grant
anything.
