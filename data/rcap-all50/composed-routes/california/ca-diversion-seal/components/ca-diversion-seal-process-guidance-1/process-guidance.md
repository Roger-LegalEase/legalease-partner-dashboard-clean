## What this route does

If you were diverted under a superior court drug diversion programme, or you
were admitted to a deferred entry of judgment programme, and you successfully
completed it, the judge may order your arrest records sealed.

The judge may do this on a written motion, on an oral motion by any party, or on
the court's own motion. Notice must go to all parties.

This is arrest sealing. It is not a dismissal, a set-aside, or an expungement.
Those are different kinds of relief with different rules.

Whether the judge grants it is up to the judge. Meeting the requirements does not
guarantee an order.

## There is no statewide form for this route

California has no statewide Judicial Council form for a § 851.90 sealing.
Counties publish their own petition and order.

We cannot tell you which local form your county uses. We have not collected or
approved a local petition and order set for any county, so we are not selecting
one for you.

**Ask the county superior court clerk or the court's self-help centre which local
form applies to a Penal Code § 851.90 sealing.** Ask for both the petition and the
proposed order — the two usually go together.

Two counties are known to publish their own: San Diego uses a local criminal form
numbered SDSC CRM-307, and Kern County publishes its own local petition. We have
not verified that either is current, so confirm with the clerk either way.

## Where it is filed

In the superior court that handled the diversion or the deferred entry of
judgment. Local practice and local forms govern.

We have not been given the name, address, or department of any court for this
route, so we are not printing one. The clerk or self-help centre will tell you
where to file.

## What happens after the judge signs an order

These steps are the court's, not yours:

- The court gives a copy of the order to you and to the prosecuting attorney.
- Within 30 days the court forwards the order to the arresting agency and to the
  other agencies that took part.
- Local records are marked "arrest sealed."
- State records are marked "arrest relief granted."

None of this has happened yet. Nothing in this document says that an order was
made, that anything was served, or that anything was filed.

## Fees

We have not been given fee information for this route. We are not telling you

If you cannot pay the filing fee, California has a general fee waiver form,
FW-001. Ask the clerk for it.

## Notice to the other parties

Notice to all parties is required on a motion under § 851.90.

We have not been given a separate service rule beyond that, so we are not telling
you how, when, or on whom to serve. Ask the clerk what notice the court expects
and how far in advance.

## When to stop and get a lawyer

Stop and talk to a lawyer if:

- The prosecutor opposes the motion.
- The court sets a contested hearing.
- There is any dispute about whether you successfully completed the programme.
- Your county has no local form you can identify.
