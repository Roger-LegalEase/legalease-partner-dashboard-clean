# Records cleared after completing a deferred sentence — what you do

Start here: on a deferred sentence, you probably do not file anything at all.

When you fulfil the terms of probation and of the deferred sentence agreement, the court strikes the
finding of guilt and discharges you. On that discharge the record of the criminal proceedings is expunged,
unless the court finds good cause not to, and the court issues an order to expunge all records and files
related to the arrest, citation, investigation, charge, finding of guilt, criminal proceedings and
probation. That is the court's own duty. There is no petition for it and nothing for you to submit.

The one condition that can hold it up is money: the record is not expunged until restitution has been paid
in full. That is a rule in the statute, not just a document to gather.

## Before anything else

- Get the order discharging you from probation and striking the finding of guilt. Ask the clerk of the
  Criminal Division that entered your deferred sentence. It establishes the date the court's duty attached.
- Get a statement from the Restitution Unit of the Vermont Center for Crime Victim Services showing
  restitution paid in full. Ask them for a current balance.
- Check your record. Complete Form 200-00331, Request for Criminal Record Search, and submit it to the
  court in the county. This packet does not include that form — get the current version from the Vermont
  Judiciary before you start filling anything in: https://www.vtcourts.gov/criminal/expungement
- Compare each document against the answers you gave and correct any answer the documents contradict.

## If the record no longer shows

The order issued. There is nothing to file and nothing to pay. Skip the rest of this.

## If the record still shows

Only then does a written request come into it — and only if you were discharged, the finding of guilt was
struck, and restitution is paid in full.

1. Read the written request through. Every fact in it is your own answer. Correct anything that is wrong.
2. Fill in the county and the docket number. They are your own case details and are left blank for you.
3. Sign and date the request in the signature block. You sign it yourself. It does not have to be
   notarised.
4. Take or send it to the Criminal Division of the Vermont Superior Court that entered the deferred
   sentence. It goes in under the existing docket, not as a new case.
5. Do not serve anyone. No service is required of you on this route, and no prosecutor has to be notified.

## Costs

There is no fee. This relief is not a chapter 230 filing, so no chapter 230 filing fee applies to it, and
the statute provides for a court-issued order without a fee. If the court asks you for a copy or
certification cost on your record search, ask the clerk what that is — that is a records cost, not a fee
for this request.

## What is left blank for you

- The county and the docket number. Take them from your own court paperwork.
- The judge and the prosecutor. This request does not name them.
- Your date of birth and identifying numbers.
- Nothing in this packet says how the court will rule, or when.

## What we do not know

How long a Vermont court usually takes to enter the order after a discharge is not something we have
established. We therefore do not tell you a number of days or weeks after which the order is "late." If
you are unsure whether the order is missing or simply not entered yet, ask the clerk of the court that
handled your case.

## What this does not reach

An expunged or sealed Vermont record may still appear in a federal criminal background check. Vermont
relief does not reach federal records, even though the Vermont Crime Information Center notifies the FBI's
National Crime Information Center. Nobody can promise you a clean federal check.

## Stop and get a lawyer if

- The court has made, or signals it will make, a finding of good cause against expungement.
- The offence is one that requires sex offender registration. Vermont keeps a confidential index of
  deferred sentences for those offences — name, date of birth, offence and docket number — accessible only
  to the Vermont Crime Information Center director and one designated staff person, for presentence
  investigations. That index stays even after the expungement.
- You were not in fact discharged, or the finding of guilt was not struck. That means the deferred
  sentence was not fulfilled.
- Restitution is still outstanding and you are asking whether the record can be cleared anyway. It cannot.
- Immigration consequences are in play.
