# What to do with what you have

## If the record no longer shows

The court entered the order and you are done. Nothing is filed and nothing is owed.

Two things are worth knowing about what the order does and does not reach.

- The court sends copies of the order to each agency, department or official named in it. Afterwards the
  court, law enforcement officers, agencies and departments answer that no record exists.
- Vermont relief does not reach federal records. An expunged or sealed Vermont record may still show up in
  a federal criminal background check, even though the Vermont Crime Information Center notifies the FBI's
  National Crime Information Center. Nobody can promise you a clean federal check.

## If the record still shows

You were discharged, the finding of guilt was struck, and restitution is paid — but the record is still
there. The next step is a written request asking the court to enter the order the statute requires.

1. Read the request through. Everything in it should be your own answer. If anything is wrong, fix it
   before you send it.
2. Fill in the county and the docket number if they are blank. They are your own case details and we do
   not fill them in for you.
3. Sign and date it in the signature block. You sign it yourself.
4. Send or take it to the Criminal Division of the Vermont Superior Court that entered the deferred
   sentence. It goes in under the existing docket, not as a new case.
5. There is no fee. This is not a chapter 230 filing, and no filing fee applies to it.
6. You do not serve anyone. Nobody has to be served with this request and no prosecutor has to be notified.

## What is left blank for you

- The county and the docket number are your own case details.
- The judge, the prosecutor, and anything about what a prosecutor will say are not filled in, because
  nobody knows them and this request does not need them.
- Your date of birth and identifying numbers are not filled in.
- Nothing in this packet says how the court will rule, or when.

## What we do not know

We do not know how long Vermont courts usually take to enter the order after a discharge, so we cannot
tell you at what point the order is genuinely missing rather than simply not entered yet. If you are
unsure, ask the clerk of the court that handled your case.

## Stop and get a lawyer if

- The court has made, or signals it will make, a finding of good cause against expungement.
- The offence is one that requires sex offender registration.
- You were not in fact discharged, or the finding of guilt was not struck.
- Restitution is still outstanding.
- Immigration consequences are in play.
