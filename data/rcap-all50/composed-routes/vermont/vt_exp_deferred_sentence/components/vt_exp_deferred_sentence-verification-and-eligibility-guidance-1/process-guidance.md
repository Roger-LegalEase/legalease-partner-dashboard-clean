# Deferred sentence: the court clears the record itself

If your case ended in a deferred sentence agreement and you finished it, clearing the record is the
court's job, not yours. When you fulfil the terms of probation and of the deferred sentence agreement,
the court strikes the finding of guilt and discharges you. On that discharge the record of the criminal
proceedings is expunged, unless the court finds good cause not to, and the court issues an order to
expunge all records and files related to the arrest, citation, investigation, charge, finding of guilt,
criminal proceedings and probation.

You do not file anything to get this. There is no form to fill in and nothing to submit. If anyone
offers you a petition packet for a deferred sentence, that is the wrong product.

One condition can hold it up. The record is not expunged until restitution has been paid in full. That
is a rule in the statute itself, not just paperwork — while a restitution balance is open, the
expungement does not happen no matter how long ago you were discharged.

## What you will be asked

- Did your case end in a deferred sentence agreement rather than an ordinary sentence?
- Have you been discharged from probation, and did the court strike the finding of guilt?
- What is the date of discharge from probation?
- Has all restitution been paid in full? The record cannot be expunged until it is.
- When you check your record, does the case still show? If it does, the expungement order may not have issued.
- Was the offence one that requires sex offender registration? Vermont keeps a confidential index for those cases even after expungement.
- Which county's Criminal Division entered the deferred sentence?
- What is the docket number?

## How to check that it happened

1. Get the order discharging you from probation and striking the finding of guilt. Ask the clerk of the
   Criminal Division that entered the deferred sentence. That order establishes the date the court's duty
   attached.
2. Get a statement from the Restitution Unit of the Vermont Center for Crime Victim Services showing
   restitution paid in full. Ask them for a current balance. An open balance is the thing that most often
   explains a record that has not been cleared.
3. Check your record. Complete Form 200-00331, Request for Criminal Record Search, and submit it to the
   court in the county. This is how you see whether the record is still there.
4. Compare each of those documents against the answers you gave. If a document and your answer disagree,
   correct your answer — the documents govern.

If the record no longer shows, the order issued and there is nothing further to do. If it still shows,
and you were discharged and restitution is paid, there is a separate written request that asks the court
to enter the order the statute requires.

## What this step does not do

- It does not tell you whether you qualify.
- It does not create, replace, or stand in for any court form.
- It does not contact the court, the Restitution Unit, or any agency on your behalf.
- It does not tell you how long to wait before treating the order as missing. How long Vermont courts
  take in practice is not something we have established, so we do not put a number on it.

## Stop and get a lawyer if

- The court has made, or signals it will make, a finding of good cause against expungement.
- The offence is one that requires sex offender registration. Vermont keeps a confidential index of
  deferred sentences for those offences — name, date of birth, offence and docket number — which only the
  Vermont Crime Information Center director and one designated staff person can see, and which is used for
  presentence investigations. That index stays even after the expungement.
- You were not in fact discharged, or the finding of guilt was not struck. That means the deferred
  sentence was not fulfilled.
- Restitution is still outstanding and you are asking whether the record can be cleared anyway. It cannot.
- Immigration consequences are in play.
