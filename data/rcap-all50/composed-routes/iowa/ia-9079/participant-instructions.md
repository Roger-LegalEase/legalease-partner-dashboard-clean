# What to do about a deferred judgment after you finish probation

Iowa — Iowa Code section 907.9(4)

This is your checklist. It is for you to keep. Do not file it.

This is not an official court form.

---

## Before you start: what this is, and what it is not

You had a **deferred judgment** and you have been discharged from probation.

When that happens, the court's criminal record about the deferred judgment is
expunged. So are counts in the same charging document that were dismissed, and
other related dismissed charges that came out of the same transaction or
occurrence, or the same common scheme or plan.

**Expunged means the court record becomes confidential and comes off public
access** under Iowa Code section 22.7. It is not accessible except by court
order. It is not erased, it is not destroyed, and it is not removed from your
record.

Two things you should know up front:

- **The deferred judgment itself is not expunged.** It stays on the state court
  administrator's deferred judgment docket. That is how courts and prosecutors
  know a deferred judgment was used, and it counts toward the limit of two
  deferred judgments if you ever apply under Iowa Code section 901C.3 later.
- **Iowa has no felony expungement.**

**There is nothing to file.** This route is outside the Iowa expungement rules
(Rules 2.80 through 2.86) and there is no form for it. If your discharge was on
or after July 1, 2013, the expungement is expected to happen on its own. Your job
is to check that it did, and to fix it if it did not.

This checklist is free. No fee has been identified for it.

---

## What you need to have ready

- Your full legal name, and any other names you have used.
- Your contact information.
- The case number of the case with the deferred judgment.
- The Iowa county the case was in.
- The date you were discharged from probation.
- What the charge was.

If you do not know something, leave it blank and say so. Do not guess.

---

## Step 1 — Confirm it really was a deferred judgment

A deferred judgment is not a conviction and it is not a dismissal. This route
only works on a deferred judgment.

If there is any sign that what you got was a conviction, stop and talk to a
lawyer. A different route may apply to you.

## Step 2 — Confirm the deferred judgment was never revoked

If the court revoked your deferred judgment, this route does not apply. Revocation
puts the case outside chapter 907.

A revoked deferred judgment becomes a conviction. If it is a misdemeanour it may
be eligible under Iowa Code section 901C.3, which is a separate route with its own
requirements. Stop here and ask about that instead.

## Step 3 — Find your discharge date, and work out which branch you are on

Whether you were discharged **before or on/after July 1, 2013** decides everything.

**On or after July 1, 2013** — there is no application to make. Go to Step 4.

**Before July 1, 2013** — an application is required, but there is no statewide
form and no rule that says what it should look like. We are not giving you a
document, because we would be guessing at it. Talk to a lawyer, or ask the clerk
of the Iowa District Court in the county of your case what they need. If the clerk
declines to act, stop and talk to a lawyer.

## Step 4 — Ask the clerk for the balance in that case

This is the step that usually matters.

The record **is not expunged until** the restitution, civil penalties, court
costs, fees, and other financial obligations ordered by the court or assessed by
the clerk **in the case that contains the deferred judgment** are paid.

An unpaid balance in that case is the usual reason an expected expungement did not
happen. It is also something you can do something about.

Call or visit the clerk of the Iowa District Court for the county of your case and
ask for **the balance in that case number**. Ask about that case number
specifically.

We do not obtain, receive, or inspect that information. You get it yourself.

You do not have to do this before anything else — there is nothing to file — but
do it before you decide that something has gone wrong.

## Step 5 — Check your answer against what the clerk tells you

You answered the question *Have you paid every financial obligation in the case
that contains the deferred judgment?*

Compare your answer to what the clerk told you in Step 4. If they disagree, the
clerk's answer is the one that counts. Correct your answers before you go further.

If there is a dispute about what you owe, or about which case you owe it in, stop
and talk to a lawyer.

## Step 6 — Look up the case on the public docket

Check whether the case still appears on the Iowa public online docket.

- **It is gone.** That is consistent with the expungement having happened.
- **It is still there.** Go back to Step 4 first. If the balance really is paid and
  the case is still showing, go to Step 7.

We cannot tell you how long the court takes. No source we have gives a timeframe.

## Step 7 — Contact the clerk about the record itself

If everything in that case is paid and the case still appears on the public
docket, contact the clerk of the Iowa District Court for the county of the case and
ask about it.

We are not printing a court address or phone number, because we have not been
given one. Look up the clerk's office for that county.

## Signing, serving and filing

None of these apply on this route.

- **Nothing to sign.** There is no document for you to sign here.
- **Nothing to serve.** There is no petition, so there is nobody to serve it on.
- **Nothing to file.** For a discharge on or after July 1, 2013 there is no
  filing at all. For a discharge before that date an application is required, but
  the document does not exist yet — see Step 3.

## Fees

No fee has been identified. This route is free. There is nothing to file, so no
filing fee applies and no fee waiver is needed.

---

## Nobody will tell you when it is done

Iowa does not notify people that a deferred judgment record was expunged. There is
no letter coming. Checking the public docket yourself, as in Step 6, is how you
find out.

---

## Stop and get a lawyer if

- Your deferred judgment was revoked.
- There is a dispute about what you owe, or about which case you owe it in.
- Your discharge was before July 1, 2013 and the clerk declines to act.
- There is any sign that what you got was a conviction rather than a deferred
  judgment.

---

## Things you must verify yourself

We have deliberately left these blank rather than guess:

- The address and phone number of the clerk's office for your county.
- How long the court takes after discharge.
- Whether your record has in fact been expunged. Nothing here says that it has.
- Whether money you owe in any **other** case affects this one.
- What document to use if your discharge was before July 1, 2013.

Confirm all of it with the clerk of the district court in the county of your case,
or with a lawyer.

This is not legal advice and it does not guarantee that anything has been or will
be expunged.
