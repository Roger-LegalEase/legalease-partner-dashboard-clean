## What this route is

You told us the outcome in your case was a **deferred judgment**, and that you
have been discharged from probation.

When you are discharged from probation on a deferred judgment, the court's
criminal record about the deferred judgment is expunged. So are any counts in the
same charging document that were dismissed, and other related dismissed charges
that came out of the same transaction or occurrence, or the same common scheme or
plan.

Expunged here means the court record becomes **confidential** and comes off
public access under Iowa Code section 22.7. It is not accessible except by court
order. It is not erased, it is not destroyed, and it is not removed from your
record.

**There is no form for this.** This route sits outside the Iowa expungement rules
(Rules 2.80 through 2.86) and has no Rule 2.86 form. For a discharge on or after
July 1, 2013, there is nothing for you to file. The expungement is expected to
happen without an application.

So this document is a **check**, not a filing.

## Two things this does not do

**The deferred judgment itself is not expunged.** It stays on the state court
administrator's deferred judgment docket. That docket is how courts and
prosecutors know a deferred judgment was used, and it counts against the limit of
two deferred judgments under Iowa Code section 901C.3 if you ever apply under that
section later.

**Iowa has no felony expungement.**

## Check 1 — Was it really a deferred judgment?

A deferred judgment is not the same as a conviction, and it is not the same as a
dismissal. This route only works on a deferred judgment.

If there is any indication the disposition was a conviction rather than a deferred
judgment, stop here and talk to a lawyer. A different route may apply.

## Check 2 — Was the deferred judgment ever revoked?

If the court revoked the deferred judgment, the case is not eligible under
chapter 907 and this route does not apply to you.

A revoked deferred judgment becomes a conviction. If that conviction is a
misdemeanour it may be eligible under Iowa Code section 901C.3, which is a
separate route with its own requirements. Stop here and talk to a lawyer or ask
about that route.

## Check 3 — Are you discharged from probation, and when?

The expungement is tied to your discharge from probation. You need the discharge
date.

Whether your discharge was before or on/after **July 1, 2013** decides which
branch you are on:

- **On or after July 1, 2013** — keep reading. There is nothing to file.
- **Before July 1, 2013** — an application is required, but there is no statewide
  form and no rule that governs it. We do not have a document for you and we are
  not going to guess at one. Talk to a lawyer, or ask the clerk of the district
  court in the county of your case what they need.

## Check 4 — Is every financial obligation in that case paid?

This is the one that usually matters.

The record **is not expunged until** the restitution, civil penalties, court
costs, fees, and other financial obligations ordered by the court or assessed by
the clerk **in the case that contains the deferred judgment** are paid.

An unpaid balance in that case is the usual reason an expected expungement did not
happen. It is also the one thing on this list you can actually fix.

**Ask the clerk of the Iowa District Court for the county of your case for the
balance in that case number.** Ask about that case number specifically.

We do not obtain, receive, or inspect that balance information. You get it
yourself.

When you have the clerk's answer, check it against what you told us above about
having paid everything. If the two disagree, the clerk's answer is the one that
counts — correct your answers.

## Check 5 — Does the case still appear on the public docket?

Look up the case on the Iowa public online docket.

- **It is gone.** That is consistent with the expungement having happened.
- **It is still there.** Go back to Check 4 first. If the balance is genuinely
  paid and the case is still showing, contact the clerk of the district court for
  that county and ask about it.

We cannot tell you how long the court takes, because no source we have gives a
timeframe. Iowa does not notify people that a deferred judgment record was
expunged, so there is no letter to wait for.

## What this costs

No fee has been identified for this. This diagnostic route is free.

There is nothing to file, so there is no filing fee, and no fee waiver applies.

## Signing and service

Neither applies. There is nothing to sign on this branch and nothing to serve —
there is no petition.

## When to stop and get a lawyer

Stop and talk to a lawyer if:

- The deferred judgment was revoked.
- There is a dispute about what you owe, or about which case you owe it in.
- Your discharge was before July 1, 2013 and the clerk declines to act.
- There is any indication the disposition was a conviction rather than a deferred
  judgment.

## What we have not told you

We have deliberately left these blank rather than guess:

- The name, address, or phone number of any court or clerk's office.
- How long the court takes to expunge the record after discharge.
- Any confirmation that your record has in fact been expunged. Nothing in this
  document says that it has.
- The judge's name, the county attorney's name, or what anyone's position is.
- Any hearing date or outcome.
- What document to file if your discharge was before July 1, 2013.
- Whether obligations in any case other than the one containing the deferred
  judgment count toward the payment requirement.

Confirm all of it with the clerk of the district court in the county of your case,
or with a lawyer.
