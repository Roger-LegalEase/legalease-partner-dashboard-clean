# Start here: the court may owe you this order without any petition

The law that covers you does not require you to file anything.

Section 411.0729 of the Government Code names no filing party. It does not say "the defendant may petition". It says the court holds a hearing on whether issuing the order is in the best interest of justice, and unless the court finds it is **not**, the court **shall** enter the order.

So the first thing to do is not to file. It is to make sure the court knows the section applies to you, and to ask.

**Nothing is filed on this route, and no filing fee arises.**

## The three facts the section turns on

Confirm all three before you contact anyone. Each one has a document behind it, and you get the document yourself — nobody here obtains it, receives it or looks at it.

**1. You are a veteran.** Of the United States Armed Forces, including the reserves, the national guard or the state guard.

**2. You completed a court-approved veterans reemployment programme, after the offence.** The programme is one approved under Chapter 42A, Subchapter H-1 of the Code of Criminal Procedure. Ask the programme, or the court that approved it, for confirmation that you successfully completed it. This is the trigger for the whole section — without it there is nothing here.

**3. It was a misdemeanour, you were placed on community supervision, and you completed all of it.** Community supervision includes deferred adjudication. Ask the clerk of the court that sentenced you, or that placed you on community supervision, for the discharge and dismissal order, or for the judgment and order of community supervision. That document also tells you which section of the law applies to you, because deferred adjudication and post-conviction probation go to different sections.

## One thing that does *not* apply to you

There is a set of basic conditions in this part of the Government Code — section 411.074 — that blocks people from most nondisclosure sections.

**Those conditions do not apply to this section.** The statute says so in terms: this section applies regardless of whether you meet the other eligibility criteria in the subchapter.

If you have been told you do not qualify for nondisclosure, that answer may have come from those conditions, and it may not be the answer here.

## Also get your DPS criminal history

Follow the Texas Department of Public Safety's form CR-63, *Procedure for Review of Personal Criminal History Record Information*. It is an instruction sheet, not a form to fill in.

It shows you every other conviction and deferred adjudication on your record, and — importantly — it shows you whether an order has already issued. There is no point filing for something you already have.

DPS states its legal staff cannot assist individuals in petitioning for an order of nondisclosure.

## Where this goes — and it is not where you were arrested

This is the sentencing court, not the county of arrest.

The court that matters here is the one that **sentenced you or placed you on community supervision**. If you are also pursuing an expunction on a different case, that one goes to the county of arrest — you may be dealing with two different courts.

## What to ask for

Contact the clerk of that court. Say that you are asking about an order of nondisclosure under **Government Code section 411.0729**, for a veterans reemployment programme, and that the section does not require a petition.

Ask whether the court will set the hearing and enter the order.

## If the answer is "file a petition"

That happens. The Office of Court Administration says so itself — it publishes a model petition and instructions for this section precisely because in practice some courts require a petition, or do not hold the hearing.

If the court or the clerk tells you they want a petition, that is not a refusal. Move on to the petition route, and use the OCA model petition and order for section 411.0729. Ask the clerk which forms they expect.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- You cannot establish that you are a veteran, or that you completed the Subchapter H-1 programme.
- There is a family violence finding, or a family violence allegation, anywhere in the record.
- Anyone disputes whether you completed the sentence, the fines, the costs or the restitution.
- The prosecutor opposes it, or asks for a hearing.
- There is any question whether the offence was violent or sexual in nature. That is the court's determination, not yours and not ours.
- The court finds that issuing the order is not in the best interest of justice.
- You are not a United States citizen, or immigration is a concern for you.
