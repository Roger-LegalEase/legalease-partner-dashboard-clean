# Sealing a record after a veterans reemployment programme

Texas calls this an **order of nondisclosure**. It seals a record. It does not erase one — read the last section before you decide it is what you want.

There are two ways this happens, and they are alternatives, not steps. **Start with the first one.**

---

# Route A — the court does it without any petition

This is the route the law actually provides, and it is where you start.

Section 411.0729 of the Government Code names no filing party. It does not say the defendant may petition. It says the court holds a hearing on whether issuing the order is in the best interest of justice, and unless the court finds it is **not**, the court **shall** enter the order.

**Nothing is filed on this route. No filing fee arises.**

## A1. Confirm the three facts the section turns on

Each one has a document behind it. You get each document yourself — nobody here obtains, receives or looks at any of them.

**You are a veteran** of the United States Armed Forces, including the reserves, the national guard or the state guard.

**You completed a court-approved veterans reemployment programme, after the offence.** It is a programme approved under Chapter 42A, Subchapter H-1 of the Code of Criminal Procedure. Ask the programme, or the court that approved it, for confirmation that you successfully completed it. This is the trigger for the whole section.

**It was a misdemeanour, you were placed on community supervision, and you completed all of it.** Community supervision includes deferred adjudication. Ask the clerk of the court that sentenced you, or that placed you on community supervision, for the discharge and dismissal order, or for the judgment and order of community supervision.

## A2. Get your DPS criminal history

Follow the Texas Department of Public Safety's form CR-63, *Procedure for Review of Personal Criminal History Record Information*. It is an instruction sheet, not a form to fill in.

It lists every other conviction and deferred adjudication on your record, and it shows you whether an order has already issued — there is no point pursuing something you already have.

DPS states that its legal staff cannot assist individuals in petitioning for an order of nondisclosure.

## A3. Know which conditions do *not* apply to you

There is a set of basic conditions elsewhere in this part of the Government Code — section 411.074 — that blocks people from most nondisclosure sections.

**They do not apply to this section.** The statute says so: this section applies regardless of whether you meet the other eligibility criteria in the subchapter.

If you have been told before that you do not qualify for nondisclosure, that answer may have come from those conditions, and it may not be the answer here.

## A4. Contact the right court — the sentencing court

This is the court that **sentenced you or placed you on community supervision**. Not the county where you were arrested.

That difference matters. If you are also pursuing an expunction on some other case, that one goes to the county of arrest, and you may be dealing with two different courts.

Contact that court's clerk. Say you are asking about an order of nondisclosure under **Government Code section 411.0729**, for a veterans reemployment programme, and that the section does not require a petition. Ask whether the court will set the hearing and enter the order.

---

# Route B — the petition, if the court asks for one

Some courts require a petition anyway, or do not hold the hearing. The Office of Court Administration says so itself, which is why it publishes a model petition and instructions for a section that does not require one.

If the clerk tells you the court wants a petition, that is not a refusal. Take this route.

## B1. Use the OCA forms for section 411.0729

There are two, and they are the court system's own documents, not ours:

- **Petition for an Order of Nondisclosure under Section 411.0729.**
- **Order of Nondisclosure under Section 411.0729** — the order you are asking the judge to sign.

Get them from the Texas Judicial Branch nondisclosure forms page, along with the OCA instructions for completing the petition. Ask the clerk which forms and which revision they expect — check the date on the form you download.

These are statewide **model** forms. They are not mandatory and they are not promulgated, but county clerks across Texas republish them and refer filers to them.

## B2. Fill in what only you can fill in

Two things in the petition are yours alone:

- **The best-interest-of-justice assertion.** The court decides whether issuing the order is in the best interest of justice. Nobody completes that assertion for you.
- **Your signature and the date.**

The order is completed except for **the judge's signature**. That is the court's.

## B3. Notarising

Subchapter E-1 does not require the petition to be notarised. County practice may differ — ask the clerk before you sign anything.

## B4. File it with the sentencing court's clerk

Same court as Route A: the one that placed you on community supervision. Not the county of arrest.

You will pay the fee that generally applies to filing a civil case. It is set by the county — ask the clerk what it is.

If you cannot afford it, file a **Statement of Inability to Afford Payment of Court Costs**. That is the Supreme Court of Texas's own bilingual form, and the clerk has to give it to you free, without you asking.

## B5. Notice, and what you do not do

The **court** notifies the state once it receives your petition, and gives the state an opportunity for a hearing. A hearing is required unless the state does not ask for one before the 45th day after receiving notice, and the court finds you entitled to file and that issuing the order is in the best interest of justice. You should not be charged for that notice.

The OCA instructions also direct the petitioner to show proof that the district attorney received a copy of the petition. Practice differs — ask the clerk whether they want you to deliver a copy and show proof, or whether the court handles it.

---

# What the order does, and what it does not do

**Sealed, not erased.** An order of nondisclosure seals your record. It does not destroy it. That is different from an expunction, which destroys records.

**Some agencies still see it.** The record stays available to the criminal justice and noncriminal justice agencies the statute lists — including school districts, charter and private schools, education service centres and commercial transportation companies. **In those contexts you may not deny the matter.** Everywhere else, ordinary private background checks will not show it.

**One order covers one offence.** If you have more than one offence you want sealed, you need more than one order — more papers and, on Route B, more filing fees, one per offence. Work out that number before you pay for anything.

**Keep a copy of the signed order.**

---

# Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- You cannot establish that you are a veteran, or that you completed the Subchapter H-1 programme.
- There is a family violence finding, or a family violence allegation, anywhere in the record.
- You have a prior conviction or deferred adjudication other than a fine-only traffic offence.
- The prosecutor opposes it, or asks for a hearing.
- Anyone disputes whether you completed the sentence, the fines, the costs or the restitution.
- There is any question whether the offence was violent or sexual in nature. That is the court's determination.
- The court finds that issuing the order is not in the best interest of justice.
- You are not a United States citizen, or immigration is a concern for you.

### 1. Modelo de Petición de la OCA conforme a la Sección 411.0729

- **Falta:** El modelo de Petición de Orden de No Divulgación conforme a la Sección 411.0729 de la Oficina de Administración de Tribunales de Texas y sus instrucciones.
- **Motivo:** La petición localizada lleva un pie de página de febrero de 2022. - **Dónde y siguiente paso:** Primero pregunte al secretario del tribunal que lo sentenció o lo puso bajo supervisión comunitaria si usará la ruta de audiencia sin presentación o si exige una petición. Si la exige, obtenga el modelo vigente en https://www.txcourts.gov/media/1454256/petition-for-nondisclosure-under-section-411-0729.pdf y las instrucciones vigentes, confirme sus fechas y presente en ese tribunal, no en el condado del arresto.
