# Stage 1 — asking the Prisoner Review Board for a certificate

Illinois — 20 ILCS 2630/5.2(e-5) and 5.2(e-6)

This sheet is for you to keep. Do not send it to the Board and do not file it with
a court.

This is not an official court form.

---

## Why you are here

Some Illinois records cannot be sealed the ordinary way, because the offense is on
the exclusion list in section 5.2(a)(3).

There is another way in. You ask the **Illinois Prisoner Review Board** for a
**certificate of eligibility**. If the Board issues one, you attach it to a
petition in the circuit court where you were convicted, and the court can then act
on a record that was otherwise shut out.

That is two separate steps, in front of two different bodies:

- **Stage 1 — the Board.** This sheet. An application to a state agency.
- **Stage 2 — the circuit court.** Only after the Board says yes.

You cannot skip stage 1. Nothing happens in court without the certificate.

---

## Please talk to a lawyer about stage 1

We want to be straight with you about this one.

A certificate application is a **discretionary rehabilitation case**. The Board is
not checking boxes. It is deciding, on everything you put in front of it, whether
you have earned this. That is not the kind of thing a form-filling tool should be
the only help you get.

Call the **Office of the State Appellate Defender, Expungement Unit, at
866-787-1776**. They handle this statewide and there is no charge to ask.

We can still help you assemble the paperwork. We just do not want you to think the
paperwork is the hard part here.

## Which application is yours

There are three possibilities and only two of them have a form.

### 1. A certificate of eligibility for SEALING — section 5.2(e-5)

This is the main route. The Board publishes two forms for it:

- the **Certificate of Sealing Acknowledgement**, and
- the **Certificate of Sealing Application**.

Both are fillable PDFs from the Board, revised 09/18/2024.

### 2. A certificate of expungement based on MILITARY SERVICE

If your eligibility rests on your military service, the Board publishes a separate
**Certificate of Expungement for Military** acknowledgement and application.

This form is **only** for military-service eligibility. It is not a general
expungement-certificate application, and it should not be handed to you unless
military service is the basis of your request.

### 3. A certificate of eligibility for EXPUNGEMENT that is not military — section 5.2(e-6)

**We do not have a form for this, and we are not going to improvise one.**

The statute plainly refers to a certificate of eligibility for expungement. But the
Board publishes a Certificate of **Sealing** application and a **military**
Certificate of Expungement application, and we have not found a general non-military
expungement-certificate application anywhere on the Board's site.

The sealing application does not cover it. The military application is
military-specific and is not a substitute. Sealing and expungement are genuinely
different remedies here, so we are not going to quietly put you on the sealing form
and call it done.

If this is your situation, stop and call the Office of the State Appellate Defender
Expungement Unit at 866-787-1776.

## The one rule that gets petitions thrown out

**Fill in the Acknowledgement form first, and send it with the application.**

The Board's guidelines say the petition must contain the Acknowledgement form along
with everything else, **or it will be rejected and returned**.

Not delayed. Returned.

---

## What you write yourself

Three parts of the application are yours and only yours:

1. **The facts of the offense** — dates, places, all the surrounding
   circumstances, and your own version of what happened.
2. **Your personal life history** — where and when you were born, your education,
   your work, whether you are married, whether you served in the military.
3. **Why you are asking for this certificate.**

We will prompt you and format what you write. **We will not write it for you.**
This is your rehabilitation showing, in your words, and it has to be true, because
of the next section.

---

## You swear this in front of a notary

The application carries a declaration that everything in it is complete, truthful
and accurate. You sign it, and you swear it **before a notary public**.

Perjury is a Class 3 felony in Illinois, punishable by up to 10 years.

That is exactly why we do not draft your narrative and never fill in a fact for
you. Do not sign anything you have not read and do not swear to anything you are
not sure of.

## Documents you have to go and get

You gather these yourself. We do not obtain them, receive them, or look at them.

| What | Where from |
| --- | --- |
| Your statewide criminal history transcript | Illinois State Police, through Access and Review — go to an Illinois law-enforcement or correctional facility or a licensed fingerprint vendor, give fingerprints and identifying information. Free on the ISP fee schedule. |
| Chicago RAP sheet *(only if you had Chicago arrests)* | Chicago Police Department. There is a local fee. The statewide transcript does not always catch Chicago arrests. |
| A certified disposition for each case | The circuit clerk in each county where you had a case. Ask about copy counts, filing method and the exact fee. |
| Diploma, GED, college diploma or transcripts | Your school, college or awarding body. |
| Employment records for the last five years | W-2s, tax returns, paycheck stubs, or a letter from your employers. |
| Proof you completed counselling *(if you did any)* | The provider. Anger management, marriage, substance-abuse or similar. |
| DD-214 or similar proof of military service *(if applicable)* | National Personnel Records Center, or your service branch. Required on the military branch. |
| Fingerprint-based criminal history | As directed in paragraph B-5 of the Board's Guidelines. |

The Board also asks for child-support status where it applies, and welcomes awards,
certificates and character letters.

**Check your answers against these documents.** If what you told us and what the
document says do not match, the document wins. Fix your answers before anything
goes to the Board.

## Sending it in

- **Where:** Illinois Prisoner Review Board, 1001 North Walnut Street, Springfield,
  Illinois 62702. Mail or deliver the original.
- **How it is put together:** staple or clip the pages so they can be separated.
  Do not bind them. Do not use plastic sleeves.
- **When:** the completed petition must be **received at least 75 days before the
  first date of the hearing schedule** to be considered on that docket. Look up the
  schedule and count backwards; 75 days is not a suggestion.
- **Copies:** send a copy to the sentencing judge of your county of conviction — or
  the chief judge of that circuit, if the sentencing judge is no longer on the
  bench — and to the current State's Attorney of that county. **Keep proof.** You
  have to be able to show the Board that those copies went out, by notarised
  affidavit or otherwise.

---

## If you have applied before and been denied

The Board will not accept a new petition **within four years** of a denial of an
earlier petition for the same person, unless the Chairman allows an exception for
compelling reasons.

If you have been denied before, say so, say when, and get advice before you spend
money and effort on a filing that may be returned.

---

## What happens next, and what we cannot tell you

The Board may set a hearing. If it does, that is a real proceeding and you should
not walk into it alone — call the Expungement Unit.

We cannot tell you whether the Board will issue a certificate. It is discretionary
and it is outside our control and outside yours. Nothing we give you says or
predicts what the Board will do.

If the Board does issue a certificate, you move to stage 2: the circuit court
petition, on the mandatory statewide Illinois forms, with the certificate attached.

---

## Things we deliberately left blank

- The Board's hearing dates and the Board's decision.
- Any fee figure.
- The notary's block, and the witness or attorney signature on the Acknowledgement
  — someone else signs those.
- The name of the sentencing or chief judge, and of the State's Attorney.
- Anything about whether your record will actually be sealed or expunged.
