# Using a Prisoner Review Board certificate to clear a record that is otherwise excluded

Illinois — 20 ILCS 2630/5.2(e-5), 5.2(e-6) and 5.2(d)(2)

This checklist is for you to keep. Do not file it with the court and do not send it
to the Board.

This is not an official court form.

## The shape of this

Your record is on the section 5.2(a)(3) exclusion list, so the ordinary route is
closed to you.

The way in is a **certificate of eligibility** from the Illinois Prisoner Review
Board. Get the certificate, then take it to the circuit court where you were
convicted.

**Two stages, two different bodies, in this order:**

| | Who decides | What you send |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stage 1** | Illinois Prisoner Review Board | The Board's own application, with your rehabilitation showing |
| **Stage 2** | The circuit court of the county of conviction | The mandatory statewide Illinois Request form, with your certificate attached |

Stage 2 cannot happen without stage 1. If the Board says no, there is nothing to
file in court.

**Office of the State Appellate Defender, Expungement Unit — 866-787-1776.**

## Which application is yours

**Seeking a certificate for SEALING (section 5.2(e-5)?)** The Board publishes a
Certificate of Sealing Acknowledgement and a Certificate of Sealing Application.
That is your route.

**Is your eligibility based on MILITARY SERVICE?** There is a separate Certificate
of Expungement for Military acknowledgement and application. That form is only for
military-service eligibility.

**Seeking a certificate for EXPUNGEMENT, and not on the basis of military service?**
We do not have a form for you, and we will not guess at one. The statute refers to
a certificate of eligibility for expungement, but the Board publishes a **sealing**
application and a **military** expungement application, and we have not found a
general non-military expungement application on the Board's site. The sealing form
does not cover it and the military form is not a substitute. Stop here and call the
Expungement Unit.

## Sealing branch only: the rule that gets petitions returned

**For the Certificate of Sealing branch, fill in the Certificate of Sealing
Eligibility Acknowledgement first and send it with the sealing application.**

The sealing guidelines say the petition must contain that acknowledgement along
with everything else, or **it will be rejected and returned**. The available
evidence does not establish the same ordering or rejection rule for the military
application. A military participant must follow the current military application
and Board instructions and must not import this sealing rule.

## Stage 1 — the Board (sealing-guideline steps)

The detailed steps through Step 5 below are the recorded Certificate of Sealing
process. Military applicants must use the current military application and Board
instructions instead. Non-military expungement applicants must stop and obtain
the Board's accepted vehicle before using any Stage 1 filing instructions.

### Step 1 — Write your own three statements

Three parts of the application are yours:

1. The **facts of the offense** — dates, places, all surrounding circumstances,
   and your own version of what happened.
2. Your **personal life history** — date and place of birth, education,
   employment, marital status, military service.
3. **Why** you are seeking this certificate.

We prompt you and format what you write. We do not write it for you, and we do not
put words in your mouth. This is your rehabilitation showing.

### Step 2 — Sign, and swear it before a notary

The application carries a declaration that everything in it is complete, truthful
and accurate. **You sign it and you swear it before a notary public.**

Perjury is a Class 3 felony in Illinois, punishable by up to 10 years. Read every
line before you sign. Do not swear to anything you are not certain of.

There is also a **Signature of Witness/Attorney** line on the Acknowledgement. That
is for someone else to sign. We leave it blank and so should you.

### Step 3 — Send it to the Board

- **To:** Illinois Prisoner Review Board, 1001 North Walnut Street, Springfield,
  Illinois 62702. Mail or deliver the original.
- **How:** staple or clip the pages so they can be separated. Do not bind them. Do
  not use plastic sleeves.
- **When:** the completed petition must be **received at least 75 days before the
  first date of the hearing schedule** to be considered on that docket. Find the
  schedule and count backwards.

### Step 4 — Send copies, and keep proof

Send a copy to:

- the **sentencing judge** of your county of conviction — or the **chief judge** of
  that circuit, if the sentencing judge is no longer on the bench; and
- the **current State's Attorney** of that county.

You have to be able to **show the Board proof** that those copies went out, by
notarised affidavit or otherwise. Keep everything.

### Step 5 — Wait for the Board

The Board may set a hearing. If it does, that is a real proceeding — call the
Expungement Unit before you go.

We cannot tell you what the Board will do or how long it will take. It is
discretionary.

**If you have been denied before:** the Board will not accept a new petition within
**four years** of a denial for the same person, unless the Chairman allows an
exception for compelling reasons.

## Stage 2 — the circuit court, only after the Board says yes

### Step 6 — Use the statewide Illinois forms

Illinois has mandatory statewide expungement and sealing forms that every Illinois
circuit court has to accept. Stage 2 runs on those forms — the **Request to
Expunge and/or Seal Criminal Records** and the papers that travel with it.

Nobody should be drafting you a custom petition for this. If someone tells you your
county needs its own special petition, that is wrong.

### Step 7 — Attach your certificate

The certificate the Board issued gets attached to the Request. That attachment is
the whole point of the exercise.

### Step 8 — Choose expunge or seal, case by case

The Request has checkboxes and the Case List has a per-case election column.
**That choice is yours and we will not make it for you.** It is a legal judgment,
the two remedies are not the same thing, and a record is usually eligible for one
or the other, not both.

Expungement generally destroys or returns the record. Sealing hides it from most
public searches but does not cut off all government and law-enforcement access.

Talk this through with the Expungement Unit before you tick anything.

### Step 9 — Sign and date the verification

You sign the Request's verification yourself, and you date it. It warns that a
false statement is perjury.

### Step 10 — File it

File with the circuit court. Your petition goes to the Chief Judge of the circuit
where you were convicted, a judge of that circuit the Chief Judge designates, or —
in counties under 3,000,000 people — the presiding trial judge.

Call the clerk first. Copy counts, e-filing rules and mailing procedures differ by
county, and we are not printing a court address because we have not been given one.

### Step 11 — You do not serve anyone

At stage 2 the **circuit court clerk** does the serving. You serve no one.

### Step 12 — Wait, and expect a possible objection

The agencies that get notice generally have 60 days to object. If nobody objects,
many courts move ahead without a hearing. If somebody objects, the court sets a
hearing with at least 30 days' notice.

If there is an objection or a contested hearing, stop and get a lawyer. Call the
Expungement Unit.

After an order is entered, agencies may have up to 60 days to process it.

## About fees

We are not printing a fee figure.

There is no statewide fee schedule in Illinois. Costs vary by county — published
examples run from roughly $60 to $235, and one county publishes $136. The Illinois
State Police charge nothing to file a petition and $60 to process a court order.
Treat every number you hear as county-specific.

**Ask your clerk what it actually costs in your county.**

If paying is hard, ask about the **Application for Waiver of Court Fees** under
Supreme Court Rule 298. If a Rule 298 waiver order has been obtained, or the fee is
otherwise waived, no fee is required.
