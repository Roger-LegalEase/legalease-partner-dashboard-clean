# The certificate saying you sent a copy to the prosecuting attorney

Whenever you file something on this route, you also send a copy to the prosecuting attorney, and you file
this certificate saying that you did.

It matters more than it looks. The 30 days the prosecuting attorney has to object runs from the day you
serve them — not from the day you file. This certificate is what records that day.

## What to do

1. Find the prosecuting attorney's current street address. Use the address their office publishes now, not
   one from an old document.
2. Write that address in.
3. Send the copy.
4. **Then** date and sign the certificate, for the day you actually sent it. Not before.
5. File the certificate.

You use one of these each time: once with the motion asking the court to dismiss the charges and the sworn
statement that goes with it, and once, later, with the application asking the court to clear the record and
the timeline that goes with it. The certificate names whichever documents you sent.

## What this certificate does not say

It does not say the prosecuting attorney agrees, or does not object, or has read anything. It does not say
the 30 days have run. It does not say whether there will be a hearing. None of that is yours to state, and
none of it is written here.

## What is left blank for you

- The court, the county and the case number. Take them from your own court paperwork. If you are not sure
  how that court styles a caption, ask the clerk before you file.
- Your signature.
- Your date of birth and any identifying numbers. Nothing here asks for them.

Nothing in this document says how the court will rule, or when. Nothing in it speaks for the prosecuting
attorney.

This is legal information, not legal advice. It does not guarantee eligibility or any court outcome.
