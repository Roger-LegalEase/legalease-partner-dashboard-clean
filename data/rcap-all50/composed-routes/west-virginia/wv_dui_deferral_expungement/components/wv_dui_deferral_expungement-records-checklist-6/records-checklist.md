# What you need to collect before anything is signed or sent

There are four documents. Two of them you need at both stages. Two of them only matter once the charges
have already been dismissed and you are asking to have the record cleared.

Nobody here collects these for you. We do not contact the Division of Motor Vehicles, the clerk or the
probation office on your behalf, and we never see any of these documents.

## Needed at both stages

**1. The Division of Motor Vehicles certification that you successfully completed the Motor Vehicle
Alcohol Test and Lock Program.**

Ask the Division of Motor Vehicles for written certification that you successfully completed the test and
lock program. The law requires the motion to dismiss to be supported by this certification, so it has to
be attached before that motion goes in. There is no form here for it — it is the Division's own document
and you ask them for it directly.

*This checks your answer to:* "On what date did you successfully complete the Motor Vehicle Alcohol Test
and Lock Program?" If the certification and your answer disagree, correct your answer.

**2. A certified copy of the order deferring the proceedings and placing you on probation.**

Ask the clerk of the court that entered it. This is the order the court made instead of entering a
judgment of guilt. It carries the length of your probation term, and that term is what the later one-year
wait is measured from.

*This checks your answer to:* "On what date did the court defer the proceedings and place you on
probation?" If the order and your answer disagree, correct your answer.

## Needed only once the charges have been dismissed

**3. A certified copy of the order dismissing the charges and discharging you.**

Ask the clerk for a certified copy.

*This checks your answer to:* "On what date did the court dismiss and discharge the case?"

**4. Written confirmation of the date your probation term expired.**

Ask the probation office that supervised you, or the clerk, for written confirmation of the date your
probation term expired.

This is the one people get wrong. The one-year wait before you can ask for the record to be cleared runs
from the date your probation expired — not from the date the case was dismissed. Those are usually
different dates, and the dismissal is the one you are more likely to remember. Use the probation expiry
date.

*This checks your answer to:* "On what date did your probation term expire?"

## Then compare everything

Go through each document against the answers you gave. Where a document and your answer disagree, the
document governs — correct your answer, and the paperwork will be corrected with it.

## What this step does not do

- It does not tell you whether you qualify.
- It does not create, replace or stand in for any court form.
- It does not contact the court, the Division of Motor Vehicles or the probation office for you.
- It does not tell you what any of these documents will say.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- Your charge is still live and you have not yet told the court you want to take part in a deferral. That
  window is short and it closes fast. Get a lawyer now — this is not the place for it.
- You held a commercial driver's licence, or were driving a commercial vehicle, at the time.
- A court ever entered an order finding that you refused the secondary chemical test.
- Your licence had been revoked before this case, in any state, for a drink or drug driving offence.
- You were charged with any other serious traffic offence in the same prosecution.
- You did not finish the test and lock program, or you were removed from it.
- You have ever been convicted of a felony, anywhere. That does not affect the motion to dismiss, but it
  blocks the request to clear the record outright.
- Anyone has said you broke the conditions of your probation.
- You have already had a case deferred and dismissed this way once before. Only one is allowed.
- What you actually want cleared is your driving record. This route never reaches it.
- Firearm rights, immigration, professional licensing, commercial driving, or federal, tribal, military
  or out-of-state records are involved.
