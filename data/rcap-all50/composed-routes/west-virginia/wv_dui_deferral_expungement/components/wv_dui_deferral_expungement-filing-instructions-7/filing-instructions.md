# Where each of these goes, and in what order

This happens in two stages, and they are separate. The first stage gets the charges dismissed. The second
stage, which cannot happen until at least a year after your probation ended, asks the court to clear the
record. You cannot do them at the same time, and the second one is not available until the first one has
happened.

Both go to the same place: the clerk of the court that deferred your case and put you on probation.

## Stage one — getting the charges dismissed

You can do this once you have satisfactorily completed the Motor Vehicle Alcohol Test and Lock Program and
kept to the conditions of your probation.

1. Read the motion and the affidavit through. Every fact in them is your own answer. Correct anything that
   is wrong.
2. Fill in the court, the county and the case number in the caption. They are your own case details and
   are left blank for you. If you are not sure how that court styles a caption, ask the clerk before you
   file.
3. Sign and date the motion.
4. Swear the affidavit. It has to be signed in front of a notary public or other official who can take an
   affidavit — do not sign it beforehand.
5. Attach the Division of Motor Vehicles certification that you completed the test and lock program. The
   law requires the motion to be supported by it.
6. File the motion, the affidavit and the certification with the clerk of the court that deferred the
   proceedings.
7. Send a copy of everything to the prosecuting attorney. Fill in their street address on the certificate
   of service from the address they currently publish, then date and sign the certificate for the day you
   actually sent it, and file it.

What happens next is not yours to do. The prosecuting attorney has 30 days after service to tell the judge
about any objections. If none are filed in that time, the court dismisses the charges. We cannot tell you
whether an objection will be filed or what the court will do.

If the charges are dismissed, you are discharged without a finding of guilt being entered against you.

## Stage two — asking for the record to be cleared

This is a separate request, and it cannot be made until at least one year has run from the date your
probation term expired.

Not from the dismissal. From the expiration of probation. Those are usually different dates, and it is the
probation expiry date that starts the clock.

1. Read the application and the timeline through. Every date on them is your own answer, checked against
   the documents you collected. Correct anything that is wrong.
2. Fill in the court, the county and the case number in the caption.
3. Leave the hearing date blank. A hearing only happens if somebody objects, and the court sets the date if
   it does.
4. Sign and date the application. This one does not have to be notarised.
5. File the application and the timeline with the same clerk.
6. Send a copy to the prosecuting attorney, fill in their address on the certificate of service, date and
   sign it for the day you sent it, and file it.

Objections are filed within 30 days after service. If objections are filed, the court holds a hearing where
everyone gets to be heard on whether you broke the conditions of your probation seriously or repeatedly.
If the court decides you did not, it orders the record cleared.

**That hearing is where automated help ends.** If somebody objects and the court sets a hearing, get a
lawyer.

## Money

The law charges no fee for either request.

Separately, a person whose case is disposed of this way has to pay the court costs that could have been
charged against someone convicted of the offence. Paying those may already have been made a condition of
your probation. We cannot tell you what that amount is — no source we work from states one — so ask the
clerk of your court.

Do not go by any fee figure you may have read for other West Virginia record-clearing routes. They are
different statutes with different rules about money.

## What the order does, and what it does not touch

If the court clears the record, it reaches all official records of the arrest, trial and conviction —
**except the records the Division of Motor Vehicles keeps.** Your driving record is not cleared by this and
cannot be. That limit is in the statute itself. If your driving record is what you actually need cleared,
this is not the route, and no route here reaches it.

Once dismissal and discharge have been made, you are not committing perjury or false swearing by not
disclosing the arrest or the trial — **except** if you are asked in connection with a later offence of the
same kind. That exception is part of the protection; it travels with it.

And be careful what you expect of it more generally. Clearing a record here does not destroy every trace
of it, does not mean no one can ever see it, and does not mean you never have to disclose it anywhere.
Some checks are required by law and can still reach it, and people applying for law-enforcement,
prosecution or corrections work are asked about records like this and have to answer.

## What is left blank for you

- The court, the county and the case number. Take them from your own court paperwork.
- The prosecuting attorney's address, and the date and signature on each certificate of service.
- Your signature on the motion and on the application, and your sworn signature and the notary's jurat on
  the affidavit.
- The hearing date on the application.
- Your date of birth and any identifying numbers.
- Nothing in this packet says how the court will rule, or when.
