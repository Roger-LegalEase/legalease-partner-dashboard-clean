# The timeline that goes with the application

This is a short list of dates. It goes in with the application asking the court to clear your record,
because the wait before you can make that request is measured from a particular date and the court needs to
see which dates are which.

There are five: when the court deferred your case and put you on probation, how long the probation term
was, when you completed the Motor Vehicle Alcohol Test and Lock Program, when the charges were dismissed
and you were discharged, and when your probation term expired.

## Check every one of them

Each date here should come from a document, not from memory:

- The deferral date and the probation term — from the order that deferred your case.
- The program completion date — from the Division of Motor Vehicles certification.
- The dismissal date — from the order dismissing and discharging you.
- The probation expiry date — from written confirmation by the probation office or the clerk.

Where a document and a date here disagree, the document wins. Correct it.

## The one that people get wrong

The wait runs from the date your **probation expired**, not the date your case was **dismissed**. Those are
usually different, and the dismissal is the one you are more likely to remember. This timeline shows both,
separately, and does not merge them.

It also does not do the arithmetic for you and does not say the wait is over. It sets the dates out so the
court can see them.

## What to do

1. Fill in the court, the county and the case number in the caption.
2. Sign and date it.
3. File it with the application.

## What is left blank for you

- The court, the county and the case number. Take them from your own court paperwork. If you are not sure
  how that court styles a caption, ask the clerk before you file.
- Your signature.
- Your date of birth and any identifying numbers. Nothing here asks for them.

Nothing in this document says how the court will rule, or when. Nothing in it speaks for the prosecuting
attorney.

This is legal information, not legal advice. It does not guarantee eligibility or any court outcome.
