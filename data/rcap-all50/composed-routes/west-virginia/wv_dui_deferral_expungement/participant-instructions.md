# Clearing a first DUI that was deferred — what you do

If a West Virginia court once put your first DUI case on hold instead of convicting you, put you on
probation, and made you complete the Motor Vehicle Alcohol Test and Lock Program, there is a route here for
you — even if that was years ago and you were told nothing could be done.

It happens in two separate stages, and they are months or years apart.

**Stage one** gets the charges dismissed, once you have completed the program and kept to the conditions of
your probation. No judgment of guilt was entered against you, and the dismissal discharges you without one.

**Stage two** asks the court to clear the record of the arrest, trial and conviction. It cannot be asked
for until at least one year has run from the date your probation term expired.

You may only need one of them. If your charges were dismissed long ago, you start at stage two. If you
finished the program but nothing ever happened after that, you start at stage one.

## Before anything

Read the collection list that comes with this packet and get the documents it names — the Division of
Motor Vehicles certification that you completed the program, and a certified copy of the order that
deferred your case. If your charges have already been dismissed, you also need the order of dismissal and
written confirmation of the date your probation ended.

Check every answer you gave against those documents. Where they disagree, the documents govern.

## The one date people get wrong

The one-year wait before stage two runs from **the date your probation term expired**, not from the date
your case was dismissed. Those are usually different dates. Use the probation expiry date, and get it in
writing from the probation office or the clerk.

## Filing

The detailed steps are in the filing instructions that come with this packet. In short: everything goes to
the clerk of the same court that deferred your case, you sign what you file, the affidavit at stage one has
to be sworn in front of a notary, and you send a copy to the prosecuting attorney and file a certificate of
service saying you did.

The prosecuting attorney then has 30 days to raise objections. That clock starts from the day you serve
them, which is why the certificate matters.

## What is left blank for you

- The court, the county and the case number.
- The prosecuting attorney's street address, and the date and signature on each certificate of service.
- Your signature everywhere, and the notary's part of the affidavit.
- The hearing date on the stage-two application — a hearing only happens if somebody objects, and the
  court sets the date.
- Your date of birth and any identifying numbers.

## Money

The law charges no fee for either request. Separately, someone whose case is disposed of this way has to
pay the court costs that could have been charged against a person convicted of the offence, and that may
already have been part of your probation. We cannot tell you the amount — ask the clerk. Ignore any fee
figure you have seen for other West Virginia record-clearing routes; those are different statutes.

## What this does not reach

**Your driving record.** The law says in terms that no Division of Motor Vehicles record is cleared by an
order made under this section. The criminal record can be cleared; the driving record cannot. If the
driving record is what you need, this is not it, and nothing here will do it.

And clearing a record is not an erase button. It does not mean no one can ever see it. Checks that are
required by law can still reach it, and people applying for law-enforcement, prosecution or corrections
work are asked about records like this and must answer.

Once dismissal and discharge have happened, you are not lying by not mentioning the arrest or the trial —
**except** when you are asked in connection with a later offence of the same kind.

## Stop and get a lawyer if

- Your charge is live and you have not yet told the court you want a deferral. That window closes very
  quickly after arrest, and it is not something this packet can help with. Get a lawyer today.
- You held a commercial driver's licence, or were driving a commercial vehicle, at the time.
- A court ever found that you refused the secondary chemical test.
- Your licence had been revoked before this case, in any state, for a drink or drug driving offence.
- Another serious traffic offence was charged in the same prosecution.
- You did not finish the test and lock program, or were removed from it.
- You have ever been convicted of a felony, anywhere. It does not stop the stage-one dismissal, but it
  blocks the stage-two request outright.
- Anyone has said you broke the conditions of your probation, or the prosecuting attorney objects and the
  court sets a hearing. That hearing is where automated help ends.
- You have already had a case deferred and dismissed this way. Only one is allowed.
- Firearm rights, immigration, professional licensing, commercial driving, or federal, tribal, military or
  out-of-state records are involved.

## One thing we cannot tell you

If you completed the program years ago and no order of dismissal was ever entered, we do not know whether
the court will still take a motion to dismiss on a file that old, or whether the file has been closed in a
way that changes the answer. Nothing in this packet claims it will. If that is your situation, ask the
clerk, and consider talking to a lawyer before you file.
