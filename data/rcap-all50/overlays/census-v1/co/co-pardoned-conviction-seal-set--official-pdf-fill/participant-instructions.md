# Filing instructions — Motion to seal a pardoned conviction

This packet is two Colorado Judicial Department forms, filed together:

- **JDF 680**, _Motion to Seal Conviction Records (Pardoned)_ — what you file.
- **JDF 681**, _Order to Seal Pardoned Conviction Records_ — the order you give the court to sign.

Both are prepared for **motion to seal a pardoned conviction** under C.R.S. § 24-72-710; Colorado Judicial Department forms JDF 680 and JDF 681.

The platform filled in what it holds about you and your case: your name, your date of birth, your address, your phone, your e-mail, the county and the case number, on both forms. Everything else is yours, and every one of those blanks is listed below by the section of the form it is in.

## The pardon is the whole of this route

C.R.S. § 24-72-710 seals a conviction **that the Governor has pardoned**. Section 3 of JDF 680 says what you must do about it: **attach the Governor's full and unconditional pardon for the convictions in this case**, and tick the box saying you have. Without the pardon there is nothing for this motion to ask for. The platform does not hold your pardon and does not attach it.

## Boxes you tick with a pen

JDF 680 is a printed form, not a fillable one — the values on your copy were placed on its ruled lines, and its **tick boxes are printed characters rather than fields, so nothing can mark them for you.** Mark these by hand:

- **Page 1, Court** — beside _Court: District County_: tick it to say whether the case was in District or County Court.
- **Page 1, 2. Agencies with custody of the records** — beside _District / County Court: (case number)_: tick it to say that the District or County Court holds records.
- **Page 1, 2. Agencies with custody of the records** — beside _District Attorney_: tick it to say that the District Attorney holds records.
- **Page 1, 2. Agencies with custody of the records** — beside _Municipal Court: (case numbers)_: tick it to say that a Municipal Court holds records.
- **Page 1, 2. Agencies with custody of the records** — beside _Sheriff’s Department_: tick it to say that the Sheriff's Department holds records.
- **Page 1, 2. Agencies with custody of the records** — beside _City Attorney_: tick it to say that the City Attorney holds records.
- **Page 2, 2. Agencies with custody of the records** — beside _Law Enforcement Agency (Identify)_: tick it to say that a law enforcement agency holds records.
- **Page 2, 2. Agencies with custody of the records** — beside _Colorado Bureau of Investigation (Required)_: tick it to say the Colorado Bureau of Investigation, which the form marks required and whose address it prints for you.
- **Page 2, 2. Agencies with custody of the records** — beside _Other:_: tick it to say that some other agency holds records.
- **Page 2, 3. Pardon** — beside _I attached the Governor’s full and unconditional pardon for the convictions in this case._: tick it to say that you have attached the Governor's full and unconditional pardon — this route does not exist without it.
- **Page 2, 4. Certificate of Service** — beside _Colorado Courts E-Filing (only available to lawyers)_: tick it to say that you served through Colorado Courts E-Filing.
- **Page 2, 4. Certificate of Service** — beside _Email or Fax to:_: tick it to say that you served by email or fax.
- **Page 2, 4. Certificate of Service** — beside _Hand Delivery, to: (name, place)_: tick it to say that you served by hand delivery.
- **Page 2, 4. Certificate of Service** — beside _Regular Mail, addressed to: (name, full address)_: tick it to say that you served by regular mail.

The Colorado Bureau of Investigation is **required** and the form prints its address for you: ATTN Identification-Seals, 690 Kipling St. STE 3000, Lakewood, CO 80215. Tick it.

JDF 681 says the same thing from the court's side: once the order is signed, the court's clerk sends a copy to the CBI and to every records custodian listed in the motion — which is why the list you tick matters. The order also carries a note to you: **the CBI charges a fee before its records are sealed**, and you contact the CBI to pay it.

## Where you file this

File both forms with the **clerk of the Colorado court that entered the conviction** — the District or County Court in the county already filled in for you. The Colorado Judicial Department publishes each courthouse's address; this packet does not state one, because the platform holds no court directory and an unsourced address in a filing instruction is worse than none. **Ask the clerk what court fee applies**, which is likewise not established here.

## What you must do before you file

1. **Fill in every item in the tables below.** Each names the form, the section and the blank.
2. **Tick every box listed under _Boxes you tick with a pen_** that applies to you.
3. **Attach the Governor's pardon.**
4. **Serve a copy on the prosecuting attorney**, then complete the certificate of service in section 4 of JDF 680 — the date, the method and who you sent it to. After you have served, not before.
5. **Sign and date section 5 of JDF 680 yourself.** Your printed name is already there; the signature and the date are yours.
6. **Leave the court's parts of JDF 681 alone** — the findings in section 3, the other orders in 4(d), and the signature and date.

## What the platform deliberately left blank

- **Your signature and its date** in section 5 of JDF 680.
- **The certificate of service in section 4** — the date, the method and the person served. Service has not happened when this packet is prepared.
- **The Division and Courtroom boxes on both forms.** The form marks that box for court use only.
- **The court's findings in section 3 of JDF 681, its other orders, and the judge's or magistrate's signature and date.**

## What this packet is not

This is a prepared set of official Colorado Judicial Department forms. It is not legal advice, it is not filed for you, and it does not decide whether your pardoned conviction will be sealed. JDF 681 sets out what the court must find: that the public interest in retaining public access to the conviction record is outweighed by the harm to your privacy, by the dangers of unwarranted adverse consequences to you, and by the intent of the full and unconditional pardon. That finding is the court's.
