# Filing instructions — Motion to seal multiple conviction records

This packet is two Colorado Judicial Department forms, filed together:

- **JDF 641**, _Motion to Seal Multiple Conviction Records_ — what you file.
- **JDF 642**, _Order to Seal Multiple Conviction Records_ — the order you give the court to sign.

Both are prepared for **motion to seal multiple conviction records** under C.R.S. § 24-72-709; Colorado Judicial Department forms JDF 641 and JDF 642.

The platform filled in what it holds about you and your case: your name, your date of birth, your address, your phone, your e-mail, the county and the case number, on both forms. Everything else is yours, and every one of those blanks is listed below by the section of the form it is in.

## The offence table is the substance of this motion

Section 4 of JDF 641 has fifteen rows and four columns — charge, case number, sentence date, and the date probation or parole supervision ended. **Every cell is yours to fill.** This is a multiple-conviction route: which convictions you are bringing is the whole of what the court is deciding, and the platform does not hold them. The supervision end date matters most of all, because the statutory waiting period in C.R.S. § 24-72-709(2) runs from it. Fill a row completely or leave it empty — a row with the charge in it and the end date missing looks finished and is not.

## Where you file this

File both forms with the **clerk of the Colorado court that handled the convictions** — the District or County Court in the county already filled in for you. The Colorado Judicial Department publishes each courthouse's address; this packet does not state one, because the platform holds no court directory and an unsourced address in a filing instruction is worse than none.

**Ask the clerk what fee applies.** It is not established in any source this packet holds, so it is not stated here.

**The Colorado Bureau of Investigation charges its own fee.** JDF 642 says so on its face, in a note addressed to you: the CBI charges a fee before its records are sealed, and you contact the CBI to pay it. That is separate from anything the court charges.

## What you must do before you file

1. **Fill in every item in the tables below.** Each names the form, the section and the blank.
2. **Complete the offence table.** Every row you use, all four columns.
3. **Attach a verified copy of your criminal history record dated within the last 20 days**, and tick Yes in section 7. If you cannot, tick No and file a copy within ten days — the form's own footnote says so.
4. **Make the choices listed under _The choices that are yours_**, including whether the district attorney consents and whether you are asking for a hearing.
5. **Serve a copy on the prosecuting attorney**, then complete the certificate of service in section 9 — the date, the method and who you sent it to. After you have served, not before.
6. **Sign and date section 10 yourself.** Your printed name is already there, because the form uses one field for the caption and the printed-name line; the signature and the date are yours.
7. **Leave sections 3 and 4 of JDF 642 alone**, apart from the case and agency details listed below. The findings, the other orders and the judge's signature are the court's.

## What the platform deliberately left blank

- **Your signature and its date** in section 10 of JDF 641.
- **The certificate of service in section 9** — the date, the method and the person served. Service has not happened when this packet is prepared.
- **The counsel signature line.** You are filing this yourself; no attorney-representation fact is held for you.
- **The Division and Courtroom boxes on both forms.** The form marks that box for court use.
- **The court's findings in section 3 of JDF 642, its other orders, and the judge's or magistrate's signature and date.**

## What this packet is not

This is a prepared set of official Colorado Judicial Department forms. It is not legal advice, it is not filed for you, and **it does not decide whether your convictions can be sealed**. Page 3 of JDF 641 prints the list of convictions that are not eligible — traffic offences and infractions, C.R.S. § 42-4-1301 violations, offences whose underlying basis involved unlawful sexual behaviour, C.R.S. § 18-6-401, and a long list of sentencing provisions. Read it against your own record before you sign.
