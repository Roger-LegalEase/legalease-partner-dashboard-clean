# What you must do — erasure of Connecticut records after an absolute pardon, under C.G.S. § 54-142a(d)

This packet is prepared for **Erasure After an Absolute Pardon, C.G.S. § 54-142a(d)**.

Connecticut law says **erasure**. Not the words other states use — using those invites the wrong questions, so this packet does not use them.

The platform filled in what it holds about you: your name, your mailing address, your telephone number and your email. Every case fact belongs to a record the platform has not seen — the pardon decision and the court record — so every one of them is a labelled dotted blank listed below, and you fill it from the record itself, never from memory.

## Which page applies to you

The date you received the absolute pardon decides the branch, and only you can establish it:

| Page | When it is yours |
| --- | --- |
| `ct-pardon-erasure-primary-filing-1` — the petition | the absolute pardon was received **before 1 October 1974**: erasure requires a petition to the Superior Court, which shall direct erasure |
| `ct-pardon-erasure-process-guidance-2` — the no-filing page | the absolute pardon was received **on or after 1 October 1974**: such records shall be erased without a petition, and there is nothing to file |

Only an **absolute pardon** reaches erasure. A provisional pardon or a certificate of employability is not an absolute pardon and does not erase anything.

## Documents you must obtain

| Document | Where you get it |
| --- | --- |
| The absolute pardon decision, showing what was granted and the date it was granted | Connecticut Board of Pardons and Paroles — ask for a copy; the platform does not obtain, receive or inspect it |
| Judicial Branch case look-up printout for the pardoned conviction | Connecticut Judicial Branch online case look-up — note that an erased case will not appear |

## The items you must supply (petition branch only)

Each is printed on the petition as a labelled dotted blank.

### ct-pardon-erasure-primary-filing-1 — Petition for Erasure of Records Following an Absolute Pardon (C.G.S. Sec. 54-142a(d))

| The blank on the document | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| Court in which the conviction was entered | the court that entered the pardoned conviction, checked against the Judicial Branch case look-up - note that an erased case will not appear |

| Docket number of the pardoned conviction | the docket number of the pardoned conviction, checked against the Judicial Branch case look-up |

| Date the absolute pardon was received | the date the absolute pardon was granted, checked against the pardon decision from the Connecticut Board of Pardons and Paroles - correct the packet if they disagree |

## What you do, in order (petition branch)

1. **Confirm the pardon type and date** against the Board of Pardons and Paroles decision.
2. **Call the clerk's office of the Superior Court** and ask how this petition should be captioned, where it should be filed, and whether any notarization or service applies locally — no source this packet is built from states any of those, and the clerk is the authority that can. Write the caption answer on the petition's dotted lines.
3. **Fill in every dotted blank** from the records listed above. Do not guess a date or a docket number.
4. **Sign and date the petition yourself.** The platform never signs for you and never dates a signature.
5. **File with the Superior Court.** **No fee is payable**: C.G.S. § 54-142a(k) provides that no fee shall be charged in any court with respect to any petition under § 54-142a, so a fee waiver does not arise.

## What you do (automatic branch)

Nothing is filed. Confirm the erasure through the Judicial Branch case look-up — an erased case will not appear — and if the case still appears, contact the clerk of the Superior Court about the status of the erasure, taking a copy of the pardon decision.

## Things the platform deliberately left blank

- **Your signature, and the date beside it.** A signature is yours alone, and a date written before you sign would be false.
- **The docket number of the petition itself.** The clerk assigns it at filing.
- **The caption and court location.** The accepted caption and format for this petition are an open question this family carries to counsel review; until it is answered, the clerk's own instruction is the only safe source, so the petition tells you to call and write what the clerk says.

## When to stop and get help instead of filing

- the pardon was provisional rather than absolute, which erases nothing;
- the pardon date cannot be established, so the branch cannot be selected;
- the court sets a hearing on the pre-1974 petition;
- you have an immigration matter.

## What this packet is not

This is a prepared petition and process page. There is no official Connecticut form for this petition — that is why it is composed — and it is not legal advice, it is not filed for you, and it does not decide whether the court will direct erasure.
