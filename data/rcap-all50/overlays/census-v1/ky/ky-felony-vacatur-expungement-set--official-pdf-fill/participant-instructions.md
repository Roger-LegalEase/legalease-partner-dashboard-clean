# Filing instructions — Vacate and expunge a Kentucky felony conviction

This packet is two official Kentucky Court of Justice forms: **AOC-496.3**, _Application to Vacate and Expunge Felony Conviction_ (the application you file), and **AOC-496.4**, _Order on Application to Vacate and Expunge Felony Conviction_ (the order the court enters), prepared under KRS 431.073 and 431.079.

The platform filled in what it holds about you and your case on both forms: your name, your address, your phone number, your date of birth, your county and your case number. Everything else is yours, and every item is listed below.

## Where you file this

File the application with the **Office of the Circuit Court Clerk in the county where the conviction occurred**. It is filed as a motion in your original criminal case, not as a new case, so the caption — court, county, division and case number — must match your existing case exactly. (KRS 431.073(1); the statewide Kentucky legal review held in the source library records the venue rule and the filing method: in person or by mail at the circuit court clerk's office; pro se e-filing is not generally available.)

## The fee, and the fee after the fee

The form states the fees on its own third page: **a $50 non-refundable filing fee per application, due at the time of filing** — "the clerk cannot take your application without proper payment of this fee" — and, **if an expungement order is granted, an additional $250 expungement fee**, which you may pay in installments if you ask the Court to set a plan using the request block on page 3. (KRS 431.073(10) and (11).) Be warned: the legal review records that at least one circuit clerk's public page quotes $300 due at filing, which conflicts with the statute — if a clerk asks for $300 at the counter, the form itself says $50 at filing.

**If you cannot pay:** the named procedure is a motion to proceed in forma pauperis with an affidavit of indigency under KRS 453.190. Whether the $50 clerk portion can be waived is recorded as unresolved — ask the circuit court clerk. For the $250, the statute provides the installment plan rather than a waiver.

## Who must be served

**You serve nobody.** The circuit court clerk serves the notice of filing on the Commonwealth's or County Attorney who prosecuted the case — the form's own "NOTICE TO COMMONWEALTH/COUNTY ATTORNEY" and "FOR CLERK USE ONLY" blocks on page 3 record exactly that, and the clerk completes the service block. The prosecutor has 60 days after service to respond, and a hearing on the application must occur no later than 120 days after filing (KRS 431.073(2); printed on the form).

## What must be attached

1. **Your current expungement eligibility certification.** The form says it in bold: "A copy of your current expungement eligibility certification must be attached to this Application." (KRS 431.079.) The certification expires **30 days after issuance**, so file while it is current.
2. **A copy of the full pardon**, only if you check the pardon basis in Section 2 — the form's own words: "a copy of which is attached."
3. **Additional sheets, if needed** — the form directs that all charges in the case be listed, including non-felony charges, and allows attached sheets when the printed lines run out.

## Deadlines, honestly stated

There is **no deadline to file** this application. Two timing rules do apply: you may not file **sooner than five years** after completion of your sentence or successful completion of probation or parole, whichever is later (paragraph 5 of the application), and your eligibility certification must still be **within its 30-day life** on the day you file.

## Sign before a notary or the circuit court clerk

Do not sign the application at home. The form's own note: the Defendant/Applicant must sign in the presence of a notary or of a circuit court clerk, so the notary or clerk can witness the signature. The signature and date lines are left blank on purpose.

Section 2 of the application says **check only one**. Read all five statements against your own record. If your basis is KRS 431.073(1)(d) — either (1)(d) box — you must also complete Section 7 on page 2, and you should read the stop list below first.

## What the platform deliberately left blank

- **Your signature and its date, and the year beside it.** You sign before the notary or clerk.
- **Your Social Security number and the Violation/Arrest date boxes** until you fill them — they are in the table above.
- **Everything the court, the clerk, or an agency records custodian completes on AOC-496.4**: every finding, every checkbox in Sections I through V, the judge's and clerk's dates, and the agency certification block. The order is included so your filing is complete to tender; the legal review records that whether the applicant tenders it or the clerk prepares it is a matter of clerk practice — bring it, and let the clerk say.
- **Section 7's narrative answers.** They are only for a (1)(d) application, and the legal review's own limitation is that they are never generated — they are your sworn story or your lawyer's drafting, not a form-fill.

## When to stop and get a lawyer

The statewide legal review in the source library names these as the exact self-help stops. Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if **any** of these is true:

1. Your application would be brought under **KRS 431.073(1)(d)** — the contested-hearing burden is clear and convincing evidence, you or counsel must appear, and Section 7 requires drafted narrative testimony.
2. **The Commonwealth files any objection.**
3. There is a question whether the offense **resulted in serious bodily injury or death**.
4. **There was an identified victim.**
5. The offense classification is disputed — Class D versus higher, or the effect of a persistent-felony-offender enhancement.
6. **You are not a United States citizen.**
7. You cannot fund the $250 and need an installment plan argued for you.
8. The case moved between district and circuit court and **the charge list is uncertain**.

## What this packet is not

This is a prepared copy of two official Kentucky Court of Justice forms. It is not legal advice, it is not filed for you, and it does not decide whether your conviction is eligible to be vacated and expunged — only a judge can do that.
