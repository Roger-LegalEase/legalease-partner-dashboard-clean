# Montana misdemeanor expungement

ONCE IN A LIFETIME: You may petition under Part 11 only once, including if you previously petitioned or received expungement. Review the entire conviction inventory displayed with this packet and expressly confirm it before generation. Anything omitted cannot be added later. Questions about using the lifetime petition now or waiting require legal help.

FRONT-LOADED PREPARATION: obtain the DOJ CRISS background check; search BOTH Montana Public Access Portals, District Courts AND Courts of Limited Jurisdiction (SEARCHES, Case Searches, By Party); obtain the final judgment for EVERY conviction. A CRISS check alone can omit misdemeanors. Reconcile discrepancies before confirming the complete inventory.

START THE FD-258 STEP NOW: obtain the free blue applicant fingerprint card from DOJ CRISS and arrange fingerprints with law enforcement or a private vendor, expecting a fee. Fingerprints validate identity under MCA 46-18-1104(3). The completed card is required before the DOJ submission, never before this court packet can be generated.

DISTRICT COURT VENUE: file a civil petition in a district court in a judicial district where at least one conviction occurred, even when the convicting court was Justice, Municipal or City Court. If more than one district qualifies, the participant chooses; this packet does not choose for you. Advice about the choice is a self-help stop. The DOJ flowchart reference to the Supreme Court does not change this district-court venue.

BEFORE FILING: contact the chosen district court clerk through the Court Locator at https://courts.mt.gov/locator/ and confirm local rules, paper filing, any required cover sheet/addendum and fee. Adopted May 2024 civil fee schedule total: $120 (not the $90 base alone); confirm current fee with the clerk. If unable to pay, obtain the published fee-waiver materials at https://www.montanalawhelp.org/ and follow the clerk instructions. This packet does not reproduce the restricted MLSA fee-waiver, ER-100 or ER-200 forms. Court copies, background checks and fingerprinting may cost extra; the CRISS request has no fee.

REVIEW AND SIGN: all listed offenses must be Montana state-court misdemeanor convictions. No general felony or automatic conviction sealing route is supplied. Confirm all fines, fees, restitution and treatment are complete and the other presumption facts are accurate. The five-year route and military route are distinct. The prosecution office must verify that you are not detained, charged or subject to pending charges. The court decides by a preponderance of the evidence; no guaranteed outcome or public-safety finding is authored.

Sign the petition declaration under penalty of perjury under Montana law, with actual signature, date, city and state. No notarization is required. The clerk assigns the new civil case number; leave it blank. Leave the judge findings, granted-conviction determinations, order date and signature blank.

FILE FIRST, THEN SERVE: file the civil petition with the chosen district court; serve a file-stamped copy on EVERY county attorney/city attorney office that prosecuted an included offense. Use mail, hand delivery or email according to confirmed local procedure. Complete a separate actual service date, method and server signature for each office and file the certificate as the clerk directs. Printed name of actual server is required before certificate filing; the server is not assumed to be the petitioner.

NOTICE: under MCA 46-18-1106 the prosecution office, not you, has fourteen days from receiving the petition to attempt to notify any victim and document the attempt. This is NOT a deadline for the office to respond to the petition. Do not contact victims to provide statutory notice. Prosecutor opposition, victim appearance or a hearing triggers legal-help handoff; an uncontested petition may be decided without a hearing.

AFTER AN ORDER: the court can grant all, some or none of the requested convictions. Check the signed order and the specific convictions it grants. If scope differs from this request, stop and reconcile the DOJ form and schedule to the actual granted scope before signing or mailing; do not submit requests for ungranted convictions. Obtain certified copies of the expungement order and each final judgment.

DOJ STAGE: only after the order, use the retained 29 April 2024 CRISS form with Misdemeanor Conviction Expungement (first request) selected. Attach the completed FD-258 card, district court expungement order and judgments; include valid photo identification by default under the adopted attachment treatment. The held source asks for Original Court Judgement and District Court Order. Ask CRISS whether certified copies suffice before mailing irreplaceable originals. PARTICIPANT DISTRIBUTION under MCA 46-18-1110(2)(b): send the order, fingerprints and DOJ form to the arresting agency, prosecution office and clerk of each convicting court, AND to DOJ. Use the complete recipient list in these instructions. This is your responsibility; court entry alone does not complete expungement. Keep copies and mailing/service proof. For more charges than fit on the four source lines, attach the complete conviction continuation schedule; the DOJ field explicitly points to it.

Mail the CRISS packet to CRISS Expungement, 2225 11th Ave., PO Box 201403, Helena, MT 59620. Questions: 406-444-3625; dojcriss@mt.gov. The form directs allowing thirty days for processing and says CRISS provides a confirmation letter. The arrest or fingerprinting date must be completed from the actual event before DOJ submission; do not invent it. The last four SSN digits are optional. Sign and date the DOJ form personally.

VERIFY AFTER PROCESSING: obtain the CRISS confirmation, re-pull your criminal record, and search BOTH court portals again. DOJ expungement means permanent destruction, deletion or erasure from its criminal history system; local arresting agencies, prosecutors and courts permanently SEAL their records. Fingerprint and investigative data are excluded by MCA 46-18-1103(2)(b). If records remain, contact the relevant custodian and obtain legal help.
