# What you must do before you file — clearing a Nevada case after a court treatment programme, a conditional dismissal or a set-aside, under NRS 176A.245, 176A.265 or 176A.295

This packet is prepared for **Sealing After a Treatment-Program Discharge, Conditional Dismissal or Set-Aside (NRS 176A.245, 176A.265 and 176A.295)**.

Nevada calls this record sealing and never expungement. NRS 176A.245, 176A.265 and 176A.295 are structurally identical — they differ only in which court treatment programme they attach to — and each carries two branches. On the subsection 1 branch sealing is **automatic** and you file **nothing**. On the subsection 2 branch — a charge under NRS 200.485, NRS 484C.110 or NRS 484C.120, conditionally dismissed or the judgment set aside — sealing follows only on your own petition, and not sooner than **seven years** after. No official form exists for that petition: the state records division publishes only sample petitions for the ordinary chapter 179 route and records that procedures vary from county to county, so the petition, proposed order and declaration in this packet are composed pleadings.

The platform filled in what it holds about you: your name, your date of birth, your mailing address, your telephone number and your email. Every case fact belongs to records the platform has not seen — your court paperwork and the court's own record — so every one of them is a labelled dotted blank listed below, and you fill it from the record itself, never from memory.

## Which branch you are on

| Branch | When it is yours | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subsection 1 — automatic | any programme case EXCEPT a charge under NRS 200.485, NRS 484C.110 or NRS 484C.120 that was conditionally dismissed or set aside | file **nothing**; the referral page says where to go |
| Subsection 2 — petition | a charge under NRS 200.485, NRS 484C.110 or NRS 484C.120, conditionally dismissed or the judgment set aside, **and** at least seven years have passed | file the petition, proposed order and declaration in this packet |

Barred on **both** branches: a charge under NRS 200.508 or NRS 200.5099 (subsection 3 of each section). If that was the charge, nothing in this packet can be filed.

## What you do, in order (subsection 2 branch only)

1. **Answer the branch screen.** If you are on the subsection 1 branch, stop here and read the referral page instead — you file nothing.
2. **Gather your court paperwork.** The court, the county, the case number, the programme, the charge wording, the resolution and its date all come from it. The clerk of the court that handled the case can confirm anything unclear.
3. **Count the seven years** from the date of the conditional dismissal or set-aside. If the period has not elapsed, or the record does not show clearly how the case was resolved, stop and get help.
4. **Fill in every dotted blank**, including every agency and officer on the proposed order — an agency left off the list keeps its records unsealed.
5. **Sign and date the petition and the declaration yourself.** The platform never signs for you and never dates a signature. The order's date and signature lines are the court's own.
6. **Ask the clerk of the court that handled the case three things before filing**: whether the clerk charges a filing fee (the sections prescribe none and this packet states no amount), whether the petition must be served on the Division of Parole and Probation or the prosecutor (the sections prescribe no service), and whether the proposed order is lodged with the petition or settled after decision (the sections are silent).
7. **File in the court that handled the case** — district, justice or municipal, as your paperwork states.
8. **What to expect**: the court shall order the records sealed without a hearing unless the Division petitions, for good cause shown, not to seal and requests one. If it does, that is a contested proceeding — get a lawyer.

## Things the platform deliberately left blank

- **Your signature, and every date beside a signature.** A signature is yours alone, and a date written before you sign would be false.
- **The judge's signature and the order's date lines.** The order is the court's to sign and date.
- **The filing fee, the service method and the proposed-order practice of your particular court.** None is established by the sections; the clerk of the court that handled the case is the authority that can answer all three — ask before you file.

## When to stop and get help instead of filing

- the charge was under NRS 200.508 or NRS 200.5099 — subsection 3 bars sealing on both branches;
- the Division petitions not to seal and requests a hearing — a contested proceeding;
- whether you fulfilled the terms and conditions imposed by the court and the Division is disputed or unclear;
- the programme was not completed and you were sentenced;
- the seven-year period has not elapsed, or its start date is uncertain;
- your discharge was dishonourable — NRS 179.2445(2) removes the presumption on any later NRS 179.245 petition;
- records sit in more than one county or more than one court;
- federal, tribal, military or out-of-state records — a Nevada order does not reach them;
- your goal is firearm rights, which sealing does not restore;
- any pending charge, and any conviction during the clean period other than a minor moving or standing traffic violation; any offence-category question; or any immigration question.

## What this packet is not

This is a prepared set of composed pleadings, screens and process pages. It is not an official Nevada form — none exists for a subsection 2 petition, which is why these pages are composed — and it is not legal advice, it is not filed for you, and it does not decide whether the court will order sealing. Sealing is not expungement: it does not authorise destruction of the records, it does not restore firearm rights, and it must not be described as having any immigration effect.
