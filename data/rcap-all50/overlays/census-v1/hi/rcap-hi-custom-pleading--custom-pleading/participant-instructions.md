# What you must do before you file — asking the Hawaii court that entered the judgment for an order expunging a conviction under HRS 706-622.5(4), 706-622.5(5), 706-622.8, 706-622.9 or 291E-64(e), and then applying to the Hawaii Criminal Justice Data Center with a copy of that order

This packet is prepared for **Hawaii Stage-One Conviction-Expungement Motions and the Stage-Two HCJDC 159(b) Application**.

This is a two-stage packet, and the two stages happen months apart. Stage one is a motion you file in your own criminal case, asking the court that convicted or sentenced you for an order expunging the record of that conviction. Stage two is the Hawaii Criminal Justice Data Center application, which cannot be submitted until the court has signed the stage-one order and you have a copy of it to attach.

Do not send the stage-two application to the Data Center until you hold the signed order. The registry records the reason plainly, and the form itself prints it: an application to expunge a conviction without a copy of the court order granting the expungement will be denied.

This packet was composed from the committed legal-design track registry and from the pinned HCJDC 159(b) bytes. Where the record does not answer a question, this packet says so instead of answering it.

## Which pages are yours

This packet carries five statutory routes. Use only the pages for the route that matches your conviction; the other routes' pages are not yours and must not be filed.

| Route | Statute | What this means for your motion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-time drug offender expungement - HRS 706-622.5(4) | HRS 706-622.5(4) | The record states the court shall issue the order to expunge, and records no ground on which the court may refuse a qualifying application. It remains the court's order to enter; this packet asks for it and cannot make it. |
| Marijuana three grams or less expungement - HRS 706-622.5(5) | HRS 706-622.5(5) | The record states the court shall grant the expungement order, and records the only proviso as the quantity: three grams or less. |
| Pre-July-2004 first-time drug offender expungement - HRS 706-622.8 | HRS 706-622.8 | The record states the court shall issue the order to expunge, subject only to the statute's proviso. This route is for sentences imposed before 1 July 2004. |
| First-time property offender expungement - HRS 706-622.9 | HRS 706-622.9 | The record states the order follows once the court makes the statutory findings, but two of those findings are the court's own to make, so the outcome is not automatic. The record also states that where the court cannot make one of them, it may still order expungement if it finds you successfully completed a substance abuse treatment programme. |
| DUI under 21 expungement - HRS 291E-64(e) | HRS 291E-64(e) | The record states this subsection is not established as mandatory: it says a person may apply, and it does not say the court shall grant. Do not treat the order as guaranteed. |

The third column is this build's plain-language reading of the controlling record. The record's own words are reproduced unedited in the next section, so you can check the reading against them.

### The controlling record's own words on each route

The sentences below are quoted verbatim from the committed legal-design record this packet was built from. They are written for the people who build and review packets, not for you, so some of them talk about what "the packet copy" may or may not say. They are reproduced here unedited rather than trimmed, so that you can see exactly what the record does and does not establish about your route.

**First-time drug offender expungement - HRS 706-622.5(4)**

> Mandatory. 'the court shall issue a court order to expunge'. The proviso states conditions, not a discretion. The court has no listed ground to refuse a qualifying application.

**Marijuana three grams or less expungement - HRS 706-622.5(5)**

> Mandatory. 'the court shall grant an expungement order'. The only proviso is the quantity.

**Pre-July-2004 first-time drug offender expungement - HRS 706-622.8**

> Mandatory. 'The court shall issue a court order to expunge', subject only to the proviso.

**First-time property offender expungement - HRS 706-622.9**

> Mandatory. 'the court shall issue', subject to the proviso.

> Mandatory once the findings are made — 'shall issue ... provided that (a) through (d)' — but two of the four are findings the court makes, so the outcome is not mechanical. There is then an express discretionary fallback: where the court cannot make the paragraph (b) finding, it 'may nevertheless' order expungement provided it finds the person successfully completed a substance abuse treatment programme. This is the only discretionary grant in any of the five statutes, and the packet copy must not describe subsection (4) relief as automatic.

**DUI under 21 expungement - HRS 291E-64(e)**

> Not established as mandatory. This subsection is drafted differently from the other four: it says the person 'may apply to the court for an expungement order' if the conditions are met, and it does not say the court shall grant it. No source read here resolves whether a qualifying application must be granted. Recorded as an open point below. It does not block the build — the vehicle, the venue and the allegations are all settled — but the packet copy must not tell a participant the order is guaranteed.

## Where this is filed, what it costs, and who must be served

| Question | What the record establishes, or the authority that answers it |
| --- | --- |
| Where does the stage-one motion go? | Into the participant's own existing penal case, in the court that entered the judgment. The registry records the filing rule as: "Stage one: file a written motion in the existing penal case, in the court that entered the judgment. Stage two: submit the HCJDC 159(b) Expungement Application to the Hawaii Criminal Justice Data Center with a copy of the signed order attached." |
| Which court is that? | The registry records that "Venue follows the offence and is a participant input rather than a track constant." The packet therefore prints a labelled blank for the court rather than naming one, and the participant reads it from their own court record. |
| Is there a filing fee at stage one? | The registry records: "No fee is prescribed by the statute on any of the five stage-one routes, and the Judiciary publishes no filing fee for a motion filed in an existing penal case. What remains open is clerk cost practice, which is recorded as a release-level question rather than answered here. An HCJDC application fee applies at stage two." No number is printed on the stage-one pages because the record answers none. |
| Is there a fee waiver at stage one? | The registry records: "None arises from the statute. Haw. R. Penal P. Form B, the Request to Proceed Without Paying Filing Fees, is the companion to the Rule 40 post-conviction petition and is not a component of an expungement packet." |
| Who must be served? | The registry records: "Haw. R. Penal P. 49(a): all written submissions to the court shall be served upon each of the parties promptly after filing. The prosecuting attorney is a party to the underlying penal case and is served under that rule. Proof of service is required by Haw. R. Penal P. 49(c), so a certificate of service is part of the stage-one filing." |
| Is any notice required? | The registry records: "No statutory notice. Not one of the five provisions requires notice to the prosecuting attorney, to a victim, to the Attorney General, to the Data Center or to any agency. The notice that exists is the service every filed document receives." |
| Must the motion be notarized? | The registry records: "None. Haw. R. Penal P. 47(d) permits an unsworn declaration in lieu of an affidavit, subscribed as true under penalty of law and dated, which removes any notarization requirement from the stage-one filing." |
| Who signs the stage-one motion? | The registry records: "The participant signs the motion personally, and signs the Haw. R. Penal P. 47(d) declaration." |
| Where does the stage-two application go? | The pinned HCJDC 159(b) form prints the destination on its own face: Hawaii Criminal Justice Data Center, Attn: Expungement, 465 South King Street, Room 102, Honolulu, HI 96813. |
| What does stage two cost? | The pinned HCJDC 159(b) form prints: "The fee for first-time expungement is $35. Non-first-time expungements are $50. The fee includes a non-refundable $10 processing fee. A duplicate copy of an expungement certificate is $20.00." The form also prints that payment must be made by money order or cashier's check issued in the United States, and that personal checks are not accepted. |
| What must be attached at stage two? | The pinned HCJDC 159(b) form prints: "If you are applying to have a conviction expunged, you MUST ATTACH A COPY OF THE COURT ORDER GRANTING THE EXPUNGEMENT." |

## What you do, in order

1. Read the route table below and find the row that matches your conviction. Use only the pages for that route; the other routes' pages are not yours.
2. Fill every dotted blank on the stage-one motion for your route, reading each value from your own court record rather than from memory.
3. Sign and date the motion and the declaration under Haw. R. Penal P. 47(d). Do not have it notarized; the registry records that the rule's unsworn declaration removes any notarization requirement.
4. File the motion in your existing penal case, in the court that entered the judgment.
5. Serve a copy on the prosecuting attorney and complete the certificate of service on the motion, as Haw. R. Penal P. 49(a) and 49(c) require.
6. Give the court the proposed order for your route.
7. Wait for the court to rule. Obtain a certified or signed copy of the order if it is granted.

10. Mail the stage-two application to the address the form prints on its own face.

## When to stop and get help instead of filing

The registry records these as self-help boundaries for these routes. Each one is a reason to stop and get help rather than to file this packet.

**First-time drug offender expungement - HRS 706-622.5(4)**

- The prosecutor opposes, or the court sets a contested hearing.
- The participant disputes whether probation was completed or whether the terms were complied with.
- A prior expungement under HRS 706-622.5 or HRS 706-622.8 anywhere in the participant's history.
- Any immigration matter.
- The participant wants to attack the underlying conviction rather than expunge it.

**Marijuana three grams or less expungement - HRS 706-622.5(5)**

- Any other criminal charge arising from the same facts and circumstances, which defeats the route.
- Any dispute about the quantity.
- A prior expungement under HRS 706-622.5 or HRS 706-622.8.
- The prosecutor opposes, or the court sets a contested hearing.
- Any immigration matter.

**Pre-July-2004 first-time drug offender expungement - HRS 706-622.8**

- A sentence imposed on or after July 1, 2004, which routes to HRS 706-622.5(4) instead.
- The participant cannot obtain the record of a sentence more than twenty years old.
- A prior expungement under HRS 706-622.5 or HRS 706-622.8.
- The prosecutor opposes, or the court sets a contested hearing.
- Any immigration matter.

**First-time property offender expungement - HRS 706-622.9**

- Any prior or subsequent felony conviction in any jurisdiction.
- A contested nonviolence finding under subsection (4)(d).
- The court cannot make the subsection (4)(b) finding and the discretionary fallback is in play.
- The prosecutor opposes, or the court sets a contested hearing.
- Any immigration matter.

**DUI under 21 expungement - HRS 291E-64(e)**

- Any commercial learner's permit or commercial driver's licence, or a conviction in a commercial motor vehicle or while transporting hazardous materials, which excludes the route outright.
- Any prior alcohol enforcement contact.
- Any subsequent alcohol or drug related enforcement contact.
- The participant is not yet twenty-one.
- The prosecutor opposes, or the court sets a contested hearing.
- Any immigration matter.
