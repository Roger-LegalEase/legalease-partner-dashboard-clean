# Filing instructions — Family Member Application to Vacate a Homicide Victim's Prostitution Conviction (Washington)

Family: `wa_vac_homicide_victim_prostitution-set` · Route: `obligation:track-only:WA:wa_vac_homicide_victim_prostitution` · Statute: RCW 9.96.060(7)
Status: **EVIDENCE_ONLY_NOT_APPROVED_FOR_DELIVERY** — this packet has not received legal or visual approval and is not a substitute for either.

This packet prepares two official Washington Courts forms:

- **CrRLJ 09.0100** — Petition and Declaration for Order Vacating Conviction (rev. 11/2024), and
- **CrRLJ 09.0200** — Order on Petition to Vacate Conviction (rev. 11/2024), the proposed order the judge completes and signs. You do not fill in the order's findings or rulings; those belong to the court.

Every instruction below is taken from the official form or from the court system's
own instruction sheet, **CrRLJ 09.0300 — Instructions for Vacating Misdemeanor and
Gross Misdemeanor Convictions** (rev. 07/2022). Where those sources do not answer a
question — for example, the filing fee — these instructions name the office that
can answer it instead of guessing. The exact sources, with their SHA-256 hashes as
held in this repository, are listed at the end.

## Read this limit first — it matters more on this route than on any other

This route is the application by a **family member of a homicide victim** to
vacate the deceased victim's prostitution conviction under **RCW 9.96.060(7)**.
The official petition this packet prepares, CrRLJ 09.0100 (rev. 11/2024), was
read first-hand for this packet, and **no printed section of it names
RCW 9.96.060(7)** and no section provides for an application by a family member
on behalf of a deceased person. Every ground printed on the form (sections 3, 5,
6 and 7) is a first-person declaration by the convicted person. Which pleading
states this route's ground — and whether this form can be used or must be
adapted — is an **open legal question this packet does not decide**; it is
recorded for attorney review in `route-options-block.json` beside this file.

**Do not file this packet without advice from a lawyer or the clerk of the
court.** The mechanics below are the official instruction sheet's mechanics for
vacate petitions in Washington courts of limited jurisdiction, written for a
defendant-petitioner; confirm with the clerk how they apply to a family-member
applicant.

## Where to file

File in **the court where the conviction you are asking to vacate was entered**
— the sentencing court. For a misdemeanor prostitution conviction this is a
Washington **court of limited jurisdiction: the district court of the county, or
the municipal court of the city, that sentenced the person whose conviction you
are asking to vacate**.

How to identify that court: it is the court named in the caption of the judgment
and on the court docket for the case. The instruction sheet directs filers to the
**clerk of the sentencing court** (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3); as a family-member
applicant, ask that clerk how to caption and file the application. File the
original petition and declaration, and the original hearing-notice document, with
that clerk (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3).

## Filing fee and fee waiver

Neither CrRLJ 09.0100 nor the official instruction sheet CrRLJ 09.0300 states a
filing fee for a petition to vacate, and this packet does not invent one. Before
filing, ask the **clerk of the sentencing court** (the same clerk's office named
above):

1. whether a filing fee applies to this application in that court,
2. the amount if one applies, and
3. whether a fee waiver is available and how to apply for it.

The clerk of the sentencing court is the authority for all three answers. Do not
rely on a fee amount from any other source, including this packet.

## Who must be served, and how

**The prosecuting attorney's office that prosecuted the case** must receive a
copy of what you file. The official instruction sheet states the method and the
timing (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3):

> On the same day that you file those documents with the clerk of the court, you
> must also provide a copy of each document (the petition and declaration, and the
> scheduling notice) to the prosecuting attorney's office that prosecuted you.

Make at least 2 copies of the petition and of the scheduling notice before
filing — one copy of each for the prosecutor's office and one for yourself
(CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3).

## Hearing

Schedule the hearing before you file: contact the clerk of the sentencing court
and ask for a date and time, then complete the scheduling form that court uses.
If the court does not require a specific form, you may use **CrRLJ 09.0150,
Notice of Hearing to Vacate Conviction** (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3). Attend the
hearing. If the petition is granted, the clerk sends a copy of the order to the
Washington State Patrol and to any local law-enforcement agency holding
criminal-history information about the person whose conviction was vacated.

## Required attachments

- **Local requirements vary.** Some courts require copies of criminal-history
  records attached to the petition. Read the local court rules or ask the clerk
  of the court where you will file (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 2.c).
- You may need information from the court file or docket (conviction date,
  counts, offenses) to complete the petition.
- The official sources held here do not state what proof of the family
  relationship, of the victim's death, or of the victim's status the court
  requires under RCW 9.96.060(7); ask the clerk of the sentencing court and a
  lawyer what documentation to file, and bring what you have to the hearing.
- Bring the proposed order (CrRLJ 09.0200) for the judge to complete if the
  petition is granted.

## Deadline

There is **no filing deadline** stated in the official form or the official
instruction sheet. Nothing in the official sources requires this application to
be filed by any particular date.
