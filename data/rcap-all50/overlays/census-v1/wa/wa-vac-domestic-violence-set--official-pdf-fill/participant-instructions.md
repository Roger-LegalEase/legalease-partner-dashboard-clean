# Filing instructions — Petition to Vacate a Domestic Violence Misdemeanor or Gross Misdemeanor Conviction (Washington)

Family: `wa_vac_domestic_violence-set` · Route: `obligation:track-pathway:WA:wa_vac_domestic_violence:adult-misdemeanor-gross-misdemeanor-vacation-under-rcw-9-96-060` · Statute: RCW 9.96.060(2)(f)
Status: **EVIDENCE_ONLY_NOT_APPROVED_FOR_DELIVERY** — this packet has not received legal or visual approval and is not a substitute for either.

This packet prepares two official Washington Courts forms:

- **CrRLJ 09.0100** — Petition and Declaration for Order Vacating Conviction (rev. 11/2024), the document you file, and
- **CrRLJ 09.0200** — Order on Petition to Vacate Conviction (rev. 11/2024), the proposed order the judge completes and signs. You do not fill in the order's findings or rulings; those belong to the court.

Every instruction below is taken from the official form or from the court system's
own instruction sheet, **CrRLJ 09.0300 — Instructions for Vacating Misdemeanor and
Gross Misdemeanor Convictions** (rev. 07/2022). Where those sources do not answer a
question — for example, the filing fee — these instructions name the office that
can answer it instead of guessing. The exact sources, with their SHA-256 hashes as
held in this repository, are listed at the end.

## Which route this packet states, and how

This route is the vacation of a **domestic-violence misdemeanor or gross
misdemeanor conviction** under RCW 9.96.060(2)(f). Section 6 of the petition is
that ground, and this packet **marks section 6's checkbox** — the one
route-determined election on the form — so the filing states which statutory
route it is. Everything printed under section 6 is a list of eligibility
conditions (RCW 9.96.060(2)(f) and related subsections): read each one and file
only if every statement is exactly true for you, including that at least 5 years
have passed since you completed the terms of the original conditions of the
sentence, that you have not been convicted of two or more domestic violence
offenses from different incidents, and the restraining-order conditions. No
other checkbox on the petition is marked, and none of the eligibility
declarations is decided for you.

## Where to file

File in **the court where you were sentenced** for the conviction you are asking to
vacate. For a misdemeanor or gross misdemeanor this is a Washington **court of
limited jurisdiction — the district court of the county, or the municipal court of
the city, that sentenced you**.

How to identify *your* court: it is the court named in the caption of your judgment
and on the court docket for the case. If you are unsure, the instruction sheet
directs you to the **clerk of the court where you were sentenced** (CrRLJ 09.0300,
section 3: "contact the clerk of the court where you were sentenced"). File the
original petition and declaration, and the original hearing-notice document, with
that clerk (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3).

## Filing fee and fee waiver

Neither CrRLJ 09.0100 nor the official instruction sheet CrRLJ 09.0300 states a
filing fee for a petition to vacate, and this packet does not invent one. Before
filing, ask the **clerk of the sentencing court** (the same clerk's office named
above):

1. whether a filing fee applies to a petition to vacate a conviction in that court,
2. the amount if one applies, and
3. whether a fee waiver is available and how to apply for it.

The clerk of the sentencing court is the authority for all three answers. Do not
rely on a fee amount from any other source, including this packet.

## Who must be served, and how

On this route there are **two** prosecutor obligations, both from the official
sources:

1. **Written notice of the petition — required by the statute recited on the
   form's own face.** Section 6 of the petition declares: "I provided the
   prosecuting attorney's office that prosecuted the offense with written notice
   of this petition. RCW 9.96.060(2)(f)(i). I filed the original notice with
   this court." You must actually give that written notice to the prosecuting
   attorney's office that prosecuted the offense and file the original notice
   with the court **before you can truthfully sign the declaration**.
2. **Same-day copies of what you file** (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3):

   > On the same day that you file those documents with the clerk of the court,
   > you must also provide a copy of each document (the petition and declaration,
   > and the scheduling notice) to the prosecuting attorney's office that
   > prosecuted you.

Make at least 2 copies of the petition and of the scheduling notice before filing —
one copy of each for the prosecutor's office and one for yourself (CrRLJ 09.0300,
sections 2.c and 3).

## Hearing

Schedule the hearing before you file: contact the clerk of the sentencing court and
ask for a date and time, then complete the scheduling form that court uses. If the
court does not require a specific form, you may use **CrRLJ 09.0150, Notice of
Hearing to Vacate Conviction** (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3). You must attend the
hearing; the judge hears the petition on the scheduled day. If the petition is
granted, the clerk sends a copy of the order to the Washington State Patrol and to
any local law-enforcement agency holding criminal-history information about you.

## Required attachments

- **The original written notice to the prosecutor** must be filed with the court
  (form face, section 6; RCW 9.96.060(2)(f)(i)).
- **Local requirements vary.** Some courts require copies of your criminal-history
  records attached to the petition. Read the local court rules or ask the clerk of
  the court where you will file whether this or any other local requirement applies
  (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 2.c).
- You may need information from the court file or docket (conviction date, counts,
  offenses) to complete the petition (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 2.c).
- Bring the proposed order (CrRLJ 09.0200) for the judge to complete if the
  petition is granted.

## Deadline

There is **no filing deadline** stated in the official form or the official
instruction sheet. The timing conditions printed in section 6 (at least 5 years
since completing the terms of the original conditions of the sentence, no new
conviction in the 3 years before filing) are **eligibility conditions that must
already be satisfied before you file** — they are not deadlines, and nothing in
the official sources requires you to file by any particular date once you are
eligible.

## Where self-help ends

Stop here and get help from a lawyer, a court facilitator, or a domestic-violence
advocate if any of the following is true:

- you are not sure this is the correct route or the correct court for your case —
  in particular, if your conviction was **as a victim** of domestic violence or
  other listed crimes, section 3 of the petition (a different route) may apply
  instead of section 6;
- any eligibility statement printed under section 6 is not exactly true for you,
  or you are not sure whether it is;
- you have not yet given the prosecutor the written notice section 6 requires
  and are unsure how;
- the prosecuting attorney objects to the petition; or
- anything in your court record does not match what this packet shows.

This packet is prepared evidence, not legal advice. It does not decide your
eligibility. It marks exactly one checkbox — the section 6 route ground recorded
in this family's production field map — and makes no other legal election; it
never signs or dates a declaration for you and never writes your sworn
narrative. The judge decides the petition; the findings, order and clerk's
sections belong to the court, and the prosecutor's role belongs to the
prosecutor.
