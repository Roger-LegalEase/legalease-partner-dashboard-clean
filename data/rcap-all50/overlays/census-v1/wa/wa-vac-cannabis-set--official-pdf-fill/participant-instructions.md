# Filing instructions — Petition to Vacate a Misdemeanor Cannabis Conviction (Washington)

Family: `wa_vac_cannabis-set` · Route: `obligation:track-pathway:WA:wa_vac_cannabis:misdemeanor-cannabis-conviction-vacation` · Statute: RCW 9.96.060(5) (the petition form cites RCW 9.96.060)
Status: **EVIDENCE_ONLY_NOT_APPROVED_FOR_DELIVERY** — this packet has not received legal or visual approval and is not a substitute for either.

This packet prepares two official Washington Courts forms:

- **CrRLJ 09.0800** — Petition and Declaration for Order to Vacate Cannabis Conviction (rev. 07/2022), the document you file, and
- **CrRLJ 09.0870** — Order on Petition to Vacate Cannabis Conviction (rev. 07/2022), the proposed order the judge completes and signs. You do not fill in the order's findings or rulings; those belong to the court.

Every instruction below is taken from the official forms or from the court
system's own instruction sheet, **CrRLJ 09.0300 — Instructions for Vacating
Misdemeanor and Gross Misdemeanor Convictions** (rev. 07/2022). Where those
sources do not answer a question — for example, the filing fee — these
instructions name the office that can answer it instead of guessing. The exact
sources, with their SHA-256 hashes as held in this repository, are listed at the
end.

## Which route this packet states, and how

CrRLJ 09.0800 is the dedicated cannabis-vacation petition. Its face prints its
statutory basis (RCW 9.96.060) and carries **no ground checkbox and no blank
naming an RCW ground** — using this form, rather than the general petition
CrRLJ 09.0100, is itself the route election (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 2.a: "If you
qualify for vacating a cannabis conviction, use form CrRLJ 09.0800"). The
eligibility this route requires, from the official sources:

- you were **21 years of age or older** at the time of each offense (declared on
  the petition's own face); and
- the conviction is a misdemeanor cannabis offense — the instruction sheet lists
  offenses under RCW 69.50.4014 from July 1, 2004 onward, RCW 69.50.401(e) from
  March 21, 1979 to July 1, 2004, RCW 69.50.401(d) from May 21, 1971 to
  March 21, 1979, and any equivalent misdemeanor ordinance (CrRLJ 09.0300,
  section 2.a).

## Where to file

File in **the court where you were sentenced** for the cannabis conviction. For a
misdemeanor this is a Washington **court of limited jurisdiction — the district
court of the county, or the municipal court of the city, that sentenced you**
(the petition's caption reads "___ Court of Washington, County/City of ___").

How to identify *your* court: it is the court named in the caption of your
judgment and on the court docket for the case. If you are unsure, the
instruction sheet directs you to the **clerk of the court where you were
sentenced** (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3). File the original petition and
declaration, and the original hearing-notice document, with that clerk
(CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3).

## Filing fee and fee waiver

Neither CrRLJ 09.0800 nor the official instruction sheet CrRLJ 09.0300 states a
filing fee for a petition to vacate, and this packet does not invent one. Before
filing, ask the **clerk of the sentencing court** (the same clerk's office named
above):

1. whether a filing fee applies to a petition to vacate a conviction in that court,
2. the amount if one applies, and
3. whether a fee waiver is available and how to apply for it.

The clerk of the sentencing court is the authority for all three answers. Do not
rely on a fee amount from any other source, including this packet.

## Who must be served, and how

**The prosecuting attorney's office that prosecuted you** must receive a copy of
what you file. The official instruction sheet states the method and the timing
(CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3, which section 2.a directs cannabis petitioners to):

> On the same day that you file those documents with the clerk of the court, you
> must also provide a copy of each document (the petition and declaration, and the
> scheduling notice) to the prosecuting attorney's office that prosecuted you.

Make at least 2 copies of the petition and of the scheduling notice before filing —
one copy of each for the prosecutor's office and one for yourself (CrRLJ 09.0300,
section 3).

## Hearing

Schedule the hearing before you file: contact the clerk of the sentencing court and
ask for a date and time, then complete the scheduling form that court uses. If the
court does not require a specific form, you may use **CrRLJ 09.0150, Notice of
Hearing to Vacate Conviction** (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3). You must attend the
hearing; the judge hears the petition on the scheduled day. If the petition is
granted, the clerk sends a copy of the order to the Washington State Patrol and to
any local law-enforcement agency holding criminal-history information about you.

## Required attachments

- **Local requirements vary.** Some courts may require copies of your
  criminal-history records attached to the petition. Read the local court rules
  or ask the clerk of the court where you will file whether this or any other
  local requirement applies (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 2.c states this for vacate
  petitions in these courts).
- You may need information from the court file or docket (conviction date,
  counts, offenses) to complete the petition.
- Bring the proposed order (CrRLJ 09.0870) for the judge to complete if the
  petition is granted.

## Deadline

There is **no filing deadline** stated in the official form or the official
instruction sheet. Nothing in the official sources requires you to file by any
particular date; the age and offense-date conditions above describe which
convictions qualify, not when you must file.

## Where self-help ends

Stop here and get help from a lawyer or a court facilitator if any of the
following is true:

- you are not sure this is the correct route or the correct court for your case;
- you were under 21 at the time of any offense you want vacated, or you are not
  sure the offense is a qualifying misdemeanor cannabis offense;
- the prosecuting attorney objects to the petition; or
- anything in your court record does not match what this packet shows.

This packet is prepared evidence, not legal advice. It does not decide your
eligibility, it never signs or dates a declaration for you, it makes no legal
election on your behalf beyond the form choice recorded above, and it never
writes a sworn narrative for you. The judge decides the petition; the findings,
order and clerk's sections (including the "Vacated / Not vacated" boxes on
CrRLJ 09.0870) belong to the court, and the prosecutor's role belongs to the
prosecutor.
