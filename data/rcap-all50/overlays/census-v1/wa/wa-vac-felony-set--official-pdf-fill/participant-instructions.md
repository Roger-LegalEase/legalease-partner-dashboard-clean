# Filing instructions — Motion to Vacate a Record of Felony Conviction (Washington)

Family: `wa_vac_felony-set` · Route: `obligation:track-pathway:WA:wa_vac_felony:adult-felony-vacation-under-rcw-9-94a-640` · Statute: RCW 9.94A.640
Status: **EVIDENCE_ONLY_NOT_APPROVED_FOR_DELIVERY** — this packet has not received legal or visual approval and is not a substitute for either.

This packet prepares two official Washington Courts forms:

- **CR 08.0900** — Motion and Declaration for Order Vacating Record of Felony Conviction (rev. 07/2025), the document you file, and
- **CR 08.0920** — Order re Vacating Record of Felony Conviction (rev. 07/2025), the proposed order the judge completes and signs. You do not fill in the order's findings or rulings; those belong to the court.

Every instruction below is taken from the official forms or from the court
system's own information sheet, **CR 08.0930 — Vacating Record of Felony
Conviction** (rev. 01/2023). Where those sources do not answer a question — for
example, the filing fee — these instructions name the office that can answer it
instead of guessing. The exact sources, with their SHA-256 hashes as held in this
repository, are listed at the end.

## Which route this packet states, and how

This route is the ordinary vacation of an eligible class B or class C felony
record under RCW 9.94A.640. The motion's face carries elections, and this packet
**marks the three that the route itself fixes**:

1. **Section I: "[X] Defendant ... asks the court"** — on this self-help route
   the movant is the defendant, not the prosecutor.
2. **Section 3: "I [X] was ... discharged under RCW 9.94A.637"** — on this route
   a certificate of discharge is required for class B and class C felonies alike
   (CR 08.0930 eligibility table).
3. **Section 4: "[X] There are no criminal charges pending against me in any
   state court or federal court (RCW 9.94A.640(2)(a))"** — the alternative box
   (a pending prostitution charge) exists only for the victim-of-certain-crimes
   route, which is a different family.

**Two elections are deliberately left for you**, because they depend on the
class of *your* felony, which this packet does not decide:

- **Section 5** — mark the class B box (no new conviction in the last 10 years)
  or the class C box (no new conviction in the last 5 years), whichever matches
  your conviction; and
- **Section 6** — mark the matching timing box: class B (at least 10 years since
  the later of release from community custody, release from confinement, or
  sentencing) or class C (at least 5 years). Do not mark the "Victim of certain
  crimes" box on this route.

Mark a box only after confirming every statement under it is exactly true for
you. Sections 2 (prohibited offenses) and 7 (rehabilitation and mitigation, both
optional and in your own words) also remain yours.

## Where to file

File in **the superior court of the county where you were convicted** — the
sentencing court. The information sheet states it directly: "you must file a
motion with the court in which you were convicted" (CR 08.0930), and the motion's
caption reads "Superior Court of Washington, County of ___".

How to identify *your* court: it is the superior court named in the caption of
your judgment and sentence. If you are unsure, contact the **clerk of the court
where you were sentenced** (CR 08.0930: "contact the clerk of the court where you
were sentenced").

## Filing fee and fee waiver

Neither CR 08.0900 nor the official information sheet CR 08.0930 states a filing
fee for this motion, and this packet does not invent one. Before filing, ask the
**clerk of the superior court where you were sentenced**:

1. whether a filing fee applies to a motion to vacate a record of conviction in
   that court,
2. the amount if one applies, and
3. whether a fee waiver is available and how to apply for it.

The clerk of the sentencing court is the authority for all three answers. Do not
rely on a fee amount from any other source, including this packet.

## Who must be served, and how

**The prosecuting attorney's office that prosecuted you** must receive a copy of
what you file. The official information sheet states the method and the timing
(CR 08.0930):

> On the same day that you file those documents with the clerk of the court, you
> must also provide a copy of the motion and notice documents to the prosecuting
> attorney's office that prosecuted you.

The information sheet also tells you where to find that office: "You can find the
address and phone number of the Prosecutor's Office here:
https://waprosecutors.org/prosecutordirectory/". Section 8 of the motion records,
on the form's own face, the date you served the motion (and notice of hearing) on
the prosecuting attorney in the county where you were convicted — complete it
truthfully after you serve. Make at least two copies of the motion and of the
notice before filing (CR 08.0930).

## Hearing

Schedule the hearing before you file: contact the clerk of the court where you
were sentenced and ask for the date and time, then complete the notice-of-motion
form that court uses (CR 08.0930). You must attend the hearing, and you should
**bring the proposed order (CR 08.0920) for the judge's signature** (CR 08.0930:
"Bring a proposed order vacating the records of your felony conviction for the
judge's signature"). If the motion is granted, the clerk sends a copy of the
order to the Washington State Patrol and to any local law-enforcement agency
holding criminal-history information about you.

## Required attachments

The motion's own face (CR 08.0900, final page) lists the attachments:

> Attach the following documents:
> - Copy of judgment and sentence
> - Copy of certificate of discharge, or other document showing completion of all
>   conditions of sentence as required under the statute
> - Copy of current criminal history

The information sheet adds that **some counties require** these copies attached
to the motion and directs you to read the local court rules or contact the clerk
of the court where you will file (CR 08.0930). You may want to review the court
file or docket for the offense to complete the form.

## Deadline

There is **no filing deadline** stated in the official forms or the official
information sheet. The time-since-conviction conditions printed on the motion
(10 years for class B, 5 years for class C) are **eligibility conditions that
must already be satisfied before you file** — they are not deadlines, and
nothing in the official sources requires you to file by any particular date once
you are eligible.

## Where self-help ends

Stop here and get help from a lawyer or a court facilitator if any of the
following is true:

- you are not sure this is the correct route or the correct court for your case —
  in particular, if you committed the offense because you were a victim of
  domestic violence, sex trafficking, prostitution, commercial sexual abuse of a
  minor, or sexual assault, the victim-survivor route (a different family, with
  different conditions) may apply instead;
- you are not sure whether your felony is class B or class C, so you cannot mark
  sections 5 and 6 with certainty;
- your offense may be a class A felony, a violent offense, a crime against a
  person, or another offense the motion's section 2 and CR 08.0930 exclude from
  vacation — those lists are long and exacting;
- you do not have a certificate of discharge, or any eligibility statement
  printed on the motion is not exactly true for you;
- the prosecuting attorney objects to the motion; or
- anything in your court record does not match what this packet shows.

This packet is prepared evidence, not legal advice. It does not decide your
eligibility. It marks exactly the three route-determined elections recorded in
this family's production field map and makes no other legal election; it never
signs or dates a declaration for you and never writes your rehabilitation or
mitigation statements. The judge decides the motion; the findings, order and
clerk's sections belong to the court, and the prosecutor's role belongs to the
prosecutor.
