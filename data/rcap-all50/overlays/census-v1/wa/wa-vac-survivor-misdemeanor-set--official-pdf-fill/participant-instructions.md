# Filing instructions — Victim and Survivor Vacation of a Misdemeanor or Gross Misdemeanor Conviction (Washington)

Family: `wa_vac_survivor_misdemeanor-set` · Route: `obligation:track-pathway:WA:wa_vac_survivor_misdemeanor:victim-survivor-conviction-vacation-route` · Statute: RCW 9.96.080 (the petition form cites RCW 9.96.060 and RCW 9.96.080)
Status: **EVIDENCE_ONLY_NOT_APPROVED_FOR_DELIVERY** — this packet has not received legal or visual approval and is not a substitute for either.

This packet prepares two official Washington Courts forms:

- **CrRLJ 09.0100** — Petition and Declaration for Order Vacating Conviction (rev. 11/2024), the document you file, and
- **CrRLJ 09.0200** — Order on Petition to Vacate Conviction (rev. 11/2024), the proposed order the judge completes and signs. You do not fill in the order's findings or rulings; those belong to the court.

Every instruction below is taken from the official form or from the court system's
own instruction sheet, **CrRLJ 09.0300 — Instructions for Vacating Misdemeanor and
Gross Misdemeanor Convictions** (rev. 07/2022). Where those sources do not answer a
question — for example, the filing fee — these instructions name the office that
can answer it instead of guessing. The exact sources, with their SHA-256 hashes as
held in this repository, are listed at the end.

## The section of the petition this route uses

This route is for a person convicted of a misdemeanor or gross misdemeanor
committed **as a result of being a victim** of sex trafficking, prostitution,
commercial sexual abuse of a minor, sexual assault, or domestic violence. The
official instruction sheet (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 2.b) directs:

> Use form CrRLJ 09.0100, Petition and Declaration for Order Vacating Conviction.
> Review and fill out Sections 1, 2, and 3 of the form.

Section 3 of the petition ("Conviction as Victim") is the ground for this route.
Its checkbox and the inner attachment checkbox are eligibility declarations that
belong to you: mark them only after confirming every statement under them is
exactly true for you, and complete the statement explaining how the crime you
committed was caused by being such a victim (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 2.b). This
packet deliberately marks no checkbox and never writes that statement — it is
your sworn narrative.

Note from the official instruction sheet (section 2.b): **you may request
assistance from the prosecuting authority in preparing and/or filing the
petition.**

## Where to file

File in **the court where you were sentenced** for the conviction you are asking to
vacate. For a misdemeanor or gross misdemeanor this is a Washington **court of
limited jurisdiction — the district court of the county, or the municipal court of
the city, that sentenced you**. Section 3 of the petition also requires that your
statement be provided to **the sentencing court, or the sentencing court's
successor** (form face, section 3).

How to identify *your* court: it is the court named in the caption of your judgment
and on the court docket for the case. If you are unsure, the instruction sheet
directs you to the **clerk of the court where you were sentenced** (CrRLJ 09.0300,
section 3). File the original petition and declaration, and the original
hearing-notice document, with that clerk (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3).

## Filing fee and fee waiver

Neither CrRLJ 09.0100 nor the official instruction sheet CrRLJ 09.0300 states a
filing fee for a petition to vacate, and this packet does not invent one. Before
filing, ask the **clerk of the sentencing court** (the same clerk's office named
above):

1. whether a filing fee applies to a petition to vacate a conviction in that court,
2. the amount if one applies, and
3. whether a fee waiver is available and how to apply for it.

The clerk of the sentencing court is the authority for all three answers. Do not
rely on a fee amount from any other source, including this packet.

## Who must be served, and how

**The prosecuting attorney's office that prosecuted you** must receive a copy of
what you file. The official instruction sheet states the method and the timing
(CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3):

> On the same day that you file those documents with the clerk of the court, you
> must also provide a copy of each document (the petition and declaration, and the
> scheduling notice) to the prosecuting attorney's office that prosecuted you.

Make at least 2 copies of the petition and of the scheduling notice before filing —
one copy of each for the prosecutor's office and one for yourself (CrRLJ 09.0300,
sections 2.c and 3).

## Hearing

Schedule the hearing before you file: contact the clerk of the sentencing court and
ask for a date and time, then complete the scheduling form that court uses. If the
court does not require a specific form, you may use **CrRLJ 09.0150, Notice of
Hearing to Vacate Conviction** (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 3). You must attend the
hearing; the judge hears the petition on the scheduled day. If the petition is
granted, the clerk sends a copy of the order to the Washington State Patrol and to
any local law-enforcement agency holding criminal-history information about you.

## Required attachments

- **Your victim statement.** Section 3 of the petition asks for your statement of
  the specific facts and circumstances proving, by a preponderance of the
  evidence, that the offense was committed as a result of being a victim; the
  petition provides an inner checkbox for attaching that statement if it does not
  fit on the form. The statement is yours to write.
- **Proof of payment** of the crime victim penalty assessment where section 3
  requires it (the petition's own text states the exception for prostitution
  related convictions), and of restitution owed to any victim other than an
  insurance provider (form face, section 3).
- **Local requirements vary.** Some courts require copies of your criminal-history
  records attached to the petition. Read the local court rules or ask the clerk of
  the court where you will file (CrRLJ 09.0300, section 2.c).
- Bring the proposed order (CrRLJ 09.0200) for the judge to complete if the
  petition is granted.

## Deadline

There is **no filing deadline** stated in the official form or the official
instruction sheet. The conditions printed in section 3 of the petition (for
example, no new conviction in the 3 years before filing, RCW 9.96.060(2)(h)) are
**eligibility conditions that must already be satisfied before you file** — they
are not deadlines, and nothing in the official sources requires you to file by
any particular date once you are eligible.

## Where self-help ends

Stop here and get help from a lawyer, a court facilitator, or a victim advocate
if any of the following is true:

- you are not sure this is the correct route or the correct court for your case;
- any eligibility statement printed in section 3 of the petition is not exactly
  true for you, or you are not sure whether it is;
- you are unsure how to write the victim statement, or whether your facts meet
  the preponderance standard the form recites;
- the prosecuting attorney objects to the petition; or
- anything in your court record does not match what this packet shows.

This packet is prepared evidence, not legal advice. It does not decide your
eligibility, it never signs or dates a declaration for you, it makes no legal
election on your behalf, and it never writes your sworn narrative. The judge
decides the petition; the findings, order and clerk's sections belong to the
court, and the prosecutor's role belongs to the prosecutor.
