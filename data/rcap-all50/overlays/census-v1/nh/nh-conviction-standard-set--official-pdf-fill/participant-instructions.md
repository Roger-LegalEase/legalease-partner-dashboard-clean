# Filing instructions — Petition to annul the record of an arrest, conviction and sentence, standard route

This packet is five New Hampshire Judicial Branch forms:

- **NHJB-2317-DSe**, _Petition to Annul Record: Offenses Resolved Prior to 01/01/2019_ — the petition you file if the conviction is before 1 January 2019.
- **NHJB-3057-DSe**, _Petition of Eligibility for Annulment of Record Conviction: For offenses resolved 01/01/2019 or later_ — the petition you file if the conviction is on or after that date.
- **NHJB-2311**, _Motion for Waiver of Filing Fee_ — file it with the petition if you cannot pay the fee.
- **NHJB-2328**, _Statement of Assets and Liabilities_ — the motion above says you have completed this, so it is filed with it.
- **NHJB-2956**, _Criminal History Record Information Release Authorization_ — how you request your own record from the State Police.

All five are prepared under RSA 651:5; New Hampshire Judicial Branch forms NHJB-2317-DSe, NHJB-3057-DSe, NHJB-2311, NHJB-2328 and NHJB-2956.

## Which route this packet is built for

This packet proceeds under **RSA 651:5** — annulment of the record of an arrest, a conviction and the sentence, on the standard route. It is the same packet for both of the runtime routes it serves, because the committed route record gives them the same five forms, the same court and the same statute:

- `obligation:track-pathway:NH:nh_conviction_standard:conviction-annulment-under-rsa-651-5`
- `obligation:track-pathway:NH:nh_conviction_standard:dwi-dui-annulment`

It is **not** the streamlined mandatory route, it is not the non-conviction route, and it is not the marijuana route under RSA 651:5-b. Each of those has its own form and its own packet.

## Two petitions, and the conviction date picks one

New Hampshire publishes a different petition for offences resolved before 1 January 2019 and for offences resolved on or after it, and the Judicial Branch's own annulment checklist tells filers to select the correct one. **This packet contains both, and you file one.** The committed record states the condition for each in its own words:

| Form | The record's condition | Requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NHJB-2317-DSe | Where the conviction date is before January 1, 2019. | conditional |
| NHJB-3057-DSe | Where the conviction date is on or after January 1, 2019 and the offence was not the highest offense of conviction, so the streamlined mandatory route does not apply. | conditional |

**The date of conviction is on your court record and this packet does not hold it**, so it is left blank on both petitions and listed below as an item you supply. Get it from the clerk of the court that handled the matter before you decide which petition to file, and do not file both.

The platform filled in what it holds about you and your case on both petitions and on the waiver papers: your name, your date of birth, your street address, your city or town, your state, your ZIP code, your e-mail, and your telephone number and the case number **wherever the form's own box is long enough to hold them**. On NHJB-2956, the State Police release, it also filled the LAST, FIRST and MI boxes from the parts of your name and wrote your address on that form's single STREET/CITY/STATE/ZIP CODE line. Everything else is yours, and every one of those blanks is listed below by the form and the section it is in.

## One offence, one petition

Both petitions say it in capitals: **PLEASE COMPLETE A SEPARATE FORM FOR EACH OFFENSE.** If you are asking the court to annul more than one matter, you need one petition for each. This packet prepares one of each form. The record also states what that costs where the charges are in different courthouses — see below.

## Where you file this

File the petition with the **Clerk of the disposing court** — the court you pick from the list at the top of the petition, which carries every circuit-court district division and every superior court. The record states the venue in its own words: “The Circuit Court District Division or Superior Court that disposed of the charge. Separate petitions for charges in different courts.” This packet does not state a courthouse address, because the platform holds no court directory and an unsourced address in a filing instruction is worse than none.

## Who else has to be told

**Nobody is served by you on this route.** The service rule states: “None by the participant.” The notice rule explains how the prosecutor learns of the petition: “The court provides a copy of the petition to the prosecutor of the underlying offence under RSA 651:5, IX. No objection period is specified for this route; the prosecutor is permitted to be heard on the interest of justice.”

So there is no step here for you. You do not mail, deliver or hand a copy of this petition to the prosecutor, to the police, or to anyone else; the court does that. Nothing in this packet is filed or sent for you either — you file the petition with the clerk, and the copy to the prosecutor is the court's to provide.

## What it costs

**The costs on this route are stated in the record this packet is built on.** That record — the committed New Hampshire legal-design memo, bound in source-receipt.json by SHA-256 — states them in its own words: “$125.00 per court location, confirmed on the current Circuit Court Filing Fees schedule and covering petitions filed at the same time in that court. $100 Department of Corrections investigation fee under RSA 651:5, IX unless the petitioner demonstrates indigency. $100 Department of Safety fee for a successful petitioner. State police fee not to exceed $100 under X(d). Whether the Department of Safety fee and the state police fee are one charge or two is recorded below.”

The schedule it names is Circuit Court Filing Fees, New Hampshire Judicial Branch — "Petition to Annul Criminal Record (per court location) $125.00", read at https://www.courts.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt471/files/documents/2021-06/filing_fees.pdf on 2026-08-06.

**One filing fee per court location, not one per petition.** The same record states how petitions filed together are charged: “Unified statewide system with no local variation identified. One petition per charge; petitions filed at the same time in one court location share a single $125.00 filing fee. The court orders a Department of Corrections investigation, which the petitioner pays for, and provides a copy of the petition to the prosecutor.” Petitions you file at the same time in one court location are a single fee between them; petitions in two court locations are two fees.

Read that paragraph twice, because it names more than the filing fee: there is also an investigation fee for the Department of Corrections report, and fees on a successful petitioner. Not all of it is settled — see **Questions the record has not settled** below — and this packet will not guess. Ask the clerk what you will be charged before you pay.

This packet does not take payment and cannot confirm what a particular clerk will charge on the day you file. The figures above are the ones the record holds, and if the clerk quotes you something different the clerk is the one collecting it — ask them before you pay.

**If you cannot pay it.** The record names the papers to file instead: “Motion for Waiver of Filing Fee, NHJB-2311, with the Statement of Assets and Liabilities, NHJB-2328. Indigency exempts the Department of Corrections and Department of Safety fees under RSA 651:5, IX. The state police fee waiver request must be accompanied by an affidavit of the petitioner's and spouse's monthly net income and assets.” Both are prepared in this packet, and both are filed with the petition.

**A note about the fee-waiver form's court list.** NHJB-2311's only court control is a list of SUPERIOR courts. If your case is in a circuit court district division — which is where many annulment petitions go — that list cannot name your court, so write the court's name on the form by hand. This packet will not choose a superior court you are not in.

## Questions the record has not settled

The committed legal-design record for this route records 2 questions it read and could not resolve. This packet states them rather than answering them, in the record's own words, because an invented answer in a filing instruction is worse than an admitted gap:

- Whether the $100 Department of Safety fee under RSA 651:5, IX and the up-to-$100 state police fee under X(d) are the same charge or two separate charges. Both paragraphs were read at gc.nh.gov on 2026-08-06 and state the fees in two places without reconciling them.
- Whether the Department of Corrections investigation is ordered before or after the prosecutor is heard, and how long it takes. This governs what the packet can tell the participant to expect and when.

Ask the clerk of the court you are filing in about anything above that affects what you pay or when you should expect a decision. This packet cannot answer them and does not try.

## What you must do before you file

1. **Get the date of conviction and the charge degree from the court record, and choose your petition.** The date decides which of the two petitions you file, and the charge degree decides how long you have had to wait under RSA 651:5, III. Neither is held by this platform and neither should be estimated.
2. **Fill in every item in the tables below.** Each names the form, the section and the blank.
3. **Get the charge facts from the court record.** Each petition asks for the RSA you were charged under, the charge, the dates, the charge degree and a description of the sentence with the date it was completed. The clerk of the court that handled the matter holds all of them; do not estimate any of them.
4. **Read the certification page before you tick anything.** Every box there is a statement you swear to under penalties of law, and several are legal characterisations of your own record — whether the RSA 651:5, III time requirements are met, whether the matter is a violent crime or a felony crime of obstruction of justice, whether an extended term under RSA 651:6 was imposed, whether an enhanced penalty applies to a second conviction. None of them is ticked for you, and none should be ticked until you know it is true.
5. **Decide whether to ask for a hearing.** The forms say the court may make its order without one after considering the Department of Corrections investigation report and any response from the State. If you want a hearing, tick the box that asks for it.
6. **Sign and date the petition yourself.** On NHJB-3057 the Date and Applicant's Signature lines are printed rules with no fillable box over them, so nothing in this packet can reach them; on NHJB-2317 the signature and its date are left blank. Either way you sign on the day you sign.
7. **Complete the signature blocks on the waiver papers yourself.** On NHJB-2311 and NHJB-2328 the whole block — name, address, city, state, zip, telephone, e-mail, signature and date — is completed by the filer at the moment of signing, and New Hampshire names every box in it sig.N, so none of it is filled in for you.
8. **Add up the three totals on NHJB-2328 yourself.** Each of the three Total $ lines — weekly take-home in item 12, money presently available in item 13, and monthly household expenses in item 14 — is blank in this packet, and the lines that feed it are blank too. The blank form New Hampshire publishes ships those three totals already set to 0.00, so that a person filling it in on a computer sees the running sum; this packet removes them, because a zero total for your income, your available money and your expenses is an answer, and it would be sworn in your name on a statement you sign under penalty of perjury. Write the real figures, and the real totals.
9. **Sign NHJB-2311 by writing /s/ and then your name.** The blank form carries "Enter /s/ before name" inside the signature box as grey placeholder text for someone typing into it on a computer, and its own tooltip says so: "If filing electronically, please type /s/ then your name to sign this document.  Ex.  /s/ John Doe". This packet delivers that box empty, so the line is clear for your signature. If you are filing electronically, type /s/ followed by your name; if you are filing on paper, sign it.
10. **Request your record from the State Police using NHJB-2956.** It goes to the Criminal Records Unit, Department of Safety, 33 Hazen Drive, Concord NH 03305. The form states a $25.00 fee for each request and asks for a self-addressed envelope. Section I carries your name across the LAST, FIRST and MI boxes and your address on the line below it — check both. The one box on that name line the platform left empty is **(MAIDEN/ALIAS)**: fill it in yourself if your record might be under a maiden name or an alias, because the platform holds no such fact for you. Follow the request method printed in the form’s INSTRUCTIONS block: an **in-person request for your own record requires only Section I**; a **third-party release requires Sections I and II**; and **every mailed request requires both sections completed and Section II notarized**. Section II is left blank for you to complete when required, including the recipient name and address, your signature and date, and the notary’s signature, date, seal and commission expiration. The source’s recipient-name dropdown lists only family and probate courts. **Before mailing, ask the Criminal Records Unit how to enter your intended recipient and complete that block; do not select an unrelated court or mail this request with Section II blank.**

## What the platform deliberately left blank

- **Your signature and the date beside it, on every form that has one.** You sign them yourself, on the day you sign.
- **The whole signature block on NHJB-2311 and NHJB-2328** — name, address, city, state, zip, telephone and e-mail. New Hampshire names every box in that block sig.N, and the block is completed at signing.
- **The certificate of service box on NHJB-2328.** **This route requires no service by you** — the record says service is “None by the participant.” and RSA 651:5, IX has the court provide the copy of the petition to the prosecutor — so the certificate stays blank. It is on the form because the same form is used where a filer does have to serve somebody; on this route you have nobody to certify sending a copy to, and a certificate of mailing is signed after the mailing in any event.
- **The counsel blocks.** You are filing this yourself; no attorney-representation fact is held for you.
- **The ruling sections marked FOR COURT USE ONLY** — page 3 of NHJB-2317, pages 3 and 4 of NHJB-3057, and page 2 of NHJB-2311. These carry the court's own order. The Case Name and Case Number headers above that section are case captions: copy the case name into NHJB-2311's printed page 2 header; its held case number is filled in above the court section.
- **Section II of NHJB-2956** — complete it for a mailed request or third-party release; all mailed requests require Section II notarized. Only an in-person request for your own record needs Section I alone. See step 10 for the recipient-name dropdown limitation and the completion steps.

## Why your whole record decides this, not just this one charge

**RSA 651:5, VI makes this a question about your whole record, not about one charge.** No annulment may be granted until the time requirements have been met for every offence on your record, and none may be granted at all if any part of that record is barred. If the petition does not qualify, RSA 651:5, VIII lets the court dismiss it **without a hearing** — and RSA 651:5, IV then bars you from petitioning again for **three years**. That is why this packet asks you to get your complete record before you file, and why it stops rather than guesses. The committed record classifies the whole-record analysis as a point this packet cannot decide for you.

The record names the things that put a whole record out of reach:

- Any violent crime as defined by RSA 651:5, XIII: capital, first or second degree murder, manslaughter or class A felony negligent homicide under RSA 630; first degree assault under RSA 631:1; aggravated felonious sexual assault or felonious sexual assault under RSA 632-A; kidnapping or criminal restraint under RSA 633; class A felony arson under RSA 634:1; robbery under RSA 636; incest under RSA 639:2, III or endangering the welfare of a child by solicitation under RSA 639:3, III; and any felonious offense involving child sexual abuse images under RSA 649-A. RSA 651:5, V.
- Any crime of obstruction of justice as defined by RSA 651:5, XIV: tampering with witnesses or informants under RSA 641:5, falsifying evidence under RSA 641:6, or any felonious offense of obstructing governmental operations under RSA 642. RSA 651:5, V.
- Any offense for which the petitioner was sentenced to an extended term of imprisonment under RSA 651:6. RSA 651:5, V.
- Any record where annulment of any part is barred under RSA 651:5, V, or where the time requirements under paragraphs III and IV have not been met for all offenses of record. RSA 651:5, VI freezes the whole record on one ineligible entry.
- Impaired-driving convictions within RSA 265-A:21, which RSA 651:5, III excepts and which run on their own ten-year clock.
- A further petition within 3 years of a denial. RSA 651:5, IV.
- Federal, military, tribal and out-of-state records.

## What happens after you file

The committed record describes what happens next, and this packet states it rather than predicting it:

> What happens after filing: the court orders a Department of Corrections investigation and report, for which the petitioner pays $100 unless indigent; the court provides a copy of the petition to the prosecutor, who may be heard on the interest of justice; and the court may decide without a hearing unless the participant requested one. On a grant the court issues a certificate stating that the person's post-conviction behavior warranted the order and notifies the state police criminal records unit, the prosecuting agency and the arresting agency. The participant then pays or has waived the Department of Safety and state police fees so the criminal history record is actually corrected, and verifies the corrected record afterwards.

**Two things about that timetable are not settled and this packet will not invent them.** The record records as an open question whether the Department of Corrections investigation is ordered before or after the prosecutor is heard, and how long it takes. Ask the clerk what to expect; nothing here can tell you.

Where the court records its decision depends on the charge degree. On NHJB-3057 the form says so on its own face: violations and class B misdemeanors are ordered on page 3, and class A misdemeanors and felonies go to the Department of Corrections for an investigation and report and are ordered on page 4. Those pages are the court's and are delivered blank.

## Where self-help ends

This packet prepares five official forms; it decides nothing. If any one of them describes your case, stop here and get advice from a **lawyer licensed in New Hampshire** before you file. The clerk of the court that handled your matter can tell you what the court requires procedurally, but a clerk cannot give you legal advice. The same record names the determinations that are not yours or ours to make at all — they are the court's:

- Violent crime, obstruction and extended-term determinations
- That the applicant is rehabilitated or that annulment is consistent with the public welfare. Those are the court's findings under paragraph I and the certificate under X(b).

Four of those are worth reading twice, because they cost money or they mislead. **RSA 651:5, IV bars a further petition more often than every three years** — if a petition to annul this matter was denied within the last three years, you are about to pay a filing fee for a petition the statute bars. **An annulment is a New Hampshire court order about a New Hampshire record**: it is not recognised federally and it does not resolve immigration consequences. **RSA 651:5, XVII does not oblige a private background-check company to remove the record** from its database. And **an annulment does not restore firearm rights.**

## What annulment does, and what it does not do

The committed record states the effect and its limits, and this packet quotes it:

> What annulment does and does not do, including the RSA 651:5, X(f) question form, sentencing and habitual-offender use under RSA 259:39, law-enforcement retention under XI(b), and the XVI and XVII rule that no one has to correct an earlier published report.

In plain terms: if the court grants it, RSA 651:5, X(a) says you are treated in all respects as if you had never been arrested, convicted or sentenced, and X(f) prescribes the only question anyone may put to you about it — **"Have you ever been arrested for or convicted of a crime that has not been annulled by a court?"** But law enforcement keeps its records under XI(b) and may still use them; a prior conviction may still be considered at sentencing on a later crime and may count towards habitual offender status under RSA 259:39; and under XVI and XVII nobody who published the record earlier has to correct or remove what they published. That last one is the statutory reason a private background-check database can keep showing the case after a court has annulled it.
