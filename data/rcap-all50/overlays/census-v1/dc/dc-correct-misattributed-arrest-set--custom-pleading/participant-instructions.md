# What you must do before you file — correcting a District of Columbia arrest record wrongly attributed to you, by motion under D.C. Code § 16-806(g)

This packet is prepared for **Motion to Correct a Misattributed Arrest Record, D.C. Code § 16-806(g)**.

No official court form exists for this motion — the statute fixes the motion's content, which is why the pages in this packet are composed pleadings, grounded on the family's recorded legal design and, for the filing steps, on the Criminal Division's own instruction sheet (Rev. April 10, 2024), verified byte-exact for this packet.

The platform filled in what it holds about you: your name, your date of birth, your mailing address, your telephone number and your email. Every case fact lives on records the platform has not seen, so every one of them is a labelled dotted blank listed below, and you fill it from the record itself, never from memory.

Each is printed on its page as a labelled dotted blank. Fill every one from the records above.

## What you do, in order

1. **Call (202) 727-4245** for an MPD appointment and get your arrest history report.
2. **Get your case disposition** from the Superior Court (email criminalcustomerservice@dcsc.gov, or Room 4001 in person).
3. **Fill in every dotted blank** from those records. Do not guess a date, a charge wording or a case number.
4. **Complete the sworn attestation yourself.** Ask the Criminal Division clerk's office (202-879-1373) what oath formality is required — no held source states whether a notary is needed in addition — then swear, sign and date the attestation yourself.
5. **Send all your documents** — arrest record, case disposition, and the motion — to criminalmotionsealteam@dcsc.gov. The Seal Team gives the motion a case number and assigns it to a judge. The court's sheet says the process can take up to six months.
6. **Serve the prosecuting office**, using the service page, after asking the Criminal Division clerk's office (202-879-1373) or the Seal Team which office prosecuted your case and its correct service address. The prosecutor need not respond unless the court orders it — do not expect an opposition by default.

## Things the platform deliberately left blank

- **Your signature, and every date beside a signature.** A signature is yours alone, and a date written before you sign — or before a copy is actually served — would be false.
- **The motion's own case number.** The Seal Team assigns it at filing.
- **Every line of your own statement.** The packet prints your account in your words and writes no advocacy for you.
- **The prosecuting office and its service address.** No held source states the routing rule between the U.S. Attorney's Office and the Office of the Attorney General; the clerk's office is the authority that can answer it.
- **Any filing fee or fee-waiver answer.** No held source states a fee, that filing is free, or a waiver procedure — ask the Criminal Division clerk's office (202-879-1373) before filing.

## When to stop and get help instead of filing

- the prosecutor files an opposition or the court orders a response;
- the court sets a hearing;
- you may in fact have been the person arrested, or it is unclear;
- fingerprints were taken at the arrest, or the person arrested presented other reliable identification;
- the record is federal, military, tribal, or from another jurisdiction;
- immigration, firearm, licensing, security clearance, childcare, healthcare, or law enforcement employment consequences are in play.

These organizations are named, with their numbers, on the court's own instruction sheet:

- Christian Legal Society - 202-710-0592
- Legal Aid DC - 202-628-1161
- Neighborhood Legal Services Program - 202-832-6577
- Public Defender Service (ask for the Duty Day Attorney for Seal cases) - 202-824-2801
- Rising for Justice - 202-638-4798

## What this packet is not

This is a prepared set of composed pleading and process pages. It is not an official court form — none exists for this motion — and it is not legal advice, it is not filed for you, and it does not decide whether the court will grant relief. Expunging destroys a record; sealing hides it from public view. This route corrects publicly available records rather than sealing them.
