# What you must do before you file — asking the Texas court that placed you on deferred adjudication to seal that record from public disclosure under Government Code § 411.0725, after you were discharged and the case was dismissed

This packet is prepared for **Petition for an Order of Nondisclosure under Tex. Gov't Code § 411.0725 (after a completed deferred adjudication, where the automatic order under § 411.072 is not available)**.

**Rule out § 411.072 first, because it is automatic and it is nearly free.** Section 411.0725 exists for people who do *not* qualify for the automatic order under Government Code § 411.072. Under § 411.072 the court issues the order itself after a discharge and dismissal — no petition, no civil filing fee, and $28 to the clerk. Under this section you file a petition and pay a county civil filing fee that OCA describes as typically about $280. **Pull your Texas DPS criminal history record and check whether the offence is already sealed before you spend anything.**

It contains the state's own forms: the Office of Court Administration model petition for this section, the OCA model order, and the Statement of Inability to Afford Payment of Court Costs that the Supreme Court of Texas approved in Misc. Docket No. 22-9090. Nothing here was invented.

**The order form covers three sections at once.** Its page 2 has a box for §§ 411.0725, 411.073 and 411.0735, and yours is 411.0725. **None of the three is marked in this packet**, because the sentence above them says the *Court* finds which section applies — marking one would assert a finding no court has made.

The platform filled in what it holds about you, in the shape each form asks for it: your name on the petition, the proposed order and the Statement, your telephone number on the petition, and your date of birth, address, telephone number and email on the Statement. The Statement asks for your date of birth twice; it is written on page 2 and left for you on page 11, for the reason given under *Things the platform deliberately left blank*.

**Your address is not written on the petition, and that is deliberate.** That block splits it across Address and City/State/Zip, and the platform holds your address as a single line and will not guess where the street ends. The Statement asks for the whole address in one blank, so there it is filled in.

**Nothing on paragraph 2 of the petition is marked for you.** Five boxes say why the automatic order is not available to you, a sixth says whether your list of prior convictions is attached, and a three-way control says which waiting period you relied on. Each is sworn, each is about your own record, and each is yours to mark.

**Nothing financial is filled in for you anywhere.** The Statement is sworn under penalty of perjury, and a guessed figure on it would be far worse than a blank one.

## What you do, in order

1. **Check whether § 411.072 already did this for free.** Pull your DPS criminal history record. If the offence is already sealed there is nothing to file. If you were entitled to the automatic order and it did not issue, that is a different route and this petition is not it.
2. **Get the two documents above.** They give you the offence wording, its grade, the cause number, the plea date, the start and end of the deferred adjudication, and the discharge and dismissal date.
3. **Work out your waiting period, which runs from the discharge and dismissal.** A misdemeanour outside Penal Code Chapters 20, 21, 22, 25, 42, 43 and 46: you may file on the discharge and dismissal itself. A misdemeanour inside those chapters: the second anniversary. A felony: the fifth anniversary.
4. **Fill in every blank on the petition** — the court, the county, the offence and whether it is a misdemeanour or a felony, the cause number, the plea date, the start and end dates of the deferred adjudication, the discharge and dismissal date, and both parts of your address. Write the cause number on the top line of page 1 by hand; that line is printed rather than a fillable box.
5. **Mark paragraph 2 yourself.** Check every one of A to E that applies, say whether your list of prior convictions is attached, and mark which of the three waiting periods you relied on.
6. **Mark the attachment controls and the fee control yourself** — whether the judgment or order placing you on deferred adjudication is attached, whether the discharge and dismissal is attached, and whether you are paying the fee or filing the Statement of Inability.
7. **Call the clerk of the court you are filing in** and ask what the civil filing fee is there. § 411.0745(b) sets the *type* of fee, not an amount; OCA describes the total as typically about $280, varying by county.
8. **If you cannot afford it, fill in the Statement of Inability** from your own records and file it with the petition. Rule 145 requires the clerk to give you that form free and without your having to ask.
9. **File the petition with the clerk of the court that placed you on deferred adjudication**, in person, electronically or by mail, with the proposed order — most courts want the order submitted with the petition. This is not the county of arrest.
10. **Ask the clerk whether that county wants proof that the district attorney received a copy.**
11. **Wait.** A hearing is required unless the State does not request one before the 45th day after receiving notice and the court makes the two findings — that you are entitled to file and that the order is in the best interest of justice.

## Things the platform deliberately left blank

- **Your signature and the date on the petition.** You sign when you actually file.
- **The cause number on the top line of the petition's first page.** That line is printed on the form rather than a fillable box, so there is nothing for the platform to write into; write it in by hand.
- **Everything on the proposed order that records what the State or the court did** — the date the court considered it, the two State-hearing boxes, the two court-hearing boxes and the hearing date, the signing date, the judge and the court/county block.
- **The three section boxes on the order's page 2**, including the § 411.0725 box that is yours. The court marks them, because the sentence above them records the court's own finding.
- **Every box in paragraph 2 of the petition** — the five reasons the automatic order is not available, whether your list of prior convictions is attached, and which of the three waiting periods you relied on. All of them are sworn statements about your own record.
- **Both parts of your address on the petition**, because that block splits what the platform holds as one line.
- **Every financial fact on the Statement of Inability.** It is sworn under penalty of perjury and the platform holds none of it.
- **The cause number on the Statement.** Its own face says the Clerk's office fills that in when you file.
- **The three date-of-birth boxes on page 11 of the Statement** — the month box, the day box and the year box under "My date of birth is / Mi fecha de nacimiento es". They are yours rather than the platform's for a mechanical reason, not a legal one: on this form the month box is one form field with the notary's month on page 12, and the platform cannot write one without writing the other, so it writes neither and leaves the whole line to you. Your date of birth is already printed on page 2 of the same Statement — copy it across.
- **The district attorney's name and address**, if that county wants the proof of delivery.

## When to stop and get help instead

The committed track registry for this route records these as the points where self-help ends, in its own words. If any of them describes your case, stop here and get advice before you file:

- Any family violence finding or family violence allegation anywhere in the record.
- Any prior conviction or deferred adjudication other than a fine-only traffic offence, where the target section carries a first-time-offender condition.
- The prosecutor opposes the petition or requests a hearing.
- Any dispute about whether the sentence, fines, costs or restitution were completed.
- Immigration consequences.
- Any question whether the offence was violent or sexual in nature, which is a court determination under Gov't Code 411.0735.
- The person may qualify under Gov't Code 411.072 and that has not been ruled out.

This packet adds four more of its own, taken from the assertions the forms in it actually make. They are this packet's, not the registry's:

- **you were convicted rather than placed on deferred adjudication, or you never received a discharge and dismissal.** § 411.0725 reaches a completed deferred adjudication only;
- the offence is driving while intoxicated under Penal Code § 49.04 or boating while intoxicated under § 49.06 — those route to § 411.0726, and the petition swears they are not yours;
- you have ever been convicted of, or placed on deferred adjudication for, any offence on the § 411.074 list the petition prints on its pages 3 and 4;
- a court made an affirmative finding that the offence for which the order is requested involved family violence.

Where self-help stops, the clerk of the court that placed you on deferred adjudication answers filing mechanics and the county's fee, and the Texas Department of Public Safety Crime Records Service issues the criminal history record the other-offence questions depend on.

## What this packet is not

This is the state's own petition and order, a conditional proof of delivery, the statewide fee-waiver Statement and their filing instructions. It is not legal advice, it is not filed for you, and it does not decide whether you are eligible.

**It does not decide whether § 411.072 would have given you the same order automatically and nearly free.** That is the first thing to rule out and the committed record treats leaving it unruled-out as a point where self-help ends.

**Nondisclosure is sealing, not expunction.** The order prohibits criminal justice agencies from disclosing the record *to the public*, and the information is still disclosed to the individuals and agencies listed in Government Code § 411.076(a). The record is not destroyed. Never say it does not exist.

**An order covers one offence.** If more than one offence is on your record, sealing them takes more than one order.
