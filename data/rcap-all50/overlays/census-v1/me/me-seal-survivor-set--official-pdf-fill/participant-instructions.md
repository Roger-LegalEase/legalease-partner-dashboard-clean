# Your Maine survivor sealing packet

This packet asks a Maine court to seal a criminal conviction that arose from sex trafficking or
sexual exploitation, under 15 M.R.S. § 2262-B and § 2264(7). It is prepared for you to check,
complete, sign and file. Nothing in it has been filed and no court has decided anything.

## What is in the packet

- **CR-307 (Rev. 06/26)** — Motion to Seal Conviction for Victims of Sex Trafficking or Sexual Exploitation. This is the motion you sign and file.
- **CR-308 (Rev. 06/26)** — Order on Motion to Seal Criminal History for Victims of Sex Trafficking or Sexual Exploitation. This is the order the judge signs. Only its caption is filled in.

## What sealing does, and what it does not do

Sealing restricts what criminal justice agencies may disseminate and what appears on a State
Bureau of Identification background check. It does not make the court case disappear, and
nothing in this packet says or implies that it does. Maine seals records; it does not
erase them.

That is a requirement of the committed record, not a summary of it. The record states:

> Participant-facing copy must say that sealing restricts what criminal justice agencies may disseminate and what appears on a State Bureau of Identification background check, and must not say or imply that the court case disappears.

## Whether a later conviction can undo this

> There is no self-reporting duty on this track and a later conviction does not unseal a § 2262-B record.

Read that against the form in your hand. CR-308 page 2 prints, in a box of its own:

> WARNING: If at any time after the date of this order the Defendant is convicted of a new crime in Maine or in any other jurisdiction, the new conviction extinguishes this order.

Those two things do not agree, and the disagreement is recorded and unresolved. The committed
record's position is that 15 M.R.S. § 2264(7) as amended by PL 2025, c. 513 governs and does
not apply to § 2262-B records, so the statutory rule is the one quoted above. What a Maine
court actually does about the printed warning is not settled in the committed record. This
packet does not repeat the form's warning as advice and does not tell you it is wrong. Ask your
lawyer or advocate before you rely on either sentence.

This is an open question on the record, not an oversight: gate `me_2264_7_cr308_warning_conflict` is
pending and asks for

- a corrected CR-308, or the court's confirmation that the printed warning box no longer states the law
- what the court actually does when a survivor is later convicted of a new crime

Until it closes, this packet is a review artifact and is not delivered to a participant.

## What signing the motion says

CR-307 item 2 is printed on the form with no box to fill. It reads:

> Defendant has been a victim of sex trafficking or sexual exploitation and the commission of the crime(s) for which the Defendant was convicted was a substantial result of sex trafficking or sexual exploitation.

Signing the motion tells the court that is true. LegalEase holds no information about whether
anyone is a survivor of trafficking or exploitation, does not ask for it, and has not filled
anything in that says it. Read item 2 before you sign.

## Documents to gather

The packet-set record names these, and each entry below is the record's own description:

- Obtain Official documentation of victim status, if the participant already holds any. § 2264(4-A)(B) is explicit that official documentation is NOT required. If it is presented it creates a presumption that participation in the offence was a substantial result of the trafficking or exploitation. Qualifying documentation includes an official record, certification or eligibility letter from a federal, state, tribal or local proceeding, including an approval notice or certification generated in a federal immigration proceeding. Your lawyer or advocate decides whether to present anything; LegalEase never asks you to produce proof and never assesses what you have.
  Obtained from: The federal, state, tribal or local proceeding that issued it, including a federal immigration proceeding.
  Entirely optional. Whether to obtain or present documentation is a strategy decision for the participant's attorney or advocate.
- Obtain Affidavit or sworn testimony from clergy, a medical professional, victim services staff, or another professional from whom the person sought counsel or assistance. § 2264(4-A)(C)(2) names this as qualifying documentation. It is prepared by the professional, not by LegalEase and not by the participant, and whether to seek one is a decision for the attorney or advocate.
  Obtained from: The professional concerned.
  Optional and strategically significant. Not obtained or drafted by LegalEase.
- Obtain Certified copy of the judgment and docket record for each conviction. Ask the clerk for a certified copy so the motion identifies each conviction correctly. There is no offence restriction on this route, so the judgment is needed for identification rather than for eligibility.
  Obtained from: Clerk of the court in which the conviction was entered.
  Recommended so the crime name, date and docket number on the motion match the court record.
- Check your answer to "What crime were you convicted of, as it is named on the judgment?" against Certified copy of the judgment and docket record for each conviction, and correct the packet if they disagree.
  Recommended so the crime name, date and docket number on the motion match the court record.

On documentation of victim status in particular, the record says:

> Official documentation is not required by § 2264(4-A)(B). If presented it creates a presumption that participation in the offence was a substantial result of the trafficking or exploitation. Frame the question so a no carries no implication, and never ask the participant to produce proof.

Whether to present any of it, and whether to rely on the § 2264(4-A)(B) presumption, is a
decision for your lawyer or advocate and not one this packet makes or asks you to make.

## The court fills these in

CR-308 is the proposed order. Everything on it except the caption belongs to the judge: whether
the State consents, whether a hearing was held, the findings of fact, and the signature.

- **Order election — the State consents and the motion is GRANTED without a hearing** — an ordering box on the order the judge signs.
- **Order election — a hearing was held and the motion is GRANTED** — an ordering box on the order the judge signs.
- **Order election — a hearing was held and the motion is DENIED** — an ordering box on the order the judge signs.
- **Prosecutor's name** — the prosecutor who consents on the State's behalf, named by the court.
- **Hearing date on the granting finding** — the date of the hearing the court held.
- **Hearing date on the denying finding** — the date of the hearing the court held.
- **Findings of fact supporting the order** — the court's own findings of fact.
