# Filing instructions — Name-only removal from the public court index

This packet is the Alaska Court System's form **TF-805**, _Request to Remove Name from Online Public Index (CourtView) Under Administrative Rule 40(b) or (c)_, prepared under Alaska Court System form TF-805 (5/25); Alaska Administrative Rule 40(b) and 40(c).

It asks the judge to substitute **“Not Published”** for your name on the online public index (CourtView). It does not seal the case or the file: the case stays public and only your name comes off the index. If you want the case itself made confidential or sealed, that is form TF-800, not this one.

The platform filled in what it holds about you and your case: your name, your address, your e-mail, your daytime phone and the case number. Everything else is yours, and every one of those blanks is listed below.

## Where you file this

File the completed request with the **clerk's office of the Alaska trial court where the case is or was pending** — the court named in the case number at the top of the form. The clerk of that court can confirm it from your case number.

Two things this packet does **not** tell you, because they are not established here and an unsourced figure in a filing instruction is worse than none:

- **Any filing fee, and whether one applies.** Ask the clerk of the court above.
- **Whether TrueFiling is available to you.** The form points at the court system's own list of TrueFiling courts. If you and the other parties are on TrueFiling, serve through it and leave the paper certificate of service on page 2 blank.

## The two grounds, and the five-year point

Administrative Rule 40 gives two grounds and the form asks you to tick one:

- **Rule 40(b)** — the issues in the case involve matters of a sensitive and highly personal nature, and publication of your name could expose a person to harassment, injury, ridicule or personal embarrassment. The judge must find that protecting your name outweighs the public's interest in disclosure and any prejudice to the opposing party.
- **Rule 40(c)** — publication of your name is likely to result in substantial physical harm to you or to members of your household, and protecting it outweighs the public's interest in disclosure.

The form carries a note about the second one, and it matters: **a removal under subsection (c) lasts five years.** After five years you must file a new request if you want your name to stay as “Not Published”.

## What you must do before you file

1. **Fill in every item in the table below.** Each names the page and the printed words beside the blank.
2. **Tick one of the two grounds**, and say in the box below it why that ground is true of your case.
3. **Sign the verification on page 1 in front of a court clerk, a notary public, or another person authorised to administer oaths.** It is sworn. Do not sign it in advance.
4. **Give a copy to every party in the case**, and complete the certificate of service on page 2 *after* you have done so.
5. **Leave the ORDER on page 2 alone.** The presiding judge completes it.

## The items you must supply

| Page | The blank on the form | What to write |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Case Name | the case name exactly as it appears on your court paperwork — in a criminal case that is usually the State of Alaska against your name |
| 1 | Party Names | the names of every party in the case, as they appear on the case caption — the platform holds your own name and not the other parties' |
| 1 | Why you believe the statements above are true | your own answer: why the ground you ticked is true of your case. The form asks the judge to weigh protection of your name against the public's interest in disclosure, so say what is particular about yours. This is yours to write and the platform never writes it for you |

## What the platform deliberately left blank

- **The verification on page 1, and your signature and its date.** It is sworn in front of an authorised person, and a date written before you swear it would be false.
- **The certificate of service on page 2** — the date, the time, who you served, and your signature. Service has not happened when this packet is prepared.
- **Every box the clerk or notary completes** — the seal, the commission expiry, and the JA/Clerk distribution line.
- **The ORDER on page 2.** Granting or denying the request, the findings under Rule 40(b) or 40(c), and the presiding judge's signature all belong to the court.

## What this packet is not

This is a prepared copy of an official Alaska Court System form. It is not legal advice, it is not filed for you, and it does not decide whether the judge will remove your name. The judge must weigh protection of your name against the public's interest in disclosure, and that finding is theirs alone.
