# What you must do before you file — Expunging a conviction right after you finish a specialty court programme

This packet is prepared for **Petition for Immediate Expungement After Completing a Specialty Court Programme, K.S.A. 21-6614(a)(3)**.

Notwithstanding the three-year rule in K.S.A. 21-6614(a)(1), and except as provided in subsections (b), (c), (d), (e) and (f), a person who has completed the requirements of a specialty court programme established under K.S.A. 20-173 may petition the district court to expunge the conviction and related arrest records. There is no waiting period. The court may waive all or part of the docket fee imposed for filing a petition under this subsection. At the hearing the court need only find that no felony proceeding is presently pending or being instituted against the petitioner under (h)(1)(B); the two-year no-felony-conviction lookback in (h)(1)(A) does not apply to this lane. The other three findings continue to apply.

Confirm that your programme was established under K.S.A. 20-173. Ordinary diversion is a different route.

## Where this is filed, what it costs, and who must be served

Each answer below is quoted from the committed record that establishes it, on a line of its own.

**filing**

File with the clerk of the district court, docketed in the original criminal action, with the Criminal Cover Sheet, and make the fee-waiver request in the same filing.

**fees**

Docket fee $176 under K.S.A. 21-6614(g)(2), plus any non-judicial personnel charge, except that on a specialty court petition under (a)(3) the court may waive all or part of the docket fee. This is the only fee waiver in K.S.A. 21-6614.

**feeWaiver**

The court may waive all or part of the docket fee imposed for filing a petition under K.S.A. 21-6614(a)(3). The waiver is discretionary and must be asked for.

**notice**

The court sets the hearing date and causes notice to be given to the prosecutor and the arresting law enforcement agency.

**service**

The clerk sends the Notice of Hearing. The participant does not serve and does not sign the certificate.

**participantSignature**

The petitioner signs the petition under penalty of perjury.

**notarization**

none

## What you do, in order

1. Obtain the programme completion record from the coordinator or court, and confirm the programme qualifies under K.S.A. 20-173.
2. Complete the listed missing case facts and check the petition against your records. Leave Option A and its waiting-period boxes blank.
3. Read the hearing-preparation guidance. Sign and date the petition yourself only when its statements are accurate.
4. If requesting a fee waiver, complete the optional request with your own facts, choose the amount requested, then sign and date it yourself.
5. File with the clerk of the district court, docketed in the original criminal action, with the Criminal Cover Sheet, and make the fee-waiver request in the same filing.
6. Docket fee $176 under K.S.A. 21-6614(g)(2), plus any non-judicial personnel charge, except that on a specialty court petition under (a)(3) the court may waive all or part of the docket fee. This is the only fee waiver in K.S.A. 21-6614.
7. The clerk sends the Notice of Hearing. The participant does not serve and does not sign the certificate.
8. Confirm the copy count with the clerk. Obtain hearing details after filing. Leave the service certificate for the clerk.
9. Bring the proposed orders to the hearing. Complete only the identified factual blanks; leave findings, court elections, ruling and judicial signature blank.
10. If relief is granted, confirm with the clerk that the signed order and KBI cover sheet are sent to the KBI.

## What this packet does not tell you

- Whether you are eligible. Nobody has assessed your record.
- Whether an older conviction is comparable to a K.S.A. 21-6614(e) offence under paragraph (19).
- What a judge will decide on the discretionary findings at K.S.A. 21-6614(h)(2), (h)(3) and (h)(4).
- What effect an expungement has under federal firearms law. K.S.A. 21-6614(k)(2) states the Kansas effect and this packet states no more than that.
- How many copies your county's clerk wants. There is no statewide answer and the packet does not invent one.

## When to stop and get help instead of filing

- The prosecutor opposes the petition.
- The court sets a contested evidentiary hearing or a person appears to testify against the petition.
- The offence classification or severity level is disputed.
- The State v. Anderson time-of-offence analysis is in play beyond the DUI rows in the Judicial Council chart.
- The participant may currently be required to register under the Kansas Offender Registration Act.
- The record may be comparable to a K.S.A. 21-6614(e) offence under paragraph (19).
- The participant needs to build a real showing on the (h)(2), (h)(3) or (h)(4) findings.
- The petition seeks expungement of a felony conviction, because of the firearms-safety finding at (h)(4).
- The participant is seeking firearms restoration as the primary goal, which is a federal and state law question beyond the order.
- Immigration consequences are in play.
- The arrest county and the convicting county differ.
- Whether the programme was a specialty court established under K.S.A. 20-173 is unclear or disputed.
- The underlying offence does not sit in the three-year lane, so the immediate route does not reach it.
- The fee-waiver request is refused and the participant wants to argue it.
