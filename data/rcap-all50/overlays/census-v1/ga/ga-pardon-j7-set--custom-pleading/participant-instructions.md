# What you must do before you file — restricting a pardoned Georgia conviction under O.C.G.A. Sec. 35-3-37(j)(7)

This packet is prepared for **Petition to Restrict Access to Criminal History Record Information Following a Pardon**.

No statewide Georgia judiciary form exists for this filing, so the pages in this packet are composed pleadings, grounded on the controlling Georgia legal review's own custom-pleading specification and on the adopted legal-design record for this track.

The platform filled in what it holds about you: your name, your mailing address, your telephone number and your email. Every case fact lives on a court record the platform has not seen, so every one of them is a labelled dotted blank listed below, and you fill it from the record itself, never from memory.

## Restrict and seal — the words matter

Georgia law provides **record restriction** (limiting who may see criminal history record information) and, separately, **sealing** (closing the clerk of court's file). Neither remedy destroys the record, and this packet never says otherwise.

**A restriction or sealing may still be used to disqualify you from employment or office** in the same manner as a first offender discharge under O.C.G.A. § 42-8-63.1, and it does not supersede disclosure required by federal law. Nothing in § 35-3-37 gives you the right to deny that a restricted arrest or conviction exists, and this packet never tells you that you may.

## What you do, in order

1. **Gather the records** listed above, and fill in every dotted blank from them. Do not guess a date, a Code section or a case number.
2. **Read every numbered statement in the petition.** Each is pleaded as your own statement. If any is not true of your case, do not file; see the help section below.
3. **Confirm the filing method with the clerk before filing.** Filed in the existing criminal case with the clerk of the convicting court. No statewide judiciary form exists, local courts control their own filing and e-filing practice, some clerks resist accepting motions in closed criminal cases, and some judges have standing preferences on proposed orders — ask the clerk before you file.
4. **Sign and date the petition yourself.** The platform never signs for you and never dates a signature. Sign "Defendant, Pro Se" where unrepresented. No notarization is required by statute, and none is added.
5. **File with the clerk of the convicting court**, in the existing criminal case under the existing case number.
6. **Serve every recipient the certificate of service names**, by certified mail, e-filing or hand delivery, and complete the certificate only when the copies actually go out.

## Money

No statutory filing fee is identified for this petition: it is a filing in an existing criminal case, not a new civil action, and the leading Georgia pro se practice does not budget one. **Whether any Georgia clerk charges a fee for such a filing is unresolved and county-specific, so this packet quotes no figure — ask the clerk of the convicting court before filing.** Whether the O.C.G.A. § 9-15-2 pauper's-affidavit practice reaches a filing in a criminal case is likewise unresolved; ask the same clerk. Local agency fees for obtaining a criminal history report vary by agency.

## Timing on this route

- The pardon is the starting event; there is no waiting period independent of it. There must be no conviction of any crime in any jurisdiction, excluding nonserious traffic offenses, at any time since the pardon was granted.
- Where a hearing is requested, it must be held within 90 days of filing.

## Things the platform deliberately left blank

- **Your signature, and every date beside a signature.** A signature is yours alone, and a date written before you sign — or before a copy is actually served — would be false.
- **The judge's name, the SO ORDERED date and the judge's signature block.** The court assigns the judge, sets any hearing and dates its own order after filing. Third-party blocks are never prefilled.
- **Every line of your own statement.** The packet prompts for and formats your account; it never writes it.

## When to stop and get help instead of filing

- The prosecuting attorney opposes the filing.
- The court sets a contested hearing or takes evidence on disputed facts.
- Any immigration consequence is in play.
- You want to attack the underlying conviction itself.
- Venue is unclear because the trial court no longer exists or the records cannot be located.
- The pardon status is unclear.
- The offense classification under O.C.G.A. Sec. 17-10-6.1 or Sec. 17-10-6.2 is disputed.
- You are still seeking the pardon - the pardon application itself is outside this packet's scope; this route begins once a pardon exists.

Where any of these is true, take this packet to a Georgia records-restriction desk or a lawyer: the Georgia Justice Project, the Cobb County Second Chance Desk, the Henry County Records Restriction Desk, or Middle Georgia Justice ("The Desk") — the four desks named by the Judicial Council of Georgia's Access to Justice self-help resources.

## What this packet is not

This is a prepared set of composed pleadings and process pages. It is not an official Georgia court form — no statewide judiciary form exists for this filing, which is why these pages are composed — and it is not legal advice, it is not filed for you, and it does not decide whether the court will grant relief. Restriction limits access; sealing closes the clerk's file; neither destroys the record.
