## READ THIS FIRST: SIX CORRECTIONS TO THE INTERNAL REFERENCE

The internal Maine reference is the strongest one in this series so far. Its core framing is right: Maine seals, it does not expunge, and the adult route is narrow. Six things are wrong, missing, or now out of date, and three of them change the product.

1. **The survivor route has an official form, and the reference does not know about it.** The reference routes every 15 M.R.S. § 2262-B case to manual review on the assumption there is no form. There is: **CR-307, Motion to Seal Conviction for Victims of Sex Trafficking or Sexual Exploitation**, and the Judicial Branch has a dedicated instruction page for it. This is official_pdf_fill with an attorney gate on the evidence, not manual-review-only.

2. **The fee is almost certainly zero, and the reference sends participants to the wrong fee-waiver forms.** The reference surfaces a $60 fee and points to CV-067 and CV-191. The $60 line in the current fee schedule applies to motions under **Maine Rules of Civil Procedure** 55(b)(2), 59, 60(b), 62, and 66. A motion to seal is filed in the underlying **criminal** proceeding under the Rules of Unified Criminal Procedure. The same schedule lists "Filing a Criminal Action, Traffic Infraction, or Civil Violation: No fee" and "Filing in Juvenile Court: No fee." CV-067 is an application under M.R. Civ. P. 91, a civil rule. Telling a participant to pay $60 and file a civil in-forma-pauperis application is wrong on both counts.

3. **The reference misses the single most important post-relief rule in Maine.** Under § 2264(7), if a person is convicted of any new crime after a sealing order, the record **must be unsealed**, and the person has an affirmative duty to file written notice of their own disqualification in the underlying criminal proceeding. If they do not and the court finds out, the burden flips to clear and convincing evidence to prove they were not convicted. PL 2025 c. 513 then carved out § 2262-A and § 2262-B records from this rule entirely. So the general track carries a lifetime self-reporting obligation and the other two do not. This has to be in the participant instructions.

4. **There is a new non-court remedy that goes straight at the background-check problem.** The same public law that created § 2262-B also enacted **10 M.R.S. c. 239**, regulating business screening services. On a participant dispute, a screening service must investigate without charge and **promptly delete** a record found to be sealed or pardoned, with a $1,000-or-actual-damages penalty plus costs and attorney's fees. This is a real, separate relief track and the reference does not mention it.

5. **Automatic sealing did not happen.** LD 1911 passed both chambers on April 14, 2026, Governor Mills vetoed it on April 24, and the Senate override failed 20-11 on April 29. Four narrower sealing bills also failed in the same session. Maine has no clean-slate mechanism. If any Maine copy anywhere in the product hints at automatic adult sealing, it is wrong.

6. **CR-218 paragraph 3 is broader than the statute.** The form says the defendant has "no other adult criminal convictions in Maine." The statute, § 2262(3), only bars convictions **since** the person fully satisfied the sentence for their most recent eligible conviction. A person with an old prior conviction that predates the completion date is not disqualified by the statute but reads as disqualified on the form. Do not automate a "no" answer here.

## TRACK 1 - General post-judgment motion to seal a conviction

**Track short name:** ME-SEAL-GEN

**Public-facing name:** Sealing a Maine criminal conviction

**Correct jurisdiction-specific terminology:** "Sealing." Never "expungement." The Judicial Branch says in terms that Maine does not have expungement and the record is not completely erased. The instrument is a **post-judgment motion**, not a petition, and it is filed in the original criminal case.

**Controlling statute:** 15 M.R.S. §§ 2261, 2262, 2263, 2264, 2265, 2266, 2267, 2269. Chapter 310-A.

**Controlling court rule:** None specific. Filed in the underlying criminal proceeding, so the Maine Rules of Unified Criminal Procedure govern. The Maine Rules of Evidence expressly do **not** apply to the hearing (§ 2264(4)).

**Other controlling official authority:** Form CR-218, Rev. 7/24. Administrative Order JB-05-26 (A. 3-26), effective March 9, 2026 (fee schedule).

**Current effective date:** Chapter created by PL 2021, c. 674 (2022). Current version reflects PL 2023, c. 409, PL 2023, c. 639, PL 2023, c. 666, and PL 2025, c. 513.

**Known future amendment, implementation, or sunset date:** None enacted. LD 1911 (automatic sealing) was vetoed April 24, 2026 and the veto was sustained April 29, 2026. LD 1917, 1918, and 1919 and a marijuana automatic-sealing bill all failed. Advocates have stated an intent to return with automatic sealing in a future Legislature. Nothing is pending that changes current practice.

**Eligible record types:** Criminal history record information relating to **one specific criminal conviction**. This is not a whole-record sweep. One motion per conviction.

**Eligible dispositions:** Conviction only. "Eligible criminal conviction" under § 2261(6) means:
- **(6)(A)** a conviction for a current or former **Class E crime**, except a current or former Class E crime under Title 17-A, chapter 11 (sexual assaults); or
- **(6)(B)** a conviction for a crime **committed before January 30, 2017** that is one of five specifically listed marijuana offenses:
  1. Aggravated trafficking, furnishing or cultivation of scheduled drugs under Title 17-A, former § 1105, where the person was convicted of **cultivating**, the drug was **marijuana**, and the crime was a **Class D** crime;
  2. Aggravated cultivating of marijuana under Title 17-A, § 1105-D(1)(A)(4);
  3. Aggravated cultivating of marijuana under Title 17-A, § 1105-D(1)(B-1)(4);
  4. Aggravated cultivating of marijuana under Title 17-A, § 1105-D(1)(D)(4);
  5. Unlawful possession of a scheduled drug under Title 17-A, former § 1107, where the drug was **marijuana** and the underlying crime was a **Class D** crime.

**Excluded offenses or circumstances:** Everything else. All Class A, B, and C crimes. All Class D crimes other than the five listed marijuana offenses committed before January 30, 2017. Class E crimes under Title 17-A, chapter 11. This exclusion set is the defining fact about Maine: the ordinary adult route reaches almost nothing.

**Waiting period:** 4 years.

**Event that starts the waiting period:** The date the person **fully satisfied each of the sentencing alternatives** imposed under Title 17-A, § 1502(2) for the eligible conviction. CR-218 spells this out as imprisonment, probation, administrative release, license suspension, fine payments, restitution, and community service. The clock does not start at conviction and does not start at release.

**Completion requirements:** All sentencing alternatives satisfied, including fines and restitution. An unpaid restitution balance means the clock has not started.

**Pending-charge restriction:** No presently pending criminal charges in Maine or in another jurisdiction (§ 2262(5)).

**Case-count or conviction-count limitations:** No lifetime cap on how many motions a person may file, but § 2262(3) and (4) function as a clean-record requirement: no conviction in Maine, no criminal charge dismissed as the result of a **deferred disposition** under Title 17-A, former chapter 54-F or chapter 67, subchapter 4, and no conviction in another jurisdiction, in each case **since** the person fully satisfied the sentence for their **most recent eligible criminal conviction** and up until the time of the order. If multiple motions are filed, the court consolidates them to one location (§ 2264(1), as amended by PL 2025, c. 513).

**Mandatory or discretionary relief:** Mandatory once proven. § 2264(5) says the court "shall grant the motion" if the person establishes each prerequisite by a preponderance of the evidence. There is no public-interest balancing test on this track. That said, the practical grant rate is low: statewide filings and grants reported for 2023 through early 2026 show roughly one grant for every three filings, with denials outnumbering grants in early 2026. Treat this as a signal that participants are filing on ineligible convictions, not that judges are exercising discretion.

**Questions that require individualized legal judgment and cannot safely be automated:**
1. Whether the conviction is a **current or former Class E crime**. Maine has reclassified offenses repeatedly, and "former Class E" means the class at the time, which requires reading the judgment and the historical statute.
2. Whether a Class E crime falls under Title 17-A, chapter 11.
3. Whether a pre-2017 marijuana conviction matches one of the five listed subparagraphs. These are cited by former statute section and subparagraph, not by offense name.
4. The exact date every sentencing alternative was satisfied, in particular whether a fine or restitution balance was ever cleared.
5. Whether an out-of-state, federal, or tribal disposition counts as a "conviction in another jurisdiction."
6. Whether any post-completion Maine case was dismissed as the result of a deferred disposition. Participants routinely describe a deferred disposition as "it got dismissed," which is exactly the disqualifier.
7. Whether an older prior conviction falls outside the § 2262(3) time window despite CR-218's broader wording.

**Process control:** Court-controlled. Not certificate-dependent, not agency-controlled, not portal-based.

**Required supporting documents:** None required. See above on what is practically necessary.

### FILING AND POST-FILING PROCESS

**Filing or submission method:** In person or by mail at the clerk's office. eFiling availability depends on the county's position in the Maine eCourts rollout.

**Filing or processing fee:** No fee is listed for this motion in Administrative Order JB-05-26 (A. 3-26), effective March 9, 2026. The schedule provides "Filing a Criminal Action, Traffic Infraction, or Civil Violation: No fee" in both Superior and District Court. The $60 post-judgment motion fee is expressly tied to M.R. Civ. P. 55(b)(2), 59, 60(b), 62, and 66 and does not reach a criminal post-judgment motion. **Treat as no fee, confirm with the clerk, and do not promise a refund.**

**Fee-waiver procedure:** Not applicable if there is no fee. If a clerk does assess a fee, in-forma-pauperis status under M.R. Civ. P. 91 exempts the litigant from schedule fees during the pendency of the action, but that is a civil rule and its application to a criminal motion is unresolved.

**Signature requirement:** Defendant signs. CR-218 has a defendant signature line and an optional attorney line. Having an attorney is not required.

**Verification or notarization requirement:** None.

**Notice recipients:** The prosecutorial office that represented the State in the underlying criminal proceeding (§ 2264(3)). A different prosecutorial office may represent the State by agreement between the two offices.

**Service method:** Not imposed on the movant by statute or form. Unresolved.

**Service timing:** Not applicable.

**Response or objection period:** None fixed. The State appears at the hearing.

**Hearing procedure:** § 2264(1) requires the clerk to set the motion for hearing after filing. § 2264(5) requires the court to hold a hearing. The Maine Rules of Evidence do not apply. Evidence may include testimony, affidavits, and other reliable hearsay as the court permits. The movant bears a preponderance burden on each prerequisite. **A hearing is mandatory on this track. There is no consent or default path.**

**Prosecutor or agency objection procedure:** No written objection procedure. The State appears and is heard.

**What occurs after filing or submission:** Clerk sets hearing. Court holds hearing. Court issues a written order granting or denying, with written findings of fact, and provides a copy to the person and to the prosecutorial office. If granted and the conviction is one maintained by the State Bureau of Identification, the court electronically transmits notice to the Department of Public Safety, Bureau of State Police, State Bureau of Identification. SBI promptly amends its records and mails notice of compliance to the person's last known address.

**Legal effect of relief:** The conviction becomes confidential criminal history record information under § 2265. It may not be disseminated by a criminal justice agency except as allowed by 16 M.R.S. § 705 and the § 2265 list. Under § 2266 the person may answer inquiries from anyone outside that list by not disclosing the conviction, without state-law sanction, and does not commit false statement, false swearing, or unsworn falsification under Title 17-A §§ 451, 452, or 453 by not disclosing it, except when responding to criminal justice agencies or when under oath during prosecution for a subsequent crime.

**Records, agencies, or uses not affected:** § 2265 permits dissemination to the subject or their designee; criminal justice agencies for administration of criminal justice and criminal justice agency employment, including use by a prosecutor in a later prosecution in open court and in charging documents; the Secretary of State for motor vehicle law compliance; victims and their representatives; the Bureau of Insurance, Bureau of Consumer Credit Protection, Bureau of Financial Institutions, and Office of Securities; professional licensing agencies; financial institutions required by law to run a check; and entities required by federal or state law to run a fingerprint-based check under Title 25, § 1542-A. Sealing does not reach federal records or private data already held by third parties, though 10 M.R.S. c. 239 (Track 5) now addresses the latter.

**Continuing disclosure obligations:** **Yes, and this is the sharpest edge in Maine.** Under § 2264(7), any new criminal conviction in Maine or another jurisdiction after the sealing order requires the record to be unsealed, and the person **shall promptly file a written notice in the underlying criminal proceeding** identifying the new conviction, including jurisdiction, court, and docket number. If the person does not file and the court learns of the conviction, the court notifies the person and offers a hearing at which the person bears a **clear and convincing** burden to prove they were not newly convicted. Failing to request that hearing results in automatic unsealing. Note that PL 2025 c. 513 exempted § 2262-A and § 2262-B records from all of this. Separately, § 2269 makes intentional unlawful dissemination of sealed records a Class E crime.

### SELF-HELP LIMITS

Stop automated self-help and direct to an attorney or legal aid when:
1. The offense class at the time of conviction is not clear from the participant's documents.
2. The conviction is a Class D marijuana offense and mapping it to one of the five listed subparagraphs requires reading the former statute.
3. Any sentencing alternative completion date is uncertain, in particular fines or restitution.
4. The participant has any post-completion criminal case in any jurisdiction.
5. The participant had a deferred disposition after completing the sentence for the eligible conviction.
6. The participant is not a US citizen. Maine sealing does not bind federal immigration authorities and the record remains available for fingerprint-based federal checks.
7. The participant wants to challenge the underlying conviction rather than seal it.
8. The State signals opposition.
9. The participant has already been denied once. Denials are common in Maine and a second filing on the same facts is not a self-help matter.
10. The participant is asking about firearm rights. Sealing does not restore them and this review does not reach that question.

## TRACK 2 - Motion to seal a conviction for engaging in prostitution

**Track short name:** ME-SEAL-PROST

**Public-facing name:** Sealing a conviction for engaging in prostitution

**Correct jurisdiction-specific terminology:** "Sealing." The offense is the **former** Class E crime of engaging in prostitution under Title 17-A, former § 853-A, repealed in 2023.

**Controlling statute:** 15 M.R.S. § 2262-A, plus §§ 2263, 2264, 2265, 2266, 2269.

**Controlling court rule:** None specific.

**Other controlling official authority:** Form CR-289, Rev. 10/24. Maine Judicial Branch instruction page on sealing prostitution convictions.

**Current effective date:** § 2262-A enacted by PL 2023, c. 409, § 2. Amended by PL 2025, c. 513, §§ 8 and 9, effective January 11, 2026.

**Known future amendment, implementation, or sunset date:** None.

**Eligible record types:** Criminal history record information relating to a conviction for engaging in prostitution under Title 17-A, former § 853-A.

**Eligible dispositions:** Conviction for that named offense only.

**Excluded offenses or circumstances:** The person must not have been convicted of a violation of Title 17-A §§ 852, 853, 853-B, or 855, or of substantially similar conduct in another jurisdiction. CR-289 renders these as aggravated sex trafficking, sex trafficking, engaging a person in prostitution, and patronizing prostitution of a minor or person with a mental disability. **The disqualifier is not time-limited on the face of the statute.**

**Waiting period:** 1 year.

**Event that starts the waiting period:** The date the person fully satisfied each sentencing alternative imposed under Title 17-A, § 1502(2) for the conviction.

**Completion requirements:** All sentencing alternatives satisfied, same list as Track 1.

**Pending-charge restriction:** **None.** § 2262(5) does not apply to this track. § 2262 opens "Except as provided in sections 2262-A and 2262-B."

**Case-count or conviction-count limitations:** None. The general clean-record conditions in § 2262(3) and (4) do not apply.

**Mandatory or discretionary relief:** Mandatory. § 2262-A says the information "**must** be sealed" if the conditions are met, and § 2264(5) requires the court to grant on a preponderance showing.

**Questions that require individualized legal judgment and cannot safely be automated:**
1. Whether the conviction is specifically former § 853-A rather than a related prostitution-adjacent offense. This is a named-offense route and does not stretch.
2. Whether any prior or later conviction is "substantially similar conduct in another jurisdiction" to §§ 852, 853, 853-B, or 855.
3. Whether the participant would be better served by the § 2262-B survivor route, which reaches any conviction and has no waiting period. Many people eligible here are also eligible there.

## TRACK 3 - Non-conviction criminal history record information

**Track short name:** ME-NONCONV

**Public-facing name:** Cases that did not end in a conviction

**Correct jurisdiction-specific terminology:** "Confidential criminal history record information." Maine does not seal or expunge non-convictions on motion. They are confidential **by operation of statute**.

**Controlling statute:** 16 M.R.S. c. 7, Criminal History Record Information Act, in particular §§ 703, 704, 705, 709.

**Controlling court rule:** None. Note that court records are governed separately from criminal history record information held by criminal justice agencies. That distinction is the whole difficulty in this track.

**Other controlling official authority:** State Bureau of Identification, Maine State Police.

**Current effective date:** In force. Chapter 7 recodified by PL 2013, c. 267.

**Known future amendment, implementation, or sunset date:** LD 1918, which would have clarified the Act's treatment of charges dismissed as the result of a plea agreement, failed in the 2026 session.

**Eligible record types:** Criminal history record information held by criminal justice agencies.

**Eligible dispositions:** § 703(2) makes the following confidential without any filing:
- Summons and arrest information without disposition, if more than one year has elapsed since the summons or arrest, no active prosecution is pending, and the person is not a fugitive;
- Law enforcement elected not to refer the matter to a prosecutor;
- The prosecutor elected not to initiate or approve criminal proceedings;
- A grand jury found insufficient evidence to return a formal charge;
- A charge dismissed by a court **with prejudice**, or dismissed with finality by a prosecutor **other than as part of a plea agreement**;
- Acquittal (a verdict or accepted plea of not criminally responsible by reason of insanity is **not** an acquittal for this purpose);
- Mistrial with prejudice;
- Termination for lack of subject matter jurisdiction;
- Termination because the court lacked jurisdiction over the defendant;
- and the remaining categories in § 703(2), which include pardoned convictions.

**Excluded offenses or circumstances:** A dismissal that **was** part of a plea agreement is not confidential. A dismissal without prejudice does not become confidential merely because it was a dismissal; it falls under the arrest-without-disposition category only if the one-year and no-active-prosecution conditions are met. This is the most commonly misunderstood point in Maine.

**Waiting period:** None for most categories. More than one year for summons or arrest without disposition.

**Event that starts the waiting period:** The date of summons or arrest, for that one category.

**Completion requirements:** None.

**Pending-charge restriction:** No active prosecution stemming from the summons or arrest, for that one category.

**Case-count limitations:** None.

**Mandatory or discretionary relief:** Automatic by operation of law. No court involvement.

**Questions that require individualized legal judgment and cannot safely be automated:**
1. Whether a dismissal was with prejudice, without prejudice, or with finality by the prosecutor.

## TRACK 5 - Business screening service correction and deletion

**Track short name:** ME-SCREENING

**Public-facing name:** Getting a sealed record off a background check

**Correct jurisdiction-specific terminology:** "Business screening service." Defined as a person regularly engaged in the business of collecting, assembling, evaluating, or disseminating criminal history record information about specific individuals for a fee. Excludes government entities and the news media.

**Controlling statute:** 10 M.R.S. c. 239, §§ 1500-AA through 1500-EE.

**Controlling court rule:** None. Enforcement is by private action.

**Other controlling official authority:** None yet. This chapter is roughly six months old.

**Current effective date:** January 11, 2026. Enacted by PL 2025, c. 513, § 1.

**Known future amendment, implementation, or sunset date:** None.

**Eligible record types:** Criminal history record information held by a private screening service.

**Eligible dispositions:** Any record whose completeness or accuracy the subject disputes. The mandatory deletion remedy applies specifically where the disputed record **is found to be sealed or the subject of a pardon**.

**Excluded offenses or circumstances:** Not applicable. This is not offense-dependent.

**Waiting period:** None. The service must respond within 30 days of receiving notice of the dispute.

**Event that starts the waiting period:** The screening service's receipt of the dispute.

**Completion requirements:** None. In practice, a sealing order or pardon must already exist for the deletion remedy.

**Pending-charge restriction:** None.

**Case-count limitations:** None.

**Mandatory or discretionary relief:** Mandatory. If the record does not accurately reflect the official SBI record, the service **shall** correct it. If the record is found to be sealed or pardoned, the service **shall promptly delete** it.

**Questions that require individualized legal judgment and cannot safely be automated:**
1. Whether a given entity meets the "business screening service" definition.
2. Whether to pursue the statutory remedy of $1,000 or actual damages plus costs and reasonable attorney's fees. That is litigation and outside self-help.
3. Whether the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act preempts or overlaps in a way that changes strategy. § 1500-EE makes compliance with the relevant FCRA provisions a safe harbor, and liability attaches only when conduct violates both.

### TRACK 6 (separately listed) - Sealing for victims of sex trafficking or sexual exploitation

**Track short name:** ME-SEAL-SURVIVOR

**Public-facing name:** Sealing a conviction connected to trafficking or exploitation

**Correct jurisdiction-specific terminology:** "Sealing." The statutory terms are "sex trafficking" and "sexual exploitation," both newly defined in § 2261.

**Controlling statute:** 15 M.R.S. § 2262-B, with §§ 2261(6)(C), 2261(8), 2261(9), 2263, 2264(1), 2264(4-A), 2264(5), 2265, 2266, 2269.

**Controlling court rule:** None specific.

**Other controlling official authority:** Form **CR-307, Motion to Seal Conviction for Victims of Sex Trafficking or Sexual Exploitation**. Maine Judicial Branch instruction page.

**Current effective date:** January 11, 2026. Enacted by PL 2025, c. 513.

**Known future amendment, implementation, or sunset date:** None.

**Eligible record types:** Criminal history record information relating to a conviction.

**Eligible dispositions:** A conviction for **any current or former crime**. § 2261(6)(C) defines "eligible criminal conviction" for a § 2262-B motion as any current or former crime. There is no class limit, no Title 17-A chapter 11 exclusion, and no offense list.

**Excluded offenses or circumstances:** None on the face of the statute.

**Waiting period:** **None.** The motion may be filed at any time after the conviction is entered.

**Event that starts the waiting period:** Not applicable.

**Sentence-completion, supervision, restitution, probation, parole, or other completion requirements:** **None.** The motion may be filed while the person is still serving the sentence.

**Pending-charge restriction:** None.

**Case-count or conviction-count limitations:** None. If multiple motions are filed, the court consolidates them to one location under § 2264(1).

**Mandatory or discretionary relief:** Mandatory. § 2262-B says the information "**must** be sealed" if the person establishes the two elements, and § 2264(5) requires the court to grant on a preponderance showing.

**The two elements:**
1. The person **has been a victim of sex trafficking or sexual exploitation**; and
2. The **commission of the crime** for which the person was convicted **was a substantial result** of that sex trafficking or sexual exploitation.

**Statutory definitions (§ 2261(8) and (9)):**
- **"Sex trafficking"** means promoting sexual exploitation by compelling a person to enter into, engage in, or remain in sexual exploitation; promoting the sexual exploitation of a person under 18; or promoting the sexual exploitation of a person with a reasonably apparent or known mental disability rendering them substantially incapable of appraising the nature of the conduct.
- **"Sexual exploitation"** means engaging in the exchange of sex for money or resources because of a person's history of trauma, adverse childhood experiences, substance use disorder, or other circumstances of victimization, exploitation, or oppression.

**Questions that require individualized legal judgment and cannot safely be automated:**
1. Whether the participant's history meets the statutory definition of sex trafficking or sexual exploitation.
2. Whether the commission of the specific crime was a "substantial result" of that history. This is the whole case and it is a factual and legal judgment.
3. What documentation to present, and whether to trigger the presumption.
4. Whether to seek the State's consent so the motion can be granted without a hearing.
5. **Everything about how the participant's account is elicited and recorded.** This is trauma-sensitive and the record created here becomes a court filing.

### Juvenile sealing (15 M.R.S. § 3308-C; JV-043, JV-040)

Two halves.

**Automatic.** For a person adjudicated of a juvenile crime **other than** one that would be murder or a Class A, B, or C crime or OUI under Title 29-A, § 2411 if committed by an adult, the court must enter an order sealing all records pertaining to the juvenile crime and its disposition **within 5 business days** of receiving appropriate notice of final discharge. Notice must come from the Department of Corrections where the disposition involved commitment, confinement up to 30 days, or a suspended disposition with probation; from the prosecuting attorney's office where the disposition included restitution, community service, or a restorative justice event with proof of completion ordered to that office; or it may come from the juvenile or the juvenile's attorney, in which case a copy must be served on the prosecuting attorney before the court may enter the order. The Judicial Branch identifies the Notice of Discharge as form **JV-040**.

**On petition.** For a person adjudicated of a juvenile crime that would be murder, Class A, Class B, Class C, or OUI, form **JV-043, Petition to Seal Juvenile Case Records** (Rev. 12/21), filed under § 3308-C(10)(A), if at least 3 years have passed since discharge from the disposition, the person has not since been adjudicated of a juvenile crime or convicted of a crime, and no juvenile or adult adjudicatory proceedings are pending. The court may grant unless it finds the general public's right to information substantially outweighs the juvenile's privacy interest. Denial is appealable. JV-043 states on its face that the petitioner must provide a copy to the prosecuting attorney's office, which is a service obligation the adult forms do not impose.

Where a petition alleges multiple juvenile crimes resolved in separate adjudications, the highest class controls whether the petition route applies to all of them.

A copy of the order, whether on petition or automatic, goes to the Department of Public Safety, State Bureau of Identification where the adjudication is one whose records SBI maintains under Title 25, § 1541. No filing fee: the fee schedule provides "Filing in Juvenile Court: No fee."

Sealing does not remove or affect the firearm prohibition under 15 M.R.S. § 393.

**Proposed strategy:** official_pdf_fill for JV-043; process_guidance for the automatic branch, centered on getting the Notice of Discharge filed, which is the step that most often does not happen on its own.

**Note on the internal reference:** it is accurate on juvenile, including the 5-business-day rule and the 3-year petition rule. It does not mention JV-040, the service obligation on the face of JV-043, the multiple-adjudication rule, or that there is no fee.

### Pardon

A full and free pardon does not erase a Maine record. It makes the pardoned conviction confidential criminal history record information under 16 M.R.S. § 703(2). Administered by the Department of Corrections and the Governor. Under 10 M.R.S. § 1500-CC(2), a business screening service must promptly delete a record found to be the subject of a pardon, which makes Track 5 the practical follow-through for a pardoned participant. Out of core scope by instruction, but the CHRI effect and the Track 5 linkage should be in intake.

### Convictions sealed under former chapter 310 (15 M.R.S. § 2268)

Legacy status, not a filing route. Criminal history record information for a conviction that received special restrictions under former § 2254 is confidential and may be disseminated only to the subject or their designee and to criminal justice agencies for administration of criminal justice and criminal justice agency employment. § 2264(7) unsealing on a new conviction applies to these legacy records. Relevant only for participants who obtained relief under the pre-2022 regime.

### Out of scope by instruction

Federal matters. Immigration remedies. Actual-innocence litigation. Identity-theft remedies. Contested post-conviction litigation.

# PART 4 - PROCESS-GUIDANCE SPECIFICATION

## 4A. Record identification and SBI record request (prerequisite to every track)

**Controlling process:** 16 M.R.S. § 709 (right to inspect and request correction of one's own criminal history record information) and the State Bureau of Identification's public record service.

**Responsible court, agency, prosecutor, solicitor, clerk, or portal:** Department of Public Safety, Maine State Police, State Bureau of Identification. Court records come from the clerk of the court of conviction.

**Prerequisites:** None.

**Records or certificates the participant must obtain:** The Maine criminal history record. Where the offense class or completion date matters, the certified judgment or docket from the court of conviction.

**Forms the participant must request or complete:** The online Maine Criminal History Record and Juvenile Crime Information Request. A notarized request is available where a more complete check is needed.

**Participant information to gather:** Full legal name and any former names, date of birth, county and court of each case, docket numbers where known, offense names as charged and as convicted, offense class, conviction date, and the date every sentencing alternative was satisfied.

**Exact sequence of actions:**
1. Run the SBI public criminal history search to establish what the state record shows.
2. If the record is incomplete or the class is unclear, request the certified judgment or docket from the clerk of the court of conviction.
3. Classify each case as conviction or non-conviction.
4. For convictions, determine class at the time of conviction and the full-satisfaction date.
5. Route: eligible Class E or listed pre-2017 marijuana to Track 1; former § 853-A to Track 2; non-conviction to Track 3; deferred disposition to Track 4; trafficking or exploitation connection to Track 6 screening.
6. If a record is already sealed or pardoned but still surfacing on a background check, route to Track 5.

**Submission method:** Online for the SBI record. In person, by mail, or by the court's own process for certified court records.

**Submission destination:** State Bureau of Identification for the criminal history record. The clerk of the court of conviction for court records.

**Fees:** SBI charges apply to the public search and to a notarized background check. The internal reference cites $31 and $41 respectively. **Those figures were not verified against SBI in this review. Confirm before publishing them.** Court copy fees under the Judicial Branch schedule are $2.00 for the first page and $1.00 per subsequent page, $5.00 for an attested copy and $5.00 for an exemplified copy, and $20.00 for a clerk record check.

**Fee-waiver or hardship procedure:** Litigants granted in-forma-pauperis status under M.R. Civ. P. 91 are exempt from Judicial Branch schedule fees during the pendency of that action where the copy or service is essential to the action. No SBI waiver identified.

**Expected next stage:** Track routing.

**What LegalEase can prepare:** The record-request walkthrough, the intake questionnaire, the disposition classification worksheet, and the routing decision.

**What LegalEase cannot complete:** The SBI request itself, payment, and the legal classification of offense class where the documents are ambiguous.

**Attorney-handoff triggers:** Ambiguous offense class. Ambiguous dismissal prejudice status. Any record the participant disputes.

## 4B. Non-conviction record verification (Track 3)

**Controlling process:** 16 M.R.S. §§ 703, 705, 709.

**Responsible body:** State Bureau of Identification and the criminal justice agency holding the record.

**Prerequisites:** A non-conviction disposition.

**Records the participant must obtain:** The disposition order and the SBI record.

**Forms the participant must request or complete:** None. A § 709 correction request where the record is inaccurate.

**Participant information to gather:** Disposition type, disposition date, whether the dismissal was with or without prejudice, whether it was part of a plea agreement, whether any prosecution is still active, and for arrest-without-disposition cases whether more than one year has elapsed.

**Exact sequence of actions:**
1. Classify the disposition against the § 703(2) list.
2. If it falls inside the list, explain that the record is already confidential by statute and that no filing is available or needed.
3. Verify what the SBI record actually shows.
4. If SBI is treating the record as public, submit a § 709 inspection and correction request.
5. If a private vendor is showing it, route to Track 5.
6. If the disposition falls outside the § 703(2) list, tell the participant plainly that Maine offers no relief for it. Do not route them to CR-218.

**Submission method:** Written request to the agency holding the record.

**Fees:** None for the correction request.

**Expected next stage:** Completion, or Track 5.

**What LegalEase can prepare:** The classification worksheet and the § 709 request letter.

**What LegalEase cannot complete:** The prejudice and plea-agreement determinations where the order is silent.

**Attorney-handoff triggers:** Ambiguous dismissal. Not-criminally-responsible disposition. Court record accessibility problems.

## 4C. Business screening service dispute (Track 5)

**Controlling process:** 10 M.R.S. §§ 1500-CC and 1500-EE.

**Responsible body:** The private business screening service.

**Prerequisites:** A sealing order or pardon, or a demonstrable discrepancy against the official SBI record.

**Records the participant must obtain:** The court's written sealing order or the pardon, and the SBI record.

**Forms the participant must request or complete:** None. The dispute is a letter.

**Participant information to gather:** The vendor name and dispute address, the report in which the record appeared, the date of the report, the record as reported, and the correct status.

**Exact sequence of actions:**
1. Identify the vendor and confirm it meets the "business screening service" definition.
2. Assemble the sealing order or pardon and the SBI record.
3. Send the written dispute, identifying the record and stating that it is sealed or pardoned and must be deleted under 10 M.R.S. § 1500-CC(2).
4. Calendar 30 days from the vendor's receipt.
5. On response, confirm whether the record was deleted, corrected, or the investigation was terminated as frivolous.
6. If terminated as frivolous, review the vendor's stated reasons and the information it says it needs.

**Submission method:** Written dispute, sent so that receipt can be evidenced.

**Submission destination:** The vendor's dispute address.

**Fees:** None. The investigation must be without charge.

**Fee-waiver or hardship procedure:** Not applicable.

**Expected next stage:** Deletion, or attorney handoff.

**What LegalEase can prepare:** The dispute letter, the supporting packet, and the 30-day follow-up.

**What LegalEase cannot complete:** Any claim for the $1,000 statutory penalty, actual damages, costs, or attorney's fees.

**Attorney-handoff triggers:** Vendor non-response after 30 days. Frivolousness determination the participant disputes. Any interest in damages. Any parallel FCRA claim.

## 4D. Juvenile automatic sealing (separately listed)

**Controlling process:** 15 M.R.S. § 3308-C(10)(C).

**Responsible body:** The Juvenile Court, with notice from the Department of Corrections, the prosecuting attorney's office, or the juvenile.

**Prerequisites:** Final discharge from the disposition for a juvenile crime other than one that would be murder, Class A, B, or C, or OUI.

**Records the participant must obtain:** Proof of discharge and completion of all disposition obligations.

**Forms the participant must request or complete:** JV-040, Notice of Discharge, where the juvenile or the juvenile's attorney is the one providing notice.

**Participant information to gather:** Court and county, docket number, adjudication class, disposition components, and completion dates.

**Exact sequence of actions:**
1. Confirm the adjudication is outside the murder, Class A, B, C, and OUI categories.
2. Confirm final discharge from the disposition.
3. Determine who is responsible for providing notice: Department of Corrections, the prosecuting attorney's office, or the juvenile.
4. If notice has not been filed, file JV-040 and serve a copy on the prosecuting attorney's office before the court may enter the sealing order.
5. Confirm the court entered the order within 5 business days of notice.
6. Confirm SBI received the order where the adjudication is one SBI maintains.

**Fees:** None. Juvenile Court filings carry no fee.

**Expected next stage:** Completion, or the JV-043 petition route if the adjudication is in the serious categories.

**What LegalEase can prepare:** The notice-responsibility determination and JV-040.

**What LegalEase cannot complete:** The court's order or the Department of Corrections notice.

**Attorney-handoff triggers:** Multiple adjudications of different classes in one petition. Any serious-category adjudication. Any firearm-rights question.

## CAN LEGALEASE GENERATE A PARTICIPANT-FACING FORM OR PACKET EVEN IF AN OUTSIDE PERSON OR AGENCY MUST LATER COMPLETE, SIGN, CERTIFY, OR APPROVE IT?

- [x] Yes

**Required explanation:** Maine has less outside dependency than most jurisdictions. There is no certificate of eligibility, no notarization, no agency pre-clearance, and no prosecutorial sign-off before filing. What remains outside LegalEase is the participant's own signature, the clerk's scheduling, the court's hearing and written order, and SBI's amendment of its records. Preparing a correctly completed CR-218, CR-289, or CR-307 is a complete and distinct act, and it is the act that determines whether a Maine participant gets a hearing on the merits or a denial. Two limits run the other way and matter more here than the general rule. First, LegalEase must not generate the substance of a § 2262-B motion: the survivor's account and the causal argument are the case, and they belong to the participant and their advocate. Second, LegalEase must not represent any Maine matter as finished at filing. Tracks 1 and 2 always require a hearing, and Track 1 carries a self-reporting duty that never expires.

## A2. Sealing reaches the criminal history record; the court record is governed separately

This review, like the internal reference, treats a § 2264 sealing order as producing one outcome. It produces two, and only one of them is squarely established.

15 M.R.S. § 2265 makes the sealed conviction confidential criminal history record information and restricts dissemination **by a criminal justice agency**. § 2264(6) makes the State Bureau of Identification amend its records. That much is clear and is what the Judicial Branch describes.

But 16 M.R.S. § 708(3)(A) provides that the Criminal History Record Information Act **does not apply** to criminal history record information contained in records of public judicial proceedings retained at or by the District Court, Superior Court, or Supreme Judicial Court, and that public access to and dissemination of those records is as provided **by rule or administrative order of the Supreme Judicial Court**. Maine courts are criminal justice agencies under § 703(4), which makes the interaction between § 2265 and § 708 a real question rather than a drafting quirk. § 2265 opens "Notwithstanding Title 16, section 704." It does not say notwithstanding section 708.

**So:** whether a sealed conviction disappears from the public court docket, from Maine eCourts, and from a courthouse terminal search is a separate question from whether it disappears from the SBI record, and it was not resolved on this pass. This is not academic. It was the live fault line in the 2026 session: the First Amendment objection that defeated LD 1911, raised by the Maine Press Association, the Maine Freedom of Information Coalition, and the New England First Amendment Coalition and adopted in the Governor's veto message, was precisely about removing case files from the public record.

**Why it matters:** it governs what LegalEase is permitted to promise. A participant who is told their conviction is sealed, and who then finds the case on a public docket search, has been mis-sold, and Maine is a state where the product's whole value proposition is a dissemination restriction rather than an erasure.

**Action:**
1. Check the Supreme Judicial Court administrative orders on public access to court records for any provision addressing Chapter 310-A sealing orders.
2. Ask the Judicial Branch directly whether a § 2264 order removes the case from public court record search.
3. Until answered, participant-facing copy must say that sealing restricts what criminal justice agencies may disseminate and what appears on a State Bureau of Identification background check, and must not say or imply that the court case disappears.

**Impact classification: release blocker for participant-facing copy.** It does not block the build.
