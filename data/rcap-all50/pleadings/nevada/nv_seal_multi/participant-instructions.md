# Sealing several Nevada records in one filing — what you need to do

This guide explains how to complete, sign and file your Nevada petition to seal
more than one record. Read it before you sign anything.

**This is not an official court form, and it is not legal advice.** A Nevada
court decides whether your records are sealed. Nothing here guarantees an
outcome.

## What this petition does, and what it does not do

Nevada calls this **record sealing**. Nevada never calls it expungement. The
Nevada Department of Public Safety states directly that sealing is not
expungement, because sealing does not authorise the destruction of the records.

- Sealing does **not** restore firearm rights. Only a pardon that does not
  restrict the right to bear arms does that.
- A Nevada order does **not** bind out-of-state agencies, and does not reach
  federal, tribal, military or out-of-state records.
- Sealed records can still be reopened in some circumstances, and certain people
  and agencies may still be allowed to inspect them.

This petition asks the district court for **one county** to seal the records held
**within that county**, including justice court and municipal court cases. If you
have records in more than one county, you need a separate petition in each
county's district court. This filing changes only where you file — it does not
change whether each record qualifies. Every record still has to qualify on its
own.

## Step 1 — Get your verified criminal history first

Before anything else, request your verified criminal history from the Nevada
Central Repository, held by the Nevada Department of Public Safety, Records,
Communications and Compliance Division. You submit an Identification File Request
for State of Nevada Records of Criminal History. It is fingerprint-based, so you
will need to be fingerprinted, and there is a fee to obtain it.

This record is a required attachment. Do not skip it and do not file without it.

## Step 2 — Check the details on your documents

Read every line of the petition and the proposed order. Confirm that:

- your full legal name and any other names you have used are correct;
- the county is right, and every record listed is held in that county;
- the case details match your verified criminal history exactly.

Some lines are left blank on purpose, marked "to be confirmed". Those are values
we will not guess for you — the case number, for example. Fill them in from your
own court records or ask the court clerk.

## Step 3 — Write your own statement of reasons

The declaration that goes with your petition includes your reasons for asking the
court to seal these records. **You write that yourself.** We do not write it for
you, and nobody else may write it for you.

## Step 4 — Sign by hand

Sign the petition, the declaration and the verification **by hand, in ink**. Do
not use a typed signature. Las Vegas Municipal Court treats a typed signature as
a reason for immediate denial.

## Step 5 — Take the packet to the prosecuting agency before you file

This is the step people get wrong. In Nevada you do **not** file first. You take
your packet to the prosecuting agency and ask them to stipulate — that is, to
agree — before it goes to the court clerk.

Which agency depends on which court holds the record:

- district court and justice court records normally go to the district attorney;
- municipal court records normally go to the city attorney for that city.

In Clark County the district attorney will not stipulate to a petition that
includes municipal court matters. So if your packet mixes municipal court cases
with justice or district court cases, it goes to the appropriate city attorney
**and** to the district attorney, and the stipulation needs a separate signature
line for each prosecuting agency.

We cannot tell you what a prosecutor will decide, and we never state their
position for them. Those signature lines stay blank until they sign.

## Step 6 — File, then serve the signed order

Once the prosecuting agency has stipulated, file the packet with the clerk of the
district court. After the judge signs the order, you serve the signed order on
each agency named in it.

**About the fee:** we cannot tell you the filing fee. Our source does not
establish one for this petition, so no amount appears anywhere in your documents.
Ask the clerk of the district court what it costs, and do not pay anything on the
strength of a number you did not get from the court.

## Step 7 — Know when to stop and get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney before filing if any of these apply:

- you have records in more than one county;
- your packet mixes municipal court matters with justice or district court
  matters, so more than one prosecuting agency has to sign;
- there is any question about your offence category — whether something is a
  category A through E felony, a gross misdemeanour or a misdemeanour, or whether
  it counts as a crime of violence. That classification is what decides a
  one-year answer versus a ten-year answer, and it is a legal judgment;
- you have a pending charge;
- you were convicted of anything during your clean period, other than a minor
  moving or standing traffic violation;
- the prosecutor refuses to stipulate. That turns your matter into a contested
  hearing, and you should not handle that alone.

## How long you have to wait

We cannot give you a single waiting period for this petition, because there isn't
one. Each record you want sealed carries the waiting period of the section that
governs it, and that depends on the offence category and on whether the offence
was a crime of violence. An attorney can tell you how that applies to your
records.
