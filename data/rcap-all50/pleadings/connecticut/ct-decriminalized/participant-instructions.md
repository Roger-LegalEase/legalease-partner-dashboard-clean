# How to complete and file your petition to destroy records of an offense that is no longer a crime

This petition asks the court to have the records **physically destroyed**. That is a different and stronger outcome than erasure, and it is a different petition from the cannabis erasure petition. Keep the two apart.

There is no form for this petition. The papers in this packet are the filing.

## 1. Look your case up first

Look the case up on the Connecticut Judicial Branch online case look-up and confirm:

- which court handled it
- the docket number
- the date of the conviction
- the statute section you were convicted under
- how many counts are on the docket

You obtain this yourself. LegalEase does not obtain, receive or inspect it.

If what you find disagrees with the answers you gave, correct the packet before you file.

## 2. Write the one part only you can write

The petition leaves one paragraph blank on purpose: **why you say the offense you were convicted of is no longer a crime.**

Whether an offense has been decriminalized is a legal question, and it carries case law. This packet will not make that statement for you. It is your position, in your own words.

## 3. Sign the petition

You sign your own petition. The sources do not say whether it has to be notarized. Ask the clerk before you go.

## 4. Ask the clerk what it costs

This is unresolved and this packet does not tell you the petition is free. Ask the clerk what a petition under section 54-142d costs, and whether anything can be waived, before you go.

## 5. File the petition

File it in:

- the Superior Court at the location where the conviction was effected; or
- the location that has custody of the records; or
- with the Judicial Department records center, if the case is old enough that the records are held there.

Because no form was found for this petition, call ahead and ask the clerk how that office wants the papers presented.

## 6. Service

The sources do not state a service requirement. Ask the clerk what that office expects when you call.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- Whether your offense has been decriminalized is unclear or someone disputes it.
- Your matter involves a violation of probation finding that rests on an offense that was decriminalized later. A Connecticut case has already addressed that situation and it did not go the way you might expect.
- The court sets a hearing.
- You have an immigration matter.

A hearing that is purely procedural does not stop this packet from being prepared. Facts that are in dispute, or a hearing that is contested, mean it is time for a lawyer.
