# How to complete and file your cannabis erasure petition

Connecticut calls this erasure. Not expungement, not sealing. Use the word erasure when you talk to the clerk.

There is no statewide form for this petition. The papers in this packet are the filing.

## 1. Look your case up first

Look the case up on the Connecticut Judicial Branch online case look-up and confirm three things:

- the docket number
- the date of the conviction
- the full list of counts on that docket

You obtain this yourself. LegalEase does not obtain, receive or inspect it.

If what you find disagrees with the answers you gave, correct the packet before you file.

## 2. Get the arrest record, or plan to swear an affidavit

The statute requires one of two things: a copy of the arrest record, or a supporting affidavit.

Ask the arresting police department, or the clerk of the court where the conviction was effected, for a copy of the arrest record. LegalEase does not obtain, receive or inspect it.

If the arrest record does not show the quantity, the affidavit is the way to supply it.

## 3. Fill in the facts only you can supply

Two things in this packet are yours, and this packet leaves them blank on purpose:

- **The quantity, or the number of plants grown inside your own primary residence for personal use.** If your conviction was for paraphernalia, it is what the item was used or intended to be used for. This is your sworn fact. Nobody supplies it for you and nobody estimates it.
- **Confirmation that every count on the docket can be erased.** If the record refers to more than one count, the petition does not reach it unless every count is entitled to erasure. Do not check this off until you have read the count list on your own record.

## 4. Sign, and swear the affidavit if you are using it

You sign the petition yourself. If you are using the affidavit route, you swear the affidavit.

The sources do not say whether a notary or another proper officer has to take that oath. Ask the clerk before you go.

## 5. There is no fee

No fee is charged for this petition.

## 6. Call the clerk before you file

Because there is no statewide form, the office you file in may have its own expectations about how the papers are captioned and laid out. Call first and ask.

## 7. File the petition

File the petition, with the arrest record or the sworn affidavit attached:

- in the Superior Court at the location where the conviction was effected; or
- at the location that has custody of the records; or
- if the conviction was in the Court of Common Pleas, Circuit Court, a municipal court or before a trial justice, in the Superior Court where venue would currently exist for criminal prosecution.

## 8. Service

Nothing in the statute was found requiring you to serve this on anyone. That has not been settled, so ask the clerk what that office expects when you call.

## 9. What happens after you file

The court will either order erasure without a hearing, or schedule a hearing.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- The arrest record does not establish the quantity and you cannot swear to it from your own knowledge.
- The case has other counts that may not be erasable.
- The court sets a hearing.
- The conviction falls outside the three categories this petition covers.
- The criminal case is still pending.
- You have an immigration matter.

A hearing that is purely procedural does not stop this packet from being prepared. Facts that are in dispute, or a hearing that is contested, mean it is time for a lawyer.
