# Expunging a Kansas city ordinance arrest record — what you need to do

This guide explains how to complete, sign and file your petition to expunge an
arrest on a city ordinance charge. Read it before you sign anything.

**This is not an official court form, and it is not legal advice.** A Kansas
municipal court decides whether your record is expunged. Nothing here guarantees
an outcome.

## Is this the right track for you?

This petition is for an **arrest** on a city ordinance charge. It is the wrong
document if:

- you were **convicted** of the ordinance violation, or you completed a
  **diversion** on it — that is a different Kansas statute; or
- you were arrested on a **state law** violation rather than a city ordinance —
  that runs through the district court under a different statute.

Check which one describes your case before going further.

## Step 1 — Check with your municipal court first. This one matters.

Kansas has hundreds of municipal courts and there is no single set of forms they
all use. Before filing, ask the municipal court of the city whose ordinance you
were charged under:

- Do you publish your own expungement form that I have to use instead?
- Do you have your own process?
- Does this city handle expungement under a charter ordinance?

Some Kansas cities run expungement under their own charter ordinance rather than
the state statute. Wichita, for example, proceeds under Charter Ordinance No. 224
and publishes its own Motion and Order. **If your city works that way, this
document may be the wrong one entirely.**

## Step 2 — Fill in the city

Your documents leave the city name blank on purpose, marked for you to confirm
with the court. We will not guess it — naming the wrong municipal court would
send your petition somewhere that cannot act on it.

## Step 3 — Know which ground you are relying on

There is **no waiting period** for this petition. You do not have to wait a
number of years. What matters is that one of these grounds fits your case:

- it was **mistaken identity**;
- a court found there was **no probable cause** for the arrest;
- you were found **not guilty**;
- the arrest was for a violation of certain ordinances adopted before 1 July
  2014; or
- expungement would be **in the best interests of justice**, and the charges have
  been dismissed, or no charges have been or are likely to be filed.

Your petition names the ground you rely on and asks the court to find it. It does
not say the court has already found it.

Be careful with the last ground. It depends partly on whether charges are
"likely to be filed" — a judgment about what a prosecutor may do in future. We
never assert that, and if there is any chance charges are still coming, speak to
an attorney first.

## Step 4 — Check the details, then sign by hand

Read every line and confirm your name and the case details against your own
records. Lines marked "to be confirmed" are values we will not guess — fill them
in from your records or ask the court clerk. Then sign and date by hand.

## Step 5 — What you write yourself

Where the petition relies on your circumstances, **you write that statement
yourself.** We do not write it for you.

## Step 6 — File it, and let the court do the notifying

File the petition and the proposed order with the municipal court. Then stop. You
do not serve anyone. When your petition is filed the court sets the hearing date
and gives notice to the prosecuting attorney and the arresting law enforcement
agency.

We cannot tell you what the prosecuting attorney or the arresting agency will do,
and we never state their position for them.

## About the cost

The municipal court **may** charge a fee as costs. We cannot tell you the amount
— our source establishes that a court may charge, but not how much — so no figure
appears anywhere in your documents. Ask the court.

**One important exception:** no fee may be charged to a person who was arrested
as a result of being a victim of identity theft. If that is your situation, tell
the court.

## What happens to your court file

Once expunged, the official court file is kept separate from other court records.
It can be seen only by a judge, designated court staff, the prosecuting attorney,
the arresting agency, or by anyone else the court specifically allows. It is not
destroyed.

## When to stop and get a lawyer

- Your city runs expungement under a charter ordinance.
- Charges from this arrest are still possible or still pending.
- You were convicted, or completed a diversion, rather than simply arrested.
- Your arrest was on a state law violation rather than a city ordinance.
