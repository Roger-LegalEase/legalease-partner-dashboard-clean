# Expunging a Kansas city ordinance conviction or diversion — what you need to do

This guide explains how to complete, sign and file your petition to expunge a
city ordinance conviction, or a diversion you completed on a city ordinance
charge. Read it before you sign anything.

**This is not an official court form, and it is not legal advice.** A Kansas
municipal court decides whether your record is expunged. Nothing here guarantees
an outcome.

## Step 1 — Check with your municipal court first. This one matters.

Kansas has hundreds of municipal courts and there is no single set of forms that
all of them use. Before you file this document, call or visit the municipal court
of the city whose ordinance you were convicted of violating, and ask:

- Do you publish your own expungement form that I have to use instead?
- Do you have your own process for expungement?
- Does this city handle expungement under a charter ordinance?

That last question matters. Some Kansas cities run expungement under their own
charter ordinance rather than under the state statute. Wichita, for example,
proceeds under Charter Ordinance No. 224 and publishes its own Motion and Order.
**If your city works that way, this document may be the wrong one entirely**, and
you should use what the court gives you.

## Step 2 — Fill in the city

Your documents leave the city name blank on purpose, marked for you to confirm
with the court. We will not guess it. Naming the wrong municipal court would send
your petition to a court that cannot act on it.

Write in the city whose ordinance you were convicted of violating — the same city
whose court handled your case.

## Step 3 — Work out how long you have had to wait

This is the part people get wrong, because the waiting period depends on what the
offence was. Counting from the later of finishing your sentence or being
discharged from probation, parole or a suspended sentence:

- **Three years** for most city ordinance convictions, and three years from the
  date you fulfilled the terms for a completed diversion.
- **No waiting period** for certain ordinance violations adopted before 1 July
  2014.
- **One year** for an ordinance violation equivalent to prostitution, where you
  can prove you were coerced by someone else.
- **Five years** for a group of driving- and vehicle-related offences, including
  vehicular homicide, driving while cancelled, suspended or revoked, failing to
  stop at an accident, and driving without liability insurance.
- **Five years** for a first drunk-driving-equivalent ordinance violation, and
  **ten years** for a second or later one.

If you are not certain which applies to you, talk to an attorney before filing.
Filing too early wastes the effort and the money.

## Step 4 — Check the details, then sign by hand

Read every line. Confirm your name, any other names you have used, and the case
details against your own court records. Some lines are left blank on purpose,
marked "to be confirmed" — the case number, for example. Fill those in from your
records or ask the court clerk.

Then sign and date the petition by hand.

## Step 5 — What you write yourself

The petition asks the court to find that your circumstances and behaviour warrant
expungement. **You write that statement yourself.** We do not write it for you,
and nobody else may write it for you.

## Step 6 — File it, and let the court do the notifying

File the petition and the proposed order with the municipal court. Then stop —
you do not serve anyone. The court sets the hearing and the court gives notice to
the prosecuting attorney and the arresting law enforcement agency. That is the
court's job, not yours.

We cannot tell you what the prosecuting attorney will do, and we never state
their position for them.

**About the fee:** we cannot tell you what this costs. Our source does not
establish a filing fee for this petition, so no amount appears anywhere in your
documents. Ask the municipal court, and do not pay anything on the strength of a
number you did not get from the court.

## Step 7 — What the court has to decide

The court must find three things before it orders expungement:

1. that you have not been convicted of a felony in the past two years, and that
   no such case is pending or about to be brought against you;
2. that your circumstances and behaviour warrant expungement; and
3. that expungement is consistent with the public welfare.

Your petition **asks** the court to find these. It does not say they are already
true — that is the court's decision to make.

## When to stop and get a lawyer

- Your city runs expungement under a charter ordinance.
- You have a felony conviction from the past two years, or a felony case pending
  or about to be brought.
- You are not sure which waiting period applies to you.
- Your case was in a Kansas district court rather than a municipal court. That is
  a different statute and a different filing.
- You were arrested but never convicted and never completed a diversion. That is
  also a different statute.

## One thing this track does not involve

There is no firearms finding here, because a city ordinance conviction is not a
felony. If you have read about firearms rights in connection with Kansas
expungement, that concerns district court cases, not this one.
