# How to complete and file your request to expunge a related case

Your expungement was granted. This request asks a court to do the same for a case, proceeding or agency matter that was connected to it - a seizure, a civil forfeiture, a petition for specialized driving privileges, or an administrative proceeding.

There is no fee for this request.

## 1. Get a certified copy of your original expungement order

This is the one thing you must have before you file.

Ask the clerk of the court that granted your expungement for a certified copy of the order. Not a photocopy - a certified copy.

You obtain this yourself. LegalEase does not obtain, receive or look at it.

## 2. Check the order against what you told us

Read the certified order and compare it with the answer you gave to "Which county granted your original expungement, and on what date?"

If they disagree, correct the packet before you file.

## 3. Write the part only you can write

One thing in this packet is yours, and the packet leaves it blank on purpose:

**How the related matter is connected to the case that was expunged.** Write it in your own words. Nobody writes it for you and nobody argues it for you - this packet formats what you write.

Say what happened and how the two matters are connected. Do not say the court has found them related. That is the court's decision, not yours and not ours.

## 4. Sign the request

You sign it yourself. Nothing in the sources says the request has to be notarised or sworn, so ask the clerk what that office expects when you call.

## 5. There is no fee

No fee is charged for this request, so there is nothing to pay and no fee waiver to apply for.

## 6. File it

File the request in a circuit or superior court in the county where the related matter happened.

File it under that matter's cause number where possible. If the county where it happened is not the same county that granted your expungement, the request still goes to the county where the related matter happened - the certified order simply comes from the other county's clerk.

Attach the certified copy of your original expungement order.

If you are not sure which court or clerk to file with, ask the clerk in the county where the related matter happened. Do not assume an address, a fee, or a local rule from another county - check the current local requirements with the court you are filing in.

## 7. What happens after you file

The court notifies the prosecuting attorney of that county. It may set a hearing, or it may grant the request without a hearing if the record conclusively establishes that you are entitled to it.

The sources do not settle whether you also have to serve the prosecuting attorney yourself. Ask the clerk when you call.

## Two things to know about Indiana records

- Records are not destroyed. In Indiana, expungement means records are sealed or restricted. The Office of Judicial Administration says plainly that court records are not deleted or destroyed.
- The case file you open with this request is public until the order is granted.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- The prosecutor objects or files a notice in opposition.
- A victim submits a statement in opposition.
- The court sets a hearing.
- The court signals that it does not think the related matter is connected to the expunged case.
- You have convictions in more than one county and the 365-day window is already partly used.
- A conviction is not yet eligible and you want to file now.
- You have already filed a Sections 2 through 5 petition.
- It is unclear which of Sections 2, 3, 4 or 5 applies to you, or the answer turns on whether an offence caused serious bodily injury.
- You are a sex or violent offender, or you are subject to registration.
- Fines, fees, costs or restitution are unpaid or in dispute.
- Charges are pending anywhere, or you are in a pretrial diversion programme.
- The record involves a commercial driver's licence.
- Immigration, firearm, licensing or CDL consequences are in play.
- You want to challenge the underlying conviction rather than clear the related matter.
