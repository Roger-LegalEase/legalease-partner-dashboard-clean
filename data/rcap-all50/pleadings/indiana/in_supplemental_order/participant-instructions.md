# How to complete and file your supplemental petition

Indiana's expungement law changes almost every year. When it changes in a way that gives more relief than you already have, you can go back to the court that granted your expungement and ask for that extra relief.

This packet is that petition.

## Before anything else: the change in the law

Everything here depends on one thing - which change in the law gives you more than you already have.

That is a legal question, not a form-filling question, and it is not settled in this packet. The blank stays blank until it has been answered for the current year. Do not fill it in from something you read online, and do not file this petition with that blank empty.

If you were told about this petition as part of a review after a legislative session, the change in the law should have been identified for you. If it was not, stop and ask.

## 1. Get a certified copy of your original expungement order

Ask the clerk of the court that granted your expungement for a certified copy of the order.

You obtain this yourself. LegalEase does not obtain, receive or look at it.

## 2. Check the order against what you told us

Read the certified order and compare it with your answer to "Which court granted your original expungement, and on what date?"

If they disagree, correct the packet before you file.

Your order has to have come first. This petition is for people whose expungement was granted before the law changed.

## 3. Write the part only you can write

Say, in your own words, what additional relief you are asking the court for. The law says the petition sets this out succinctly, so keep it short and specific.

Nobody writes it for you and nobody argues it for you - this packet formats what you write.

Do not say the court must grant it, or that you are entitled to it. Whether you were granted relief before the change in the law, and whether you are otherwise entitled to what the change provides, is for the court to find.

## 4. Sign the petition

You sign it yourself. Nothing in the sources says it has to be notarised, so ask the clerk what that office expects when you call.

## 5. Fees

Whether there is a filing fee for a supplemental petition has not been settled. Ask the clerk what the fee is, if any, and what to do if you cannot pay it. Do not assume it is free and do not assume a figure from another kind of filing.

## 6. File it

File the petition in the court that granted your original expungement.

Attach the certified copy of that order.

If you are unsure which court or clerk that is, the certified order will tell you. Do not assume an address or a local rule from another county - check the current local requirements with the court you are filing in.

## 7. Serve the prosecuting attorney

Serve a copy on the prosecuting attorney following the ordinary Indiana Trial Rules. Ask the clerk to confirm how that office expects service to be made.

Fill in the date and the method on the certificate of service yourself, after you have actually served it. Nothing in this packet fills that in for you.

## 8. What happens after you file

If the court finds you were granted relief before the change in the law and that you are otherwise entitled to what the change provides, it grants the petition consistent with that change.

## Two things to know about Indiana records

- Records are not destroyed. In Indiana, expungement means records are sealed or restricted. The Office of Judicial Administration says plainly that court records are not deleted or destroyed.
- The case file you open with this petition is public until the order is granted. You already have relief - weigh that before you file.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- Nobody has identified which change in the law gives you more relief.
- The prosecutor objects or files a notice in opposition.
- A victim submits a statement in opposition.
- The court sets a hearing.
- You have convictions in more than one county and the 365-day window is already partly used.
- A conviction is not yet eligible and you want to file now.
- You have already filed a Sections 2 through 5 petition.
- It is unclear which of Sections 2, 3, 4 or 5 applies to you, or the answer turns on whether an offence caused serious bodily injury.
- You are a sex or violent offender, or you are subject to registration.
- Fines, fees, costs or restitution are unpaid or in dispute.
- Charges are pending anywhere, or you are in a pretrial diversion programme.
- The record involves a commercial driver's licence.
- Immigration, firearm, licensing or CDL consequences are in play.
- You want to challenge the underlying conviction rather than add to the relief you have.
