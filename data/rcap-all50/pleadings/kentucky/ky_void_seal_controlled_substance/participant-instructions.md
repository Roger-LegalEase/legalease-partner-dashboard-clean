# How to complete and file your motion to void and seal

Kentucky calls this voiding and sealing. It is not expungement. Use those words when you talk to the clerk.

Your papers go into the criminal case you already have. There is no new case and no new case number.

## What is in this packet

- The motion asking the court to void the conviction and seal the records.
- A certificate of service, on the last page.

One more paper has to go with them, and it is not in this packet:

- **AOC-334, Order Voiding Conviction and Sealing Records.** This is the court's own official form — the order you are asking the judge to sign. Get it from the Kentucky Court of Justice legal forms page, form 334. You tender it with the motion. Nobody here writes it for you, because it is a court order and it is the court's form.

## 1. Check your dates against your own paperwork

You told us when you completed treatment, probation or your sentence. Before you file, check that answer against your own proof of completion. If the two disagree, correct the packet before it goes anywhere.

If you have proof of completion, keep it with the packet. Ask your treatment provider or probation officer for a completion letter, or ask the clerk for the discharge order. The statute does not require you to file it, but judges commonly expect to see it.

## 2. Read what you are signing

Two things in this packet are yours and yours alone:

- **Whether this was your first conviction for possession of a controlled substance in Kentucky.** That is your own statement of fact. Nobody supplies it for you and nobody works it out for you from a record. If anyone disagrees with you about it, stop and talk to a lawyer.
- **Which agencies are likely to hold records of this case.** That list comes from you.

## 3. Sign the motion

You sign the motion yourself, in the signature block. There is no special signing rule beyond ordinary practice.

**The motion is not sworn.** No notary is required. Do not have it notarized.

## 4. Make a copy for the prosecutor

Whoever prosecuted your case — the county attorney or the Commonwealth's attorney — gets a copy. Send it by mail or hand it to their office.

Then sign the certificate of service on the last page and fill in how and when you sent it. Sign that page only after you have actually sent the copy.

No summons is issued in this proceeding. Serving that copy yourself is how the prosecutor learns about the motion.

## 5. File it

File the motion with the circuit court clerk, in your original criminal case, and tender AOC-334 with it as the proposed order.

The clerk records this as a motion in the existing case and delivers it to the judge who decided your case.

## 6. There is no filing fee

There is no filing fee for this proceeding.

If a clerk asks you to pay one, do not pay and do not argue. Stop and talk to a lawyer.

## What sealing does, and what it does not do

Sealing is not expungement. The records still exist. They are sealed. Some records are excepted by KRS 27A.099 and are not sealed at all.

There is one more thing worth knowing before you decide. If the court voids this conviction, a misdemeanor can then be expunged under KRS 431.078 without the usual five-year wait. Voiding and sealing first, expungement second — in that order, and only after the court has entered an order voiding the conviction.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- Anyone disputes that this was your first conviction for possession of a controlled substance.
- You are trying to decide between voiding-and-sealing and expungement. That choice has lifetime consequences.
- You are not a United States citizen. A voided conviction may still count as a conviction for immigration purposes.
- A clerk demands a filing fee.
- You have ever had a possession charge dismissed after completing a deferred prosecution programme.
- You have ever had a conviction voided under this statute before. It can only happen once.
