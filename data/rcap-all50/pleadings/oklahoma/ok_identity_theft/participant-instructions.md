# How to complete and file your Oklahoma petition to clear a record that is not yours

This packet asks an Oklahoma district court to expunge (seal) a record that was created in your name through identity theft, or that otherwise identifies the wrong person — where someone else used your name or identifying information without your consent.

These instructions are for you only. Do not file this page with the court.

## Before you pay anything

- Oklahoma now seals some records automatically, without a petition and without cost. Before filing a paid petition, check first whether your record is one Oklahoma seals without a petition. If it is, you should not need this paperwork.
- Get a certified copy of the court record and the order or judgment showing how the case ended, from the district court clerk in the county where the case was filed.
- Request your own criminal history record from the Oklahoma State Bureau of Investigation (OSBI). It shows what the state holds, which arrests exist in the county, and whether anything has already been sealed.

## Complete the petition

- Check every pre-filled answer against the certified court record and your OSBI record, and correct the paperwork if they disagree.
- Describe the offense exactly in the words used on the record, not by any list or category number.
- Fill in the case number and the county exactly as they appear on the court papers. Where the paperwork shows a bracketed item such as the docket number, copy the correct value from your court record or confirm it with the court clerk.
- If you have more than one arrest in the same county, they may be combined in one petition. Arrests in different counties need separate petitions filed in each county.

## Sign

- Sign and verify the petition yourself. Ask the court clerk whether your county requires the signature to be notarized.
- Leave the judge's signature line on the proposed order blank. The court signs it.
- The proposed order must name every agency that holds the records — the prosecuting agency, the arresting agency, OSBI, and any other agency with records of the case.

## File and pay

1. File the petition and the proposed order with the district court clerk in the county where the arrest information is located.
2. Ask the clerk what the court filing fee is. No official amount is published for this petition, so do not pay any amount you have not confirmed with the clerk.
3. Clearing the court record is free. Clearing the arrest record requires a $150 OSBI processing fee, paid by cashier's check or money order only — OSBI does not accept personal checks — and local law enforcement agencies may charge their own fees.

## The hearing and what an order does

- The court sets a hearing and gives 30 days' notice to the prosecuting agency, the arresting agency, OSBI, and any other agency holding records. You attend the hearing.
- If granted, the records are sealed from public view. Some records are fully sealed; others remain available to law enforcement. Sealing is not physical destruction, a sealed record can be unsealed later on a court finding, and an Oklahoma order does not reach federal, tribal, military, or out-of-state records, does not restore firearm rights, and does not fix immigration issues.
- After the order, get certified copies of the signed order for OSBI and the local agencies.
- If anyone disputes that the record is about the wrong person, if the prosecutor objects, if a tribal court record exists for the same events, or if anything above does not match your situation — stop and talk to a lawyer or legal aid before going further.
