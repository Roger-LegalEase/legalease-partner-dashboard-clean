# How to Complete and Send Your Tennessee Request Asking the District Attorney General to Prepare Your Illegal Registration or Voting Expunction Petition

These instructions are for you, the person sending the letter. **Do not send this page** — it is a checklist.

## What this packet is

This packet is the letter you send to the office of the district attorney general for the judicial district of the convicting court, asking that office to prepare the petition and proposed order to clear a conviction for **illegal registration or voting**.

**This letter is not the petition.** Tennessee law requires the district attorney general's office to prepare the petition and the proposed order and give them to you to file. This packet does not draft either of them and does not pretend to. What it gives you is your own written request and your own record of the facts you rely on, so that office can make its own determination.

## Read this first

**Nothing here is automatic.** A charge that was dismissed, and a diversion you completed, stay on background checks until somebody files a petition. This is the single most common Tennessee misunderstanding, and it is why this packet exists.

**There is no official Tennessee form for any of this.** Both state form indices were checked directly and neither publishes an expunction form of any kind. What varies locally is the clerk's own paperwork, so ask your clerk before you file.

**Check the section number before you file.** The chapter was renumbered in 2025 and again on codification in 2026, and clerks and published guidance may still be using the old numbers — the state's own expunction page still reasons from a section that has been nothing but definitions since April 2025. Your packet carries the current citation. Confirm it with the clerk or the district attorney general's office before you file, because a petition citing the wrong section can be rejected either way.

## What the law requires here

- **Fifteen years** must have elapsed since you completed the sentence. This is the longest waiting period in the whole chapter — three times what applies to misdemeanours and the lowest felony class, and half again what applies to the middle felony classes.
- **The clock starts when the sentence is complete**, which means every fine, restitution amount, court cost and other assessment paid, every term of imprisonment or probation completed, and every condition of supervised or unsupervised release met. If anything is outstanding, the sentence is not complete and the clock has not started.
- The offence must have occurred **before** any conviction for an offence that is not eligible to be cleared.
- You must not previously have been granted expunction under this subsection or the two related ones.

**This subsection reaches one named offence and no other.** If it is not clear that your conviction was under that exact section rather than some other elections or fraud provision, get legal help rather than guessing.

**This is not restoration of your voting rights.** Restoration is a separate remedy under separate machinery. How the two interact was not established from any official source, and nothing in this packet claims anything about it. If restoration is what you actually want — and for most people on this route it is — ask a lawyer about that separately.

## Before you send it

1. **Work out your sentence completion date** and count fifteen years from it.
2. **Get the judgment** showing the offence and its exact statutory section, and whatever record you hold showing when the sentence was completed. Enclose copies.
3. **Get your state control number early.** It comes from your own criminal history, and the order cannot be completed without it. Fill it into the bracket before you file.
4. **Ask the office whether it has its own request form.**
5. **Sign and date the letter.** Notarisation is not required.

## After you send it

- If the office prepares the petition and the proposed order, **you** file them with the clerk of the convicting court and pay the clerk's fee. The amount is set by a fee statute that is not published on any official state site, so no figure appears anywhere in this packet. Ask the clerk.
- The clerk then serves the petition on the district attorney general. **You do not serve anybody.**
- That office may submit recommendations within 60 days, with a copy to you, and may file evidence under seal. The court cannot rule sooner than 61 days after service.
- A certificate from the state bureau must be attached before an order can be entered. That is the court's own paperwork, not yours.
- If a petition is denied, there is no further petition for at least two years.

## What Tennessee expunction does not reach

It does not reach federal, military, tribal or out-of-state records. If you have a security clearance question or an immigration matter, get legal advice before you file anything.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if there is any question whether the conviction was under that exact section rather than another elections or fraud provision; you want your voting rights restored, which is a separate remedy; the office declines to prepare the petition or indicates it will oppose; eligibility is disputed; you have any other conviction anywhere; or you have unpaid court costs, fines, restitution or other assessments. If self-help stops here, you keep this letter and your record of the facts — take them with you.
