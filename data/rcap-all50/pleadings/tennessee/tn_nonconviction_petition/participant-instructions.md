# How to Complete and File Your Tennessee Petition to Clear a Charge That Did Not End in a Conviction

These instructions are for you, the person filing. **Do not file this page with the court** — it is a checklist, not a court document.

## What this packet is

This packet is a petition asking the court that handled your case to remove and destroy all public records of a charge that did **not** end in a conviction — a dismissal, a no true bill, an arrest and release without charge, a nolle prosequi, a not-guilty verdict, or one of the section's other listed grounds.

## Read this first

**Nothing here is automatic.** A charge that was dismissed, and a diversion you completed, stay on background checks until somebody files a petition. This is the single most common Tennessee misunderstanding, and it is why this packet exists.

**There is no official Tennessee form for any of this.** Both state form indices were checked directly and neither publishes an expunction form of any kind. What varies locally is the clerk's own paperwork, so ask your clerk before you file.

**Check the section number before you file.** The chapter was renumbered in 2025 and again on codification in 2026, and clerks and published guidance may still be using the old numbers — the state's own expunction page still reasons from a section that has been nothing but definitions since April 2025. Your packet carries the current citation. Confirm it with the clerk or the district attorney general's office before you file, because a petition citing the wrong section can be rejected either way.

## This route is free

The statute says the records must be removed and destroyed **without cost to you**, and the legislature's stated intent is that no fee is ever charged where a charge was dismissed for a reason other than completing a diversion programme. **If you are being asked to pay for this route, stop.** That is a signal something is wrong — either about the fee or about which route you are on.

## Before you file

1. **Confirm the charge did not end in a conviction on any count.** If any count in the case produced a conviction, this is not your route: a mixed case goes to the partial-removal analysis instead.
2. **Confirm the dismissal did not come from completing a diversion programme.** If it did, a different route applies and that one is not free.
3. **Confirm the court has a record of your case.** If your arrest never produced a court file at all, a different section applies — and that one carries a mandatory clerk's fee with no waiver.
4. **Get your state control number early.** It comes from your own criminal history, and the order cannot be completed without it. Fill it into the bracket before you file.
5. **File separately in each county.** If you have cases in more than one county, each needs its own petition. Ask each clerk whether separate paperwork is needed for multiple charges.

- **Leave the court's blanks alone.** The judge's name, any hearing date and the case-number stamp are supplied by the court after you file, not by you.
- **Fill in every item shown in [BRACKETS]** from your own records before you file.
- **You sign it.** Notarisation is not required by the governing section, but county practice can differ — ask your clerk whether they want it notarised.

## Filing, service and what happens next

- The clerk serves the petition on the district attorney general. **You do not serve anybody.**
- No certificate from the state bureau is needed on this route unless your case came through diversion.
- After the judge signs the order, the court sends it to the state bureau **within 30 days**. Nothing changes on a background check until it arrives, so do not expect an immediate result.

## What Tennessee expunction does not reach

It does not reach federal, military, tribal or out-of-state records. If you have a security clearance question or an immigration matter, get legal advice before you file anything.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if there is any dispute with the district attorney general about eligibility; you have any other conviction anywhere, including federal or out-of-state; you have unpaid court costs, fines or restitution; there is any DUI, sexual offence or registration matter, or any domestic violence or child-victim offence; you have federal, military, tribal or out-of-state records; there is an immigration or security-clearance question; any count in the case resulted in a conviction; the dismissal followed a diversion programme; anyone is asking you to pay for this route; or it is disputed or unclear whether any court case or court file ever existed.
