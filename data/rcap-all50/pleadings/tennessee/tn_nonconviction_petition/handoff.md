## Mechanism

Created by Public Chapter 268 of 2025, effective 2025-04-24, and codified by Public Chapter 608 effective 2026-03-25. All public records of a person charged with a misdemeanour or felony must, upon petition by that person to the court having jurisdiction in the previous action, be removed and destroyed without cost to the person where the charge was dismissed other than by diversion completion, a no true bill was returned, the person was arrested and released without being charged, a nolle prosequi was entered, a verdict of not guilty was returned, or on the section's further grounds including qualifying implied-consent dismissals, abatement by death, mistaken identity and racial-justice-protest convictions. Subsection (a)(2) extends the same no-cost treatment to a successfully defended order of protection and to expired bonds under § 38-3-109. There is no waiting period. The TBI certificate under § 40-32-102(c) is not required for a § 40-32-106 expunction unless the expunction resulted from successful completion of pretrial or judicial diversion. Under § 40-32-110 expunction restores the person, in contemplation of law, to the status occupied before arrest, indictment, information, trial and conviction, and under § 40-32-102(e) the expunged records are confidential and unlawful release is a Class A misdemeanour.

## Open counsel flags

1. Expunction is not automatic. A dismissed charge or a completed diversion stays on background checks until a petition is filed. This is the most common Tennessee misunderstanding and the packet leads with it.
2. No official Tennessee participant form exists. Both state form indices were inspected directly and neither publishes an expunction form of any kind; this is a negative finding established from the authoritative index, not a failure to search. A controlled custom instrument is therefore the only available strategy, and it is not represented as an official form.
3. Chapter 32 was reorganized by Public Chapter 268 of 2025 and codified by Public Chapter 608 effective 2026-03-25, and clerks and published guidance may still use the pre-reorganization numbering — the state's own expunction page still reasons from a section that has been nothing but definitions since 2025-04-24. A petition citing the wrong section is a rejection risk either way, so the packet carries the current citation and instructs the participant to confirm it with the clerk or the district attorney general before filing.
4. Tennessee expunction reaches no federal, military, tribal or out-of-state record, and a diversion guilty plea may remain visible on a clearance check.
5. The governing section prescribes no caption form and no contents for the participant's instrument. The caption follows the ordinary criminal-case style of the court having jurisdiction, with the filer designated as the source designates them. What individual clerks and courts in fact expect is not established and no county practice was surveyed; the caption is a visual- and counsel-review item.
6. This route is free. Section 40-32-106(a)(1) requires the records to be removed and destroyed without cost to the person, and the general assembly's stated intent is that no fee ever be charged where the charge was dismissed for a reason other than successful diversion completion. A participant being asked to pay for this route is a stop condition.
7. A separate petition is required in each county where the person has a case; the state's guidance directs filers to check with the clerk because separate forms may be required for multiple charges.
8. No certificate from the state bureau is required on this route unless the expunction resulted from successful completion of diversion.
9. This route no longer reaches an arrest for which the court with jurisdiction has no history within its records. That population is governed by a different section and a different, fee-bearing vehicle with a clerk record-search and certification step that has no analogue here. The two mechanisms are not merged.
10. Participant education point recorded in the pin: a person being charged with a crime must be informed that if the charges are dismissed or nolle prossed, or no charges are filed, they are entitled on request to removal and destruction of all public records without cost. The current text of that provision could not be retrieved from an official source, so the packet names the right and its statutory home without quoting the provision.
11. Any count in the case that resulted in a conviction takes the participant off this route and onto the partial-removal analysis.
12. The state control number is a manual-completion item read from the participant's own criminal history. It stays a bracket in generated output; the order cannot be completed without it, and the participant is told to capture it early.
13. The shared renderer's fixed section heading "VI. VERIFICATION" is a Pennsylvania-derived artefact of the frozen renderer contract, not a Tennessee drafting choice. Nothing in the governing section requires verification. This is a visual- and counsel-review item.
14. No fee figure is quoted anywhere in this packet. The source records no retrievable amount, and the filing-fee field is left null rather than filled with a circulating number.
15. No fee-waiver instrument is included and the fee-waiver field is left null, because the source establishes no waiver mechanism for this route.
16. The governing section states no waiting period. None is pleaded, none is imported from a neighbouring section, and the waiting-period field is left null.
17. Notarization is not prescribed by the governing section and no verification statute exists to cite, so verificationStatute.citation is left null and the signature block carries no penalty citation. County practice may differ, and the packet directs the participant to ask the clerk.

The AOC publishes no expunction form at all, confirmed on 2026-08-06, and directs filers to check with the court clerk because separate forms may be required for multiple charges. That makes local clerk paperwork the only form variable, and it is county-specific rather than statewide. No county form is adopted or promoted by this memo.

T.C.A. § 40-6-204 could not be confirmed from any official Tennessee source and is not asserted; no official act retrieved cites, quotes or amends it. T.C.A. §§ 40-15-102 through 40-15-107 are the pretrial diversion scheme, confirmed by official cross-reference from the enacted text of Public Chapter 268 and by the TBI. None of the three is printed in the Code Bill extract and none is claimed to be. None is the vehicle for a participant-facing record-clearing route: the record-clearing consequence of completing pretrial diversion lives in Chapter 32 at § 40-32-106(d), not in Chapter 15. These do not reinstate the statewide codification blocker, which the captain receipt cleared.
- (release_blocker, governing_mechanism) Current authority reveals statutory record-clearing routes that are outside the adopted nine-slot mechanism inventory and are therefore not built here. Public Chapter 268 created T.C.A. § 40-32-107(c), expunction after a conviction for illegal registration or voting under § 2-19-107 on a fifteen-year wait, and § 40-32-109, expunction of an arrest record where the court with jurisdiction has no record of the arrest, with a clerk search and certification. Public Chapter 719 rewrote the § 40-32-107(d) post-pardon route, replacing an open-textured judicial nonviolent-crime finding with a closed seven-item felony exclusion list, which substantially broadens it. Public Chapter 1061 added § 40-32-107(e), the recovery-court route, effective 2026-07-01 with no offence-date limitation. Each petitions under § 40-32-108. Adding any of them would change the adopted denominator, which a normalization worker may not do, so they are recorded for a captain-authorized inventory amendment instead.
- (release_blocker, filing_process) Which participants presenting on the § 40-32-106(a)(1)(C) arrested-and-released-without-charge ground in fact have a court file and which do not. That ground reaches exactly the population most likely to have no court record, so the screening question is asked, but Tennessee publishes no way for a participant to confirm the answer before filing and no county practice was surveyed. The consequence is a routing risk between a free route and a fee-bearing one rather than a doubt about either mechanism.
