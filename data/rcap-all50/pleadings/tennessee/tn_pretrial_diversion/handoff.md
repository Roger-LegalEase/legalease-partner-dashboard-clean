## Mechanism

Prosecution is suspended under a memorandum of understanding with the district attorney general; the person does not plead guilty and is not found guilty. On successful completion the charges are dismissed, and under T.C.A. § 40-32-106(d)(1) the person may then petition for expunction of the public records where the charges were dismissed as a result of successful completion of a pretrial diversion programme pursuant to §§ 40-15-102 through 40-15-107. The provision is permissive as to filing and mandatory as to nothing: a petition is required and expunction is not automatic. Section 40-32-106(d)(3) requires the appropriate clerk's fee under § 8-21-401, so unlike a straight dismissal this route is not free. Section 40-32-106(d)(2) bars expunction where the diverted offence was a sexual offence or a violent sexual offence as defined in § 40-39-202. The TBI certificate under § 40-32-102(c) is required here, because the exemption for § 40-32-106 expunctions is expressly carved back where the expunction resulted from successful completion of pretrial diversion.

## Open counsel flags

1. Expunction is not automatic. A dismissed charge or a completed diversion stays on background checks until a petition is filed. This is the most common Tennessee misunderstanding and the packet leads with it.
2. No official Tennessee participant form exists. Both state form indices were inspected directly and neither publishes an expunction form of any kind; this is a negative finding established from the authoritative index, not a failure to search. A controlled custom instrument is therefore the only available strategy, and it is not represented as an official form.
3. Chapter 32 was reorganized by Public Chapter 268 of 2025 and codified by Public Chapter 608 effective 2026-03-25, and clerks and published guidance may still use the pre-reorganization numbering — the state's own expunction page still reasons from a section that has been nothing but definitions since 2025-04-24. A petition citing the wrong section is a rejection risk either way, so the packet carries the current citation and instructs the participant to confirm it with the clerk or the district attorney general before filing.
4. Tennessee expunction reaches no federal, military, tribal or out-of-state record, and a diversion guilty plea may remain visible on a clearance check.
5. The governing section prescribes no caption form and no contents for the participant's instrument. The caption follows the ordinary criminal-case style of the court having jurisdiction, with the filer designated as the source designates them. What individual clerks and courts in fact expect is not established and no county practice was surveyed; the caption is a visual- and counsel-review item.
6. A petition is still required after diversion. Completing diversion dismisses the case but leaves it visible until a separate petition is filed; the source records this as the second most common Tennessee misunderstanding.
7. This route is not free, which is the asymmetry a participant must be warned about: diversion is a non-conviction outcome, but a straight dismissal is free and this is not. The amount is set by a separate fee statute whose current text was not retrievable from an official source, so no figure is quoted anywhere in this packet. The widely circulated figure appears only on a superseded page and does not appear in the enacted text.
8. A sexual offence or violent sexual offence as defined by the registration statute can be diverted but cannot be expunged. A person can complete diversion on such an offence and still be unable to clear the record, and the packet says so plainly rather than selling a petition.
9. The certificate from the state bureau is required on this route and an order must not be entered without it. It is a court instrument completed by court staff; this packet does not generate it, complete it, or assert it has been obtained.
10. The order is marked to the suspension-of-prosecution provision for a pretrial diversion case, which is how the bureau records it. That marking is part of the court's instrument, not the participant's.
11. No indigency or fee-waiver provision appears in the reorganized sections. The bureau operates an unpublished indigency procedure for its own separate certificate fee, using a form signed by the judge that it supplies on request and does not publish; that reaches the certificate fee and not the clerk's fee. No waiver mechanism for the clerk's fee was identified.
12. The enacted text uses two different ranges for the pretrial diversion scheme within one act. Whether that is a scrivener's error, and whether codification corrected it, was not established.
