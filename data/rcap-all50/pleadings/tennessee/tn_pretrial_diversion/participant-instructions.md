# How to Complete and File Your Tennessee Petition to Clear a Case After You Completed Pretrial Diversion

These instructions are for you, the person filing. **Do not file this page with the court** — it is a checklist, not a court document.

## What this packet is

This packet is a petition asking the court to remove and destroy the public records of charges that were dismissed because you **successfully completed a pretrial diversion programme** — the arrangement where prosecution is suspended under a memorandum of understanding with the district attorney general, you do not plead guilty, and you are not found guilty.

## Read this first

**Nothing here is automatic.** A charge that was dismissed, and a diversion you completed, stay on background checks until somebody files a petition. This is the single most common Tennessee misunderstanding, and it is why this packet exists.

**There is no official Tennessee form for any of this.** Both state form indices were checked directly and neither publishes an expunction form of any kind. What varies locally is the clerk's own paperwork, so ask your clerk before you file.

**Check the section number before you file.** The chapter was renumbered in 2025 and again on codification in 2026, and clerks and published guidance may still be using the old numbers — the state's own expunction page still reasons from a section that has been nothing but definitions since April 2025. Your packet carries the current citation. Confirm it with the clerk or the district attorney general's office before you file, because a petition citing the wrong section can be rejected either way.

## Two warnings specific to this route

**Completing diversion did not clear your record.** The case was dismissed, but it stays visible until this petition is filed and granted. That is the second most common Tennessee misunderstanding.

**This route is not free.** A clerk's fee applies. That is the asymmetry to know about: diversion is a non-conviction outcome, but a straight dismissal is free and this is not. The amount is set by a separate fee statute whose current text is not published on any official state site, so this packet quotes no figure. Ask your clerk. A number you may find circulating online appears only on a superseded page and is not in the enacted law.

There is also no fee-waiver provision in these sections for the clerk's fee. The state bureau operates an unpublished indigency procedure for its own separate certificate fee, using a form the judge signs that it supplies on request — but that is the certificate fee, not the clerk's fee.

## Before you file

1. **Confirm your diversion was successfully completed.** If the diversionary period was not completed, the sentence goes into effect and the offence is not cleared.
2. **Check the offence type.** A sexual offence or violent sexual offence as defined by the registration statute **can be diverted but cannot be cleared.** If that is your offence, this petition will not help you, and you should know that now rather than after you pay.
3. **Get your state control number early.** It comes from your own criminal history, and the order cannot be completed without it. Fill it into the bracket before you file.
4. **Ask the clerk what the fee is** before you file.

- **Leave the court's blanks alone.** The judge's name, any hearing date and the case-number stamp are supplied by the court after you file, not by you.
- **Fill in every item shown in [BRACKETS]** from your own records before you file.
- **You sign it.** Notarisation is not required by the governing section, but county practice can differ — ask your clerk whether they want it notarised.

## Filing, service and what happens next

- The clerk serves the petition on the district attorney general. **You do not serve anybody.**
- On this route a certificate from the state bureau **is** required, and the order must not be entered without it. That certificate is the court's own paperwork, completed by court staff — not yours, and not part of this packet.
- After the judge signs the order, the court sends it to the state bureau **within 30 days**.

## What Tennessee expunction does not reach

It does not reach federal, military, tribal or out-of-state records. If you have a security clearance question or an immigration matter, get legal advice before you file anything.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if the offence was a sexual offence or a violent sexual offence as defined by the registration statute — it could be diverted but it cannot be cleared; the diversion was not successfully completed or completion is disputed; your goal involves federal employment or a security clearance, since a judicial diversion guilty plea may remain visible there; there is any dispute with the district attorney general; you have any other conviction anywhere; or you have unpaid court costs, fines or restitution.
