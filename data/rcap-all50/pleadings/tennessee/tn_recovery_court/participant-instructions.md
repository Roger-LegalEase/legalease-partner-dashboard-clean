# How to Complete and Send Your Tennessee Request Asking the District Attorney General to Prepare Your Recovery Court Expunction Petition

These instructions are for you, the person sending the letter. **Do not send this page** — it is a checklist.

## What this packet is

This packet is the letter you send to the office of the district attorney general for the judicial district of the convicting court, asking that office to prepare the petition and proposed order under the recovery court provision that took effect on 1 July 2026.

**This letter is not the petition.** Tennessee law requires the district attorney general's office to prepare the petition and the proposed order and give them to you to file. This packet does not draft either of them and does not pretend to. What it gives you is your own written request and your own record of the facts you rely on, so that office can make its own determination.

## Read this first

**Nothing here is automatic.** A charge that was dismissed, and a diversion you completed, stay on background checks until somebody files a petition. This is the single most common Tennessee misunderstanding, and it is why this packet exists.

**There is no official Tennessee form for any of this.** Both state form indices were checked directly and neither publishes an expunction form of any kind. What varies locally is the clerk's own paperwork, so ask your clerk before you file.

**Check the section number before you file.** The chapter was renumbered in 2025 and again on codification in 2026, and clerks and published guidance may still be using the old numbers — the state's own expunction page still reasons from a section that has been nothing but definitions since April 2025. Your packet carries the current citation. Confirm it with the clerk or the district attorney general's office before you file, because a petition citing the wrong section can be rejected either way.

## Read this before anything else: what this route does NOT do

**It does not clear a driving-under-the-influence conviction.** That conviction is itself ineligible, and the provision expressly says the court shall not grant relief where the offence you want cleared involves a motor vehicle and the use of alcohol or a controlled substance. That is an absolute bar even if you meet every other condition.

What this provision actually does is **lift a bar**. Normally, a conviction that is ineligible to be cleared blocks you from clearing offences that came after it. This provision lets a person who carries **one** such conviction clear a **different**, otherwise-eligible offence committed **at least ten years after** it.

If what you want is the driving conviction itself cleared, this is not that route, and no route in this packet does it.

## What the law requires here

- The offence you want cleared is itself eligible to be cleared, and does not involve a motor vehicle and the use of alcohol or a controlled substance.
- It occurred **at least ten years after** your conviction under the driving-under-the-influence section. This is an interval between two events, not a wait from when you finished your sentence.
- Every requirement of the sentence for that offence is fulfilled — every fine, restitution amount, court cost and other assessment paid, every term of imprisonment or probation completed, every condition of release met.
- You successfully completed a **certified recovery court programme**.
- You have not previously been granted expunction under the related subsections.
- You have **not more than one** conviction under the driving-under-the-influence section.

**You get no presumption.** This subsection is named neither in the rebuttable presumption that helps some petitioners nor in the violence-weighing direction that applies to pardon petitioners. The court simply weighs your interest against the best interests of justice and public safety.

## Before you send it

1. **Get both judgments** — the one for the offence you want cleared, showing its exact section and offence date, and the one for the earlier driving conviction, showing its date. You need both dates to show the ten-year interval. If you cannot establish them, go to the clerks of both courts first.
2. **Find whatever the recovery court programme gave you on completion.** What document proves completion, and who issues it, was not established from any official source, so enclose what you have and do not claim it is something it may not be.
3. **Get your state control number early.** It comes from your own criminal history, and the order cannot be completed without it. Fill it into the bracket before you file.
4. **Ask the office whether it has its own request form.**
5. **Sign and date the letter.** Notarisation is not required.

## After you send it

- If the office prepares the petition and the proposed order, **you** file them with the clerk of the convicting court and pay the clerk's fee. The amount is set by a fee statute that is not published on any official state site, so no figure appears anywhere in this packet. Ask the clerk.
- The clerk then serves the petition on the district attorney general. **You do not serve anybody.**
- That office may submit recommendations within 60 days, with a copy to you, and may file evidence under seal. The court cannot rule sooner than 61 days after service.
- A certificate from the state bureau must be attached before an order can be entered. That is the court's own paperwork, not yours.
- If a petition is denied, there is no further petition for at least two years.

## What Tennessee expunction does not reach

It does not reach federal, military, tribal or out-of-state records. If you have a security clearance question or an immigration matter, get legal advice before you file anything.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if you want the driving-under-the-influence conviction itself cleared — the provision forbids it; the offence you want cleared involves a motor vehicle and the use of alcohol or a controlled substance; you have more than one conviction under that section; the ten-year interval cannot be established from the judgments; the office declines to prepare the petition or indicates it will oppose; eligibility is disputed; you have any other conviction anywhere; or you have unpaid court costs, fines, restitution or other assessments. If self-help stops here, you keep this letter and your record of the facts — take them with you.
