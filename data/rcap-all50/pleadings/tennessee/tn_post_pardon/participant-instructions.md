# How to Complete and Send Your Tennessee Request Asking the District Attorney General to Prepare Your Pardon Expunction Petition

These instructions are for you, the person sending the letter. **Do not send this page** — it is a checklist.

## What this packet is

You received a pardon from the Governor. **The pardon by itself did not clear your record** — it opened the door to clearing it. This packet is the letter you send to the office of the district attorney general for the judicial district of the convicting court, asking that office to prepare the petition and the proposed order.

**This letter is not the petition.** Tennessee law requires the district attorney general's office to prepare the petition and the proposed order and give them to you to file. This packet does not draft either of them and does not pretend to. What it gives you is your own written request and your own record of the facts you rely on, so that office can make its own determination.

## Read this first

**Nothing here is automatic.** A charge that was dismissed, and a diversion you completed, stay on background checks until somebody files a petition. This is the single most common Tennessee misunderstanding, and it is why this packet exists.

**There is no official Tennessee form for any of this.** Both state form indices were checked directly and neither publishes an expunction form of any kind. What varies locally is the clerk's own paperwork, so ask your clerk before you file.

**Check the section number before you file.** The chapter was renumbered in 2025 and again on codification in 2026, and clerks and published guidance may still be using the old numbers — the state's own expunction page still reasons from a section that has been nothing but definitions since April 2025. Your packet carries the current citation. Confirm it with the clerk or the district attorney general's office before you file, because a petition citing the wrong section can be rejected either way.

## What the law requires here

Three things, and only three. They are cumulative and they are the whole list:

1. The judge finds your conviction was **not** for one of seven named felonies, or for an attempt, conspiracy, facilitation or solicitation to commit one of them. The seven are first degree murder, second degree murder, especially aggravated kidnapping, aggravated child abuse, especially aggravated robbery, commission of an act of terrorism, and a sexual offence requiring registration as a sexual offender or violent sexual offender or any sexual offence involving a minor.
2. You petitioned for and received a **positive vote of the board of parole** to receive a pardon.
3. You **received a pardon** from the Governor.

**Nothing else is required by this subsection.** There is no waiting period, no sentence-completion checklist, no bar for a previous expunction, and no requirement that the offence predate some other conviction. Those conditions exist in neighbouring subsections and they do not apply here. If anyone tells you otherwise, they may be reading the wrong subsection.

**One thing changed in 2026 that matters.** Violence used to be a bar. It is now a factor the court weighs on the merits, and a petitioner on this subsection gets **no rebuttable presumption** that the petition should be granted. How courts actually weigh it is not established — and it is the factor that decides these petitions. Your letter states your account without arguing it.

## Before you send it

1. **Get a copy of your pardon** and of the record of the board of parole vote, and enclose copies.
2. **Get a copy of the judgment** showing the offence and its exact statutory section, and enclose a copy.
3. **Get your state control number early.** It comes from your own criminal history, and the order cannot be completed without it. Fill it into the bracket before you file.
4. **Ask the office whether it has its own request form.** No statewide form exists and whether any individual office publishes one is unknown. If yours does, its form governs over this letter.
5. **Sign and date the letter.** Notarisation is not required.

## After you send it

- If the office prepares the petition and the proposed order, **you** file them with the clerk of the convicting court and pay the clerk's fee. The amount is set by a fee statute that is not published on any official state site, so no figure appears anywhere in this packet. Ask the clerk.
- The clerk then serves the petition on the district attorney general. **You do not serve anybody.**
- That office may submit recommendations within 60 days, with a copy to you, and may file evidence under seal. The court cannot rule sooner than 61 days after service.
- A certificate from the state bureau must be attached before an order can be entered. That is the court's own paperwork, not yours.
- If a petition is denied, there is no further petition for at least two years.

## What Tennessee expunction does not reach

It does not reach federal, military, tribal or out-of-state records. If you have a security clearance question or an immigration matter, get legal advice before you file anything.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if the office declines to prepare the petition or indicates it will oppose; eligibility is disputed by that office; your conviction may fall within one of the seven excluded categories, or within an attempt, conspiracy, facilitation or solicitation form of one; the violence question is genuinely contested; you do not yet hold a pardon — the clemency application is a separate proceeding before the board of parole and the Governor and is not part of this; you have any other conviction anywhere; or you have unpaid court costs, fines, restitution or other assessments. If self-help stops here, you keep this letter and your record of the facts — take them with you.
