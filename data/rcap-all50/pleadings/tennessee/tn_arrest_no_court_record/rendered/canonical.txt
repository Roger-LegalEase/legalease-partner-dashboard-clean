PETITION FOR EXPUNCTION OF AN ARREST RECORD WHERE THE COURT HAS NO HISTORY OF THE ARREST, PURSUANT TO T.C.A. § 40-32-109

STATUTORY AUTHORITY

T.C.A. § 40-32-109(a) — A person whose arrest record exists, but for whom the court with jurisdiction over the offence for which the person was arrested has no history of the arrest within the court's records, may petition that court. The subsection uses the permissive 'may petition'. Upon filing, the clerk serves the petition on the district attorney general for that judicial district.
T.C.A. § 40-32-109(b) — Both the petitioner and the district attorney general may file evidence with the court.
T.C.A. § 40-32-109(c) — The clerk's office searches the court's records and certifies to the court whether there is any history of the arrest.
T.C.A. § 40-32-109(d) — The court reviews the clerk's certification and all evidence and may enter the order if it finds there is no history of the arrest within the court's record.
T.C.A. § 40-32-109(e) — A person petitioning under the section must be charged the appropriate clerk's fee.
T.C.A. § 40-32-109(f) — Applies the chapter's restoration effect: the petitioner is entitled to have all public records destroyed, is restored in contemplation of law to the status occupied before the arrest, must not suffer adverse effects or collateral disabilities by virtue of the offence, and is not guilty of perjury for failing to acknowledge the arrest in response to any inquiry.
T.C.A. § 40-32-102(c)(1) — Expressly exempts the court from submitting a certificate where the relief is granted pursuant to this section.
