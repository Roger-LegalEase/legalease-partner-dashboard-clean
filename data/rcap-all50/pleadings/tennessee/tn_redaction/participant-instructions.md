# How to Complete and File Your Tennessee Motion to Remove the Records of Counts That Did Not End in a Conviction

These instructions are for you, the person filing. **Do not file this page with the court** — it is a checklist, not a court document.

## What this packet is

This packet is a motion for **partial removal** in a mixed case — one where some counts were dismissed and at least one count produced a conviction. It asks the court to remove the records of the counts that did not end in a conviction.

## Read this first

**Nothing here is automatic.** A charge that was dismissed, and a diversion you completed, stay on background checks until somebody files a petition. This is the single most common Tennessee misunderstanding, and it is why this packet exists.

**There is no official Tennessee form for any of this.** Both state form indices were checked directly and neither publishes an expunction form of any kind. What varies locally is the clerk's own paperwork, so ask your clerk before you file.

**Check the section number before you file.** The chapter was renumbered in 2025 and again on codification in 2026, and clerks and published guidance may still be using the old numbers — the state's own expunction page still reasons from a section that has been nothing but definitions since April 2025. Your packet carries the current citation. Confirm it with the clerk or the district attorney general's office before you file, because a petition citing the wrong section can be rejected either way.

## Be clear about what you get

**This is partial removal, not clearing the case.** The count or counts that produced the conviction are untouched by this motion, and stay on your record. If you expect the whole case cleared, this is not that, and you should stop and get advice.

**Paper records may not be covered.** The relief is established for electronic databases. Whether it also reaches the paper file was not resolved from any official source, and this packet does not claim it either way. If you specifically need the paper file cleared, get legal help.

**Whether a fee applies was not confirmed.** The no-cost rule is written around the non-conviction grounds and whether it reaches a partial removal in a mixed case is unresolved. No figure is quoted. Ask your clerk.

## Before you file

1. **Get the count-by-count dispositions from the clerk's record.** The motion carries a schedule listing each count and what happened to it, as you read it from that record. If you cannot establish the dispositions from the clerk's record, stop.
2. **Identify which counts produced a conviction.** Those are excluded from the motion and stay.
3. **Get your state control number early.** It comes from your own criminal history, and the order cannot be completed without it. Fill it into the bracket before you file.
4. **File under the existing docket**, not as a new case.

- **Leave the court's blanks alone.** The judge's name, any hearing date and the case-number stamp are supplied by the court after you file, not by you.
- **Fill in every item shown in [BRACKETS]** from your own records before you file.
- **You sign it.** Notarisation is not required by the governing section, but county practice can differ — ask your clerk whether they want it notarised.

## Filing, service and what happens next

- The clerk serves the motion on the district attorney general. **You do not serve anybody.**
- After the judge signs the order, the court sends it to the state bureau **within 30 days**.

## What Tennessee expunction does not reach

It does not reach federal, military, tribal or out-of-state records. If you have a security clearance question or an immigration matter, get legal advice before you file anything.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if you expect the whole case cleared — this remedy leaves the conviction in place; you need the paper file cleared and not only the electronic databases, which is the limitation that was not resolved; the count-by-count dispositions cannot be established from the clerk's record; there is any dispute with the district attorney general; you have any other conviction anywhere; or you have unpaid court costs, fines or restitution.
