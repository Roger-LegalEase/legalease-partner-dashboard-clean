# How to Complete and Send Your North Carolina Records-Conformity Request

These instructions are for you, the sender. This request is not a court document and nothing gets filed with any court — it is your own signed letter to an agency that still shows a charge that was automatically expunged.

## What this request does

Under N.C. Gen. Stat. § 15A-146(a4), certain dismissed charges are expunged automatically, by operation of law. But in these automatic cases the clerk of court is not required to send the expunction order to other agencies — so a police department, a state agency, or the State Bureau of Investigation may still show the charge simply because nobody told them. This letter states your account, cites the law, and asks the agency to check its own records against the court's records and, if the check confirms the expunction, to bring its records in line.

Two things this letter deliberately does not do: it does not claim the agency broke any rule, and it does not certify that the expunction happened. You are asking the agency to verify and conform — not accusing it.

## What the expunction means (the legal effect)

When a record is expunged, N.C. Gen. Stat. § 15A-153 restores you to the status you occupied before the arrest, charge or conviction. The court's own confidential file is maintained by the clerk and the Administrative Office of the Courts; this letter does not touch the court record — it only asks a non-court record holder to update its copy.

## Before you send

1. **Get your own SBI record check first.** Order a right-to-review copy of your criminal history from the State Bureau of Investigation. That is how you identify which agencies still show the charge. Nobody else obtains this for you.
2. **Confirm the timing.** This route only applies after the automatic expunction has actually run — its outer edge is at least 210 days from the final disposition date. If you are still inside the 180-to-210-day window, wait. If the automatic expunction never happened, this letter is the wrong tool — a court petition route applies instead.
3. **Check the recipient.** North Carolina expunction does not reach federal agencies or out-of-state agencies. This letter is for North Carolina record holders only.
4. **Confirm the agency's mailing address** against the agency's own published contact details. Do not guess.
5. **Complete every open item** in [BRACKETS]: the county and file number as shown on your record, the final disposition date, and the enclosure copy.
6. **Sign and date the request.** A plain signature is fine; no notarization is needed.

## Sending and following up

- Send the letter by mail with proof of posting, or through whatever written channel the agency publishes.
- There is no fee for the request itself.
- If the agency writes back asking for documentary proof of the expunction, the clerk of court offers an application for a certificate of verification of a prior expunction (form AOC-G-260, which may carry a clerk's fee). Respond with what the agency asks for — that certificate process is separate from this letter.
- Keep copies of everything.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if: the agency refuses to act or does not respond within its published time for records requests — a second letter is not the answer; the agency says the expunction did not occur or the case is outside the automatic rule; the stale record has already cost you a job, a license or housing (a legal claim may exist and deadlines may be running); or any immigration matter is in play — do not send this letter without advice first, because expunged records can be impossible to retrieve later when an immigration case needs them.
