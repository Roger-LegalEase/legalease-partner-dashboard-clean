# How to Send Your Georgia Request Asking a Jail to Restrict Its Booking Records

These instructions are for you, the person sending the letter. **Do not send this page** — it is a checklist.

## What this packet is

This is a **letter, not a court filing**. No court, prosecutor or clerk is involved. You send it directly to the county or municipal jail or detention center that booked you, asking that facility to restrict the records it holds for an offence that has **already been restricted**.

Why it exists: restriction by the Georgia Crime Information Center and sealing at the clerk of court **do not reach a jail's own records**. The jail keeps its own booking file, and it only has to act if you ask it to.

## What this does — and what it does not do

Georgia law does not use the word that people usually reach for here, and has not since 2013. The two remedies are **record restriction** (limiting who may see criminal history record information) and **sealing** (closing the clerk of court's file). **Neither one destroys the record.** A restricted or sealed record can still be used to disqualify you from employment or public office in the same way a first offender discharge can, and it does not override disclosure that federal law requires. Never say the record does not exist.

## You must already have the restriction

Prior restriction of the offence is the legal precondition of this request. You cannot use this letter to get a record restricted in the first place — if the offence has not been restricted yet, a restriction route comes first.

You do not have to prove the restriction to us to generate the letter. You do have to enclose your proof before you send it.

## Before you send it

1. **Enclose your proof that the offence is already restricted** — either your criminal history report showing the cycle as restricted, or the restricting order.
2. **Identify the right facility.** It is the county or municipal jail or detention center that booked you for that offence.
3. **Find your booking number** if you can. If you cannot, say so in the letter and ask the facility to locate the record by your name and the arrest date.
4. **Confirm the records-unit mailing address** with the facility, and fill it into the bracket.
5. **Ask the facility whether it has its own required form or intake route** for this kind of request. Some may; if yours does, its form governs over this letter.
6. **Sign and date the letter.** No notary is needed.

## Sending it and following up

- **Send it by a method that records the date** — certified mail is the usual choice. The facility's 30-day clock runs from your request, so the date matters and you want proof of it.
- **The facility must restrict access within 30 days of your request.**
- **Follow up at day 31** if you have heard nothing.

## What this letter cannot do

It reaches **county and municipal jail and detention center records only**. It does not reach private mugshot websites or background-check companies that already copied the data. Those are a separate problem and this letter will not solve them.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and get help from an attorney or legal aid if the facility refuses your request or does not respond within 30 days; if the offence has not in fact been restricted; or if the records you are worried about are held by a private mugshot publisher or background-check vendor. Georgia has free record-clearing desks — the Judicial Council's Access to Justice self-help pages list them.
