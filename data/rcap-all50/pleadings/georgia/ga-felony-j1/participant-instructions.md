# How to Complete and File Your Georgia Restricting a Felony Charge That Was Resolved Without a Conviction

These instructions are for you, the person filing. **Do not file this page with the court** — it is a checklist, not a court document.

## What this packet is

This packet is a petition asking the court to **restrict access** to the criminal history record information for a **felony charge** that was dismissed, nolle prossed, or ended in a not-guilty finding — in a case where you were convicted of a **separate misdemeanour** that was not a lesser included offence of that felony.

**It restricts the felony charge only.** It does not reach the misdemeanour conviction. That conviction stays on your record.

## What this does — and what it does not do

Georgia law does not use the word that people usually reach for here, and has not since 2013. The two remedies are **record restriction** (limiting who may see criminal history record information) and **sealing** (closing the clerk of court's file). **Neither one destroys the record.** A restricted or sealed record can still be used to disqualify you from employment or public office in the same way a first offender discharge can, and it does not override disclosure that federal law requires. Never say the record does not exist.

## The deadline is the most important thing on this page

You must file **within four years of the arrest**. This is a filing deadline that runs from your arrest date — it is not a waiting period, and it works the opposite way to every other Georgia route. Once four years have passed, this route closes. Check your arrest date first.

## Before you file

1. **Get a copy of the final disposition** from the clerk of the court that accused or convicted you, and attach it as Exhibit A.
2. **Check the disposition against your own answer** to how the felony charge was resolved. If they disagree, correct the packet before filing.
3. **Decide where to file.** If you were accused or convicted, file in that court. If the felony charge was dismissed, file in the superior court of the county where the arrest happened.
4. **Get your own Georgia criminal history report** if you are relying on it or the court expects it. You can obtain one from most Georgia sheriff's offices and police departments; requirements vary by agency. We never collect, inspect or authenticate it.

- **Leave the court's blanks alone.** The judge's name, the hearing date, the case-number stamp and the judge's signature line on the proposed order are filled in by the court after you file, not by you.
- **Fill in every item shown in [BRACKETS]** from your own records before you file.

## The one question we cannot answer for you

The petition turns on whether the misdemeanour you were convicted of was **not a lesser included offence** of the felony charge. That is a legal conclusion, and we do not make it. The petition pleads it as **your** assertion. If it is not obvious to you from reading the two Code sections side by side, get legal help before you file.

## Where it gets filed

The petition is filed **in your existing criminal case, under the existing case number**, styled *State of Georgia v.* your name, in the court that handled the case. It is **not** a new civil action, it is not captioned "IN RE", and it does not get a Civil Action number.

The court that handled your case might have been a **State Court, a Superior Court, a Magistrate Court, a Probate Court or a Municipal Court**. Do not assume Superior Court. Read the court's name off your own court record and write it into the caption.

- **Confirm the filing method with the clerk before you file.** There is no statewide Georgia court form for this. Every court runs its own filing and e-filing practice, some clerks resist accepting motions in cases that are closed, and some judges have their own preferences about proposed orders. One phone call to the clerk saves a rejected filing.
- **Ask the clerk about cost.** No filing fee is set by the statute for a filing made in an existing criminal case, and whether your clerk charges anything is a county-by-county question we cannot answer for you. Ask before you file. We do not quote a figure because none is published.
- **You sign it; nothing needs to be notarised.** No verification and no notary is required by statute on this route.

## Filing and serving

- File the signed petition, Exhibit A and the proposed order with the clerk.
- **Serve both** the arresting law enforcement agency **and** the prosecuting attorney. This route requires notice to both, which is broader than the other restriction routes. Confirm the agency's service destination with that agency, and the prosecutor's address with the clerk.
- Complete the certificate of service naming both recipients.
- A hearing is held within 90 days if one is requested.

## After the court rules

Get certified or file-stamped copies of the signed order from the clerk (a copy fee may apply). The order names the Georgia Crime Information Center and the agency custodians — an order that does not name the Center is not actionable, so check that the signed order still names it before you rely on it.

If a jail or detention center still shows the booking record after your offence is restricted, there is a separate written request you can send directly to that facility. Restriction by the Center and sealing at the clerk do not automatically reach a jail's own records.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if there is any dispute about whether the misdemeanour was a lesser included offence of the felony; the prosecuting attorney opposes the filing; the court sets a contested hearing or takes evidence; the four-year window from your arrest is close; the arresting agency disputes service or the record; the trial court no longer exists or the records cannot be located; you want to challenge the underlying conviction itself; or any immigration matter is involved. Georgia has free record-clearing desks — the Judicial Council's Access to Justice self-help pages list them.
