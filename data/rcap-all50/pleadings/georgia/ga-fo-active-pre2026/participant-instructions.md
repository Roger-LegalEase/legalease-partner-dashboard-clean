# How to Complete and File Your Georgia Restricting and Sealing an Active First Offender Case

These instructions are for you, the person filing. **Do not file this page with the court** — it is a checklist, not a court document.

## What this packet is

This packet is a petition asking the court that gave you a **First Offender sentence before July 1, 2026** to **restrict and seal** your case information — where your First Offender status has **not** been revoked and no adjudication of guilt has been entered.

This is the route for a case that is still active, or where a discharge has not yet been entered. If you were already exonerated and discharged before July 1, 2026, a different petition applies to you.

## What this does — and what it does not do

Georgia law does not use the word that people usually reach for here, and has not since 2013. The two remedies are **record restriction** (limiting who may see criminal history record information) and **sealing** (closing the clerk of court's file). **Neither one destroys the record.** A restricted or sealed record can still be used to disqualify you from employment or public office in the same way a first offender discharge can, and it does not override disclosure that federal law requires. Never say the record does not exist.

## What happens after you file

- The court **shall** order restriction and sealing **within 90 days** of your filing. You do not have to prove privacy harm or argue the interests of justice; those findings were removed from the statute, so relief on a properly filed petition is mandatory.
- The clerk then seals every document in its custody **within 60 days**.
- Law enforcement agencies, jails and detention centers must restrict **within 30 days** of receiving a copy of the order.

## Before you file

1. **Confirm you were actually sentenced under the First Offender Act.** If you are not sure, stop and get legal help — everything on this route depends on it.
2. **Get the final disposition** showing the First Offender sentence, and attach it as Exhibit A.
3. **Confirm your First Offender status has not been revoked** and that no adjudication of guilt has been entered at any time.
4. **Find out which office prosecuted the case** for that county — the District Attorney or the Solicitor-General. Both the clerk of court and that office receive notice.

- **Leave the court's blanks alone.** The judge's name, the hearing date, the case-number stamp and the judge's signature line on the proposed order are filled in by the court after you file, not by you.
- **Fill in every item shown in [BRACKETS]** from your own records before you file.

## Notice is not consent

The prosecuting attorney receives **notice** of your petition. Notice is not a request for permission. There is no advance-consent step on this route, the statute creates no objection period, and there is no objection-triggered hearing branch. If the prosecutor does object anyway, that is a reason to get legal help — not a different kind of case.

## Where it gets filed

The petition is filed **in your existing criminal case, under the existing case number**, styled *State of Georgia v.* your name, in the court that handled the case. It is **not** a new civil action, it is not captioned "IN RE", and it does not get a Civil Action number.

The court that handled your case might have been a **State Court, a Superior Court, a Magistrate Court, a Probate Court or a Municipal Court**. Do not assume Superior Court. Read the court's name off your own court record and write it into the caption.

- **Confirm the filing method with the clerk before you file.** There is no statewide Georgia court form for this. Every court runs its own filing and e-filing practice, some clerks resist accepting motions in cases that are closed, and some judges have their own preferences about proposed orders. One phone call to the clerk saves a rejected filing.
- **Ask the clerk about cost.** No filing fee is set by the statute for a filing made in an existing criminal case, and whether your clerk charges anything is a county-by-county question we cannot answer for you. Ask before you file. We do not quote a figure because none is published.
- **You sign it; nothing needs to be notarised.** No verification and no notary is required by statute on this route.

## Filing and serving

- File the signed petition, Exhibit A and the proposed order as a criminal petition **in the existing case**, with the clerk of the court that sentenced you.
- Send notice to the **clerk of court** and to the **prosecuting attorney**. Registered mail, certified mail or statutory overnight delivery is sufficient notice; hand delivery or e-filing may also be used.
- Complete the certificate of service.

## After the court rules

Get certified or file-stamped copies of the signed order from the clerk (a copy fee may apply). The order names the Georgia Crime Information Center and the agency custodians — an order that does not name the Center is not actionable, so check that the signed order still names it before you rely on it.

If a jail or detention center still shows the booking record after your offence is restricted, there is a separate written request you can send directly to that facility. Restriction by the Center and sealing at the clerk do not automatically reach a jail's own records.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if your First Offender status was revoked and an adjudication of guilt was entered; you are unsure whether you were sentenced as a first offender at all; the prosecuting attorney opposes the petition; the court sets a contested or evidentiary hearing; you want to attack the underlying case; or any immigration matter is involved. Georgia has free record-clearing desks — the Judicial Council's Access to Justice self-help pages list them.
