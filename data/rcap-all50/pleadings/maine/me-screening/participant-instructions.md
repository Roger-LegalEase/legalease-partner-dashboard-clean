# How to Complete and Send Your Maine Background-Check Dispute Letter

These instructions are for you, the sender. This letter is not a court document and nothing gets filed with any court — it is your own correspondence to a private background-check company.

## What this letter does

Maine law (10 M.R.S. § 1500-CC) makes a business screening service — a company in the business of collecting and selling criminal history record information about people — investigate, without charging you, when you dispute the completeness or accuracy of your record. The company must compare its record against the official record kept by the State Bureau of Identification, correct anything that does not match, and — if your record is sealed or pardoned — promptly delete it. Within thirty days of receiving your dispute, the company must tell you the results.

This letter does not fix the official state record or the court record; it only reaches the private company's copy. Do not expect a specific deletion date — the law requires prompt deletion when the sealed or pardoned status is confirmed, but timelines are the company's to meet.

## Before you send

1. **Get a copy of your sealing order or pardon.** This is the document the whole letter turns on. Ask the clerk of the court that signed your sealing order for a copy, or obtain a copy of your pardon. Enclose a **copy** — never the original.
2. **Optional but strongly recommended:** order your own official Maine criminal history record from the Maine State Police, State Bureau of Identification, and enclose a copy — especially if the company's entry is wrong on facts beyond the sealing or pardon.
3. **Find the company's dispute address.** It is usually printed on the background report itself or published on the company's website. If you cannot find it, contact the company and ask; do not guess.
4. **Complete every open item.** Anything in [BRACKETS] must be filled in before sending: your date of birth, the entry copied exactly as the company printed it (the charge, the court, the case number and the disposition), the sealing-order date and court (copied from your enclosed order), and anything else that is wrong about the entry.
5. **Sign and date the letter.** You sign personally.

## Sending

- Send the letter and copies of your enclosures to the company's dispute address — and to no one else.
- Send it in a way that proves receipt (for example, certified mail with return receipt, or another tracked method).
- **Calendar thirty days from the day the company receives it.** That is the deadline for the company to tell you the results.

## After you send

- The company may ask for more information, or may say it considers the dispute frivolous — if so, it must state its specific reasons and describe what it needs.
- Keep everything: your copy of the letter, the proof of receipt, and any response.

## When to get a lawyer

Talk to an attorney or legal aid if: the company does not respond within thirty days of receiving your dispute; the company calls your dispute frivolous and you disagree; you want to pursue money damages (Maine law provides a separate court remedy — that is litigation and beyond this letter); the company is a national vendor and a federal Fair Credit Reporting Act claim may apply; your record was never sealed or pardoned and the dispute is about establishing what the true record is; or you are unsure the company is a "business screening service" at all (government agencies and the news media are not).
