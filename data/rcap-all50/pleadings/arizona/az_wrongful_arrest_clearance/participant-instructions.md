# How to Complete and File Your Arizona Petition for Notation of Clearance

These instructions are for you, the person filing. Do not file this page with the court — it is a checklist, not a court document.

## What this petition does

Under Arizona law (A.R.S. § 13-4051), a person who was wrongfully arrested, indicted, or otherwise charged may ask the superior court for an order that puts a notation on all court, police and agency records saying the person has been cleared, and directing those agencies not to release copies of the records except on court order.

This remedy is different from sealing the record. The records still exist; the order adds the clearance notation and restricts release of copies. A person who has notice of the order and fails to comply with it can be liable in damages.

The court makes the final decision. Filing this petition does not guarantee any outcome. Arizona law contemplates a hearing, and the judge issues the order only if the judge believes justice will be served.

## Before you file

1. **Complete every open item in the petition.** Anything shown in [BRACKETS] must be filled in or confirmed before filing — especially the docket or case number for the arrest or charge.
2. **Check the county and court.** The petition is filed in the superior court. Confirm with the clerk which county superior court is correct — ordinarily the county where the arrest, indictment or charge happened.
3. **Tell your story in your own words.** The petition includes your statement of why the arrest or charge was wrongful. This must be accurate and in your own words. Review it carefully before signing.
4. **List the records.** Identify the court, police and agency records you want the clearance notation entered on, and attach the records list.
5. **Sign and date the petition.** You sign your own petition, in the signature block.
6. **Ask the clerk about notarization.** Our source materials do not state whether this petition must be notarized. Ask the clerk before filing.
7. **Ask the clerk about the filing fee.** Our source materials do not state a filing fee for this petition. Ask the clerk about the current fee. If you cannot afford it, ask whether a fee waiver (deferral) application is available.

## Filing and serving

- File the signed petition (with your records list) with the clerk of the superior court in the correct county.
- Our source materials do not state a service requirement for this petition. Ask the clerk whether you must deliver a copy to the prosecuting agency or anyone else, and how.
- The court may set a hearing. Attend any hearing the court schedules and be prepared to explain, with your evidence, why the arrest or charge was wrongful.

## After the court rules

- If the judge grants the petition, the order directs the notation of clearance on the identified records and restricts release of copies.
- Keep certified copies of the order for your files.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if any of these happen: the other side disputes your evidence or the hearing becomes contested; your petition is denied and you want to challenge the denial; or immigration consequences may be in play. The burden of showing the arrest or charge was wrongful is on you, and it is not a simple checklist.
