# How to Complete and File Your Boston Municipal Court Consolidated Sealing Petition

These instructions are for you, the person filing. Do not file this page with the court — it is a checklist, not a court document.

## What this petition does

Massachusetts law (G.L. c. 276, § 100C) lets a court seal certain criminal records that ended without a conviction. The Boston Municipal Court Department, under its Amended Standing Order No. 1-09, allows one consolidated petition to seal three or more criminal records from two or more BMC divisions, instead of filing one form per case.

Important vocabulary: in Massachusetts, sealing and expunging are different things. Sealing does not destroy the record; it restricts who can see it. This petition asks for sealing only.

The court makes the final decision. Filing this petition does not guarantee any outcome.

## Who can use this consolidated route

- You have **three or more** criminal records from **two or more** Boston Municipal Court divisions, and
- Each record ended without a conviction (for example: dismissal, nolle prosequi, no probable cause, or not guilty).

If you have fewer records, or records from only one division, use the ordinary sealing route with one statewide form per case. Records from District Court, Juvenile Court or Superior Court are not covered by this petition.

## Before you file

1. **Get your own CORI.** Request your personal criminal record (CORI) from the Massachusetts Department of Criminal Justice Information Services. LegalEase never collects, inspects or authenticates it — you obtain it yourself.
2. **Check your case list against your CORI.** The attached schedule must list every BMC case you want sealed, with the division and docket number for each. Correct the schedule if it disagrees with your CORI.
3. **Write your own good-cause narrative.** The judge weighs the reasons for sealing (the factors from Commonwealth v. Pon). Your narrative must be in your own words: what specific problems these records have caused you, and what you have done since the cases ended.
4. **Confirm the correct division.** File in the BMC division for where you live. If you no longer live in BMC territory, file in the division where your most recent eligible record is from. Confirm with the Clerk-Magistrate.
5. **Complete every open item.** Anything shown in [BRACKETS] must be filled in or confirmed before filing.
6. **Sign the petition.** Your signature is required in the signature block. Our source materials record no notarization requirement, but confirm with the Clerk-Magistrate.
7. **Ask about fees.** Our source materials identify no filing fee for this petition, but confirm with the Clerk-Magistrate. If a fee applies and you cannot afford it, ask about a fee waiver.

## Filing and what happens next

- File the signed consolidated petition, with your case schedule and narrative, in the correct BMC division.
- Ask the Clerk-Magistrate whether you must deliver copies to anyone else; service is governed by the Standing Order.
- After filing, the Standing Order provides for a preliminary hearing, public notice, and a final hearing. The judge may ask the Clerk-Magistrate or the Probation Department for more information about your listed cases.
- Attend every hearing the court schedules.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if: you do not meet the three-records/two-divisions threshold; some of your records are outside the Boston Municipal Court Department; the judge requests additional information you are unsure how to provide; or a contested hearing is set.
