# How to complete and file your North Dakota petition to seal

This packet asks the court to seal a North Dakota felony conviction. You qualify to ask only if you have not been convicted of any new crime for at least five years before you file.

These instructions are for you only. Do not file this page with the court.

## Before you start

- Get the court record and final disposition order for the case from the clerk of court, or search the case at ndcourts.gov public access. Your petition has to describe the record accurately.
- Request your own North Dakota criminal history from the Bureau of Criminal Investigation (BCI) through the Attorney General's office (a fee applies). The petition must list your whole record; memory is not a safe basis.
- Get proof that all imprisonment and probation are complete (a discharge order from the clerk, or a completion letter from your probation officer).
- If restitution was ordered, get a payment history from the clerk showing restitution paid in full.

## Complete the petition

- Check every pre-filled answer against your court records and your BCI record, and correct the paperwork if they disagree.
- Fill in the case number, and the county or municipality, exactly as they appear on your court papers. Where the paperwork shows a bracketed item such as the docket number, copy the correct value from your court record or confirm it with the clerk of court.
- Write the reasons-for-sealing section in your own words. This part is yours alone: the court must find good cause and that you have been reformed, and no one can assert that for you.
- List your full legal name and every other name you have used, your address history from the offense date to now, your complete criminal history (North Dakota, other states, federal, and foreign), all prior and pending charges, and any prior pardon, sealing, or record-return requests.

## Sign

- Sign and date the petition and the proof of service yourself. North Dakota law does not require this petition to be notarized or sworn.
- Leave the judge's signature line on the proposed order blank. The court signs it.

## Serve, then file

1. First, serve the petition and the proposed order on the prosecuting attorney — the city prosecutor for a municipal case, or the county State's Attorney for a district court case. Ask the clerk of court for the correct office and address if you are unsure.
2. Then file the petition, the proposed order (it is required — a petition without one is incomplete), and the proof of service with the clerk of the court that handled your criminal case.
3. Ask the clerk what the filing fee is, if any. No official fee amount is published for this petition, so do not pay any amount you have not confirmed with the clerk.

## What happens next

- The prosecutor may contact law enforcement, witnesses, victims, and correctional authorities for input.
- A hearing may not be held earlier than 45 days after filing unless the prosecutor agrees to a faster schedule. You must attend and prove your case by clear and convincing evidence.
- Sealing covers court and prosecution records only. It does not erase BCI or FBI records, does not fix immigration issues, and does not restore firearm rights. If the petition is denied, the court may bar refiling for up to one year.
- If the prosecutor, a victim, or a witness objects — or anything above does not match your situation — stop and talk to a lawyer or legal aid before going further.
