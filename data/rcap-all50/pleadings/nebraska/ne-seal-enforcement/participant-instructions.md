# Making a Nebraska agency clear a record that should already be sealed

This guide explains how to complete, sign and file your petition asking a
Nebraska district court to order an agency to comply with the record-sealing law.
Read it before you sign anything.

**This is not an official court form, and it is not legal advice.** A Nebraska
district court decides whether to order compliance. Nothing here guarantees an
outcome.

**We strongly recommend talking to an attorney before you file this.** This is a
court action against a government agency, which is a step up in difficulty from
most record-clearing paperwork. That is a recommendation, not a rule — but take
it seriously.

## First: is this the right action for you?

This is an **enforcement** action. It is not a request to seal your record. It
assumes your record **already should have been sealed** under Nebraska's
record-sealing law, and that an agency has not done it.

So before anything else, confirm two things:

1. Your case actually qualified for sealing under the law, and
2. the time period for that has run, or whatever triggers it has happened, and
3. the record is **still public** anyway.

If your case never qualified for sealing in the first place, this action will not
help you and there is nothing to enforce.

If someone has told you to file a motion in your old criminal case instead, this
is the correct route — the Nebraska Supreme Court said so in *State v. Coble*.

## What this action cannot fix

An order from a Nebraska court under this law will **not** reach:

- records held by a **private data broker** or on a **privately maintained
  website** — the commercial background-check sites;
- **federal** agencies;
- **out-of-state** agencies.

If your real problem is that a commercial background-check website is showing your
record, this action will not solve it. Please know that before you spend anything.

## Step 1 — Identify exactly who failed to comply

Your documents leave the respondent blank on purpose. You have to name the
specific officer, agency or political subdivision that failed to seal or is still
releasing the record.

We will not guess this. Naming the wrong respondent defeats the action.

**If you cannot work out which agency is at fault, stop and get a lawyer.** That is
one of the situations where self-help runs out.

## Step 2 — Choose where to file

The law gives you a choice. You can file in:

- the district court of **any district where the records involved are located**, or
- the district court of **Lancaster County**.

That choice is yours to make. We do not make it for you. If you are unsure which
is better for your situation, ask an attorney.

## Step 3 — Check the details, then sign by hand

Read every line. Confirm your name, any other names you have used, and the case
details against your own records. Lines marked "to be confirmed" are values we
will not guess — fill them in from your records or ask the court clerk. Then sign
and date by hand.

## Step 4 — What you write yourself

The description of how the record is still showing up publicly is **yours to
write.** We do not write it for you, and nobody else may write it for you. Be
specific and factual about what you saw and where.

## Step 5 — Before you file: two things we could not answer

We want to be straight with you about the limits of what we know.

**How to serve the agency.** Nebraska's rule for serving a state agency or
political subdivision was not something our source covered. So we have **not**
included a certificate of service and we have **not** guessed at a method. Ask the
clerk of the district court, or an attorney, how the agency must be served. Do not
assume regular mail is enough.

**Whether you need extra documents.** The law lets you bring this as a general
action to compel compliance, or specifically as a mandamus action. We have drafted
the general action, because the statute allows it directly. But Nebraska's
mandamus procedure was not surveyed, and a mandamus action may require extra
paperwork — an application and affidavit, and an alternative writ. If you intend
to proceed by mandamus specifically, ask an attorney what else is required. Those
extra documents are not in this packet.

**About the fee:** we cannot tell you what this costs. No fee is established in our
source, so no amount appears anywhere in your documents. Ask the clerk.

## Step 6 — What your petition actually says

Your petition **alleges** that the record should have been sealed and is still
public, and asks the court to order compliance. It does not say a court has
already found the agency at fault, and it does not say what the agency thinks
about it. Those are not yours or ours to state.

## When to stop and get a lawyer

- You cannot identify the non-compliant agency.
- Your case never qualified for sealing in the first place.
- The agency contests the action and appears through a lawyer.
- The record you are worried about is on a private background-check site.
- You would rather be represented. This is a lawsuit against a government body.
