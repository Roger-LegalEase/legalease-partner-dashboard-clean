# How to Complete and File Your DC Motion to Correct a Misattributed Arrest Record

These instructions are for you, the person filing. **Do not file this page with the court** — it is a checklist, not a court document.

## What this packet is

This is a motion asking the Superior Court to **correct** publicly available records showing an arrest that was attributed to you but was not yours — you were incorrectly identified or named.

**This is correction, not closure of the file.** It is a different remedy from the motions that close or clear a record, and it does a different thing: it fixes records that name the wrong person. If what you actually want is your own record closed or cleared, that is a different motion.

## What the law requires here

Three things:

1. **You attest under oath** that you were incorrectly identified or named. This is your sworn statement. Nobody makes it for you and nobody makes it on your behalf.
2. **No fingerprints were taken** at the arrest.
3. **The arrested person presented no other reliable identification.**

Those last two are why the record could attach to the wrong person in the first place. If fingerprints were taken, this route does not fit.

## Before you file

1. **Write your own account** of how the misidentification happened, in your own words.
2. **Gather anything you have** that supports it, and enclose copies only.
3. **Ask the Criminal Division whether a notary is needed.** The motion carries a sworn attestation. Whether the court also wants it notarised is not stated in our sources, so ask before you file rather than after it is rejected.

## Where it goes

You file in the **Superior Court of the District of Columbia, Criminal Division**. Clerk intake runs through the Division's sealing intake team. **One motion per case number** — if you have more than one case, each needs its own motion.

Confirm the Division's current standing orders and filing practice before you file. Practice has changed and this packet cannot confirm the current position for you.

## Who you serve

You serve **the prosecutor of record**. For a District case that is either the United States Attorney's Office for the District of Columbia or the District of Columbia Office of the Attorney General, depending on the case. **Which one applies to your case, and the right address for each, is a question this packet cannot answer** — it is unresolved in our sources. Confirm it with the Criminal Division before you serve, and fill in the bracket.

The prosecutor does not have to respond unless the court orders it. On initial review the court may dismiss or deny; otherwise it decides whether a hearing is needed.

## Cost

The court's own instruction sheet states no fee for this motion, and no fee schedule confirming that was located. **No figure appears anywhere in this packet.** Ask the Criminal Division before you file. Our sources do not address a fee waiver either.

## What you fill in, and what you leave alone

- Fill in every item shown in [BRACKETS] from your own records.
- **Leave the court's blanks alone.** The judge's name, any hearing date and the case-number stamp are supplied by the court after you file.
- You sign the declaration under penalty of perjury.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if the prosecutor files an opposition or the court orders a response; the court sets a hearing; the record is federal, military, tribal or from another jurisdiction; or there are immigration, firearm, licensing, security clearance, childcare, healthcare or law enforcement employment consequences in play.
