# How to Complete and File Your DC Motion to Seal a Fugitive-from-Justice Arrest Record

These instructions are for you, the person filing. **Do not file this page with the court** — it is a checklist, not a court document.

## What this packet is

This is a motion asking the Superior Court to seal the record of an arrest made in the District because **another jurisdiction wanted you held as a fugitive**.

## What the law requires here

- You were arrested in the District as a fugitive and **waived extradition**, and were then released or detained under the extradition statute.
- The fugitive case **reached final disposition**.
- You **appeared before the proper official in the other jurisdiction**.

If all of that is so, the Court **shall** grant the motion where sealing is in the interests of justice.

**The appearance is your showing.** You have to establish that you appeared before the proper official in the originating jurisdiction. This packet does not supply or argue that for you — it sets out your own account.

## One thing to know about what sealing here gets you

Records sealed as fugitive-from-justice arrests are **not** available to the licensing bodies, the school and childcare employers, or the senior government employers that the statute lists. That is a real benefit and it is specific to this route.

## Before you file

1. **Gather what you have** about the extradition waiver, the final disposition of the fugitive case, and your appearance in the other jurisdiction. Enclose copies only.
2. **Write your own statement of your known history** and your own account of why sealing is in the interests of justice.
3. **Check the date.** If a previous motion of this kind was denied within the last year, stop and get advice before filing again.

## Where it goes

You file in the **Superior Court of the District of Columbia, Criminal Division**. Clerk intake runs through the Division's sealing intake team. **One motion per case number** — if you have more than one case, each needs its own motion.

Confirm the Division's current standing orders and filing practice before you file. Practice has changed and this packet cannot confirm the current position for you.

## Who you serve

You serve **the prosecutor of record**. For a District case that is either the United States Attorney's Office for the District of Columbia or the District of Columbia Office of the Attorney General, depending on the case. **Which one applies to your case, and the right address for each, is a question this packet cannot answer** — it is unresolved in our sources. Confirm it with the Criminal Division before you serve, and fill in the bracket.

The prosecutor does not have to respond unless the court orders it. On initial review the court may dismiss or deny; otherwise it decides whether a hearing is needed.

## Cost

The court's own instruction sheet states no fee for this motion, and no fee schedule confirming that was located. **No figure appears anywhere in this packet.** Ask the Criminal Division before you file. Our sources do not address a fee waiver either.

## What you fill in, and what you leave alone

- Fill in every item shown in [BRACKETS] from your own records.
- **Leave the court's blanks alone.** The judge's name, any hearing date and the case-number stamp are supplied by the court after you file.
- You sign the declaration under penalty of perjury.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if the prosecutor files an opposition or the court orders a response; the court sets a hearing; the record is federal, military, tribal or from another jurisdiction; or there are immigration, firearm, licensing, security clearance, childcare, healthcare or law enforcement employment consequences in play. Also stop if a victim submits a statement, or if a prior motion was denied inside the one-year window.
