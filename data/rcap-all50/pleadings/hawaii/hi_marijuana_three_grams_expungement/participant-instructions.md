# How to Complete and File Your Hawaii Marijuana (Three Grams or Less) Expungement Motion

These instructions are for you, the person filing. Do not file this page with the court — it is a checklist, not a court document.

## What this process does — two stages

**Stage one (this packet):** a written motion filed in your own existing criminal case, in the court that sentenced or convicted you, asking for an expungement order under HRS § 706-622.5(5) for a marijuana conviction involving three grams or less.

**Stage two (separate, later):** after the judge signs the order, you submit the Expungement Application (form HCJDC-159B) to the Hawaii Criminal Justice Data Center with a copy of the signed order attached. **Do not submit stage two until you have the signed court order.** This packet does not include the HCJDC form; the Data Center charges an application fee for it.

For this route the law says the court "shall grant an expungement order" — the one condition is the quantity: three grams or less. If there is any dispute about the amount, stop and get legal help before filing.

## Before you file

1. **Read your own court record.** The case number, the court name, and the judgment date come from your own records. Fill in every item shown in [BRACKETS].
2. **Confirm the court.** The motion is filed in the existing criminal case, in the court that entered the judgment. Confirm the exact court name from your court record.
3. **Check the disqualifiers.** This route is defeated if another criminal charge arose from the same facts and circumstances, or if you already received an expungement under HRS 706-622.5 or HRS 706-622.8.
4. **Sign the motion and the declaration.** Your declaration is signed as true under penalty of law and dated — no notary is needed.
5. **Ask the clerk about costs.** No filing fee is prescribed by the statute for a motion in an existing criminal case, but ask the clerk whether any cost applies. (There is no fee-waiver form in this packet — the court's fee-waiver form belongs to a different kind of case.)

## Filing and serving

- File the signed motion, your declaration, and the proposed order with the clerk of the court that entered the judgment.
- Serve a copy on the prosecuting attorney promptly after filing — the prosecutor is a party to your criminal case — and complete the certificate of service. Confirm the prosecuting attorney's address with the clerk.
- If the court sets a hearing, attend.

## After the judge signs the order

- Get certified or file-stamped copies of the signed order from the clerk (a copy fee may apply).
- Only then move to stage two: complete the HCJDC Expungement Application (form HCJDC-159B), attach a copy of the signed order, note the order's number and date in the application, and submit it to the Hawaii Criminal Justice Data Center with its application fee.

## When to get a lawyer

Stop and talk to an attorney or legal aid if: there is any dispute about whether the amount was three grams or less; another criminal charge arose from the same facts; you previously received an expungement under HRS 706-622.5 or 706-622.8; the prosecutor opposes the motion or the court sets a contested hearing; or any immigration matter is involved.
