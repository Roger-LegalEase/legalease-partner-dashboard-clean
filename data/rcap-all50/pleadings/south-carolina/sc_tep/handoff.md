## Mechanism

Article 3 of Chapter 22, Title 17 is the Traffic Education Program Act.
§ 17-22-310 gives each circuit solicitor prosecutorial discretion to establish a
programme for persons who commit traffic-related offences punishable **only by a
fine and loss of four points or less**, and bars participation where the offence
resulted in death or serious bodily injury. § 17-22-320 confines eligibility to a
person with no significant history of traffic violations and allows participation
only once.

§ 17-22-330(A) provides that on successful completion the administering
governmental agency **shall** effect a noncriminal disposition of the offence,
with no record maintained except by the programme to prevent a second use.
Subsections (C) and (D) allow termination and reinstatement for violating the
conditions, and require termination where the person receives a subsequent
traffic violation in the six months following the ticket.

§ 17-22-940(D) names the traffic education program director as an attestor, and
§ 17-22-940(E)(1) exempts § 17-22-330(A) from the SLED verification fee.

## Route decision

**Drafting is barred, on two independent grounds.**

**First, this is an official-form route.** The committed South Carolina evidence
states that South Carolina expungements start with either the Solicitor's Office
or the summary court and **use the official SCCA 223-series forms**. Drafting a
custom application or order would replicate SCCA 223A1 or SCCA 223B1, which is
prohibited outright. Official-PDF overlay work belongs to lane D/E.

**Second, the destruction-application provision itself is not established for
this track.** On the alcohol programme, § 17-22-530(B) expressly provides the
application to the court to destroy records. There is no equivalent in the
committed description of the traffic programme: the source describes only
§ 17-22-330(A), (C) and (D), and reproduces no destruction-application
subsection. Section 17-22-350 sits in the authority list — very likely the
counterpart to § 17-22-550 on the alcohol side — but its text was not read, so
the statutory hook for the destruction application on this track is unconfirmed.

Even if the official-form bar were lifted, that second gap would independently
bar drafting: the instrument's statutory basis is unknown.

## Open counsel flags

- **Destruction-application provision not established (blocking).** The statutory
  hook must be confirmed before any instrument is built.
- **Verification — source silent.** No verification or notarization statute is
  identified; verification is a feature of the official form.
- **Waiting period — none** beyond successful completion and the noncriminal
  disposition. But note the six-month trap below.
- **Six-month reinstatement trap.** A subsequent traffic violation within six
  months of the ticket terminates the programme and reinstates the offence.
  Screening must confirm this has not happened before any relief is described.
- **Narrow eligibility.** Fine-only offences with loss of four points or less, no
  significant history of traffic violations, once only, and nothing that resulted
  in death or serious bodily injury.
- **Attestation is obtained, never asserted.** § 17-22-940(D) names the programme
  director as attestor; LegalEase never attests completion or states that it
  occurred.
- SLED verification fee is not charged for this route under § 17-22-940(E)(1).
- **Registry release blocker (open):** the single-incident fee treatment under
  § 17-22-940(G) as amended by 2018 Act No. 254.
