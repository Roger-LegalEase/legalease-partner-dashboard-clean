# Asking for immediate sealing on the day your case is dismissed or you are acquitted

Illinois — 20 ILCS 2630/5.2(g)

This sheet is for you to keep. Do not file it with the court.

This is not an official court form.

---

## What this is

Illinois has a rule that lets certain records be sealed **immediately**, in the
courtroom, on the day the case ends.

It applies to an arrest, or a charge that did not start with an arrest, where the
case ends in an **acquittal** (you are found not guilty) or a **dismissal with
prejudice**, and where the arrest or charge happened **on or after January 1,
2018**.

There is one catch, and it is the whole point of this sheet: the request has to be
filed with the circuit clerk **on the same day, during the same hearing** in which
your case is disposed of. The judge who is sitting on your case that day decides it
right there in that hearing.

**Sealing is not expungement.** Sealing hides the record from most public searches.
It does not erase the record, and government and law-enforcement access is not all
cut off.

---

## Your lawyer files this, not you

The law is written around a defendant who has a lawyer. It says the defendant's
attorney may immediately petition the court, on the defendant's behalf.

So this packet is prepared **for your lawyer** — the private attorney, appointed
counsel or public defender who is on your case. It is theirs to review, adopt, sign
and file during the hearing.

We cannot be in the courtroom and we cannot file anything during a hearing. And
this is not something to file yourself after you leave court that day. If you do
not have a lawyer on the case, this route is not open to you the way it is written;
see **If something does not fit** below.

---

## What is in the packet

1. **The petition** — the request for immediate sealing. Two things on it are
   blank on purpose: your verification, and your lawyer's signature.
2. **The proposed order** — the order for the judge. It is deliberately blank as to
   what the judge decides. Nobody fills in an outcome ahead of time.
3. **This sheet.**

---

## What you need to have ready

- Your full legal name, and any other names you have used.
- Your contact information.
- The county and the case number of the case you are appearing in.
- The court date at which the case may be dismissed or you may be found not guilty.
- The name and contact details of your lawyer or public defender.

If you do not know something, leave it blank and say so. Do not guess. We have left
the court name, county, case number, courtroom, judge and prosecutor blank in your
draft because we were not given them.

If you can, get your Illinois criminal history report — sometimes called a RAP
sheet — before you go, so the case numbers, charges, dates and outcomes can be
checked. It is not required for this route, but it helps.

---

## Step 1 — Give the packet to your lawyer before the court date

Do this ahead of the hearing, not on the morning of it. Your lawyer needs time to
read it, decide whether it is right for your case, change anything they want
changed, and sign it.

Your lawyer decides whether to file it. We draft it; they decide.

## Step 2 — Sign your verification

There is a verification block on the petition. That is you swearing your part of it
is true. **You sign that yourself.** Nobody signs it for you.

Nothing here needs a notary.

We have not printed any penalty wording next to the verification, because we were
not given the wording Illinois uses for this particular petition. Your lawyer will
know what belongs there.

## Step 3 — Your lawyer files it with the circuit clerk during the hearing

This is the part that has no second chance on the day. The petition goes to the
circuit clerk **on the same day, during the same hearing** in which your case is
disposed of.

## Step 4 — A copy goes to the State's Attorney, in open court

Your lawyer hands a copy to the State's Attorney right there in the courtroom.

That is the only person who gets served. Nobody mails anything to anyone. There is
no notice going out to police departments or to the Illinois State Police on this
route.

## Step 5 — The judge decides during the same hearing

The judge who is presiding enters an order granting or denying the request during
that same hearing.

We cannot tell you what the judge will do, and nothing in your packet says what the
judge will do.

---

## About fees

We are not printing a fee figure, because there is not a reliable one to print.

The source review for this route says there is no fee for it. Separately, Illinois
filing and notification costs vary by county, and there is no statewide fee
schedule — so treat any number you hear as a range, not a quote.

If a fee does come up in your county and paying it would be hard, ask the clerk
about the **Application for Waiver of Court Fees** under Supreme Court Rule 298.
Ask your lawyer about this before the hearing, not during it.

---

## If you miss the window, you have not lost anything

If the petition does not get filed during the hearing, that is not the end of it.

Ordinary sealing of a non-conviction under 20 ILCS 2630/5.2(c)(3)(A) stays open
**at any time**. It is a separate, slower route with its own forms, and you can
start it whenever you are ready.

Missing the immediate-sealing window costs you nothing permanent.

---

## If something does not fit

Stop and get advice from a lawyer if:

- You do not have a lawyer or a public defender on the case. This route is written
  around a represented defendant, and there is no self-file version of it.
- The arrest or charge happened **before January 1, 2018**. Those records are
  outside this rule.
- The dismissal is **without prejudice**. That is not within this rule.
- The charge is a minor traffic offense.
- Your case has already been disposed of and you have left court.

None of those means nothing can be done. Most of them mean a different route —
often ordinary sealing under § 5.2(c)(3)(A).

The **Office of the State Appellate Defender, Expungement Unit, 866-787-1776**, is
the statewide place to call for help with any of this.

---

## Things we deliberately left blank

We left these out rather than guess:

- The court name, county, circuit or district, courtroom, case number and judge.
- The name of the State's Attorney, and whether they will object.
- Your lawyer's name and signature.
- Any fee amount.
- The date and outcome of the hearing.
- Whether the court will grant the request.

Your lawyer fills in what belongs there.
