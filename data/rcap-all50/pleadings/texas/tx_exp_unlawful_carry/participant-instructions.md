# How to complete and file your petition to clear an old unlawful-carry conviction

Texas calls this **expunction**. Use that word with the clerk. It is not nondisclosure, which seals a record instead of destroying it and is filed somewhere else entirely.

This is a **new civil case**. It is not filed in your old criminal case. It gets a new cause number when you file it.

Most people have never heard of this route. It exists because the law on carrying a handgun changed on 1 September 2021, and it reaches convictions for offences committed **before** that date.

## What is in this packet

- The petition asking the court to order your records expunged.
- The proposed Order of Expunction for the judge to sign.
- A separate attachment listing any private background-check companies.
- This instruction sheet, which you keep. It is never filed.

Two more things have to go with your filing, and neither is written for you here:

- **A certified copy of your judgment of conviction.** See step 1. This one is not optional.
- **A Statement of Inability to Afford Payment of Court Costs**, but only if you cannot pay the filing costs. That is the Supreme Court of Texas's own bilingual form, and the clerk has to give it to you free, without you asking, under Rule 145.

## 1. Get the certified judgment first

Two facts decide whether this route is open to you, and neither can come from memory:

- **The date the offence happened.** It has to be before 1 September 2021.
- **The subsection you were convicted under.** It has to be Penal Code 46.02(a) as that section read before that date. Another subsection of 46.02 will not do.

Ask the clerk of the court that convicted you for a certified copy of the judgment, and read both facts off it.

If you cannot confirm the date and the subsection from court records, stop. Do not file on a guess — a wrong subsection defeats the petition.

## 2. Get your DPS criminal history

Follow the Texas Department of Public Safety's form CR-63, *Procedure for Review of Personal Criminal History Record Information*. It is an instruction sheet, not a form to fill in. It sets out fingerprinting through the DPS FAST vendor — a $10 fingerprinting fee plus a $15 record fee — or a hard-card route by mail.

You need it for four reasons:

- It carries the **TRN**, the incident number that has to go on the order.
- It gives you the arrest date and the arresting agency.
- It helps you build the agency list.
- It shows you whether anything has already happened to the record.

DPS will not help you with the petition. Its own material says its legal staff cannot assist with one, and that DPS may oppose an expunction petition.

## 3. Call the district clerk and ask for the agency list

Every district clerk in Texas has to compile a list of agencies and e-mail addresses and keep it on the clerk's website. Ask for it by name.

Two things about that list:

- The clerk is **not** responsible for it being complete. It is a starting point. Add anything you know of that is missing.
- Every e-mail address on it saves you money. The clerk cannot charge you anything to send the papers electronically, but the clerk must charge **$25 for each listed entity that cannot receive an electronic transmission** — and the same charge structure comes back again at the order stage.

While you have the clerk on the phone, ask whether they want a certified disposition attached as well, and whether the clerk or you prepares the notice of hearing. Both vary by county.

## 4. Build the two lists carefully

**The agency list.** List every state and local official, agency and entity you reasonably believe holds records of this arrest, plus every central federal depository. Two hard rules:

- Never list the same state or local agency twice.
- Never list separate divisions of the same agency with different contacts or addresses.

**The private-entity list.** Any background-check company that has reported this arrest goes on a **separate** attachment. Never merge it into the agency list.

## 5. Fill in what only you can fill in

The petition has to carry your full name, your sex, your race, your date of birth, your driver's licence number, your social security number and the address you lived at when you were arrested. None of that is filled in for you. You write it in by hand.

Before you do, ask the clerk how that county handles sensitive information in a filing that is public until the order issues. Counties differ and this one is worth asking about.

Anything you cannot supply must either appear or be accompanied by a short explanation of why it is not included.

## 6. Sign and verify

You sign the petition, and the petition must be verified.

There are two blocks in the packet: a **notarial verification** block, and an **unsworn declaration under penalty of perjury**. Use whichever your county accepts — some clerks take the declaration and some want the notary. Ask when you call about the agency list. Execute one, not both.

## 7. File it

File the petition with the **district clerk of the county where you were arrested, or where the offence was alleged to have happened** — not where you live. You can file through eFileTexas, in person, or by mail where the clerk allows it.

If the arrest was for a fine-only offence, a justice court or a municipal court of record in either of those counties is also available to you.

You will pay a filing fee. It is the ordinary civil filing fee that county charges for an ex parte petition in district court, and it is set by the county rather than by statute — ask the clerk what it is. In a justice court or municipal court of record it is a flat $100. If you cannot pay, file the Statement of Inability to Afford Payment of Court Costs with the petition.

## 8. What happens next, and what you do not do

You do **not** serve anybody. The clerk sends the petition and the notice of hearing to each agency and entity you listed, by certified mail return receipt requested or by secure e-mail, electronic transmission or fax. A state or local agency that has an e-mail address on the list must accept electronic service.

You do not have to chase the FBI. The clerk does not notify the central federal depositories — DPS does that when it receives the papers.

The court will set a hearing **no earlier than the 30th day** after you file.

Keep a certified copy of the order when it is signed.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- You cannot confirm the offence date or the subsection from court records.
- The offence happened on or after 1 September 2021.
- The State opposes your petition, or asks the court to keep the records.
- The arrest produced several charges that ended in different ways.
- Any felony charge came out of the same incident, even against someone else, even if it was never filed against you.
- You ever failed to appear, skipped bail, or left the jurisdiction after being released on bond.
- The arrest may have been made on a warrant for violating probation or community supervision.
- You were arrested in one county and the offence was alleged to have happened in another.
- You are not a United States citizen, or immigration is a concern for you.
- You want to challenge the underlying case rather than clear the record.
