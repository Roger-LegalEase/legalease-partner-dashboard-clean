# How to complete and file your papers after a veterans or mental health court dismissal

Texas calls this **expunction**. Use that word with the clerk. It is not nondisclosure, which seals a record instead of destroying it and is filed somewhere else entirely.

This route is fast, free, and narrow. Three things to know before anything else:

**It is free.** The court may not charge you any fee or assess any cost for an order entered on this route. There is a second law that says the same thing a different way. If a clerk asks you for money, do not pay it — see the last section.

**There is a deadline, not a waiting period.** The court can enter the order **no later than the 30th day** after your case was dismissed, or after the court receives the dismissal information. Waiting does not help you here. Waiting closes the door.

**The prosecutor has to agree.** The attorney who represents the State has to consent before the court can use this route. Nobody here asks for that consent, negotiates it, or can get it for you. If it does not come, or if the 30 days run out, you are not finished — see "If the fast route closes" below.

## What is in this packet

- The petition asking the district court to enter the expunction order.
- The proposed Order of Expunction for the judge to sign.
- The information package the statute requires you to give the court.
- An affidavit saying you have not already used this route on this ground.
- A separate attachment listing any private background-check companies.
- This instruction sheet, which you keep. It is never filed.

## 1. Check that this is one of the two programmes

This route reaches exactly two things:

- A **veterans treatment court programme** under Government Code Chapter 124.
- A **mental health court programme** under Government Code Chapter 125.

A dismissal after any other programme — drug court, an ordinary diversion, anything else — is not this route. If you are not sure which programme yours was, ask the court that ran it before you file.

## 2. Check the calendar

Find the date your case was dismissed. The court can enter this order no later than the **30th day** after that, or after the court receives the dismissal information.

If that date has passed, do not file this packet. Go to "If the fast route closes".

## 3. Check that you have not used this ground before

You can get one expunction on each ground, once. You have to file an affidavit saying so, and it is in this packet.

An expunction you got on some **other** ground does not close this one. An expunction you already got on **this** ground does.

## 4. Get your DPS criminal history

Follow the Texas Department of Public Safety's form CR-63, *Procedure for Review of Personal Criminal History Record Information*. It is an instruction sheet, not a form to fill in. It sets out fingerprinting through the DPS FAST vendor — a $10 fingerprinting fee plus a $15 record fee — or a hard-card route by mail.

It carries the **TRN**, the incident number that has to go on the order, and it gives you the arrest date, the arresting agency and a starting point for the agency list.

DPS will not help you with this. Its own material says its legal staff cannot assist, and that DPS may oppose an expunction.

## 5. Call the district clerk and ask for the agency list

Every district clerk in Texas has to compile a list of agencies and e-mail addresses and keep it on the clerk's website. Ask for it by name.

The clerk is **not** responsible for it being complete — it is a starting point, so add anything you know of that is missing.

Every e-mail address on the list matters. The clerk cannot charge anything to send papers electronically, but must charge **$25 for each listed entity that cannot receive an electronic transmission**, and the same charge structure comes back at the order stage.

## 6. Build the two lists carefully

**The agency list.** Two hard rules:

- Never list the same state or local agency twice.
- Never list separate divisions of the same agency with different contacts or addresses.

**The private-entity list.** Any background-check company that has reported this arrest goes on a **separate** attachment. Never merge it into the agency list.

## 7. Fill in what only you can fill in

The papers have to carry your full name, your sex, your race, your date of birth, your driver's licence number, your social security number and the address you lived at when you were arrested. None of that is filled in for you. You write it in by hand.

Ask the clerk how that county handles sensitive information in a filing that is public until the order issues. Counties differ.

Anything you cannot supply must either appear or be accompanied by a short explanation of why it is not included.

## 8. Sign, verify, and swear the affidavit

You sign the petition, and it must be **verified**.

There are two blocks in the packet: a **notarial verification** block, and an **unsworn declaration under penalty of perjury**. Use whichever your county accepts — ask when you call about the agency list. Execute one, not both.

The one-expunction-per-ground **affidavit is separate** and is required by the statute in its own right. It is not an alternative to the verification. You sign that too.

## 9. File it — in a district court

File with the **district clerk of the county where you were arrested, or where the offence was alleged to have happened** — not where you live. You can file through eFileTexas, in person, or by mail where the clerk allows.

It has to be a **district court**. Justice courts and municipal courts of record are not available on this route, even if the offence was fine-only.

## 10. If a clerk asks you for money

The court may not charge any fee or assess any cost for an order entered on this route, and a separate statute requires the district-court filing fee to be waived on this entitlement.

If a clerk asks you to pay, do not pay and do not argue. Stop and talk to a lawyer.

## If the fast route closes

If the prosecutor does not consent, or the 30 days run out, **you are not out of options.** The ordinary expunction petition route is still open to you, because your entitlement comes from a different statute than the fast route does. It is slower and it is not free, but it is there.

Ask about that route rather than giving up on the record.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- The attorney representing the State does not consent, or opposes the expunction, or asks the court to keep the records.
- The 30-day window has closed.
- You have already had an expunction on this same ground.
- Your programme was not a Chapter 124 veterans treatment court or a Chapter 125 mental health court.
- A clerk demands a filing fee.
- The arrest produced several charges that ended in different ways.
- Any felony charge came out of the same incident, even against someone else, even if it was never filed against you.
- You ever failed to appear, skipped bail, or left the jurisdiction after being released on bond.
- You were arrested in one county and the offence was alleged to have happened in another.
- You are not a United States citizen, or immigration is a concern for you.
- You want to challenge the underlying case rather than clear it.
