# How to complete and file your application to clear an arrest that was not yours

This route is different from every other Texas expunction, in three ways that matter before you do anything else.

**It does not start at a court.** Your application goes to a prosecutor — the attorney who represents the State in felony prosecutions in the county where you live, or where the offence was said to have happened. That office reviews it and, if it checks out, sends it on to the district court and asks the judge to sign the order.

**There is no hearing.** Once the prosecutor forwards it, the court enters a final order without one.

**What you get is narrower than an ordinary expunction.** Your name and identifying information are taken out of the arrest record and the actual arrested person's information is put in instead. The file itself is not destroyed or returned, and the index references stay. The arrest record survives — you leave it.

## What is in this packet

- The verified application to the prosecutor. This is the main document.
- A petition and a proposed order for the district court stage, so the prosecutor has them to forward.
- A separate attachment listing any private background-check companies.
- This instruction sheet, which you keep. It is never filed.

Two more things have to go with it, and neither is written for you here:

- **Authenticated fingerprint records.** See step 1. These are not optional.
- **A Statement of Inability to Afford Payment of Court Costs**, if you cannot pay court costs at the later stage. That is the Supreme Court of Texas's own bilingual form, and the clerk has to give it to you free, without you asking, under Rule 145.

## 1. Book your fingerprints

Book an appointment at a Texas DPS FAST location and get **authenticated fingerprint records**. They are a mandatory part of the application, and they are the whole point of it: they are the evidence that you are not the person who was arrested.

You submit them with the application yourself. Nobody here receives them or looks at them.

If you cannot get authenticated fingerprint records, stop. The application will not work without them.

## 2. Be clear with yourself about what happened

This route is open in exactly two situations:

- Your identifying information was written down wrong by mistake — a clerical error, and that is the only reason your information is in the record.
- Someone else who was arrested gave your name and identifying information without your permission, and that deception is the only reason your information is in the record.

If you gave permission, this is not your route.

If you were in fact the person arrested, this is not your route.

If your information is in that record for any other reason, this is not your route.

You do not have to have been arrested at all for this to apply. If your identifiers simply ended up in an arrest record, that is enough.

## 3. Get your DPS criminal history

Follow the Texas Department of Public Safety's form CR-63, *Procedure for Review of Personal Criminal History Record Information*. It is an instruction sheet, not a form to fill in. It sets out fingerprinting through the DPS FAST vendor — a $10 fingerprinting fee plus a $15 record fee — or a hard-card route by mail.

It carries the **TRN**, the incident number that has to go on the order, and it shows you the arrest date, the arresting agency and what agencies are involved.

DPS will not help you with the application. Its own material says its legal staff cannot assist with one.

## 4. Leave blank anything you genuinely do not know

This is the one place in the Texas record-clearing system where that is the right answer. You were not the person arrested, so you will not know some of the arrest details.

The prosecutor's job is to fill in the arrest information you did not have, attach the agency list and forward the whole thing to the district court. Do not guess at facts about an arrest that was not yours.

## 5. Call the district clerk and ask for the agency list

Every district clerk in Texas has to compile a list of agencies and e-mail addresses and keep it on the clerk's website. Ask for it by name.

The clerk is **not** responsible for it being complete — it is a starting point. And every e-mail address on it saves money: the clerk cannot charge anything to send papers electronically, but must charge **$25 for each listed entity that cannot receive an electronic transmission**, and the same charge structure comes back at the order stage.

## 6. Build the two lists carefully

**The agency list.** Two hard rules, and on this route they come from the statute that governs your application specifically:

- Never list the same state or local agency twice.
- Never list separate divisions of the same agency with different contacts or addresses.

**The private-entity list.** Any background-check company that has reported this arrest goes on a **separate** attachment. Never merge it into the agency list.

## 7. Fill in what only you can fill in

The papers have to carry your full name, your sex, your race, your date of birth, your driver's licence number, your social security number and your address. None of that is filled in for you. You write it in by hand.

Ask how that county handles sensitive information in a filing that is public until the order issues. Counties differ.

## 8. Sign and verify

The application must be **verified**. You sign it.

There are two blocks in the packet: a **notarial verification** block, and an **unsworn declaration under penalty of perjury**. Practice differs on which is accepted, and this route goes to a prosecutor's office rather than a clerk, so ask that office which they want before you execute either. Execute one, not both.

## 9. Deliver the application

Deliver the verified application, with your authenticated fingerprint records, to the **attorney representing the State in the prosecution of felonies** in the county where you live, or in the county where the offence was said to have happened.

Not to a court. Not to the clerk. To that prosecutor's office.

## 10. What happens next — and where it can stop

The prosecutor reviews and verifies what you have said. If it checks out, that office supplements the arrest information you did not know, attaches the agency list, sends the application to the district court and asks for the order. The court then enters a final order without a hearing.

**If the prosecutor does not verify and forward it, nothing reaches the court.** Nobody here can make that happen or do it for you. If the office declines, stop and talk to a lawyer.

Keep a certified copy of the order when it is signed.

## Stop and talk to a lawyer if

- Anyone disputes whether you are the person who was arrested.
- The prosecutor's office declines to verify or forward your application.
- You cannot get authenticated fingerprint records.
- You gave permission for someone to use your information.
- Your information is in that record for some reason other than a clerical error or someone else's deception.
- The State opposes the application, or asks the court to keep the records.
- You want to challenge the underlying case rather than clear your name out of it.
- You are not a United States citizen, or immigration is a concern for you.
