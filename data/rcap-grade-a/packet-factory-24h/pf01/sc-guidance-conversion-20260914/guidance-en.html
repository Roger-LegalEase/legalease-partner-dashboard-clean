General Sessions charges ending without conviction

About this route

Ask the solicitor about a General Sessions charge discharged, dismissed, nolle prossed or ending in acquittal under § 17-1-40. Bring the disposition and any plea arrangement involving other charges so the office can apply the correct fee exception.

What you submit

Apply and sign through the solicitor’s office. This guide is not an application, petition or proposed order and supplies no document for you to file with a court. LegalEase does not replace the mandatory official form.

Who handles the process

The circuit solicitor coordinates the application and required verification or attestations; a judge signs an authorized order. Only the responsible officials complete their findings and signatures.

Before proceeding

Do not treat this guide as a finding that you qualify. Ask the solicitor to resolve missing records, uncertain statutory coverage, prior uses, other charges and the applicable waiting period before signing or paying.

Where to go

Use the Solicitor’s Office in the South Carolina judicial circuit where the offense occurred. Ask its expungement staff for the office-supplied SCCA223A1 GS and its current appointment, payment and signing instructions.

Start here

Request your SLED criminal history and the court docket and disposition. Then contact the circuit solicitor’s expungement staff with the county, exact charge, case number and disposition.

Documents to gather

  • Court docket and certified disposition: Ask the clerk of the court that handled the case for the docket and, where available, a certified disposition. The docket carries the warrant or indictment number, the court level and the disposition date the application needs.
  • SLED criminal history record: Request your own criminal history record from SLED. It is not required for this route, but it is the only reliable way to see every charge on your record before you choose which to apply for.

Keep these boundaries

  • Do not file this guide as a court pleading or fill, sign or submit a LegalEase replacement order.
  • Do not take possession of the official application during the expungement process; arrange your signature with the solicitor’s office.
  • Do not complete SLED verification, program or official attestations, the solicitor’s consent, judicial findings or a judge’s signature.
  • Do not use personal checks; confirm the separate certified checks or money orders and payees with the office.

Timing

Ask the solicitor to calculate any waiting period from your actual records and confirm readiness before payment or signature. Completion of a program alone does not replace the required official processing.

Steps and separate fees

  • Gather the records listed above.
  • Ask the solicitor to assess this exact route and supply SCCA223A1 GS.
  • Confirm the separately stated fees and any route-specific exemptions: No $250 solicitor administrative fee, unless the charge was dismissed, discharged or nolle prossed as part of the plea arrangement under which the defendant pled guilty and was sentenced to other charges, in which case the $250 applies. No $25 SLED verification fee. No $35 clerk of court filing fee where the charge was discharged, dismissed, nolle prossed or the applicant was acquitted.
  • The § 17-22-940(A)(1) exemption is the operative relief on this route and is automatic rather than discretionary. The solicitor may waive the $250 fee only where a person has been falsely accused as a result of identity theft, § 17-22-940(J). Each solicitor's office also maintains a private donation account which may defray up to fifty per cent of the administrative fee on a first-come first-served basis, § 17-22-940(A)(2).
  • Arrange the official signatures and any director or judicial attestation with the office.
  • Ask how to obtain completion confirmation and an available copy of the order.

After the office acts

Follow the office’s required verification and attestation sequence. The solicitor handles filing and distribution of the signed order. Ask for confirmation and your available copy after completion; do not assume every record has changed immediately.

When to get legal help

If the solicitor declines consent, stop this self-help route. Section 17-22-940(I) provides for retained counsel to seek a circuit court determination. Seek legal help for disputed eligibility, conflicting records or immigration consequences; this guide provides no contested court petition.
